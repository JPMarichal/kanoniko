## Illustration (Ilustración) — Concept Reference

### Definition (Rhetorical)
An illustration is a concrete instance — narrative, comparative, or descriptive — that makes an abstract truth visible, felt, and memorable. From Latin *illustrare*: "to light up." Not decoration but illumination.

Spurgeon's metaphor: "A window in the wall of the sermon" — doctrine is structure; illustration lets light in.

### Taxonomy by Form
- **Anecdote:** brief true narrative (personal or third-party)
- **Parable:** fictional narrative with analogical meaning
- **Analogy:** structural comparison between two domains
- **Metaphor/Simile:** compressed comparison (single image)
- **Hypothetical scenario:** invented "what if" situation
- **Historical incident:** true event from history
- **Quotation:** words of an authority, witness, or poet
- **Word picture:** vivid description creating mental imagery
- **Contrast / Before-after:** juxtaposition of two states
- **Object lesson:** physical object as teaching anchor
- **Testimony:** (SUD-specific) speaker's own spiritual witness — the most powerful form because it invites the Spirit

### Five Functions in Discourse
1. **Clarify** — make the abstract understandable
2. **Prove** — show by concrete instance that something is true
3. **Apply** — show how truth operates in real life
4. **Capture attention** — recapture the distracted listener
5. **Create emotional resonance** — move from intellectual assent to felt conviction

### Structural Position
Concept → Evidence (scripture/quote) → **Illustration** → Application
The illustration is the emotional bridge between evidence and application.

### Quality Criteria (Good Illustration)
1. **Pertinent** — serves the exact point, no digressions
2. **Proportional** — length doesn't exceed importance of point
3. **Authentic** — rings true (whether real or hypothetical)
4. **Emotionally precise** — right emotion at right intensity
5. **Fresh** — surprises by recognition, not familiarity
6. **Culturally accessible** — intelligible to actual audience
7. **Self-effacing** — speaker is not the hero of their own story

### Bad Illustration Signs
- Overshadows the point it should illuminate
- Forced analogy that breaks under scrutiny
- Recycled cliché with no personal connection
- Self-aggrandizing anecdote
- Emotional manipulation without doctrinal substance
- So complex it needs its own explanation

### Dual Role in Alejandría

**As Resource (cross-product):**
- ALL products must be richly illustrated where applicable (discursos, artículos, dossiers)
- Each illustration has an objective (clarify/prove/apply/attention/resonance)
- Subject to FCD when used as citation or insertion; may be paraphrased for brevity
- Templates must include illustration section/guidance

**As Product (extractable library):**
- Extracted from existing corpus: general conference talks and Church magazines are primary sources
- May be paraphrased (not always verbatim) — must be brief and easy to cite/apply
- Metadata: objective, paraphrased (bool), author, source, topic tags
- Extraction skill: `/ilustracion` — specialized in mining illustrations from corpus
- Backlog tracks extraction priorities (conference talks > magazines > manuals > books)
- Stored in `prods/ilustraciones/`

### Relationship to Other Devices
- **Example** proves by instance; illustration illuminates by narrative/comparison
- **Analogy** is one form of illustration, not a synonym
- **Metaphor** is compressed; illustration is developed
- **Parable** is fictional illustration; not all illustrations are parables
- **Testimony** is illustration + spiritual proof + covenant obligation (SUD-specific)
