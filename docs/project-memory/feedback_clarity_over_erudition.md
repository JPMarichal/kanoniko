---
name: Clarity over erudition in articles
description: Prefer clarity for the target reader over academic completeness. Cut scholarly details that confuse more than they clarify.
type: feedback
---

When writing articles for the blog, prefer clarity for the target reader over academic thoroughness. If a scholarly detail (textual criticism, manuscript history, etc.) adds confusion rather than understanding, cut it.

**Why:** In the Mary Magdalene article, I added a note about the Pericope Adulterae (John 7:53–8:11) being a later textual insertion. The user said: "Causa más confusión que claridad." The reader doesn't need to know about manuscript traditions to understand that the adulterous woman is not Mary Magdalene.

**How to apply:** Before including an academic detail, ask: does this help the reader understand the main argument, or does it open a rabbit hole? If the latter, leave it out. The article's job is to inform and persuade, not to demonstrate erudition.
