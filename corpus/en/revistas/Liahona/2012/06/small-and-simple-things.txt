[SIDEBAR] <ul class="bullet"> <li> <p data-aid="127250875" id="aside1_p1"><span class="page-break" data-page="9"></span>Well in advance of the meeting, make sure that the presiding authority approves the music that has been selected.</p> </li> <li> <p data-aid="127250876" id="aside1_p2">Several days in advance, contact the accompanist so he or she knows which hymns to practice for the meeting.</p> </li> <li> <p data-aid="127250877" id="aside1_p3">For sacrament meetings, consider the spirit that each hymn will bring. Opening hymns in sacrament meeting express praise and gratitude to God and for the Restoration of the gospel. Sacrament hymns reflect on the sacrament or on the Savior’s sacrifice. Closing hymns can inspire the congregation to recommit to the covenants they have renewed and can testify of the gospel principles members have learned.</p> </li> <li> <p data-aid="127250878" id="aside1_p4">For Relief Society or priesthood meetings, consult with the instructor. He or she may want to suggest a hymn related to the lesson. If the instructor does not have a preference, leaders could suggest a hymn that will supplement the topic of the lesson.</p> </li> </ul> <aside class="more-info" id="aside1_aside1"> <p data-aid="127250879" id="aside1_aside1_p1">See <em>Hymns,</em> <span class="legacy-cross-ref" data-legacy-href="../../../null">380–81</span>; <em>Handbook 2: Administering the Church</em> (2010), <a class="cross-ref" href="/study/manual/handbook-2-administering-the-church/music/music?lang=eng&amp;id=title20#title20">14.4</a>; <a class="cross-ref" href="/study/manual/handbook-2-administering-the-church/music/music?lang=eng&amp;id=title27#title27">14.6</a>.</p> </aside> [/SIDEBAR]
