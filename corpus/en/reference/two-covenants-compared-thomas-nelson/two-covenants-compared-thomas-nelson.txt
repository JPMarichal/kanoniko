The General Epistles, sometimes called the “Catholic Epistles” (the Greek term

katholikos means “universal”) include the epistles of James; 1 and 2 Peter; 1, 2,

and 3 John; and Jude. In contrast to the Pauline epistles, which are named accord-

ing to their recipients, the General Epistles are designated by the name of their

author. These letters, found near the end of the New Testament, are for the most

part addressed to the church in general or to groups of churches (3 John is clearly

addressed to an individual and 2 John may be)—hence the term “general.”

Like the Pauline epistles, the General Epistles follow the customary form for Hel-

lenistic Greek correspondence: an introduction listing the author and recipients

with a greeting and thanksgiving, followed by the body or substance of the letter,

and a conclusion.

The original destinations of the General Epistles are often uncertain and the

greetings are correspondingly general. James writes to Jewish Christians “scattered

abroad,” Peter to Christians throughout Asia Minor, and the Johannine letters were

probably likewise written to churches and individuals in Asia Minor. Jude writes to

an unnamed region troubled by false teachers.
