Vanities of the World

Following his sacred interview with both the Father and the Son, Joseph Smith "was entangled again in the vanities of the world" (D&C 20:5). Vanity, according to Webster, is something that is of no real value, useless, or empty.

The "vanities" with which Joseph had become "entangled," were the valueless, "foolish errors Â� and weakness[es] of youth." They were not "great or malignant sins:" but, rather, the "levity" which the young Prophet felt was "not consistent with that character which ought to be maintained by one who was called of God" (JS-H 1:28).

If pursued, vanity can bring condemnation (D&C 84:55). This may have something to do with the Lord's admonition to make proper use of one's stewardship of time and talents, to be "anxiously engaged" in good causes (D&C 58:26-28; 88:118-24), rather than to waste either time or talent on frivolity, carnality or other valueless pursuits of this life.
