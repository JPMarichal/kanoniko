Dust of the Earth

"For dust thou wast, and unto dust shalt thou return," declared Deity to Adam (Moses 4:25; Gen. 3:19; italics added). When Adam fell he became mortal or of the dust of this earth. Adam's corporeal body was created from the dust of this earth; in other words, it was organized from those elements which belong to this planet Earth, making it an "earthy" body (Moses 3:7; Abr. 5:7; Gen. 2:7; Alma 42:2; D&C 77:12; 128:14). Elder Russell M. Nelson noted that "compounds derived from dust-elements of the earth-are combined to make each living cell in our bodies" (En., May 1987, p. 10).

When man dies, "the dust return[s] to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it" (Eccl. 12:7). On the day of resurrection, the "sleeping dust" is restored to its perfect frame as the spirit is inseparably united with a glorified, heavenly body that is no longer quickened by mortal blood, but by spirit (D&C 138:17; Lev. 17:11; 1 Cor. 15:50; TPJS, 199-200, 326).

During the Millennium, "old men shall die; but they shall not sleep in the dust, but they shall be changed in the twinkling of an eye" (D&C 63:51). Thus, there will be no graves in the dust of the ground during this period of time, for those who die shall be instantaneously resurrected.
