Good Shepherd

The Lord refers to his people as his sheep (D&C 10:59, 60; 112:14; 3 Ne. 15:24), his flock (D&C 6:34; 35:27), and his fold (D&C 10:59), and to himself as the "good shepherd" (D&C 50:44; Hel. 7:18; John 10:14). According to Webster's Unabridged Dictionary, a scriptural shepherd is "one charged with the religious care and guidance of others." One with this title safeguards the flock from wolves, or spiritual predators (Acts 20:29).

Our relationship to the good shepherd was described by Alma: "I say unto you, that the good shepherd doth call you; yea, and in his own name he doth call you, which is the name of Christ; and if ye will not hearken unto the voice of the good shepherd, to the name by which ye are called, behold, ye are not the sheep of the good shepherd. And now if ye are not the sheep of the good shepherd, of what fold are ye? Behold, I say unto you, that the devil is your shepherd, and ye are of his fold." (Alma 5:38-39.)
