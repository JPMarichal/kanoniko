Hoyt W. Brewster, Doctrine and Covenants Encyclopedia ( 1988)

Foster, Robert D.

Four verses in section 124 are devoted to chastising Robert D. Foster (D&C 124:115-18). In particular, he was admonished to help in the building of the Nauvoo House. There are about three dozen references to him in Joseph Smith's history, with the majority of them mentioning his misdeeds.

Foster served as surgeon-general of the Nauvoo Legion, but one month following his excommunication in April 1844, he was court-martialed "for unbecoming and unofficer-like conduct" (HC 6:355, 362). The close proximity of this date to that of the martyrdom of the Prophet Joseph-June 27, 1844-appears to be no coincidence. A letter sent by Foster from Carthage, Illinois, dated June 20, 1844, makes reference to the "thousands of armed men" who were gathering for their unhallowed deeds (HC 6:520). He is mentioned as an "accessory" to the murders of Joseph and Hyrum on several occasions and even admitted as much (HC 7:146, 169, 513). The following is a reported conversation Foster had with Abraham C. Hodge in November 1845:

"I am the most miserable wretch that the sun shines upon," said Foster. "If I could recall eighteen months of my life I would be willing to sacrifice everything I have upon earth, my wife and child not excepted. I did love Joseph Smith more than any man that ever lived, if I had been present I would have stood between him and death." To this Hodge replied, "Why did you do as you have done? You were accessory to his murder." Foster then said: "I know that, and I have not seen one moment's peace since that time. I know that Mormonism is true, and the thought of meeting (Joseph and Hyrum) at the bar of God is more awful to me than anything else." (HC 7:513.) No further mention is made of Foster.
