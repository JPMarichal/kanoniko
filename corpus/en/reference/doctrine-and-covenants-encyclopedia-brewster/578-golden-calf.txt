Golden Calf

The reference to the golden calf with which Almon Babbitt was enticing the people of Nauvoo apparently refers to his counseling the Saints to leave that place, contrary to divine counsel, in order to seek for worldly gain (D&C 124:84; DCC, 658).

The golden calf is a symbol of worldly worship as contrasted to true divine devotion. It originated in the days of the exodus of the children of Israel from Egypt, when Aaron was persuaded to fashion an image of a calf out of gold as an object of worship. The people wanted something tangible to worship rather than developing the faith necessary to worship the true and living God. Moses destroyed the original golden calf by burning it, grinding it into powder, and then strewing it upon the water, which he then made the people drink. (Ex. 32.)

"Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (Matt. 6:24).
