Hoyt W. Brewster, Doctrine and Covenants Encyclopedia ( 1988)

Prison

The word prison is used several different ways in the Doctrine and Covenants. One usage refers to an earthly incarceration (D&C 122:6). Other citations refer to the spiritual confinement of the unbelieving or to the condition experienced when the spirit is separated from the body (D&C 76:73; 128:22; 138:8-10, 18-21, 28-37, 42).

The unbelieving prisoners are released from their captivity in the darkness of their spiritual prison and brought forth into the light first through the preaching of the gospel by the faithful who also inhabit the spirit world, and next through the performance of sacred, saving ordinances by the living in behalf of those who have departed mortality. In addition, all are released from a prison, or state of bondage, when their spirits are reunited with their bodies in the glory of the resurrection, made possible by Him who holds the keys to those prison doors.
