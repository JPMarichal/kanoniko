God of Enoch

Jesus Christ refers to himself only once as "the God of Enoch, and his brethren" (D&C 45:11). In the context of this statement, the Lord refers to this title as having originated with the people of the Church: "Ye say [I am] the God of Enoch, and his brethren."

The "God of Enoch" is the same God who was the God of all Old Testament people, he who came to earth in the meridian of time as Jesus the Christ. He it was who proclaimed himself to be the "Great I Am" to Moses and the children of Israel (Ex. 3:2-15); who claimed that identity while in the flesh (John 8:57-58); and who revealed himself as such to a latter-day prophet: "Hearken and listen to the voice of him who is from all eternity to all eternity, the Great I Am, even Jesus Christ" (D&C 39:1).

He is not only the God of "Enoch, and his brethren" but also of all the great patriarchs and peoples of past, present, and future ages.
