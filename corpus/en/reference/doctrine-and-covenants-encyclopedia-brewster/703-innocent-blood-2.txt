Innocent Blood (#2)

In recounting the tragedy at Carthage, John Taylor spoke of the "innocent blood" of the martyrs, Joseph and Hyrum Smith (D&C 135:7). Their innocence stems not from living lives of perfection, for they too fought occasional failings in mortal moments. However, their victorious struggles had won for them a degree of spirituality unequaled by the majority of mankind. The innocence of Joseph and Hyrum is expressed in Joseph's own prophetic proclamation, as he resolutely faced the road to martyrdom. "I am going like a lamb to the slaughter," he said, "but I am calm as a summer's morning; I have a conscience void of offense towards God, and towards all men. I shall die innocent, and it shall yet be said of me-he was murdered in cold blood." (D&C 135:4.)

The Prophet's uncle, John Smith, was later to state: "I was in jail with him [Joseph] and his brother Hyrum a few hours before they were killed, and I can testify before God, that they died innocent of any crime, and that they sealed their testimony with their blood" (CR, Apr. 1927, p. 85: italics added).
