Hoyt W. Brewster, Doctrine and Covenants Encyclopedia ( 1988)

Forgiveness

One of the great promises of the gospel is found in the Lord's declaration that he will "forgive sins unto those who confess their sins before me and ask forgiveness, who have not sinned unto death" (D&C 64:7). While there are certain sins which can never be expiated, God alone being the judge of those sins, mortal man has been commanded "to forgive all men" (D&C 64:8-10; Mosiah 26:31).

The bruised and bleeding Redeemer, hanging from the torturous cross, was himself the epitomy of the virtues he had preached when he pleaded, "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34).

God's forgiveness carries with it the promise of divine forgetfulness: "Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the same is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them no more" (D&C 58:42). Man's forgiveness of his neighbor's follies and transgressions should be no less magnanimous as he seeks to erase them from his memory.
