Hoyt W. Brewster, Doctrine and Covenants Encyclopedia ( 1988)

Confirm

The word confirm is used in three different ways in the Doctrine and Covenants. Its initial appearance (20:10) is in the sense of verification, as a corroborative witness. It is possibly used in this same sense in several other scriptural citations (D&C 27:12; 84:42, 48), although in these instances, and in several others (D&C 84:18, 30; 107:40), an ordinance or ordination is implied.

The most common use of the term confirm in the Church today is in connection with the baptism of the Holy Ghost and "fire" (D&C 20:41; 33:11; 39:6). This is an ordinance, following the baptism of water, wherein one is confirmed a member of the Church by the laying on of hands. At this time the new member is commanded to receive the gift of the Holy Ghost (D&C 20:41, 43, 68; 33:15).

The Prophet Joseph said, "Baptism by water is but half a baptism, and is good for nothing without the other half-that is, the baptism of the Holy Ghost" (TPJS, 314). "The Gift of the Holy Ghost by the laying on of hands, cannot be received through the medium of any other principle than the principle of righteousness, for if the proposals are not complied with, it is of no use, but withdraws" (TPJS, 148). This gift is found only within The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (TPJS, 199; JD 14:304; CHMR 1:154).
