Prudent

In the same manner in which wile can be applied both negatively and positively (1 Cor. 3:19; Proverbs 12:15), the term prudent can also be seen both ways. The Lord declared himself to be prudent (2 Ne. 20:13; Isa. 10:13); yet, he speaks harshly against those who are "prudent in their own sight," for the things of God shall be hid from them (2 Ne. 15:21; Isa. 5:21; D&C 76:9; 128:18). Webster states that one who is prudent is capable of directing or conducting oneself wisely and judiciously-cautious, circumspect, and discreet in conduct. The "prudent" against whom the Lord speaks, are those who feign prudence or who appear to be "prudent in their own sight" but are blind and "looking beyond the mark," which is the "stone of Israel," even Jesus the Christ (Jacob 4:14-16; D&C 50:44).

To exercise prudence (D&C 89:11; Abr. 3:21) is to use discretion or to take the best course of action.
