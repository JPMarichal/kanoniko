Garfield Beach

GARFIELD BEACH was the name of a popular bathing resort on the east shore of the Great Salt Lake, near its southern limit. It was named in honor of General James A. Garfield, who visited the location in 1875. About this time the Utah and Nevada Railroad line was extended and ran near the shore where the bathing resorts of Black Rock, Garfield and Lake Point were established.

At Garfield a hotel was erected and a pier leading to a small dancing hall where the famous Magnus Olson orchestra discoursed good music. A number of bath houses and also an extensive bowery were built. A steam tug, formerly known as the "City of Corinne," was later named "General Garfield," and made pleasure trips on the lake.

After Saltair was built and the smaller bathing resorts closed, Garfield became the location of the magnificent plants of the American Smelting and Refining Company and nearby were erected the colossal concentrators of the Utah Copper Company, known as the Magna and Arthur plants.
