Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Glencoe Branch

GLENCOE BRANCH, Woodruff Stake, Lincoln Co., Wyoming, consisted of a few families of Latter-day Saints who resided in the mining town of Glencoe situated about six miles south of Kemmerer. The saints who had located temporarily at Glencoe were organized as a branch of the Church Aug. 25, 1901, with Martin McPhie as presiding Elder. This organization was continued until the summer of 1903, when the mines at Glencoe closed down and the camp was practically vacated. Only one family of saints resided at Glencoe in 1928. During its existence the Glencoe Branch had a Sunday school, a Relief Society, and a Deacons Quorum. There was a Sunday school at Glencoe as late as 1922, when the mines had resumed temporary operations.
