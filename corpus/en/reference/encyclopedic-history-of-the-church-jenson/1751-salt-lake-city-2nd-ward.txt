Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Salt Lake City 2nd Ward

SALT LAKE CITY 2ND WARD, Liberty Stake, one of the original nineteen wards of Salt Lake City, organized Feb. 22, 1849, consists of Latter-day Saints residing in that part of Salt Lake City which is bounded on the north by 6th South St. (or the 9th Ward), east by 6th East St. (or the 1st Ward), south by 9th South St. (or Liberty Ward), and west by 3rd East St. (or the 3rd Ward). These boundaries are the same as when the ward was organized; later the southern boundary was extended as settlers took up land south of 9th South St., but the organization of Liberty Ward in 1907 reduced the 2nd Ward to its former limits.

At the time of its organization, John Lowry was set apart as Bishop. He, with James Leach and Horace Drake, soon afterwards made a ditch from the mouth of Emigration Canyon to convey water to the settlers of the ward.

The first Scandinavian saints who arrived in Utah in 1852, located in the 2nd Ward and the first Scandinavian meetings in Utah were also held in this ward.

An adobe school house was erected on 7th South St. between 4th and 5th East streets in 1852. This was replaced by a brick building in 1883, which, after the erection of a fine ward chapel on the corner of 7th South and 5th East streets, was used as a knitting factory.

From the time of its organization the 2nd Ward belonged to the Salt Lake Stake of Zion, but when Liberty Stake was organized in 1904, it became part of that stake.

John Lowry, the first Bishop of the Salt Lake City 2nd Ward, was succeeded in 1851 by Joseph C. Kingsbury, who was succeeded in 1854 by Isaac Hill, who was succeeded in 1864 by Phineas H. Young, who was succeeded in 1871 by James Leach (acting Bishop), who was succeeded in 1877 by Samuel Peterson, who was succeeded in 1890 by Leonard G. Hardy, who was succeeded in 1899 by Heber C. Iverson, who was succeeded in 1919 by Henry B. Elder, who was succeeded in 1921 by Albert J. Elggren, who acted Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the ward had 1,584 members, including 301 children.
