Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

American Fork

AMERICAN FORK, Alpine Stake, Utah Co., Utah, is an incorporated city, situated in the north end of Utah County, 14 miles northwest of Provo, the county seat, and 33 miles south of Salt Lake City. It is a growing and important city and the center of business in that part of Utah County lying north of Utah Lake. The center of American Fork is about 3 1/4 miles southwest of the mouth of American Fork Canyon and about a mile north of Utah Lake. /b American Fork is the center of a prosperous farming and fruit-growing district.

American Fork was settled by Latter-day Saints in the fall of 1850 and organized as a regular bishop's ward May 25, 1851, with Leonard E. Harrington as Bishop. He presided about 32 years, or until he died, June 21, 1833; he was succeeded Sept. 2, 1883, by William Michael Bromley, who was succeeded in 1889 by George Halliday, who in 1894 was succeeded by Wm. D. Robinson, who presided until July 14, 1901, when American Fork was divided into four bishop's wards, viz., American Fork 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th wards. These four wards together with Alpine and Highland (organized in 1915) now (1930) constitute the Alpine Stake of Zion. American Fork was incorporated as Lake City, June 4, 1853, but the name was afterwards changed to American Fork.

American Fork constituted a part of the Utah Stake of Zion until 1901, since which it has been the headquarters of the Alpine Stake. American Fork had 695 inhabitants in 1860, 1,115 in 1870, 1,825 in 1880, 1,942 in 1890, 2,732 in 1900, 3,220 in 1910, 3,290 in 1920 and 3,641 in 1930.
