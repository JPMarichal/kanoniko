Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Douglas Branch

DOUGLAS BRANCH, St. Joseph Stake, Cochise Co., Arizona, consisted of Latter-day Saints residing in and near the town of Douglas, situated near the boundary line between Mexico and Arizona, about 25 miles south of Bisbee, about 40 miles southeast of Tombstone and about 100 miles southeast of Thatcher, the headquarters of the St. Joseph Stake of Zion.

A few L. D. S. families who had located in Douglas were organized as a branch of the Church May 24, 1926, with J. Parley Bigelow as presiding Elder. This branch was organized as a regular bishop's ward Oct. 13, 1907, with J. Parley Bigelow as Bishop. The ward was disorganized May 28, 1909, and a branch organization effected with John T. Butler as presiding Elder. The Douglas Branch, according to the reports, went out of existence in 1910 and is now (1930) a branch of the California Mission.
