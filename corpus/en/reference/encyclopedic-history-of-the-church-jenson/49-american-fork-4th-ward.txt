Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

American Fork 4th Ward

AMERICAN FORK 4TH WARD consists of Latter-day Saints residing in the northeast part of the city of American Fork. It extends northward to the Highland Ward, east to the Manila Ward in the Timpanogos Stake, is separated from the American Fork 1st Ward on the south by Main St., and from the American Fork 3rd Ward on the west by 1st East St. The fine brick meeting house, or chapel, in which the saints of the American Fork 4th Ward worship, is situated on 3rd North St., between 4th and 5th East streets.

The American Fork 4th Ward was created July 14, 1901, when the city of American Fork was divided into four wards, and Warren B. Smith was sustained as Bishop of the 4th Ward; he was succeeded in 1915 by Earl S. Greenwood, who in 1928 was succeeded by Lot Robinson, who acted as Bishop of the ward Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the ward had 1,024 members, including 231 children.
