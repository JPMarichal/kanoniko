Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Declo Ward

DECLO WARD, Burley Stake, Cassia Co., Idaho, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in a farming district lying east of Burley. Declo, a station on the Burley and Idaho main branch of the Oregon Short Line Railroad, is nine miles east of Burley, near the south bank of the Snake River and on the state highway leading from Burley to Pocatello. It is also on the old Oregon Trail. The meeting house, a modern brick building erected in 1920, has an auditorium capable of seating 300 people.

Declo was formerly called Marshfield, and the saints there were organized as the Marshfield Branch in December, 1909, with Hyrum S. Lewis as presiding Elder. He was succeeded in 1913 by George C .Darrington, who presided over the Marshfield Branch until it was organized as the Declo Ward July 30, 1916, with Lorenzo E. Olson as Bishop. He was succeeded in 1927 by Raymond M. Whittier, who was succeeded in 1929 by John C. Darrington, who presided in 1930. The numerical strength of the Declo Ward Dec. 31, 1930, was 500, including 114 children. The total population in the Declo Precinct was 1,088 in 1930. Declo Ward belonged to the Cassia Stake until 1919, when it became part of the Burley Stake.
