Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

American Fork 3rd Ward

AMERICAN FORK 3RD WARD consists of Latter-day Saints residing in the northwest part of the city of American Fork and extends northward to the Highland Ward. It is separated from the American Fork 4th Ward on the east by 1st East Street and from the American Fork 2nd Ward on the south by 1st North Street. Westward the ward extends to Lehi. A fine meeting house, or chapel, occupies a central position in the ward, being situated on the corner of 3rd North and 2nd West streets.

American Fork 3rd Ward came into existence July 14, 1901, when the city of American Fork was divided into four wards, and John R. Hindley was chosen as Bishop of the American Fork 3rd Ward. He acted in that capacity until 1924, when he was succeeded by Jesse M. Walker, who in 1928 was succeeded by John Hunter, who in 1930 was succeeded by G. Edward Abel, jun., who acted as Bishop of the American Fork 3rd Ward Dec. 31, 1930. On this date the ward had a membership of 778, including 175 children.
