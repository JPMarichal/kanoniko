Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Thomas Ward

THOMAS WARD, Blackfoot Stake, Bingham Co., Idaho, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in a farming district on the northwest side of Snake River. The meeting house, a substantial edifice, built of lava rock in 1905, is located 10 miles southwest of Blackfoot and 25 miles northeast of Aberdeen.

The first Latter-day Saint settlers in that part of Snake River Valley now included in the Thomas Ward belonged to the Basalt Ward (Shelley Stake) and later to the Riverside Ward, but as the distance from either of these places to Thomas was considerable, a branch Sunday school, a Relief Society and a Y. M. M. I. A. were organized there in the nineties. The Thomas Ward was organized Nov. 30, 1902, with John R. Williams as Bishop. He acted until 1927, when he was succeeded by Philip B. Dance, who presided over the ward Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the Thomas Ward had 587 members, including 112 children. The total population of the Thomas Precinct was 1,004 in 1930.

Thomas Ward belonged to the Bingham Stake of Zion until 1904, when it became part of the Blackfoot Stake.
