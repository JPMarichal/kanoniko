Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Virden Ward

VIRDEN WARD, St. Joseph Stake, Hildalgo Co., New Mexico, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in the village of Virden and vicinity. This village in situated on the north hank of the upper Gila River, in the midst of a farming district, 8 miles by nearest road southeast of Duncan, Arizona, 6 miles south southeast of Franklin, the only other Mormon town on the upper Gila, and 54 miles east southeast of Thatcher, the headquarters of the St. Joseph Stake of Zion.

The first settlers in that part of the country now included in the Virden Ward were non-Mormon ranchmen. Among the early L. D. S. settlers who located in the Virden district were exiles from Colonia Diaz and other colonies in Mexico, who had to leave their homes in these colonies on account of the rebellion in Mexico in 1912. The first of these exiles arrived in Virden in March, 1915, and a number of them purchased the so-called Gila Ranch property, consisting of 825 acres of land, for $50,000. These saints were organized into a branch of the Church Dec. 29, 1915, called the Virden Branch (with Peter Mortensen as presiding Elder), honoring a Mr. Virden, the president of the Gila Ranch Company who had treated the brethren well from the beginning. The Virden Branch was organized as a ward June 17, 1916, with Willard E. Jones as Bishop. He was succeeded in 1926 by Junius E. Payne, who acted Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the Virden Ward had 455 members, including 110 children. The total population of Virden Precinct was 628 in 1930; of these 196 resided in the village of Virden.
