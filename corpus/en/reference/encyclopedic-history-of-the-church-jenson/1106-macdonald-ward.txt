Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Macdonald Ward

MACDONALD WARD, St. Joseph Stake, Cochise Co., Arizona, consisted of the Latter-day Saints residing on the San Pedro River, above St. David. It was a scattered farming settlement in which the people lived in comfortable houses but were somewhat slow in making improvements. The center of the ward, where the school house stood, was five miles south of the St. David townsite. The first irrigation ditch, through which water was conveyed upon the land later included in the Macdonald settlement, was made by non-Mormons, who in 1882 sold out to Henry J. Horne and other Latter-day Saints, who, with their respective families, located there that year. Other settlers arrived in 1883 and the saints residing above St. David, numbering about half a dozen families, were organized as a ward with Henry J. Horne as Bishop. The new ward was named in honor of Alexander F. Macdonald, who took a lively interest in the founding of the settlement. More settlers moved in, and in 1884 a school house was built. In September, 1885, the Macdonald Ward organization was discontinued and the settlement attached to the St. David Ward.
