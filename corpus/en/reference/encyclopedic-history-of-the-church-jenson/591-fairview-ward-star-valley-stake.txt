Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Fairview Ward, Star Valley Stake

FAIRVIEW WARD, Star Valley Stake, Lincoln Co., Wyoming, consists of the saints residing in and adjacent to the village of Fairview, which is situated on Crow Creek, on the west side of Salt River in Star Valley. The townsite is on the south side of the creek about a mile due east of where it emerges from the mouth of Crow Creek Canyon, 4 1/2 miles southwest of Afton, and about 40 miles northeast of Montpelier, Idaho, the nearest railroad point. The village is surrounded by good farming land, most of it level and productive. Fairview is the third place of importance and size in the Star Valley Stake. Fairview dates back to 1885, when the first L. D. S. settlers located on Crow Creek. The saints on said creek were organized as a branch of the Church Jan. 27, 1889, belonging to Afton Ward, but on July 2, 1889, the branch was organized as a ward with John C. Dewey as Bishop. He was succeeded in 1895 by Charles W. Porter, who in 1898 was succeeded by Ole A. Jensen (presiding Elder), who in 1899 was succeeded by Franklin G. Tolman (Bishop), who in 1907 was succeeded by Albert A. Barrus, who in 1913 was succeeded by Orlando Barrus, who in 1924 was succeeded by Robert H. Hillstead, who was succeeded in June, 1930, by Joseph L. Brown, who presided Dec. 31, 1930, on which date the ward had 427 members, including 113 children.
