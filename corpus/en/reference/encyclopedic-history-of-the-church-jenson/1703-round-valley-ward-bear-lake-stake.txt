Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Round Valley Ward, Bear Lake Stake

ROUND VALLEY WARD, Bear Lake Stake, Rich Co., Utah, consisted of the Latter-day Saints residing in the so-called Round Valley, lying immediately west of Laketown. This valley, which is two miles long and two miles wide, is divided near the center of Big Creek, the village of Round Valley being on the south side of the valley. The land in the valley is very productive and all kinds of hardy fruits are raised, notwithstanding the high altitude.

The first Latter-day Saint settlers in Round Valley arrived there in 1869, and all the saints in the valley consisted of only one ward until 1898, when the saints residing in the south end of the valley were organized into a separate ward. The two wards (Round Valley and Meadowville) existed side by side until Meadowville ceased to exist in 1898, after which most of the members who had formerly constituted the Meadowville Ward were attached to the Laketown Ward as a matter of preference.

The saints residing in the south end of Round Valley, who had formerly belonged to the Laketown Ward, were organized as a branch of the Church Dec. 11, 1892, with Isaac Thomas Price as presiding Elder. Brother Price was ordained a Bishop May 10, 1893, when the Round Valley Branch was organized as a regular bishop's ward. Bishop Price died May 5, 1912, and Lehi N. Earley succeeded him as Bishop. He acted in that capacity until 1928, when the Round Valley Ward was disorganized because of the decrease of the actual membership of the Church, and the remnant of these members were transferred to the Laketown Ward.
