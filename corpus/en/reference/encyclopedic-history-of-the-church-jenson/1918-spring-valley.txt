Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Spring Valley

SPRING VALLEY, Nevada, consisted of the Latter-day Saints residing in Spring Valley, the uppermost valley of that string of small valleys which lies above Meadow Valley or Panaca. The valley is reached by passing through a canyon four miles long, extending northward up from Eagle Valley. Spring Valley, although the highest and coldest of all the valleys in the neighborhood, contains some excellent farming and meadow land and by a judicious and well-regulated distribution of water for irrigation purposes could easily sustain a population of at least 25 families. Spring Valley was settled by four families of Latter-day saints in 1865. Other settlers moved in, until the valley had 15 families. Later 22 families of saints in Spring Valley lived in a fort. Nelson Franklin Millett had charge of the little settlement in Spring Valley, which constituted a part of the Eagle Valley Ward, where it remained until the exodus of the saints from Nevada took place in 1871.
