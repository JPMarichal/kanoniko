Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Sharon Ward, Bear Lake Stake

SHARON WARD, Bear Lake Stake, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in a farming district embracing the extreme northwest corner of Bear Lake Valley. The center of the ward, where the meeting house stands, is about 11 miles northwest of Paris.

Sharon Ward is an outgrowth of Liberty and was known originally as North Liberty. John Thomas Lyon was the first president of the branch, which was organized Feb. 4, 1897, and named Sharon, in honor of the birthplace of the Prophet Joseph Smith: Sharon, Windsor Co., Vermont. Bro. Lyon acted until 1899, when he was succeeded by Samuel E. Hymas, who acted as presiding Elder until 1910, when the branch was organized as a ward, with Samuel E. Hymas as Bishop. He presided until 1926, when he was succeeded by John Gambling, who acted as Bishop of the Sharon Ward Dec. 31, 1930.

The total membership of the Sharon Ward Dec. 31, 1930, was 140, including 31 children; the population of the Sharon Precinct was 146.
