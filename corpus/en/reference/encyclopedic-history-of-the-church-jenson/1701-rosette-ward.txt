Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Rosette Ward

ROSETTE WARD, Curlew Stake, Box Elder Co., Utah, consists of Latter-day Saints residing in an open valley (a scattered farming settlement). The center of the ward is five miles west of the Park Valley Ward center, and 18 miles northwest of Kelton, the nearest station on the Southern Pacific Railroad. It is also about two miles from the Raft River Mountains and near Pine Canyon Creek.

Rosette Ward is an outgrowth of the Park Valley Ward and came into existence on May 22, 1910, when the west part of the Park Valley Ward was organized as the Rosette Ward with Jacob Kunzler as Bishop. Bishop Kunzler was succeeded in 1913 by Arta M. Seeley, who in 1928 was succeeded by Fred J. Hirschi, who presided Dec. 31, 1930, at which time the Church membership of the ward was 64, including 19 children. The total population of the Rosette Precinct was 77 in 1930. Quite a number of the saints in the Rosette Ward are of Swiss origin.
