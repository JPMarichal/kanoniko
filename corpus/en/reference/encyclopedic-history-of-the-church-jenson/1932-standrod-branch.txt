Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Standrod Branch

STANDROD BRANCH was a temporary branch organized in 1898 for the benefit of a few Latter-day Saints residing in the vicinity of One Mile Creek, about 15 miles southeast of Almo, Cassia Co., Idaho. The branch was named Standrod in honor of Judge Standrod of Idaho. A school house, which was also used as a meeting house, was erected in 1898 directly on the boundary line between Utah and Idaho, one half of the building being in Utah and the other half in Idaho. Edward H. Morris was appointed presiding Elder of the branch, but was succeeded in 1900 by Fred A. Peterson. A Sunday school was commenced in the district as early as 1892 by Sisters Elizabeth Lee and Alzina Barnes, but shortly afterwards Samuel P. Hardy became superintendent and acted until 1898, when he was succeeded by John Higham. The branch during its somewhat brief existence belonged to the Almo Ward.
