Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Miami Ward

MIAMI WARD, Saint Joseph Stake, Gila Co., Arizona, consists of Latter-day Saints residing in the mining town of Miami in Gila County. Miami is the terminus of the Bowie-Globe branch of the Southern Pacific Railroad and is 84 miles northwest of Thatcher. The Latter-day Saints at Miami own a modern chapel and a recreation hall. The chapel was built in 1916 at a cost of about $3,000, and the recreation hall in 1926 at a cost of $4,500. A number of saints having located at Miami were organized as a branch of the Church in 1912 with Parley P. Wilkins as presiding Elder. He was succeeded in 1913 by Lehi Larsen, who in 1914 was succeeded by George Jasper Walser, who presided until Sept. 3, 1916, when the Miami Branch was organized as a ward with George J. Walser as Bishop. He acted in that capacity until 1926, when he was succeeded by Nephi Lot Smith (a son of the late Lot Smith of early Church history fame), who presided Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the Miami Ward had 498 members, including 120 children. The total population of the Miami town was 7,693 in 1930.
