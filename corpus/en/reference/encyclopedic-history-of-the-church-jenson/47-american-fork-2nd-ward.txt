Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

American Fork 2nd Ward

AMERICAN FORK 2ND WARD consists of Latter-day Saints residing in the southwest part of the city of American Fork. It is separated from the American Fork 3rd Ward on the north by 1st North St., and from the American Fork 1st Ward on the east by Center St.; south the ward extends to Utah Lake and west to Lehi. American Fork 2nd Ward owns a fine brick meeting house, somewhat centrally located on 1st South St., between 1st and 2nd West streets. This building was erected in 1903 at a cost of $10,000.

When the city of American Fork on July 14, 1901, was divided into four wards, the southwest part of the city was organized as the American Fork 2nd Ward with Joseph H. Storrs as Bishop. He presided in that capacity Dec. 31, 1930, on which date the ward had a membership of 957, including 176 children.

In 1929 and 1930 the 2nd ward chapel was remodeled and an amusement hall, a two-story brick building, was erected at a cost of $50,000. The combined chapel and amusement hall also contains a bishop's room, a Relief Society room, and 12 class rooms.

The amusement hall is equipped with a modern stage and is claimed to be one of the finest ward recreation halls in the Church. The Denver and Rio Grande Railroad and also the Salt Lake and Utah Railroad have stations within the limits of the American Fork 2nd Ward. Nearly all the residents of the ward are Latter-day Saint farmers.
