Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

North Ogden Ward

NORTH OGDEN WARD, Ogden Stake, Weber Co., Utah, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in the town of North Ogden and immediate vicinity. This town is situated on rising ground which extends to the base of the mountains east and north. Nearly all the inhabitants are L. D. S. farmers who own rich and fertile lands well adapted for raising all kinds of cereals, vegetables and fruit. About one-half of the people residing in the ward live on the townsite, the other half in a scattered condition on their respective farms. The townsite of North Ogden is surveyed into four-acre blocks, each block being surveyed into half-acre lots. The ward can boast of a beautiful meeting house, a modern school house, a number of stores and fine residences.

North Ogden as a settlement dates back to 1850 and the early settlers belonged to the North Ward of the Weber Stake, but were organized as a separate branch in 1852 with Thomas Dunn as presiding Elder. He was succeeded by the following brethren: Henry Holmes as presiding Elder, 1863-1877, and Amos Maycock, 1877-1882; Thomas Wallace, 1882-1894; James Ward, 1894-1912; Frederick Barker, 1912-1924, and Harold S. Campbell, 1924-1930, as Bishops.

North Ogden Precinct had 687 inhabitants in 1870 and 1,045 in 1930; of these 953, including 172 children, were Latter-day Saints.
