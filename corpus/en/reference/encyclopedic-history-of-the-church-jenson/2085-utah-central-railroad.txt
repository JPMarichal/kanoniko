Utah Central Railroad

UTAH CENTRAL RAILROAD was originally a line running from Ogden to Salt Lake City, Utah, connecting the Union Pacific Railroad with the capital of the territory of Utah.

When construction of the transcontinental railroad was commenced in 1866, it was expected that Salt Lake City would be one of the most important stations on the road, but the U. S. survey for the line ran 36 miles further north, through Ogden, which became the railroad center of the Rocky Mountain region. Realizing the importance of connecting Salt Lake City with the railroad, Pres. Brigham Young undertook to construct a branch line for this purpose and at a meeting held in Salt Lake City March 8, 1869, the Utah Central Railroad Company was incorporated with Brigham Young as president; Wm. Jennings, vice-president; Jesse W. Fox, chief engineer; John W. Young, secretary, and Daniel H. Wells, treasurer. Track laying was commenced at Ogden Sept. 22, 1869,' and the line completed to Salt Lake City (36 miles) Jan. 10, 1870.

To this line in 1881 was added the Utah Southern Railroad, running south from Salt Lake City to Juab, Juab Co., Utah, and the Utah Southern Extension, running from Juab southwest to Frisco, Beaver Co., Utah. In 1889 the Utah Central Railroad was amalgamated with the Oregon Short Line and Utah Northern railroads, which later became the Oregon Short Line Railroad.
