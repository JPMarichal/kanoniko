Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

American Fork 1st Ward

AMERICAN FORK 1ST WARD consists of Latter-day Saints residing in the southeast part of the city of American Fork, being separated from the American Fork 4th Ward on the north by Main St., and from the American Fork 2nd Ward on the west by Center St. Eastward the ward extends to the Pleasant Grove 2nd Ward and south to Utah Lake. Nearly all the inhabitants residing in that part of American Fork are farmers. The ward owns a chapel, a fine brick building with an auditorium capable of seating 400 people; it also contains a Relief Society room, a prayer room and eight class rooms.

At a quarterly conference held at American Fork, July 14, 1901, attended by Apostles George Teasdale and Reed Smoot, American Fork was divided into four wards, viz., American Fork 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th wards. Charles D. Patterson was sustained as Bishop of the American Fork 1st Ward; he acted in that capacity until 1903, when he was succeeded by James T. Gardner, who in 1928 was succeeded by Thomas A. Greenwood, who acted as Bishop of the ward Dec. 31, 1930. On that date the ward had 1,007 members including 243 children.
