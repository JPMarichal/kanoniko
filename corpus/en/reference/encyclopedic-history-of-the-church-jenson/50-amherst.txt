Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Amherst

AMHERST, Lorain Co., Ohio, is a township about 50 miles southwest of Kirtland, Ohio, where Parley P. Pratt was one of the early settlers and where he located with his young wife late in 1827. Here he later (1831) had an interesting experience in escaping from a sheriff who wanted to arrest him for preaching the gospel. (See Parley P. Pratt's autobiography, pp. 30, 40-53). A branch of the Church was organized in Amherst in 1831, and on Jan. 25, 1832, an important conference was held there, at which considerable business was done, and a number of Elders were called by revelation (Doc. & Cov. Sec. 75) to the ministry and appointed to take missions to various parts of the United States. At that conference also Joseph Smith the Prophet was by unanimous vote sustained as president of the high priesthood. For several years the Amherst Branch of the Church was an important station, or headquarters, for some of the missionaries laboring in Ohio.
