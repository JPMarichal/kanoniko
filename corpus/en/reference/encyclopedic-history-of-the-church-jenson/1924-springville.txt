Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Springville

SPRINGVILLE, Kolob Stake, Utah Co., Utah, one of the prettiest cities for location in Utah, is situated in the east end of Utah Valley, below the mouth of Hobble Creek, from which stream the inhabitants get water for irrigation purposes. Springville consists of four L. D. S. wards and represents industry and prosperity. The great majority of the inhabitants of the city are Latter-day Saints. Springville is six miles southeast of Provo (the seat of Utah County) on the lines of the Salt Lake and Los Angeles Railroad, the Denver and Rio Grande Western Railroad and the Salt Lake and Utah Railroad. It has a flouring mill and woollen factory, and also extensive steel works. The population of Springville was 1,357 in 1860; 2,312 in 1880; 3,422 in 1900, and 3,748 in 1930.

Springville was first settled in October, 1850, by Aaron Johnson and others. The town was incorporated as a city in 1852. Aaron Johnson was the first Bishop of Springville, which was organized as a ward soon after the settlement of the place. He presided until 1870, when he was succeeded by William Bringhurst, who in 1883 was succeeded by Nephi Packard, who acted as Bishop of Springville until 1892, when the ward was divided into four wards, namely, the Springville 1st Ward with John Tuckett as Bishop, the Springville 2nd Ward with Loren H. Harmer as Bishop, the Springville 3rd Ward with George R. Hill as Bishop, and the Springville 4th Ward with Joseph Loynd as Bishop. The Church population of the four wards Dec. 31, 1930, was 3,455, including 554 children.
