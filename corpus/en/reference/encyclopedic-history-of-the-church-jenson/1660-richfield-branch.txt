Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Richfield Branch

RICHFIELD BRANCH, Blaine Stake, Lincoln Co., Idaho, consists of Latter-day Saints residing at Richfield, a station on the Wood River branch of the Oregon Short Line Railroad, and in the fertile farming district surrounding the town. Richfield is 15 miles northeast of Shoshone and 25 miles southwest of Carey, the stake headquarters. The L. D. S. meeting house, a brick structure, has an auditorium capable of seating 250 people.

The Latter-day Saints at Richfield were organized as a branch of the Carey Ward May 2, 1920, with William R. Robinson as presiding Elder. He was succeeded in 1921 by Ferrin L. Manwill, who acted in that capacity until July 31, 1927, when the organization became an independent branch (reporting to the stake presidency); Ferrin L. Manwill was continued as president. At that time the branch had a membership of 120. Bro. Manwill was succeeded in 1929 by Earl Crowther, who was succeeded in 1930 by Ferrin L. Manwill (2nd term). The membership of the branch Dec. 31, 1930, was 170, including 51 children, out of a population in the Richfield Precinct of 915.
