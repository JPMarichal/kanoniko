Fort Utah

FORT UTAH is the name of the first settlement in Utah Valley, or Utah County, Utah. It was commenced in April, 1849, and consisted of houses built in a parallellogram, enclosing an area of about 20 by 30 rods near an ancient mound, about two miles west from what is now the center of Provo City. The houses, which faced inward, were mostly built of cottonwood logs in solid, continuous line, the rear walls forming the outer walls of the enclosure. Where vacancies between the houses occurred the space was filled in with pickets about 12 feet long set in the ground close together for protection in case of attack from hostile Indians. The settlers also built a general stock corral on the east side of the fort, outside the wall, besides private corrals behind the respective houses with gates, or back-door openings.

In the center of the fort a platform about 15 feet high was erected upon the top of which was placed a cannon (a twelve-pounder) which was occasionally fired to impress the Indians with a proper respect for the whites. Farming was conducted by the pioneer settlers on the east, south and west of the fort, mostly on the west side looking toward Utah Lake. The colonists named their location "Fort Utah" from the beginning. This fort was the commencement of the beautiful city of Provo. (See Provo.)
