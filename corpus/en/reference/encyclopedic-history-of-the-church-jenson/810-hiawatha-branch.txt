Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Hiawatha Branch

HIAWATHA BRANCH, Carbon Stake, Carbon Co., Utah, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in the coal-mining camp of Hiawatha which is situated on Miller Creek, at the mouth of a canyon about four miles north of Mohrland, and about 18 miles southwest of Price. Hiawatha is situated on the Utah Railway owned by an independent railway company connecting with the D. & R. G. Western Railroad at Helper, which is about 21 miles northeast of Hiawatha. It is a thriving mining camp in which a number of Latter-day Saints have become residents.

About the year 1909 the development of the coal mines at, Hiawatha was commenced, and among the first coal-miners employed at these mines were some Latter-day Saints hailing from different settlements in Emery County, who were organized into a branch of the Church June 20, 1920, with Seymour Oliphant as presiding Elder. He was succeeded in 1927 by Stanley H. Edwards, who acted as president of the branch Dec. 31, 1930. The branch at that time had a membership of 229 souls, including 69 children. The total population of Hiawatha Precinct was 977 in 1930.
