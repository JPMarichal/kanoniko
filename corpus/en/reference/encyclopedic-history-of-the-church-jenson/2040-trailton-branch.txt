Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Trailton Branch

TRAILTON BRANCH, Shelley Stake, Bingham Co., Idaho, consisted of a few families of saints residing on Blackfoot River, about 30 miles upstream from the junction of Blackfoot and Snake Rivers. It is a farming district where dry farming did not prove a success. The few families who had settled in that part of the country were organized into a temporary branch of the Church July 7, 1918, with Joseph Davis a presiding Elder. But that organization was kept up only about one year as the people moved away discouraged, owing to the drouth. Both meetings and Sunday schools were discontinued in 1919, and in 1930 only two families of saints remained in the Trailton district.
