Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Black Hawk War

BLACK HAWK WAR (The), which cost the settlers of southern Utah about seventy lives, and vast numbers of horses and cattle, was carried on by the notorious Indian chief, Black Hawk. In his youth that chief was friendly to the Mormon settlers, but he turned hostile in 1865 and waged war against the people in the southern counties of Utah, causing, among other things, the temporary vacation of a number of new settlements which had been founded previously by the saints. After carrying on hostilities for a number of years, Black Hawk repented of his enmity and lived at peace with the white settlers until the time of his death, which occurred Sept. 26, 1870, at Spring Lake Villa, Utah Co., Utah. (See Deseret News, Sept. 26, 1870.)
