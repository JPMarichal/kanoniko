Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Cassia Stake Academy

CASSIA STAKE ACADEMY was founded for the benefit of the Latter-day Saints in 1887. At that time the Cassia country was but sparsely settled, yet in addition to the small schools functioning in the settlements, the Cassia Stake Academy, offering instruction in higher grade work, was commenced at Oakley in a log cabin Nov. 18, 1889. Later a fine modern school building was erected.

The academy soon was in a position to offer a three-year high school course, supplemented by theology, to which was later added training in animal husbandry and dairying. On account of the facilities offered by the state schools, which would have caused a duplication of secular studies, the Cassia Stake Academy was closed in 1921 and a L. D. S. seminary established instead. From 1911 to 1921 Joseph Mills, B. A., presided over the school.
