Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Belgian Conference

BELGIAN CONFERENCE, or District, of the French Mission, consists of the Latter-day Saints residing in Belgium, a small kingdom in western Europe, bordering on the North Sea and bounded by France, Germany, Luxemburg and Holland. The headquarters of the district are Liege, and on Dec. 31, 1930, the district had a total Church membership of 344, including 33 children.

Mischa Markow, a Hungarian, is believed to have introduced the restored gospel in Belgium. He was baptized in Constantinople, Turkey, in 1887, by Elder Ferdinand F. Hintze, who shortly afterwards ordained him an Elder. Having returned to Hungary, Elder Markow commenced to preach and traveled over various parts of Europe, preaching the gospel wherever he could find listeners, and as he spoke several languages, he found many hearers. At Antwerp, Belgium, he made the acquaintance of a family named Easelman, and baptized six members of that family; he then wrote to the president of the Swiss and German Mission asking that missionaries be sent to Antwerp, which was done, and shortly afterwards a branch of the Church was organized in that city.

From this time Belgium became a L. D. S. missionary field, which for some time constituted a part of the Netherlands Mission, but when the French Mission was reopened in 1923, Belgium was transferred to that mission.
