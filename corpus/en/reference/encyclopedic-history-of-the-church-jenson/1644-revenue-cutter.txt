Andrew Jenson, Encyclopedic History of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ( 1941)

Revenue Cutter

REVENUE CUTTER (The) was the name of a skiff of sole leather, which was part of the equipment of the original band of Utah Pioneers in 1847. This boat had been the property of Ira Eldredge in Nauvoo, Ill., and had carried his family over the Mississippi and Missouri rivers. The boat was used by the Pioneers in crossing the Loupe Fork on April 26, 1847, and doubtless on many other occasions. It was carried in a wagon, and on Sunday, May 9th, a meeting of the Pioneers was announced to convene at the Revenue Cutter Wagon. On June 16, 1847, the Pioneers arrived at a point on the Platte River near the site of Casper, Wyoming, where they remained a few days and established a ferry, constructing several rafts, and where ten of the Pioneers remained behind to take charge of the ferry in order to assist the companies of saints already on their way west. A number of Oregon emigrants also were ferried over by the Pioneers at this place. Here the Revenue Cutter is mentioned as being used in connection with the rafts.
