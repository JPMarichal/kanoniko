Daniel’s conviction to stand firm for God provided bookends to the narrative portion of the book that bears his name. In chapter

1, he refused to defile himself by eating the king’s food (v. 8). By chapter 6, the empires had shifted, but Daniel’s core values had not changed. He still refused to compromise his faith for the sake of personal comfort.

Like his friends in the fiery furnace, Daniel believed God could protect him, but he didn’t have any guarantees. Through it all, one thing was certain: Daniel would rather spend a night in a den of lions than shrink from what he believed was right. That kind of obedience allowed God to work freely—and bring glory to Himself.
