Augustine, Confessions ( 399)

Chapter 14

Concerning the Manner in Which Joy and Sadness May Be Brought Back to the Mind and Memory

And yet we could never speak of them, did we not find in' our memory not merely the sounds of the names, according to the images imprinted on it by the senses of the body, but the notions of the things themselves, which we never received by any door of the flesh, but which the mind itself, recognising by the experience of its own passions, entrusted to the memory, or else which the memory itself retained without their being entrusted to it.
