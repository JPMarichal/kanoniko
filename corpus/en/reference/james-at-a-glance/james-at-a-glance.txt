However, several factors indicate that this may be the earliest New Testament writing (c. a.d. 46–49): (1) there is no mention of Gentile believers or their relationship to Jewish Christians; (2) the allusions to the teaching of Christ bear so little verbal agreement with the Synoptic Gospels that James probably precedes the composition of the Gospels; (3) James uses the Greek term for “synagogue” in 2:2 (nkjv: “assembly”) in addition to the term “church” (5:14), thus indicating a very simple church organization of elders and teachers (3:1; 5:14) which was patterned after the Jewish synagogue; and (4) James does not mention the issues involved in the Jerusalem Council of Acts 15 (a.d. 49).

THEMES AND LITER ARy STRuCTuRE

James is the Proverbs of the New Testament because it is written in the terse,

moralistic style of wisdom literature. It is evident that James was profoundly influ-enced by the Old Testament as well as by the teaching of Jesus. Because of the many subjects in this epistle, it is difficult to outline. The outline used here is: the test of faith (1:1–18); the characteristics of faith (1:19—5:6); and the triumph of faith (5:7–20).
