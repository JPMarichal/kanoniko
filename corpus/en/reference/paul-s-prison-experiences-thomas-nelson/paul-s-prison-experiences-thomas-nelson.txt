Paul’s first lengthy imprisonment, recorded in Acts 21:27—28:31, resulted

from false accusations by Jews in Jerusalem that Paul had brought a Gentile into the temple area (21:28, 29). Because he was a Roman citizen, Paul was offered protection by the Roman authorities from the Jerusalem mob and was transported under guard to Caesarea (23:11–35). After hearings before the Jewish Sanhedrin (22:30—23:10), the Roman governor Felix (24:1–21), the succeeding governor Festus (25:1–12), and King Agrippa (25:13—26:32), Paul was taken to Rome (27:1—28:16). The Book of Acts closes with Paul under house arrest in Rome awaiting his hearing before the emperor (28:17–31).
