Baruch Spinoza, On the Improvement of the Understanding ( 1662)

Part 16

(1) But before all things, a means must be devised for improving the understanding and purifying it, as far as may be at the outset, so that it may apprehend things without error, and in the best possible way.

(2) Thus it is apparent to everyone that I wish to direct all science to one end and aim, so that we may attain to the supreme human perfection which we have named; and, therefore, whatsoever in the sciences does not serve to promote our object will have to be rejected as useless.

(3) To sum up the matter in a word, all our actions and thoughts must be directed to this one end.
