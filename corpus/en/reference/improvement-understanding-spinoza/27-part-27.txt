Baruch Spinoza, On the Improvement of the Understanding ( 1662)

Part 27

(1) The second mode of perception cannot be said to give us the idea of the proportion of which we are in search.

(2) Moreover its results are very uncertain and indefinite, for we shall never discover anything in natural phenomena by its means, except accidental properties, which are never clearly understood, unless the essence of the things in question be known first.

(3) Wherefore this mode also must be rejected.
