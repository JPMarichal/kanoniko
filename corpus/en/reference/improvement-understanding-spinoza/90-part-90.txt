Baruch Spinoza, On the Improvement of the Understanding ( 1662)

Part 90

(1) Let us also beware of another great cause of confusion, which prevents the understanding from reflecting on itself.

(2) Sometimes, while making no distinction between the imagination and the intellect, we think that what we more readily imagine is clearer to us; and also we think that what we imagine we understand.

(3) Thus, we put first that which should be last: the true order of progression is reversed, and no legitimate conclusion is drawn.
