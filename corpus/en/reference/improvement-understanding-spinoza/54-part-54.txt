Baruch Spinoza, On the Improvement of the Understanding ( 1662)

Part 54

(1) Whence it follows that if there be a God, or omniscient Being, such an one cannot form fictitious hypotheses.

(2) For, as regards ourselves, when I know that I exist, I cannot hypothesize that I exist or do not exist, any more than I can hypothesize an elephant that can go through the eye of a needle; nor when I know the nature of God, can I hypothesize that He or does not exist.

(3) The same thing must be said of the Chimaera, whereof the nature implies a contradiction.

(4) From these considerations, it is plain, as I have already stated, that fiction cannot be concerned with eternal truths.
