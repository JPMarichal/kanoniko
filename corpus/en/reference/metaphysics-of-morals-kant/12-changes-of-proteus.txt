Ovid, Metamorphoses ( 7)

Changes of Proteus

He ceas'd in his relation to proceed, Whilst all admir'd the author, and the deed; But Theseus most, inquisitive to know From Gods what wondrous alterations grow. Whom thus the Calydonian stream address'd, Rais'd high to speak, the couch his elbow press'd. Some, when transform'd, fix in the lasting change; Some with more right, thro' various figures range. Proteus, thus large thy privilege was found, Thou inmate of the seas, which Earth surround. Sometimes a bloming youth you grac'd the shore; Oft a fierce lion, or a furious boar: With glist'ning spires now seem'd an hissing snake, The bold would tremble in his hands to take: With horns assum'd a bull; sometimes you prov'd A tree by roots, a stone by weight unmov'd: Sometimes two wav'ring contraries became, Flow'd down in water, or aspir'd in flame.
