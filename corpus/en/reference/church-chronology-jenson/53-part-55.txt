Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The winter of 1856-57 was excessively severe, snow falling to a depth of eight feet in various place in the valleys of Utah. The harvest of 1857 was the best Utah ever had up to that time. Influenced by falsehoods, circulated by Judge W.W. Drummond and others, the Federal government sent an army to Utah, when the citizens organized for self-defense. The Elders were called home from foreign missions, and the Saints who had settled in Carson Valley, on Salmon river, on Green river and in Southern California were advised to abandon their locations and return to places nearer the headquarters of the Church.

January 4, 1857 (Sunday)

Daniel H. Wells was set apart as second Counselor to Pres. Brigham Young, in place of the late Jedediah M. Grant.

January 9, 1857 (Friday)

San Bernardino, Cal, was visited by a violent earthquake.

February 4, 1857 (Wednesday)

A reformation meeting was held in No. 42 Islington, Liverpool, England, and on the following day the presiding brethren of the British mission, including Apostles Orson Pratt and Ezra T. Benson, renewed their covenants by baptism. This was followed by a general renewal of covenants throughout the mission.

March 1857

The 43rd quorum of Seventy was organized in Tooele County, Utah, with John Shields, James Bevan, Thomas Lee, Francis D. St. Jeor, George Atkin, Hugh S. Gowans and Geo. W. Bryan as presidents.

March 2, 1857 (Monday)

The 41st Quorum of Seventy was organized in Salt Lake County, Utah, with John Van Cott, Wm. C. Dunbar, Knud Peterson, Thomas Morris, Leonard I. Smith, Wm. Casper and Levi N. Kendall as presidents.

March 12, 1857 (Thursday)

Reformation meetings were held at Swansea, Wales, after which the presiding Elders, and subsequently all the Saints in that mission, renewed their covenants by baptism.

March 20, 1857 (Friday)

Henry Mitchell Johnson formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in G.S.L. City.

March 28, 1857 (Saturday)

The ship George Washington sailed from Liverpool, England, with 817 Saints, under the direction of James P. Park, bound for Utah via Boston.

March 30, 1857 (Monday)

Judge W.W. Drummond, in framing the letter of his resignation as chief justice of Utah, wrote the most wicked and abominable falsehoods against Gov. Brigham Young and the people of Utah, thereby influencing the government to send troops against the "Mormons."

April 4, 1857 (Saturday)

Cache County, Utah, was organized; Peter Maughan, probate judge.

April 6, 1857 (Monday)

The 27th annual conference on the Church convened in G.S.L. City; it was continued till the 8th; 350 Elders were called on missions.

April 15, 1857 (Wednesday)

Feramorz Little, having arrived in the States, with the Utah mail, wrote a letter to the New York Herald, refuting Drummond's falsehoods.

April 20, 1857 (Monday)

The Nauvoo Legion held a grand parade in G.S.L. City; the election of officers took place, and a new system for the government of Utah militia was inaugurated.

April 23, 1857 (Thursday)

A company consisting of about seventy missionaries, bound for Europe and other parts of the world, left G.S.L. City with handcarts. They arrived at Florence, Neb., June 10th, making the trip to the Missouri river in 40 1/2 traveling days. (They rested 7 1/2 days.)

April 24, 1857 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young and many others started from G.S.L. City on a tour to the settlements on Salmon river, Oregon (now Idaho). They returned May 26th.

April 25, 1857 (Saturday)

The ship Westmoreland sailed from Liverpool, England, with 544 Saints, mostly Scandinavians, under the direction of Mathias Cowley. It arrived at Philadelphia May 31st, and the emigrants reached Iowa City by rail June 9th.

May 1857

The Tithing Office Block wall in G.S.L. City was finished

May 1857

The 46th quorum of Seventy was organized at Payson and Santaquin, Utah Co., with James B. Bracken, John Thomas Hardy, Benjamin F. Stewart, Wm. Carrol McClellan, Geo. W. Hancock and Wm. B. Maxwell as presidents.

May 1857

A temporary settlement called Genoa, was located for the benefit of emigrating Saints, on Beaver Creek, near Loup Fork, Neb., about one hundred miles west of Florence. The settlers consisted mostly of Saints from the St. Louis branch (Mo.).

May 6, 1857 (Wednesday)

The Saints who were settling Washington, in southern Utah, were organized into a branch of the Church with Robert D. Covington as president. He was ordained a Bishop Aug. 1, 1858.

May 9, 1857 (Saturday)

The 45th quorum of Seventy was organized at Provo, with Robert T. Thomas, James Goff, Robert C. Moore, Isaac Bullock, Lewis C. Sabrisky, Wm. Marsden and Charles Shelton as presidents.

May 13, 1857 (Wednesday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt was murdered by Hector H. McLean, near Van Buren, Ark.

May 15, 1857 (Friday)

The 47th quorum of Seventy was partly organized at Ephraim, Sanpete Co., Utah, with Tore Thurston, James A. Lemmon, Joseph Clements and Nils Bengtsen as presidents. Most of the members of the new quorum were ordained Seventies on the 17th.

May 16, 1857 (Saturday)

The 48th quorum of Seventy was organized at Manti, Sanpete Co., with Daniel Henrie as senior president.

May 18, 1857 (Monday)

The 49th quorum of Seventy was organized at Nephi, Juab Co., with John A. Woolf, Samuel Pitchforth, Timothy S. Hoyt, Geo. Kendall, Miles Miller, John Burrowman and David Webb as presidents.

May 19, 1857 (Tuesday)

The 50th quorum of Seventy was partly organized at Spanish Fork, Utah Co., with Dennis Dorrity as one of the presidents.

May 20, 1857 (Wednesday)

The 51st quorum of Seventy was organized at Springville, Utah Co., with Alexander F. McDonald, Noah T. Guyman, Lorenzo Johnson, Spicer W. Crandall, Abraham Day and Hamilton H. Kerns as presidents.

May 21, 1857 (Thursday)

The 52nd quorum of Seventy was organized at Provo, Utah, with Alfred D. Young as senior president. Quite a number of members were ordained on the 25th.

May 21, 1857 (Thursday)

On the same day the 44th quorum of Seventy was organized at American Fork, Utah Co., Utah, with Wm. Hyde, James McGaw, Shadrach Driggs, Wm. Greenwood, James W. Preston, Wm. Fotheringham and Thomas Taylor as presidents.

May 28, 1857 (Thursday)

The U.S. 2nd dragoons, 5th and 10th infantry and Phelps' Battery of the 4th artillery—2,500 men—were ordered out as an expedition to Utah, by order of Gen. Winfield Scott.

May 30, 1857 (Saturday)

The ship Tuscarora sailed from Liverpool, England, with 547 Saints, under the direction of Richard Harper. It arrived at Philadelphia July 3rd, and the emigrants continued by rail to Burlington, Iowa, in the vicinity of which most of them sought temporary employment.

June 7, 1857 (Sunday)

The 53th and 54th quorum of Seventy were organized at Ogden, Utah, by Joseph Young and Albert P. Rockwood, with Rufus Allen and James Brown 3rd as senior presidents.

June 12, 1857 (Friday)

Senator Stephen A. Douglas, in a political speech, delivered at Springfield, Ill., characterized "Mormonism" as a loathsome ulcer of the body politic, and recommended that Congress should apply the knife and cut it out.

June 14, 1857 (Sunday)

The 42nd quorum of Seventy was organized at Fillmore, Utah, with Hiram Mace, David N. Raney, Andrew Love, J.W. Radford, Edward Frost, Allen Russel and John Felshaw as presidents.

June 27, 1857 (Saturday)

The American ship Lucas sailed from Sydney, N.S.W., Australia, with 69 Saints, in charge of Elder Absalom P. Dowdle, bound for Utah.

July 1857

The 55th quorum of Seventy was organized at Kaysville, and the 56th quorum at Farmington, Davis Co., Utah.

July 11, 1857 (Saturday)

Alfred Cumming, of Georgia, was appointed governor of Utah.

July 15, 1857 (Wednesday)

Indian Agent Thomas S. Twiss wrote a libellous letter to the government at Washington, D.C., about the "Mormons."

July 18, 1857 (Saturday)

The Tenth Infantry, the vanguard of the Utah expedition, took up the line of march from Fort Leavenworth for the West, under the command of Col. E. B. Alexander. The artillery and Fifth Infantry followed a few days later. The command of the whole expedition was given to Gen. W.S. Harney.

July 18, 1857 (Saturday)

The ship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool England, with 36 Saints, under the direction of Charles Harman. It arrived safely at Philadelphia, Pa.

July 24, 1857 (Friday)

The people of G.S.L. City and vicinity celebrated the 10th anniversary of the arrival of the Pioneers by a feast, near the head of Big Cottonwood Canyon. While the festivities were going on, Abraham O. Smoot and Judson Stoddard arrived from Independence, Mo., without the mails, the postmaster there having refused to forward them. They reported that General Harney with 2,000 infantry, and a proportionate number of artillery and cavalry, were ordered to Utah.

August 1, 1857 (Saturday)

The Utah militia was ordered to be kept in readiness for an expedition to the mountains, to prevent the entering of the approaching army, if necessary.

August 7, 1857 (Friday)

Apostles John Taylor and Erastus Snow and other missionaries arrived in G.S.L. City from the East.

August 7, 1857 (Friday)

The first part of the "Utah Army," consisting of the Tenth Infantry and Phelps' Battery, arrived at Fort Kearney.

August 14, 1857 (Friday)

Geo. Scholes, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died at Big Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

August 14, 1857 (Friday)

A company of the Carsen Valley settlers returned to G.S.L. City.

August 15, 1857 (Saturday)

Col. Robert T. Burton and James W. Cummings left G.S.L. City for the East, with seventy men, for the purpose of protecting the emigrant trains and observing the movements of the approaching army.

August 21, 1857 (Friday)

Col. Burton's expedition arrived at Ft. Bridger; on the 30th it reached Devil's Gate.

August 28, 1857 (Friday)

Col. Albert Sidney Johnston was appointed successor to Gen. W.S. Harney as commander of the Utah expedition.

September 4, 1857 (Friday)

Part of Wm. Walker's company of immigrating Saints, including Thos. B. Marsh, formerly a member of the Twelve Apostles, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 8, 1857 (Tuesday)

Capt. Stewart Van Vliet, of Gen. Harney's staff, arrived in G.S.L. City and the following day had an interview with President Young. After a few days' stay he returned to his escort on Ham's Fork, and thence proceeded to Washington, where he used his influence in favor of the Saints.

September 11, 1857 (Friday)

The Mountain Meadow massacre took place.

September 12, 1857 (Saturday)

The last of Israel Evans' handcart company, consisting of 154 souls and 31 handcarts, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 12, 1857 (Saturday)

Jesse B. Martin's wagon company of immigrants arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 13, 1857 (Sunday)

Chr. Christiansen's handcart company and Mathias Cowley's wagon company of immigrants arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 14, 1857 (Monday)

Delegate John M. Bernhisel started from G.S.L. City for Washington, D.C., in company with Capt. Steward Van Vliet and others.

September 14, 1857 (Monday)

Joseph A. Kelting, with a company of Saints, sailed from Sydney, Australia, bound for Utah.

September 15, 1857 (Tuesday)

Gov. Brigham Young declared the Territory of Utah under martial law and forbade the troops to enter G.S.L. Valley. Large numbers of armed militia were ordered to Echo Canyon and other points to intercept the soldiers and prevent their access to the Valley.

September 17, 1857 (Thursday)

Col. Philip St. George Cooke left Ft. Leavenworth with the second division of the "Utah Army." He arrived at Ft. Bridger Nov. 19th.

September 22, 1857 (Tuesday)

Col. Robt. T. Burton and three other men camped within half a mile of the "Utah Army" (Col. E.B. Alexander's command), near Devil's Gate.

September 23, 1857 (Wednesday)

Col. Burton's men met the advance companies of the "Utah Army," and from that time were their "immediate neighbors" until they arrived at Ham's Fork.

September 26, 1857 (Saturday)

Capt. Wm. G. Young's train arrived in G.S.L. City with the last of this season's immigration. Among the returning Elders in this train was A. Milton Musser, who returned home from a five year's mission to India and England, during which he had circumnavigated the globe, traveling as a missionary "without purse and scrip."

September 29, 1857 (Tuesday)

General Daniel H. Wells left G.S.L. City for Echo Canyon, where he established headquarters. About one thousand two hundred and fifty men, from the several militia districts, were ordered to Echo Canyon, where they engaged in digging trenches across the canyon, throwing up breast works, loosening rocks on the heights, etc., preparing to resist the progress of the army.

October 1857

The "Mormon" settlements in Carson Valley were broken up; most of the settlers returned to G.S.L. City in the beginning of November.

October 1857

Samuel W. Richards succeeded Apostle Orson Pratt as president of the European mission.

October 5, 1857 (Monday)

Lot Smith, with a small company of men, surprised and burned two trains of government stores, near the Big Sandy and Green river.

October 10, 1857 (Saturday)

The officers of the Utah expedition held a council of war at Ham's Fork, and decided that the army should march to G.S.L. Valley via Soda Springs. The following day the march was commenced, but after several days of slow and exhaustive traveling, the expedition was forced to return.

October 16, 1857 (Friday)

Major Joseph Taylor and Wm. R.R. Stowell, of the Utah militia, were taken prisoners by the U.S. troops near Ft. Bridger.

November 4, 1857 (Wednesday)

Col. Albert Sidney Johnston joined his command on Ham's Fork, with a small reinforcement.

November 6, 1857 (Friday)

Five hundred animals perished from cold and starvation around the U.S. army camp on Black's Fork.

November 16, 1857 (Monday)

The "Utah Army" went into winter quarters at Camp Scott, two miles from the site of Ft. Bridger and 115 miles from G.S.L. City.

December 4, 1857 (Friday)

Capt. John R. Winder was appointed to take charge of a picket guard, to be stationed at Camp Weber, at the mouth of Echo Canyon, to watch the movements of the U.S. soldiers during the winter. Two weeks later, when deep snow fell in the mountains, this guard was reduced to ten men. The remainder of the militia returned to their homes for the winter.

December 14, 1857 (Monday)

The Utah legislature convened in G.S.L. City and organized by electing Heber C. Kimball president of the Council and John Taylor speaker of the House.

December 21, 1857 (Monday)

The Utah legislature unanimously concurred in the message, policy and actions of Gov. Brigham Young, in stopping the army, etc.

December 22, 1857 (Tuesday)

An act disorganizing Green River County and attaching it to G.S.L. County, was approved.
