Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Utah Northern Railway was opened from Ogden to Franklin, Idaho. A large number of Indians joined the Church. Work on the St. George Temple was pushed forward with vigor. The United Order was introduced among the Saints.

January 2, 1874 (Friday)

Sally W. Phelps, relict of the late Judge Wm. W. Phelps, was killed by a skylight falling from a building near the Townsend House, Salt Lake City.

January 12, 1874 (Monday)

The Utah legislature (21st session) convened in Salt Lake City and organized by electing Lorenzo Snow president of the Council, and Orson Pratt speaker of the House.

January 29, 1874 (Thursday)

Bishop David H. Holliday, of Santaquin, Utah Co., died.

February 5, 1874 (Thursday)

The Utah Northern Railway was opened for traffic between Brigham City and Ogden.

February 12, 1874 (Thursday)

Bishop John Proctor, of the 10th Ward, Salt Lake City, died.

February 16, 1874 (Monday)

In the House of Representatives at Washington, D.C., Geo. Q. Cannon presented a memorial from the Utah legislature, asking Congress to appoint a commission to investigate Utah affairs, about which the anti-Mormons had made serious complaints.

February 18, 1874 (Wednesday)

Peter Van Valkenberg, of Union, Salt Lake Co., was shot and killed near his residence. The murderers were soon afterwards captured.

March 1874

Apostle Joseph F. Smith succeeded Lester J. Herrick as president of the European mission.

March 2, 1874 (Monday)

In the U.S. House of Representatives Geo. Q. Cannon introduced a bill for admitting Utah into the Union as a State.

March 7, 1874 (Saturday)

Mrs. Judson, of Fillmore, was burned to death.

April 1, 1874 (Wednesday)

A box, containing valuable records, was deposited in the wall of the St. George Temple.

April 4, 1874 (Saturday)

David Martin Perkins, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Pleasant Green, Salt Lake Co.

April 11, 1874 (Saturday)

Robert Lang Campbell, clerk at the Historian's Office, died in the 12th Ward, Salt Lake City.

April 20, 1874 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young and Geo. A. Smith, and party, arrived in Salt Lake City, from St. George, where they had spent the winter.

April 20, 1874 (Monday)

A party of representative men from Australia visited Salt Lake City, on a tour of inspection.

May 2, 1874 (Saturday)

The Fairview Coal Mining and Coke Company was incorporated.

May 3, 1874 (Sunday)

Geo. D. Watt was excommunicated from the Church, at Kaysville, Davis Co., for apostasy.

May 6, 1874 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 155 Saints, in charge of Lester J. Herrick. The company arrived at New York May 21st, and at Salt Lake City May 30th.

May 7, 1874 (Thursday)

The 44th annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City. The principal subject dwelt upon by the speakers was the "United Order", which was organized with Brigham Young as president. The conference was continued until the 10th.

May 14, 1874 (Thursday)

St. Mark's Cathedral (Episcopal) in Salt Lake City was consecrated.

May 15, 1874 (Friday)

Hon. Cyrus W. Field, originator of the Atlantic cable system, and Mr. Kingsley, an eminent English gentleman, accompanied by other men of prominence, arrived at Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 22, 1874 (Friday)

General Alexander W. Doniphan, favorably known in Church History during the Missouri persecutions in 1838, visited Salt Lake City.

May 24, 1874 (Sunday)

The Sevier Stake of Zion was partly organized by Apostles John Taylor and Orson Pratt, with Joseph A. Young as president and Albert K. Thurber as his first counselor.

May 24, 1874 (Sunday)

Mons. Henri Rochefort, the celebrated Communist leader, who had recently escaped from imprisonment in the French penal settlement, New Caledonia, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 29, 1874 (Friday)

A hurricane did much damage at Ogden.

June 1874

The Utah Educational Bureau was established in Salt Lake City, by Dr. John R. Park.

June 2, 1874 (Tuesday)

One hundred Goshute Indians were baptized by the Indian interpreter Wm. Lee in Deep Creek, Tooele Co., Utah. Hundreds of Indians were subsequently baptized at other places, and there was a general religious movement among the Lamanites.

June 10, 1874 (Wednesday)

Ex-Judge Solomon P. McCurdy, an old and inoffensive man, was assaulted and abused by Thomas Hackett, a soldier.

June 11, 1874 (Thursday)

A party of soldiers from Camp Douglas, under command of Major Gordon, broke into the jail at Salt Lake City and rescued their comrade, Thomas Hackett, who had been confined there for assaulting Solomon P. McCurdy the previous day.

June 11, 1874 (Thursday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 243 Saints (131 British, 91 Swiss and German, 10 Dutch and 11 Icelandic), in charge of Joseph Birch. The company arrived at New York June 23rd, and at Salt Lake City July 2nd.

June 23, 1874 (Tuesday)

The so-called Poland bill, "in relation to courts and judicial officers in the Territory of Utah," was approved, having been passed by the U.S. House of Representatives and Senate.

June 24, 1874 (Wednesday)

The steamship Idaho sailed from Liverpool, England, with 806 Saints (about 700 Scandinavian and 110 British), in charge of O.C. Carstensen. The company arrived at New York July 6th, and at Salt Lake City July 15th.

July 1874

This month was remarkable for much lightning, thunder and rain storms in Utah.

July 1, 1874 (Wednesday)

Patriarch Thomas Kington died at Wellsville, Cache Co.

July 4, 1874 (Saturday)

General Phil. H. Sheridan and party arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

July 8, 1874 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 81 Saints, in charge of John Keller. The company arrived at New York July 21st, and at Salt Lake City, July 30th.

July 17, 1874 (Friday)

Thomas Williams, treasurer of Z.C.M.I., and of the Salt Lake Theatre, died suddenly in Salt Lake City.

July 19, 1874 (Sunday)

The Glenwood branch, Sevier Co., Utah, was organized as a Ward; Archibald T. Oldroyd, Bishop.

July 24, 1874 (Friday)

The anniversary of the entrance of the Pioneers into Salt Lake Valley was celebrated by a grand juvenile jubilee, in the large Tabernacle, Salt Lake City. Four thousand musicians and singers participated.

August 2, 1874 (Sunday)

Edward L. Sloan, one of the founders of the Salt Lake Herald, died in Salt Lake City.

August 3, 1874 (Monday)

At the general election, Geo. Q. Cannon was re-elected Utah's delegate to Congress. He received 22,260 votes, and Robert N. Baskin, the Liberal candidate, 4,513.

August 3, 1874 (Monday)

An attempt was made by the "Liberal Party" to get possession of the polls of election in Salt Lake City. U.S. Marshal Maxwell and a horde of armed assistants rendered aid. Mayor Daniel H. Wells was mobbed, and considerable rioting done at the City Hall, but the plot proved a failure.

August 20, 1874 (Thursday)

Loptur Johnson, a native of Iceland, was accidentally killed, near Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

August 27, 1874 (Thursday)

General John E. Smith, the new Camp Douglas commander, arrived in Salt Lake City. He succeeded Gen. Henry A. Morrow.

August 29, 1874 (Saturday)

John McDonald, sen., fell from a haystack and was killed, in Salt Lake City.

September 1874

Wm. Fotheringham was arrested at Beaver on a charge of polygamy, and placed under $2,000 bonds.

September 2, 1874 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 558 Saints, mostly British, Swiss and German, in charge of John C. Graham. the company arrived at New York Sept. 14th, and at Salt Lake City Sept. 23rd.

September 11, 1874 (Friday)

The U.S. marshal seized the county clerk's office of Tooele County, upon an order issued by Judge McKean.

September 30, 1874 (Wednesday)

A terrible fire destroyed considerable grain and hay, at Huntsville, Weber Co.

October 4, 1874 (Sunday)

Jay Gould, accompanied by a distinguished party of wealthy railway gentlemen, arrived in Salt Lake City.

October 4, 1874 (Sunday)

Mrs. Elizabeth Adams, of Bountiful, Davis Co., was shot and killed by an unknown person, while engaged in reading in her own house.

October 5, 1874 (Monday)

Arthur Pratt, Fanny Stenhouse and others were excommunicated from the Church by the High Council, in Salt Lake City. On the 8th Andrew Cahoon was cut off. All these for apostasy.

October 6, 1874 (Tuesday)

Ephraim Green, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Oxford, Oneida Co, Idaho.

October 10, 1874 (Saturday)

Ann Eliza Webb Young, one of Pres. Brigham Young's wives, was excommunicated from the Church.

October 11, 1874 (Sunday)

Bishop Andrew H. Scott, of Provo (2nd Ward), Utah Co., died.

October 11, 1874 (Sunday)

The First Presbyterian Church in Salt Lake City was dedicated.

October 14, 1874 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 155 Saints, under the direction of Wm. N. Fife. The company arrived at New York Oct. 26th, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 5th.

October 22, 1874 (Thursday)

The first number of the Utah Scandinav, an anti-Mormon weekly newspaper, was issued in Salt Lake City, in the Danish-Norwegian language. After about three years' run it ceased publication.

October 26, 1874 (Monday)

Geo. Reynolds, who had been indicted by the grand jury for polygamy appeared in court and was placed under $2,500 bonds, awaiting trial.

October 29, 1874 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young, who for some time had been unwell, left Salt Lake City for the South, accompanied by Geo. A. Smith and other prominent men. They arrived at St. George Nov. 11th.

November 6, 1874 (Thursday)

A frightful hurricane did considerable damage in Tooele County.

November 9, 1874 (Monday)

John D. Lee, of Mountain Meadows celebrity, was arrested at Panguitch, Piute Co.

November 12, 1874 (Thursday)

Geo. Q. Cannon was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of polygamy, and placed under $5,000 bonds.

November 25, 1874 (Wednesday)

Phinehas Richards, brother of the late Pres. Willard Richards, and father of Apostle Franklin D. Richards, died in Salt Lake City.

December 4, 1874 (Friday)

Wm. Hepworth Dixon, a celebrated English author, visited Salt Lake City.

December 9, 1874 (Wednesday)

Major Edward P. Duzette, celebrated drummer of the Nauvoo brass band, died at Rockville, Kane Co.

December 25, 1874 (Friday)

The workmen of the St. George Temple had a Christmas assembly in the St. George Tabernacle.

December 28, 1874 (Monday)

Gov. Geo. L. Woods left Salt Lake City for the East, Samuel B. Axtell having been appointed his successor.
