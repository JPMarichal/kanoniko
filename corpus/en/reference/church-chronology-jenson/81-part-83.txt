Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The prosecutions under the Edmunds law for polygamy and unlawful cohabitation were continued, and nearly every settlement of the Saints were raided by U.S. deputy marshals, in search of polygamists. Fearing the impossibility of a fair trial, hundreds of the brethren and many families went into exile, some of whom sought refuge in Mexico and others in Canada. Nearly all the leaders of the Church were in hiding, and the situation throughout Utah was truly critical.

Chas. Harmon, jun., of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., taken before Com. McKay, examined and discharged.

December 7, 1886 (Tuesday)

Several houses at Lehi, Utah Co., were raided by U.S. deputy marshals, who arrested Bishop Thos. R. Cutler, Edwin Standring, James Kirkham, George Kirkham, John L. Gibb, Samuel James, John Hart and William Yates, for u.c.

December 8, 1886 (Wednesday)

Secretary Arthur L. Thomas was nominated a member of the Utah Commission by Pres. Cleveland.

December 8, 1886 (Wednesday)

The Lehi prisoners (except Wm. Yates, who was sick) were brought to Salt Lake City and placed under bonds, after examination before Com. McKay.

December 9, 1886 (Thursday)

John England, of Tooele, was arrested for u.c., taken to Salt Lake City and placed under $1,500 bonds, after examination before Com. McKay.

December 11, 1886 (Saturday)

Daniel Corbett, of the 2nd Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested for u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

December 13, 1886 (Monday)

Pres. Angus M. Cannon was arraigned in the Third District Court, where he was arrested again on three more charges, two for u.c. and one for polygamy.

December 13, 1886 (Monday)

John P. Sorensen, of Salt Lake City, was arrested, charged with polygamy, brought before Com. McKay and placed under bonds.

December 13, 1886 (Monday)

In the First District Court (Judge Henderson), at Ogden, James May, of Calls' Fort, Box Elder Co.; Fred. W. Ellis, of Pleasant View, Weber Co., Thomas B. Helm and Henry B. Gwilliam, of South Hooper, were, each, sentenced to six months' imprisonment in the Penitentiary and $100 fine.

December 14, 1886 (Tuesday)

In the Third District Court, Thomas Allsop, of Sandy, who plead guilty to a three count indictment for u.c., was sentenced by Judge Zane to fifteen months' imprisonment and $50 fine, and sent to the Penitentiary.

December 15, 1886 (Wednesday)

After a lengthy examination before Com. McKay, all the charges against Pres. Angus M. Cannon were dismissed, and he was discharged.

December 16, 1886 (Thursday)

Edward M. Dalton was foully murdered by U.S. Deputy Marshal Wm. Thompson, jun., at Parowan, Iron Co. The murderer was arrested.

December 16, 1886 (Thursday)

Matthew Pickett, of Tooele, was arraigned in the Third District Court, charging him with u.c. He plead not guilty.

December 16, 1886 (Thursday)

Thomas Jeremy and Peter Gillespie, of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City were arrested, charged with u.c. and placed under bonds.

December 17, 1886 (Friday)

Under the auspicies of the Old Folk's Committee, the "Old Folks," widows and orphans, of Salt Lake County, were treated to a free entertainment in the Theatre, Salt Lake City, the Home Dramatic Club playing "Confusion."

December 18, 1886 (Saturday)

After examination before Com. McKay, the polygamy case against John P. Sorensen was dismissed for lack of evidence.

December 20, 1886 (Monday)

After the usual examination before Com. McKay, the u.c. case against Peter Gillespie, of Tooele, was dismissed, and the defendant discharged.

December 21, 1886 (Tuesday)

In the Second District Court, at Beaver, the grand jury indicted the murderer, Wm. Thompson, jun., for manslaughter.

December 21, 1886 (Tuesday)

Bishop Wm. E. Bassett, of the 20th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested and placed under $10,000 bonds, being indicted by the grand jury for polygamy.

December 24, 1886 (Friday)

Elder John Horspool died at Ogden.

December 26, 1886 (Sunday)

Elder John Hindley died at American Fork, Utah Co.

December 27, 1886 (Monday)

John P. Jones and John Lee Jones, of Enoch, Iron Co., and Joseph H. Thurber, of Greenwich, Piute Co., were imprisoned in the Penitentiary, the two former for u.c., and the latter for polygamy and u.c., all having been sentenced by Judge Boreman in the Second District Court, at Beaver.

December 27, 1886 (Monday)

The 18th Ward (Salt Lake City) Independent School House was formally opened.

December 28, 1886 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, after trial, the jury returned a verdict of guilty against Abraham Chadwick, of Ogden, and N.C. Mortensen, of Huntsville, for u.c.

December 29, 1886 (Wednesday)

Helon H. Tracy, of Ogden, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 29, 1886 (Wednesday)

Brigham Y. Hampton, having served his term of imprisonment for alleged conspiracy, was released from the county jail.

December 30, 1886 (Thursday)

Mary M.D. Nebeker, relict of Peter Nebeker, died at Willard City, Box Elder co.

December 30, 1886 (Thursday)

Peter Petersen, of Richville, Morgan Co., was imprisoned in the Penitentiary, having been sentenced by Judge Henderson, in the First District Court at Ogden, to six months' imprisonment and $100 fine for u.c.
