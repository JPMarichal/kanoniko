Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Indian difficulties in the South, known as the Black Hawk war, became more serious; companies of militia were sent from the North to protect the settlers; but nearly all the settlements on the upper Sevier and those in Kane County were deserted by their inhabitants, who moved to the older and stronger towns for safety. Grasshoppers destroyed the crops in different parts of the Territory. No Church teams were sent this year to the Missouri river for the poor, in consequence of which the immigration was comparatively small.

January 1867

The Utah legislature petitioned Congress to repeal the anti-bigamy law of 1862, and the general assembly of Deseret prayed for admission into the Union as a State.

January 1867

The Indians made a raid on Pine Valley, Washington Co., and captured a band of horses. Capt. Andrus, with a company of cavalry, followed them, recovered most of the horses and killed seven Indians.

January 7, 1867 (Monday)

John Lowry, sen., one of the first settlers of Sanpete Valley, died at Manti.

January 15, 1867 (Tuesday)

The Deseret Telegraph Line was opened to St. George.

January 23, 1867 (Wednesday)

Amasa M. Lyman, in a communication addressed to the Latter-day Saints in all the world, acknowledged his doctrine, annulling the atonement of Jesus Christ, to be false.

February 24, 1867 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled west of the river Jordan, west of G.S.L. City, were organized into a Ward called Brighton, with Andrew W. Cooley as Bishop.

February 25, 1867 (Monday)

Patriarch Mark Anthony Coombs died at Beaver, Utah.

February 26, 1867 (Tuesday)

James W. Huntsman died at Shoal Creek, Washington Co., Utah.

March 21, 1867 (Thursday)

The Deseret Telegraph Co., incorporated Jan. 18, 1867, was organized, with Brigham Young as president.

March 21, 1867 (Thursday)

The Indians made a raid on the stock of Richfield and Glenwood, Sevier Co. killing Jens Peter Petersen and wife (Charlotte Amalie) and Miss Smith, all of Richfield.

March 29, 1867 (Friday)

Geo. Davis was accidentally killed near G.S.L. City.

April 6, 1867 (Saturday)

The 37th annual conference of the Church was commenced in G.S.L. City. It was continued till the 8th.

April 20, 1867 (Saturday)

Richfield, Sevier Co., was deserted by its inhabitants because of Indian trouble. About the same time the other settlements in Sevier and those in Piute County were abandoned for the same cause, as well as the settlements of Berryville, Winsor, Upper and Lower Kanab, Shunesberg, Springdale and Northup, and many ranches in Kane County; also the settlements of Panguitch and Fort Sandford, in Iron County.

April 22, 1867 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young and company left G.S.L. City, on a tour to "Dixie," from which they returned May 15th, after traveling seven hundred miles.

June 1, 1867 (Saturday)

Lois Lund was killed and Jasper Robertson wounded, near Fountain Green, Sanpete Co., by Indians, who also drove off 40 horses.

June 1, 1867 (Saturday)

Twenty Saints sailed from London, England, on the ship Hudson, bound for Utah. The vessel arrived at New York July 19th.

June 2, 1867 (Sunday)

Major John W. Vance, of Alpine, Utah Co., and Heber Houtz, of G.S.L. City, were waylaid and killed by Indians on Twelve Mile creek, Sanpete Co., Utah.

June 3, 1867 (Monday)

Geo. W. Rogers was accidentally killed in G.S.L. City, by the falling of a bank of earth.

June 14, 1867 (Friday)

The Indians made a raid on Beaver, Utah, and captured a large herd of stock.

June 21, 1867 (Friday)

The steamship Manhattan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 480 Saints, under the direction of Archibald N. Hill. It arrived in New York July 4th, and the emigrants continued the journey to North Platte, a station on the Union Pacific Railroad, 391 miles west of Omaha. From that place the journey across the plains was commenced, Aug. 8th, with ox teams, under the direction of Capt. Leonard G. Rice, and the company arrived in G.S.L. City Oct. 5th.

July 1867

Apostle Franklin D. Richards succeeded Brigham Young, jun, as president of the European mission.

July 6, 1867 (Saturday)

The Saints in Pine Valley, Washington Co., Utah, were organized into a separate Ward, with Wm. Snow as Bishop.

July 19, 1867 (Friday)

The grasshoppers appeared in vast numbers and did great damage to the crops in Utah during the following few weeks.

July 21, 1867 (Sunday)

The Indians made a descent upon the stock on Little creek, near Parowan, Iron Co., but were driven back by a company of cavalry.

July 24, 1867 (Wednesday)

Brigham Willard Kimball, a son of Heber C. Kimball, died on Pole creek, while returning from a mission to England.

August 3, 1867 (Saturday)

Robert Todd, sen., of Tooele, was found dead on the Jordan bottoms, G.S.L. Co.

August 4, 1867 (Sunday)

Anson V. Call died on Laramie plains, while returning home from a mission to England.

August 13, 1867 (Tuesday)

The Indians made a raid on Springtown, Sanpete Co., killing James Meeks and Andrew Johansen. They also wounded another man and captured a band of horses.

August 20, 1867 (Tuesday)

The Harmony branch, Washington Co., Utah, was organized into a Ward by Apostle Erastus Snow, with Wilson D. Pace as Bishop.

August 24, 1867 (Saturday)

The Provo meeting house (81 feet long and 47 feet wide, with a tower 80 feet high) was dedicated.

September 5, 1867 (Thursday)

John Hay, of Capt. Wm. L. Binder's company of militia, was killed by Indians, near Fayette, Sanpete Co.

September 18, 1867 (Wednesday)

The Indians made another raid on Beaver, and drove off two hundred head of horses and cattle.

October 6, 1867 (Sunday)

The first conference held in the large Tabernacle, in G.S.L. City, was commenced. It continued until the 9th. This structure, which had just been completed, was 250 feet long and 150 feet wide, with its immense roof, arched without a pillar. Height of interior, 68 feet from floor to ceiling. During the conference 163 missionaries were called to strengthen the settlements in southern Utah, and the Saints were called upon to assist liberally the following year towards emigrating all the poor Saints from Great Britain. On the 8th Joseph F. Smith was chosen to fill the vacancy in the Council of the Twelve Apostles, occasioned by the apostasy of Amasa M. Lyman.

October 22, 1867 (Tuesday)

Vilate Murray Kimball, wife of Pres. Heber C. Kimball, died in G.S.L. City.

November 13, 1867 (Wednesday)

The Union Pacific Railway was completed to Cheyenne.

November 21, 1867 (Thursday)

The first number of the Deseret Evening News was issued in G.S.L. City; Geo. Q. Cannon, editor.

December 1, 1867 (Saturday)

Benjamin Stringham was appointed to preside over the Saints at Bennington (now Leeds) and Harrisburg, Washington Co., Utah, as acting Bishop.

December 17, 1867 (Tuesday)

Bishop Caleb G. Edwards died at Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

December 24, 1867 (Tuesday)

Millersburgh and other small towns in southern Utah, on the Rio Virgen, were almost completely destroyed by a flood.

December 25, 1867 (Wednesday)

John James and wife, of Willard, Box Elder Co., were accidentally drowned in Sand creek.
