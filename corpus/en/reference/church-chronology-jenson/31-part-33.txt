Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Council of Twelve Apostles and the First Quorum of Seventy were organized at Kirtland, O. The Book of Doctrine and Covenants was accepted by the Church, and Joseph Smith, jun., obtained some Egyptian rolls of papyrus containing the writings of Abraham, etc.

February 1835

The Northern Times, a weekly newspaper supporting democracy, was commenced by the Saints at Kirtland, O.

February 14, 1835 (Saturday)

At a special meeting held in Kirtland twelve Apostles were chosen by the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon according to revelation (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 18:37), namely: Thos. B. Marsh, David W. Patten, Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, Orson Hyde, Wm. E. McLellin, Parley P. Pratt, Luke S. Johnson, Wm. Smith, Orson Pratt, John F. Boynton and Lyman E. Johnson. Brigham Young and Heber C. Kimball were ordained and blessed the same day.

February 15, 1835 (Sunday)

Orson Hyde, David W. Patten, Luke S. Johnson, Wm. E. McLellin, John F. Boynton and Wm. Smith were ordained Apostles.

February 21, 1835 (Saturday)

Parley P. Pratt was ordained to the Apostleship. Thos. B. Marsh and Orson Pratt, who were absent on missions, were not ordained until their return in April.

February 28, 1835 (Saturday)

The organization of the First Quorum of Seventy was commenced at Kirtland.

March 28, 1835 (Saturday)

An important revelation concerning the order of the Priesthood was given to Joseph Smith, jun., at Kirtland. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 107.)

May 2, 1835 (Saturday)

Elders Brigham Young, John P. Greene and Amos Orton were appointed to preach the gospel to the Lamanites.

May 4, 1835 (Monday)

The Twelve left Kirtland on their first mission as Apostles.

July 3, 1835 (Friday)

Michael H. Chandler arrived at Kirtland to exhibit four Egyptian mummies and some rolls of papyrus, covered with hieroglyphic figures and devices. They were afterwards purchased by some of the Saints, and Joseph the Prophet translated some of the characters on the rolls. One was found to contain the writings of Abraham, subsequently published in the Pearl of Great Price; another the writings of Joseph in Egypt.

August 17, 1835 (Monday)

At a general assembly of the Church, held at Kirtland, the Book of Doctrine and Covenants was approved, and thus became a law of faith and practice to the Church.

September 14, 1835 (Monday)

Oliver Cowdery was appointed to act as Church Recorder, and Emma Smith to make a selection of sacred hymns, according to revelation. (Doc. and Cov., 25:11.)

October 25, 1835 (Sunday)

The Twelve returned to Kirtland from their mission to the East.

October 29, 1835 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith, jun., was abused by his brother William in a council meeting, held at Kirtland.

November 27, 1835 (Friday)

Christian Whitmer, one of the Eight Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, died in Clay County, Missouri.

December 16, 1835 (Wednesday)

Wm. Smith became enraged in a debating school, held at Kirtland, and used violence upon the person of his brother Joseph Smith, jun., and, others.

December 26, 1835 (Saturday)

Joseph Smith, jun., with other Elders, commenced studying the Hebrew language, having previously commenced reading Greek. Mr. Seixas, a competent professor of languages, was subsequently employed as teacher.

December 26, 1835 (Saturday)

A revelation, concerning Lyman Sherman, was given through Joseph Smith, jun., at Kirtland. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 108.)
