Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Edmunds anti-polygamy law was passed by Congress, which later introduced legal proceedings of an extra-ordinary character in Utah. New Stakes of Zion were organized in Castle Valley, Utah, and in Salt river, Ariz. The first branches of the Church were organized in Cassia County, Idaho.

January 2, 1882 (Monday)

Pres. John Taylor moved into the Gardo House, Salt Lake City. A public reception was given, in which over two thousand people participated.

January 8, 1882 (Sunday)

The Salt Lake Assembly Hall was dedicated.

January 9, 1882 (Monday)

The Utah legislature (25th session) met in Salt Lake City, and organized by electing Joseph F. Smith president of the Council, and Francis M. Lyman speaker of the House.

January 9, 1882 (Monday)

Hans F. Petersen, the first Latter-day Saint missionary to Norway, died at Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

January 10, 1882 (Tuesday)

The Utah election case was argued in the U.S. House of Representatives, and, after a hot debate, deferred to the Committee on Elections.

January 25, 1882 (Wednesday)

Elizabeth Hoagland Cannon, wife of Geo. Q. Cannon, died in Salt Lake City, while her husband was attending to his public duties in Washington, D.C.

February 1882

The Latter-day Saint missionaries in the Southern States were subject to much persecution.

February 13, 1882 (Monday)

Wm. Jennings was elected mayor of Salt Lake City.

February 15, 1882 (Wednesday)

Elizabeth A. Whitney, widow of Bishop Newel K. Whitney, died in Salt Lake City, aged 81 years.

February 16, 1882 (Thursday)

The Edmunds anti-polygamy bill was passed by the U.S. Senate. As soon as this became known in Utah, three petitions, asking Congress to send a deputation to investigate affairs in the Territory, before undertaking any hostile legislation against the people, were prepared and received about 75,000 signatures. They were treated with indifference.

February 17, 1882 (Friday)

A family of seven, named Teckett, was killed by an avalanche in Big Cottonwood Canyon.

February 24, 1882 (Friday)

Elder William Henry Butler, of Kaysville, Davis Co., Utah, died at Birmingham, England, where he labored as a missionary.

February 25, 1882 (Saturday)

After four hours' investigation, the U.S. Committee on Elections decided that neither Cannon nor Campbell was entitled to a seat in Congress.

March 7, 1882 (Tuesday)

Thos. B.H. Stenhouse, formerly prominent in the Church, died at San Francisco, Cal.

March 10, 1882 (Friday)

The Utah legislature adjourned after 60 days' session; 72 acts were passed, of which 16 were vetoed by Gov. Murray. Among the latter was one that appropriated $40,000 for the completion of the Deseret University. Garfield County was organized during this session.

March 12, 1882 (Sunday)

Richard V. Morris, Bishop of the 19th Ward, died in Salt Lake City.

March 14, 1882 (Tuesday)

The Edmunds anti-polygamy bill was passed by the United States House of Representatives. A few days later it was signed by Pres. Chester A. Arthur, and thus became law.

March 16, 1882 (Thursday)

The first number of Morgenstjernen, afterwards the Historical Record, was issued in Salt Lake City; Andrew Jenson, editor.

April 6, 1882 (Thursday)

The 52nd annual conference was commenced in Salt Lake City; it was continued four days; 127 missionaries were called.

April 10, 1882 (Monday)

A constitutional convention, consisting of delegates from all the counties of Utah and authorized by the late legislature, met in Salt Lake City for the purpose of framing a State Constitution and again petitioning Congress to admit Utah into the Union as a State. Regular meetings were held until the 27th, when the "Constitution for the State of Utah" was adopted by unanimous vote.

April 11, 1882 (Tuesday)

A large company of missionaries left Salt Lake City, for the United States and Europe.

April 12, 1882 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 343 Saints, including 11 returning missionaries, under the direction of John Donaldson. the company arrived in New York, April 24th, and at Ogden and Salt Lake City, May 1st.

April 10, 1882 (Wednesday)

Hon. Geo. Q. Cannon delivered a powerful speech in the U.S. House of Representatives, in vindication of the people of Utah.

April 10, 1882 (Wednesday)

On this and the following day the Utah election case was argued in the U.S. House of Representatives, and Geo. Q. Cannon was denied his seat in Congress on account of polygamy.

April 23, 1882 (Sunday)

Professor James L. Barfoot, curator of the Deseret Museum, died in Salt Lake City.

May 4, 1882 (Thursday)

Anders Christensen a prominent Elder, died at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

May 17, 1882 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 392 Saints, including a number of returning Elders, under the direction of Wm. R. Webb. They arrived in New York May 27th, and in Salt Lake City June 4th.

May 22, 1882 (Monday)

The constitution adopted by the constitutional convention was ratified by a general vote of the people of Utah Territory.

June 1882

Thurber Ward, Rabbit Valley, Utah, was organized by Apostles Francis M. Lyman and John H. Smith; Geo. Brinkerhoff, Bishop.

June 4, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints at Burrville, Sevier Co., were organized as a Ward, by Apostles Francis M. Lyman and John Henry Smith; Wm. H. Cloward, Bishop.

June 5, 1882 (Monday)

The Walker Opera House, Salt Lake City, was opened with a concert by the Careless Orchestra.

June 6, 1882 (Tuesday)

The State convention again met in Salt Lake City and prepared a petition to Congress for Utah's admission into the Union. Wm. H. Hooper, John T. Caine, James Sharp, Wm. W. Riter, Franklin S. Richards, David H. Peery and Wm. D. Johnson, jun, were chosen as delegates to present the same to Congress.

June 10, 1882 (Saturday)

Levi W. Hancock, one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies, died at Washington, Washington Co.

June 11, 1882 (Sunday)

Dingle Dell branch, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, was organized as Cottonwood (later Dingle) Ward; Samuel A. Wilcox, Bishop.

June 16, 1882 (Friday)

Pres. Chester A. Arthur nominated Alex, Ramsey, of Minnesota, A.S. Paddock, of Nebraska, G.L. Godfrey, of Iowa, A.B. Carlton, of Indiana, and James R. Pettigrew, of Arkansas, as members of the Utah Commission, provided for in the Edmunds law.

June 17, 1882 (Saturday)

Liberty Park, Salt Lake City, was formally opened to the public.

June 21, 1882 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 932 souls, in charge of Robert R. Irvine. They arrived at New York July 2nd, and at Ogden July 9th.

June 24, 1882 (Saturday)

Nathan E. Tenney was shot and killed by Mexicans at St. Johns, Apache Co., Ariz., while endeavoring to act as peacemaker between contending parties.

June 27, 1882 (Tuesday)

Mary Angell Young, widow of Pres. Brigham Young, died in Salt Lake City.

June 28, 1882 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks from Salt Lake, Utah, Juab, Tooele, Davis and Weber Counties enjoyed a grand feast in Salt Lake City. The festivities were continued the following day.

July 9, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the bench lands west of North Ogden, Weber Co., Utah, were detached from the North Ogden Ward, and organized as Pleasant View Ward; Edward W. Wade, Bishop.

July 16, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Wilson school district, Weber Co., were separated from West Weber and organized as Wilson Ward; Brigham H. Bingham, Bishop.

July 17, 1882 (Monday)

The Deseret Hospital, Salt Lake City, was dedicated and opened for business in the 12th Ward.

July 17, 1882 (Monday)

Harriet Whittaker Taylor, wife of Pres. John Taylor, died in Salt Lake City.

July 19, 1882 (Wednesday)

Elder Jacob Samuel Ferrin was killed by Indians, near San Carlos, Ariz.

July 21, 1882 (Friday)

The Clover Ward, Tooele Co, was organized; Francis De St Jeor, Bishop.

July 22, 1882 (Saturday)

A company of Saints, comprising 13 Icelanders and 5 British, sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Arizona, bound for Utah.

August 1, 1882 (Tuesday)

The first number of the Utah Journal was issued in Logan, Cache Co., Utah, in place of the Logan Leader, suspended.

August 6, 1882 (Sunday)

J.D. Farmer, a merchant of Salt Lake City, was lost in the Great Salt Lake, while bathing; the body could not be found.

August 8, 1882 (Tuesday)

Edward Martin, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in the 14th Ward, Salt Lake City.

August 8, 1882 (Tuesday)

The U.S. Congress adjourned; the Senate amendment to the civil appropriation bill (commonly known as the Hoar amendment), authorizing the governor of Utah to fill offices, supposed to be vacant in that Territory, was one of the last acts passed.

August 13, 1882 (Sunday)

At a special conference held at Castle Dale, Emery Co., attended by Apostles Erastus Snow and John H. Smith, the Emery Stake of Zion was more fully organized, with Christen G. Larsen as president and Orange Seeley and Rasmus Justesen counselors. Orangeville Ward was organized; Jasper Robertson, Bishop; Henning Olsen was ordained Bishop of the Castle Dale Ward.

August 16, 1882 (Wednesday)

David G. Bigler, a brakeman on the Utah Central Railway, was accidentally killed, near River Side Station.

August 18, 1882 (Friday)

The Utah Commission, consisting of five men, appointed by the President of the United States, arrived in Salt Lake City. They went to work almost immediately, preparing for the November election.

August 18, 1882 (Friday)

Bishop Geo. W. Ward died at Willard, Box Elder Co.

August 20, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Parley's Canyon, Salt Lake Co., were organized as Mountain Dell Ward; Wm. B. Hardy, Bishop.

August 23, 1882 (Wednesday)

A large company of missionaries left Salt Lake City for the United States and Europe.

August 29, 1882 (Tuesday)

The Presidency of the Church issued an epistle to the Saints, advising all who could legally register and vote under the Edmunds law, to do so.

September 1882

Branches of the Church were organized by Francis M. Lyman, president of the Tooele Stake of Zion, at Oakley, Little Basin, Albion, Cassia and Almo, Cassia County, Idaho, where a number of Saints had recently located. The several branches were organized as the Cassia Ward; Wm. C. Martindale, Bishop.

September 2, 1882 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 662 Saints, including 16 returning missionaries, under the direction of William Cooper. The company arrived in New York Sept. 12th, and in Salt Lake City Sept. 21st.

September 3, 1882 (Sunday)

Sandy Ward, Salt Lake Co., was organized out of a part of Union Ward; Ezekiel Holman, Bishop.

September 16, 1882 (Saturday)

Gov. Eli H. Murray, on the strength of the Hoar amendment, issued a proclamation appointing a great number of men to fill local offices, claimed to be vacant on account of the August election not being held. The incumbents, who held over under the statute, however, refused to recognize the governor's appointees as their successors, and the case was taken into the courts.

October 1, 1882 (Sunday)

The west part of Brighton Ward, Salt Lake Co., was organized as Pleasant Green Ward; Lehi N. Hardman, Bishop.

October 6, 1882 (Friday)

The semi-annual conference of the Church, which continued three days, was held in Salt Lake City; 78 missionaries were called.

October 9, 1882 (Monday)

Abraham Hoagland Cannon was ordained one of the First Seven Presidents of Seventies.

October 13, 1882 (Friday)

George Teasdale and Heber J. Grant were chosen by revelation to fill the vacancies in the Council of Twelve Apostles, caused by the death of Orson Pratt and the recent re-organization of the First Presidency. Seymour B. Young was chosen by the same revelation as one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies. These three brethren were ordained on the 16th.

October 17, 1882 (Tuesday)

About sixty missionaries bound for the United States and Europe, left Salt Lake City.

October 21, 1882 (Saturday)

The steamship Abyssinia sailed from Liverpool, England, with 416 Saints, including 28 returning missionaries, under the direction of Geo. Stringfellow. They landed in New York Nov. 3rd, and arrived in Salt Lake City Nov. 10th.

October 24, 1882 (Tuesday)

Melvina H. Snow, widow of Willard Snow, died in Salt Lake City.

October 29, 1882 (Sunday)

Francis M. Pomeroy, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Mesa, Maricopa Co., Ariz.

November 4, 1882 (Saturday)

An important political ratification meeting was held by the People's Party in the Salt Lake Theater.

November 7, 1882 (Tuesday)

A general election was held in Utah, in which the People's Party candidate, John T. Caine, received 23,039 votes, and the "Liberal" candidate, Philip T. Van Zile, only 4,884 votes.

November 13, 1882 (Monday)

The Utah Commission submitted their first report to the government on the situation in Utah.

November 25, 1882 (Saturday)

Apostle John Henry Smith succeeded Apostle Albert Carrington as president of the European mission.

November 28, 1882 (Tuesday)

Gen. Phil. H. Sheridan arrived in Salt Lake City, on an inspection tour to the military posts in the Territory.

November 28, 1882 (Tuesday)

The Coveville branch, near Richmond, Cache Co., was organized as Coveville Ward; John C. Larsen, Bishop.

November 28, 1882 (Tuesday)

Riverdale branch, Oneida County, Idaho, was organized as a Ward; Peter Preece, Bishop.

December 6, 1882 (Wednesday)

The first Latter-day Saint meeting house in Snake River Valley, Idaho, was completed at Egin (Parker).

December 10, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints who had located on Salt river, Maricopa Co., Ariz., were organized as the Maricopa Stake of Zion, by Apostles Erastus Snow and Moses Thatcher, with Alexander F. Macdonald as president, and Henry C. Rogers and Charles I. Robson as counselors. Jonesville (now Lehi), Mesa, and the Tempe branch were organized as Wards, with Thos. E. Jones, Elijah Pomeroy and David T. LeBaron as their respective Bishops.

December 18, 1882 (Monday)

Thos. E. Ricks, of Logan, Utah, was called to preside as Bishop over the Saints in Snake River Valley, Idaho. Soon afterwards they were organized as Bannock Ward.

December 21, 1882 (Thursday)

Artemesia Snow, wife of Apostle Erastus Snow, died in St. George.

December 30, 1882 (Saturday)

Hon. Wm. H. Hooper died in Salt Lake City.

December 31, 1882 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the San Pedro river, Ariz., were organized by Apostles Erastus Snow and Moses Thatcher as St. David Ward; David P. Kimball, Bishop.
