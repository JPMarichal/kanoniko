Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

During this and the preceding year Joseph the Prophet preached many powerful sermons and uttered a number of important prophecies. While on a visit to Dixon, Ill., he had a narrow escape from being kidnapped under legal pretense and taken to Missouri. The revelation on celestial marriage was given and the first missionaries sent to the Society Islands.

January 2, 1843 (Monday)

Joseph Smith prophesied that he should not go to Missouri dead or alive.

January 4, 1843 (Wednesday)

Joseph Smith was on trial before Judge Pope, of Springfield, on the accusation of being an accessory to the shooting of ex-Governor Boggs of Missouri.

January 5, 1843 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith was proven innocent and acquitted.

January 10, 1843 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith and company arrived at Nauvoo from the trip to Springfield.

January 16, 1843 (Monday)

The ship Swanton sailed from Liverpool with 212 Saints for New Orleans, led by Lorenzo Snow. The emigrants arrived at Nauvoo April 12th.

January 17, 1843 (Tuesday)

The Saints being overjoyed because of Joseph Smith's release, meetings of prayer and thanksgiving were held at Nauvoo.

February 7, 1843 (Tuesday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt arrived at Nauvoo from his mission to England.

February 9, 1843 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith received by revelation three grand keys, by which bad angels, or spirits, may be known. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 129.)

March 1843

A "Young Gentlemen's and Ladies' Relief Society" was organized at Nauvoo, with Wm. Cutler as president.

March 1843

Joseph Smith studied the German language.

March 3, 1843 (Friday)

The Illinois legislature passed a bill for repealing the Nauvoo city charter, which, however, was not approved.

March 4, 1843 (Saturday)

O. Porter Rockwell was taken prisoner in St. Louis by the Missourians.

March 8, 1843 (Wednesday)

The ship Yorkshire sailed from Liverpool, England, with 82 Saints on board, led by Thomas Bullock; the emigrants arrived at Nauvoo, May 31st, via New Orleans.

March 15, 1843 (Wednesday)

Joseph Smith prophesied that O. Porter Rockwell would get away honorably from the Missourians.

March 21, 1843 (Tuesday)

The ship Clayborne sailed from Liverpool with 106 Saints.

April 2, 1843 (Sunday)

"Important Items of Instruction" were given by Joseph Smith, at Nauvoo, who also prophesied "that the commencement of the difficulties which will cause much bloodshed previous to the coming of the Son of Man, will be in South Carolina." (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 130.)

April 6, 1843 (Thursday)

At a conference held in the Temple, at Kirtland, O., it was decided that all the Saints residing at that place should remove to Nauvoo, Ill.

April 6, 1843 (Thursday)

An important conference, which continued its sessions till the 8th, was commenced on the floor of the Temple, at Nauvoo, Ill. Joseph Smith prophesied that Christ would not come until he (Joseph) was eight-five years of age.

April 10, 1843 (Monday)

About one hundred and fifteen Elders were called on missions to different States, at a special conference held at Nauvoo.

April 13, 1843 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith preached to the British Saints, who had arrived at Nauvoo the day previous.

April 23, 1843 (Sunday)

Six brass plates and a skeleton were found by Mr. R. Wiley and others, near Kinderhook, Pike Co., Ill.

May 3, 1843 (Wednesday)

The first number of the Nauvoo Neighbor, a newspaper, was issued at Nauvoo, instead of the Wasp, suspended.

May 16, 1843 (Tuesday)

On this and the following day Joseph Smith made some important remarks about the celestial glory, at Ramus, Ill. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 131.)

May 18, 1843 (Thursday)

Returning to Nauvoo from his visit to Ramus, Joseph Smith dined with Judge Stephen A. Douglas, at Carthage, Hancock Co., Ill. During the conversation which took place Joseph prophesied that Judge Douglas would aspire to the Presidency of the United States, and added that if he ever turned his hand against the Latter-day Saints, he should feel the hand of the Almighty upon him, etc.

May 23, 1843 (Tuesday)

Addison Pratt, Noah Rogers, Benjamin F. Grouard and Knowlton F. Hanks were set apart for a mission to the Pacific Islands.

May 26, 1843 (Friday)

Joseph Smith gave endowments, and also instructions on the Priesthood and the new and everlasting covenant, to Hyrum Smith, Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball and others, at Nauvoo.

June 1, 1843 (Thursday)

Addison Pratt, Benjamin F. Grouard, Knowlton F. Hanks and Noah Rogers left Nauvoo on their missions to the Pacific Islands.

June 8, 1843 (Thursday)

Elias Higbee died in Nauvoo.

June 11, 1843 (Sunday)

A conference was held at Lima, Ill., and the branch at that place reorganized, with Isaac Morley as president, and Gardiner Snow, Bishop.

June 13, 1843 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith left Nauvoo with his wife Emma to visit her sister, living near Dixon, Lee County, Ill.

June 23, 1843 (Friday)

Joseph Smith was arrested and brutally treated by Joseph H. Reynolds, sheriff of Jackson Co., Mo., and Constable Harmon T. Wilson, of Carthage, Ill., without legal process, and only through interference of friends at Dixon saved from being kidnapped and taken to Missouri.

June 24, 1843 (Saturday)

The corner stones of the Masonic Temple at Nauvoo were laid.

June 24, 1843 (Saturday)

Joseph Smith secured a writ of habeas corpus and started towards Ottawa to have his case examined by Judge John D. Caton, but, arriving at Pawpaw Grove, the company learned that Judge Caton was not at home, and, therefore, returned to Dixon the following day.

June 25, 1843 (Sunday)

News of Joseph Smith being kidnapped reached Nauvoo, and 175 men immediately started on horseback to his rescue.

June 26, 1843 (Monday)

Joseph Smith started under guard towards Quincy, Ill.

June 27, 1843 (Tuesday)

The company traveling with Joseph Smith was met by the brethren from Nauvoo, when it was decided that instead of going to Quincy to have the writ of habeas corpus examined, the prisoner and escort should proceed to Nauvoo.

June 30, 1843 (Friday)

Joseph Smith and company arrived at Nauvoo, nearly the whole city turning out to meet him. In the afternoon he addressed the people, giving the history of his arrest. While he was speaking Officers Reynolds and Wilson started for Carthage and tried to raise a mob; afterwards they petitioned Gov. Thos. Ford for militia to take Joseph out of Nauvoo by force.

July 1, 1843 (Saturday)

Joseph Smith was tried before the municipal court of Nauvoo on a writ of habeas corpus and acquitted.

July 2, 1843 (Sunday)

Joseph Smith had a pleasant interview with several Pottawattamie chiefs who had come to visit him, and a very good impression was made upon the Indians.

July 2, 1843 (Sunday)

The steamboat Maid of Iowa returned to Nauvoo, after a very adventurous trip in search of Joseph. The brethren who had participated in that river expedition, numbering about eighty, were blessed by the Prophet.

July 3, 1843 (Monday)

A number of Elders were called to visit the various counties of Illinois, to preach the gospel and disabuse the public mind with regard to Joseph Smith's arrest.

July 3, 1843 (Monday)

Charles C. Rich and a company of twenty-five men, who had been out searching for the Prophet, returned to Nauvoo, having traveled about five hundred miles on horseback in seven days.

July 4, 1843 (Tuesday)

Nauvoo was visited by about one thousand gentlemen and ladies from St. Louis, Quincy and Burlington.

July 7, 1843 (Friday)

Mr. M. Braman arrived at Nauvoo as a messenger from the governor, to learn the particulars of Joseph Smith's late arrest.

July 8, 1843 (Saturday)

Bishop George Miller arrived at Nauvoo from the Pineries with 157,000 feet of lumber and 70,000 shingles for the Temple.

July 12, 1843 (Wednesday)

The revelation on celestial marriage was written in the presence of Hyrum Smith and Wm. Clayton. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 132.)

August 11, 1843 (Friday)

General James Adams, of Springfield, died at Nauvoo.

August 31, 1843 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith moved into the Nauvoo Mansion.

September 5, 1843 (Tuesday)

The ship Mitoka sailed from Liverpool with 280 Saints, bound for Nauvoo.

September 15, 1843 (Friday)

Joseph Smith opened the Nauvoo Mansion as a hotel.

September 30, 1843 (Saturday)

Reuben Hedlock and other missionaries from Nauvoo arrived at Liverpool, England.

October 3, 1843 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith gave a dinner party in the Nauvoo Mansion to about two hundred Saints.

October 6, 1843 (Friday)

A special conference of the Church, which continued its sessions on the 8th, was commenced at Nauvoo, Ill. Serious complaints were made against Sidney Rigdon.

October 8, 1843 (Sunday)

At a meeting of the special conference at Nauvoo, Sidney Rigdon was sustained as a Counselor to Joseph Smith, although the Prophet said, "I have thrown him off my shoulders, and you have again put him on me; you may carry him, but I will not."

October 9, 1843 (Monday)

Addison Pratt, Noah Rogers, Benjamin F. Grouard and Knowlton F. Hanks sailed from New Bedford, Mass., on board the ship Timoleon, for the Pacific Islands.

October 20, 1843 (Friday)

John P. Greene returned to Nauvoo, from a mission to the State of New York, with about one hundred emigrants.

October 21, 1843 (Saturday)

The ship Champion sailed from Liverpool with 91 Saints, bound for Nauvoo.

October 22, 1843 (Sunday)

Apostles Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball and George A. Smith returned to Nauvoo from a mission to the Eastern States.

November 3, 1843 (Friday)

Knowlton F. Hanks, one of the missionaries to the Pacific Islands, died. He was the first Latter-day Saint Elder who died and was buried at sea.

November 6, 1843 (Monday)

Erastus Snow returned to Nauvoo with a company of immigrants from Massachusetts.

November 29, 1843 (Sunday)

Philander Avery was kidnapped from the neighborhood of Warsaw and carried forcibly across the Mississippi river to Missouri.

December 2, 1843 (Saturday)

Apostles Orson Hyde, Parley P. Pratt, Wilford Woodruff and George A. Smith and Elder Orson Spencer received their endowments at Nauvoo, Ill.; 35 persons were present.

December 2, 1843 (Saturday)

Daniel Avery was kidnapped from Bear Creek, Hancock Co, Ill., by a company of Missourians, and imprisoned in Monticello jail, Lewis Co., Mo., where his son Philander was already confined.

December 7, 1843 (Thursday)

The German brethren met at the Assembly Room at Nauvoo, chose Bishop Daniel Garn as their presiding Elder, and organized to have preaching done in their own language.

December 18, 1843 (Monday)

John Ellioth, a schoolmaster, was arrested and brought to Nauvoo, where he was tried and found guilty of having kidnapped Daniel Avery and son.

December 19, 1843 (Tuesday)

The Nauvoo Legion paraded near the Temple, was inspected by the officers and instructed to prepare for meeting the mob, which was gathering in the neighborhood.

December 21, 1843 (Thursday)

The city council of Nauvoo signed a petition to Congress, praying for redress for the Missouri persecutions.

December 22, 1843 (Friday)

David Holman's house, near Ramus, Hancock Co., Ill, was burned by the mob.

December 25, 1843 (Monday)

O. Porter Rockwell arrived in Nauvoo from nearly a year's imprisonment in Missouri without conviction, during which time he was subjected to very cruel treatment.

December 25, 1843 (Monday)

Daniel Avery was liberated from his imprisonment in Missouri, his son having previously escaped.

December 29, 1843 (Friday)

Forty policemen were sworn into office in the city of Nauvoo.
