Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The prosecutions under the Edmunds anti-polygamy law were carried on with great hostility by the Federal officials and courts in Utah and Idaho. A large number of polygamists were imprisoned and many others went into exile, some going into Mexico.

January 1885

The Saints who had settled at different points on the Fremont river (Dirty Devil), east of Rabbit Valley, were organized as Blue Valley Ward; Henry Giles, Bishop.

January 1885

Peter Olsen, missionary in Norway, was imprisoned five days on bread and water, for preaching the gospel.

January 6, 1885 (Tuesday)

After several days' examination, the Seventh School District lawsuit (Salt Lake City) was submitted to Judge Charles S. Zane, who two days later gave his decision in favor of the district trustees.

January 8, 1885 (Thursday)

Alexander F. Macdonald, Christopher Layton and John W. Campbell arrived at Corralitos, Chihuahua, Mexico, to rent or buy land, on which to locate such families of Saints as were being driven into exile, because of their family relations in the United States.

January 10, 1885 (Saturday)

Bingham County, Idaho, was created out of a small part of Oneida County, by an act of the Idaho legislature.

January 12, 1885 (Monday)

Addison Everett, a prominent Elder of the Church and a Pioneer of 1847, died at St. George.

January 12, 1885 (Monday)

Mary Ann Fielding, widow of Joseph Fielding, died in Salt Lake City.

January 12, 1885 (Monday)

Elders Ferdinand F. Hintze and Anton Lauritzen were mobbed at Ribe, Denmark.

January 13, 1885 (Tuesday)

George S. Grant, son of the late Pres. Jedediah M. Grant, was accidentally shot and killed in Oakley, Cassia Co., Idaho.

January 16, 1885 (Friday)

The Morgan smelter, situated a few miles south of Salt Lake City, was destroyed by fire.

January 18, 1885 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the lower Muddy, Lincoln Co., Nev., were organized as Overton Ward; Isaiah Cox, Bishop.

January 19, 1885 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court confirmed the action of the Utah courts in refusing to admit Rudger Clawson to bail.

January 20, 1885 (Tuesday)

Pres. Angus M. Cannon was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of u.c.

January 20, 1885 (Tuesday)

Alexander F. McDonald and companions returned to Corralitos, Chihuahua, Mexico, from an exploring tour to some of the valleys lying on the east slope of the Sierra Madre Mountains, and found several families of exiles who had arrived from the United States.

January 23, 1885 (Friday)

Rudger Clawson's sentence for polygamy was confirmed by the Supreme Court of Utah. The case was appealed to the United States Supreme Court.

January 23, 1885 (Friday)

Jacob S. Boreman qualified as associate justice, in place of Stephen P. Twiss, resigned.

January 24, 1885 (Saturday)

After several days' examination before Commissioner McKay, Pres. Angus M. Cannon was placed under $1,500 bonds.

January 24, 1885 (Saturday)

Daniel H. Wells succeeded Apostle John H. Smith as president of the European mission, the latter sailing for America.

January 28, 1885 (Wednesday)

Royal B. Young, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of polygamy and u.c.

January 29, 1885 (Thursday)

Royal B. Young was placed under $2,000 bonds, after his preliminary examination before Com. McKay.

January 30, 1885 (Friday)

Agnes McMurrin, Royal B. Young's alleged plural wife, was on trial in the Third District Court, on a charge of perjury.

January 31, 1885 (Saturday)

Jacob S. Boreman was appointed judge of the Second Judicial District of Utah.

February 1885

A difficulty between the land owners in Utah County and the several canal companies of Salt Lake County, caused by the overflow of Utah lake, was settled by arbitration.

February 1, 1885 (Sunday)

Pres. John Taylor delivered his last public discourse in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City. In the evening, he and Pres. Geo. Q. Cannon secreted themselves, in order to avoid the Federal officials, who were carrying on their high handed judicial proceedings in the Territory. Elder L. John Nuttall accompanied them as private secretary, Charles H. Wilcken as driver, and Charles H. Barrell as general aid.

February 3, 1885 (Tuesday)

A law passed by the Idaho legislature, prohibiting all "Mormons" from voting, was approved by Gov. Bunn.

February 8, 1885 (Sunday)

Col. Heber P. Kimball died in Salt Lake City.

February 8, 1885 (Sunday)

Elders Matts S. Mattson and H. Persson were mobbed in Aabyholm, Sweden.

February 9, 1885 (Monday)

Ogden experienced a hot political contest at the election, but the Peoples' Party succeeded in getting a majority of votes for their candidates.

February 9, 1885 (Monday)

The Trenton branch, Cache Co., was organized as a Ward; James B. Jardine, Bishop.

February 9, 1885 (Monday)

A number of Saints going into exile because of their family relations, left Snowflake, Ariz., for Mexico. On their arrival at Luna Valley, New Mexico, on the 15th, they were organized into a traveling company, with E.A. Noble as captain. The company had increased to about seventy souls.

February 10, 1885 (Tuesday)

Thomas Bullock, one of the Pioneers of 1847, and formerly Pres. Brigham Young's secretary, died at Coalville, Summit Co.

February 11, 1885 (Wednesday)

Pres. Angus M. Cannon was arraigned before the Third District Court and allowed until the following Friday to plead. He then plead not guilty.

February 12, 1885 (Thursday)

Elder William Willes returned to Salt Lake City, from his mission to India.

February 12, 1885 (Thursday)

Elders Niels W. Petersen and Mads P. Madsen were mobbed, while holding a meeting in Kallundborg, Denmark.

February 13, 1885 (Friday)

Alta, Little Cottonwood Canyon, was almost entirely destroyed by a snowslide, and about fifteen persons were killed.

February 17, 1885 (Tuesday)

Dr. John D.M. Crockwell died in Salt Lake City.

February 20, 1885 (Friday)

Ferdinand F. Hintze's case was called in the Third District Court, and the prosecutors discovered that the absent defendant was not under bonds.

February 25, 1885 (Wednesday)

Elder Francis M. Lyman, jun., who was arrested the day previous, was arraigned before the court at Weinhem, Germany, accused of holding a meeting and preaching "Mormonism," and sentenced to one day's imprisonment, after suffering which he was banished from the country.

March 1885

The impossibility of securing a fair trial in the Utah Federal courts caused a number of leading men to voluntarily go into exile.

March 1885

Wm. Fotheringham, of Beaver, was indicted by the grand jury, arrested and placed under bonds, being charged with u.c.

March 1, 1885 (Sunday)

Apostle Moses Thatcher and other prominent men arrived at the camp of the Saints on the Casas Grandes river, Chihuahua, Mexico. Soon afterwards explorations were made in the surrounding country.

March 2, 1885 (Monday)

Parley P. Pratt, son of the late Apostle Parley P. Pratt, was arrested in Salt Lake City, charged with u.c.

March 2, 1885 (Monday)

The free mail delivery system was introduced in Salt Lake City.

March 2, 1885 (Monday)

The first number of the Salt Lake Evening Democrat, a daily anti-Mormon newspaper, was published in Salt Lake City, by the Salt Lake Democrat Company.

March 2, 1885 (Monday)

Elders Wm. F. Garner and Christian F. Christensen had a narrow escape from being lynched by a mob in Mitchell County, North Carolina, where they labored as missionaries.

March 4, 1885 (Wednesday)

Ole L. Hansen, of Brighton, Salt Lake Co., charged with u.c., was arraigned before the Third District Court and plead not guilty.

March 6, 1885 (Friday)

Wm. H. Pitts, of the firm of Godbe, Pitts & Co., died in Salt Lake City.

March 7, 1885 (Saturday)

Captain Noble's company of Arizona exiles, arrived at a point on the Casas Grandes river, near Ascencion, Chihuahua, Mexico, where they formed a temporary encampment.

March 8, 1885 (Sunday)

Elders Charles W. Penrose, Lorenzo Waldram, jun., and Wm. W. Burton arrived at Liverpool, England, as missionaries from Utah.

March 8, 1885 (Sunday)

Bishop John Hunt's wife was burned to death at Snowflake, Ariz.

March 8, 1885 (Sunday)

The first Latter-day Saint Sunday School in Mexico was commenced at Corralitos, Chihuahua; James Gale, supt.

March 11, 1885 (Wednesday)

The Church blacksmith shop at the mouth of Little Cottonwood Canyon, was burned.

March 12, 1885 (Thursday)

The jury returned a verdict of guilty against Thos. Simpson for polygamy.

March 13, 1885 (Friday)

The Gardo House, Salt Lake City, was searched by U.S. deputy marshals, who subpoenaed a number of witnesses.

March 14, 1885 (Saturday)

In the Third District Court, Thos. Simpson was sentenced to two years' imprisonment for polygamy and taken to the Penitentiary.

March 14, 1885 (Saturday)

The case against Laban Morrill, of Circle Valley, Utah, for u.c., was dismissed in the Second District Court, at Beaver.

March 16, 1885 (Monday)

Thomas Holland was drowned at Baker's Spring, near Utah Lake.

March 17, 1885 (Tuesday)

John Nicholson, associate editor of the Deseret News, was arrested, charged with u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds, to answer before the grand jury.

March 19, 1885 (Thursday)

U.S. deputy marshals raided the houses of Geo. Q. Cannon, Geo. Dunford and Mrs. J.C. Little, in an unsuccessful search for witnesses in polygamy cases.

March 22, 1885 (Sunday)

The U.S. Supreme Court rendered a decision annulling the test oath formulated by the Utah Commission. By this ruling a number of persons were restored to the elective franchise.

March 24, 1885 (Tuesday)

Parley P. Pratt was arrested in Salt Lake City on a charge of polygamy and u.c.

March 24, 1885 (Tuesday)

Joseph Pidcock, of Ogden, was found dead near Montpelier, Bear Lake Co, Idaho.

March 25, 1885 (Wednesday)

A grand musical concert, under the direction of Prof. Geo. Careless, was given in the Salt Lake Theatre.

March 27, 1885 (Friday)

Eli B. Kelsey died in Salt Lake City.

March 28, 1885 (Saturday)

Elder Abraham Coon died in Salt Lake City.

March 30, 1885 (Monday)

Orson P. Arnold, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 1885

A number of flowing artesian wells were made in Salt Lake City and vicinity.

April 1885

A. Milton Musser was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,000 bonds.

April 2, 1885 (Thursday)

The Utah Commission made a verbal report to President Cleveland at Washington, D.C., on their work in Utah.

April 2, 1885 (Thursday)

Jacob S. Boreman was appointed judge of the First Judicial District of Utah, the second time.

April 4, 1885 (Saturday)

John Pack, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

April 4, 1885 (Saturday)

Wm. W. Roundy was appointed to preside in the camp of the Saints, located north of the town of Casas Grandes, Chihuahua, Mexico.

April 4, 1885 (Saturday)

The 55th annual conference of the Church convened in Logan, Utah, Franklin D. Richards presiding. It was continued three days. On the second day (April 5th), an epistle from the First Presidency was read, and a committee was appointed to draft a petition to the President of the United States, praying for protection against the tyrannical acts of the Federal officials in Utah.

April 7, 1885 (Tuesday)

Charles I. Robson, counselor to the president of the Maricopa Stake, and Bishop Oscar M. Stewart, of Alma, were each sentenced to ninety days' imprisonment, at Yuma, Ariz., for u.c.

April 9, 1885 (Thursday)

The Saints encamped on the Casas Grandes river, Mexico, were ordered to leave the State of Chihuahua, in fifteen days.

April 9, 1885 (Thursday)

The Tennessee legislature passed a law, forbidding the teaching of polygamy in that State.

April 10, 1885 (Friday)

In the District Court at Phoenix, Ariz., the jury returned a verdict of guilty against A.P. Spilsbury, indicted for u.c.

April 11, 1885 (Saturday)

At Phoenix, Ariz., A.P. Spilsbury and Geo. T. Wilson were each sentenced to six month's imprisonment, and Chas. I. Robson, Hyrum S. Phelps, Oscar M. Stewart and James Wilson to three months' imprisonment each for u.c. The following day (April 12th), they were taken to Yuma prison.

April 11, 1885 (Saturday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool with 187 "Mormon" passengers, including 19 returning missionaries, in charge of Louis P. Lund; it arrived in New York April 22nd, and the company reached Salt Lake City, on the 28th.

April 13, 1885 (Monday)

Orson P. Arnold, of Salt Lake City, plead guilty to the charge of unlawful cohabitation, in the Third District Court, and, promising to obey the law in the future, was discharged on paying $300 fine.

April 13, 1885 (Monday)

Elders Wm. F. Garner, of North Ogden, and Christian F. Christensen, of Kanosh, were arrested in Carter County, Tenn., accused of preaching polygamy.

April 14, 1885 (Tuesday)

James Thompson, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c.

April 14, 1885 (Tuesday)

Elders Wm. F. Garner and Christian F. Christensen were imprisoned at Elizabethtown, Tenn.

April 15, 1885 (Wednesday)

Edward Brain, of the 20th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $2,000 bonds.

April 15, 1885 (Wednesday)

Apostle George Teasdale and other Elders arrived at the City of Chihuahua, Mexico, to plead the cause of the Saints encamped on the Casas Grandes river. The next day (16th) they had an interview with the governor, who referred the question of ejectment to the Federal government.

April 16, 1885 (Thursday)

Judge Zane rendered a decision declaring the Territorial liquor law valid and sustaining the action of the county court against certain liquor dealers.

April 17, 1885 (Friday)

Clara D. Young, wife of John W. Young, died in Salt Lake City.

April 17, 1885 (Friday)

Emil O. Olsen, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and placed under $1,000 bonds.

April 19, 1885 (Sunday)

Pres. Wm. D. Hendricks, of the Oneida Stake, Idaho, was arrested in Logan, Utah, on a charge of u.c.

April 20, 1885 (Monday)

Wm. A. Rossiter, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and placed under $1,000 bonds.

April 20, 1885 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court sustained the decision of the Utah courts, in Rudger Clawson's polygamy case, but decided in favor of giving the murderer Fred Hopt a fourth trial.

April 21, 1885 (Tuesday)

David E. Davis, of Clover, Tooele Co., was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 22, 1885 (Wednesday)

Samuel H.B. Smith, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 24, 1885 (Friday)

U.S. deputy marshals searched the Temple Block for the purpose of making arrests, but found no one they wanted.

April 24, 1885 (Friday)

Bishop Hiram B. Clawson, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 24, 1885 (Friday)

The first number of the Home Sentinel, a weekly newspaper, was published by Jakeman & Harrington, at Manti, Sanpete Co.

April 27, 1885 (Monday)

The trial of Pres. Angus M. Cannon was commenced in the Third District Court, Salt Lake City.

April 27, 1885 (Tuesday)

Abraham H. Cannon, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 27, 1885 (Tuesday)

The jury returned a verdict of guilty against Pres. Angus M. Cannon, for u.c.

April 27, 1885 (Tuesday)

Bishop James C. Hamilton, of Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and polygamy, brought to Salt Lake City, and placed under $1,500 bonds.

April 30, 1885 (Thursday)

The trial of A. Milton Musser was commenced in the Third District Court.

April 30, 1885 (Thursday)

John Aird, who plead guilty to the charge of u.c., promised to obey the law and was sentenced to $300 fine; in default of payment he was sent to the Penitentiary.

May 1885

The 84th quorum of Seventy was organized by Seymour B. Young in the Bannock Stake, Idaho; Swen Jacobs, sen., Walter Paul, Arvis C. Dille, Joseph H. Brown, L.E. Shurtliff, Walter G. Paul and Wm. H. Walker, presidents.

May 1885

Marcus L. Shepherd and David Levi were arrested at Beaver, Utah, on a charge of u.c..

May 1885

Apostles Brigham Young and Moses Thatcher visited the City of Mexico, and obtained permission from the Federal government for the Saints to remain in Chihuahua.

May 1885

Elder Niels Hansen, who labored as a missionary in Frederikshavn, Denmark, was ordered out of the country.

May 1, 1885 (Friday)

Claudius V. Spencer, who had been indicted for u.c., plead guilty in the Third District Court, and, promising to live within the law, Judge Zane suspended sentence.

May 2, 1885 (Saturday)

A grand mass meeting was held in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, to protest against the oppressive course of the Federal officials in the Territory. A declaration of grievances and protest, addressed to the President and people of the United States, were adopted, and John T. Caine, John W. Taylor and John Q. Cannon were chosen as a delegation to proceed to Washington with the documents. Similar mass meetings were held in the various cities and towns of the Territory.

May 2, 1885 (Saturday)

The jury in the Third District Court returned a verdict of guilty against A. Milton Musser, James C. Watson and Parley P. Pratt. The latter, who had been indicted for u.c., plead guilty to the charge and was sentenced to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine, and taken to the Penitentiary.

May 4, 1885 (Monday)

O.L. Hansen, of Brighton, indicted for u.c., was acquitted in the Third District Court.

May 8, 1885 (Friday)

Wm. D. Newsom, of the 11th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of polygamy, and the preliminary examination commenced before Com. McKay. Lucy Devereau, defendants' plural wife, one of the witnesses in the case, was taken to the Penitentiary for refusing to testify.

May 9, 1885 (Saturday)

Pres. Angus M. Cannon, A. Milton Musser and James C. Watson were each sentenced to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine for u.c., and taken to the Penitentiary.

May 9, 1885 (Saturday)

Samuel Humphreys was arrested at Nounnan Valley and Chas. Simpson at another place in Bear Lake Co., Idaho, on a charge of polygamy. They were both taken to Blackfoot for examination.

May 9, 1885 (Saturday)

Wm. D. Newsom was admitted to $3,000 bail, and Lucy Devereau released from custody, being placed under $200 bonds.

May 10, 1885 (Sunday)

Eight armed deputy marshals arrested Wm. D. Pratt, of Wilford, and John L. Roberts, of Rexburg, Bingham Co., Idaho, in the night, on a charge of u.c., or polygamy, and started for Blackfoot the following day. Both plead guilty and were sentenced to imprisonment May 23rd following.

May 11, 1885 (Monday)

U.S. Marshal Fred. T. Dubois, of Idaho, and five assistants, armed to the teeth, visited Paris, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, in search of polygamists.

May 13, 1885 (Wednesday)

The Utah delegation (Caine, Cannon and Taylor) had an interview with President Cleveland, at Washington, D.C.

May 13, 1885 (Wednesday)

Isaac Groo, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds, after pleading guilty to the charge before Com. McKay.

May 13, 1885 (Wednesday)

J.D. Jones, of Idaho, was sentenced to $300 fine for u.c., and promised to obey the law in the future.

May 14, 1885 (Thursday)

After several days' trial, the jury in the Third District Court returned a verdict of not guilty in the case against Officer Thomas F. Thomas, who had been accused of assaulting the negro, who killed Capt. Andrew Burt, Aug. 25, 1883.

May 14, 1885 (Thursday)

James Taylor, of Ogden, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500.

May 15, 1885 (Friday)

Moroni Brown and Francis A. Brown, of Ogden, were arrested on the charge of u.c., and each placed under $1,500 bonds.

May 15, 1885 (Friday)

Wm. Fotheringham was adjudged guilty of u.c. by the jury in the Second District Court, Beaver, after a lengthy trial, although no proof of his guilt had been produced, except for "holding out."

May 15, 1885 (Friday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, with 174 Saints, including 15 returning missionaries, under the direction of N.M. Hodges. On the 27th it arrived in Salt Lake City, June 2nd.

May 19, 1885 (Tuesday)

Lucy Devereau was again sent to the Penitentiary for refusing to answer certain questions before the grand jury in the third District Court.

May 19, 1885 (Tuesday)

Joseph M. Phelps, of Montpelier, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of u.c.

May 20, 1885 (Wednesday)

In the Second District Court (Judge Boreman), Wm. Fotheringham was sentenced to three months' imprisonment and $300 fine, and taken to the Penitentiary.

May 21, 1885 (Thursday)

Aurelius Miner, of Salt Lake City was arrested on a charge of u.c.

May 21, 1885 (Thursday)

Hiram B. Clawson, Bishop of the 12th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,500 bonds.

May 21, 1885 (Thursday)

David Lee, who plead guilty to the charge of u.c., was discharged on paying a fine of $300.

May 21, 1885 (Thursday)

Elder John P. Ibsen, while preaching the gospel in a private house on Bornholm, Denmark, was arrested and brought to Ronne, where he was tried and imprisoned three days for preaching. Soon afterwards he was sent as a prisoner to Copenhagen.

May 22, 1885 (Friday)

The grand jury having found an indictment against Isaac Groo, of Salt Lake City, for u.c., he was re-arrested and placed under $1,500 bonds, to await trial.

May 23, 1885 (Saturday)

In the District Court at Blackfoot, Idaho, Judge Morgan sentenced Bishop George Stuart, of Malad, Wm. J. Pratt, of Wilford, and John T. Roberts, of Rexburg, each to four months' imprisonment in the Boise Penitentiary and $300 fine; John Winn, an old man, of Battle Creek, Oneida Co., and Charles W. Simpson, of Montpelier, each to a fine of $300, and Samuel Humphreys to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine.

May 23, 1885 (Saturday)

Elder August Valentine, who labored as a missionary on Bornholm, Denmark, was arrested for preaching the gospel. He was brought to Copenhagen, and there imprisoned for five days, after which he was banished from the country.

May 24, 1885 (Sunday)

Joseph S. Staker was ordained the first Bishop of Annabella Ward, Sevier Co., Utah.

May 25, 1885 (Monday)

Apostle Franklin D. Richards returned from a trip to the East, during which he visited Pueblo, Independence, Richmond (Mo.) Carthage, Nauvoo (Ill,) and other places known in Church history.

May 25, 1885 (Monday)

Peter Nebeker died at Willard, Box Elder Co., Utah.

May 25, 1885 (Monday)

Elders Wiley G. Cragun and Franklin A. Fraughton were mobbed in South Carolina; Fraughton received forty lashes with a whip and Cragun was shot in the chin.

May 27, 1885 (Wednesday)

Charles Seal, of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and placed under $1,000 bonds.

May 27, 1885 (Wednesday)

The case of James Taylor, of Ogden, was dismissed for the time being, because of an error in the indictment.

May 28, 1885 (Thursday)

After a preliminary examination before Com. McKay, Charles Seal was arraigned on two charges of polygamy and bound over in $3,000 bonds, to await the action of the grand jury.

May 28, 1885 (Thursday)

Alfred Best, of Mill Creek, was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of u.c. and placed under $1,000 bonds.

May 28, 1885 (Thursday)

Elder Aug. Valentine left Copenhagen for England, being the first Elder banished from Denmark for preaching the gospel.

May 31, 1885 (Sunday)

Elder John P. Ibsen was imprisoned in Copenhagen, Denmark, awaiting his banishment from the country.

June 1885

Diphtheria was raging in Salt Lake City and vicinity.

June 1885

Edmund Ellsworth, of Arizona, was sentenced to $300 fine, for u.c., and, not being able to pay it, was imprisoned at Yuma.

June 1885

Wm. J. Flake, having served his sentence in the Yuma prison, Ariz., was released.

June 1885

Elders Ferdinand F. Hintze, Christian N. Lundsten, Jens Nielsen and Neils Hansen, missionaries from Utah, were banished from Jutland, Denmark, for preaching the gospel.

June 2, 1885 (Tuesday)

James H. Nelson was arrested in Ogden, on a charge of u.c. Deputy Marshals Perkins and Brown, who attempted to enter Nelson's house, without a search warrant, received rough treatment from Mrs. Nelson.

June 4, 1885 (Thursday)

The first number of Svenska Harolden (a weekly), the first Swedish newspaper in Utah, was issued in Salt Lake City, by the Swedish Publishing Company, recently organized.

June 4, 1885 (Thursday)

The grand jury having found an indictment against Alfred Best, of Mill Creek, he was re-arrested and placed under $1,000 bonds.

June 4, 1885 (Thursday)

Elder John P. Ibsen was brought on board the steamer Milo, at Copenhagen, Denmark, by the police-officers, having been banished from the country for preaching the gospel.

June 6, 1885 (Saturday)

Wm. Wilding died in the 17th Ward, Salt Lake City, 102 years of age.

June 6, 1885 (Saturday)

Bishop Dennison L. Harris, died at Monroe, Sevier Co.

June 9, 1885 (Tuesday)

N.P. Jeppesen, of Logan, and two others, were drowned in Salmon river, Idaho, by being carried over the falls.

June 10, 1885 (Wednesday)

The 85th quorum of Seventy was partly organized by Jacob Gates and Edward Stevenson at Kanab, Kane Co., Utah; Reuben Broadbent, Chas. S. Cram, William J. Jolly, Svend M. Anderson and William H. Clayton, presidents.

June 11, 1885 (Thursday)

The motion for new trials in the cases of Pres. Angus M. Cannon and A. Milton Musser was argued in the Supreme Court of Utah.

June 12, 1885 (Friday)

Brett's Circus performed in Salt Lake City.

June 12, 1885 (Friday)

Isaac B. Nash, of Franklin, Oneida Co., Idaho, was arrested on a charge of u.c., and, after a preliminary examination before Commissioner House, at Oxford, placed under $1,500 bonds.

June 13, 1885 (Saturday)

The Supreme Court of Utah affirmed the decision of the court below, against Thomas Simpson for polygamy.

June 13, 1885 (Saturday)

Andrew W. Cooley, of Brighton, Salt Lake Co., who had been indicted for u.c., gave himself up to the marshal and was put under $1,000 bonds.

June 13, 1885 (Saturday)

The 86th quorum of Seventy was partly organized by Jacob Gates and Edward Stevenson, at Panguitch, Garfield Co.; John W. Norton, Albert W. Norton, Albert H. Riding and S.A. Johnson, presidents.

June 18, 1885 (Thursday)

Policeman Andrew Smith, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. Elizabeth Ann Starkey, one of the witnesses, was fined $50 and sentenced to one day's imprisonment for refusing to answer certain questions before the Commissioner.

June 18, 1885 (Thursday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, with 541 Saints, including 30 returning missionaries, under the direction of Jorgen Hansen. They arrived at New York July 1st, and at Salt Lake City July 7th.

June 21, 1885 (Sunday)

In the Parowan Stake quarterly conference, the two Parowan Wards were united into one Ward, with Charles Adams as Bishop.

June 21, 1885 (Sunday)

The 87th quorum of Seventy was organized by Abraham H. Cannon, at Plain City, Weber Co.; Wm. Geddes, J.P. Folkmann, Alonzo Knight, Charles Featherstone, William S. Geddes and Hans Poulsen, presidents.

June 22, 1885 (Monday)

The examination of Charles L. White's case was continued before Commissioner McKay; the defendant was placed under $2,000 bonds, and Miss Starkey, who still refused to answer, taken back to the Penitentiary.

June 22, 1885 (Monday)

Benjamin F. Steward, presiding Elder at Benjamin, Utah Co., was killed by lightning, while sitting in his carriage near his residence.

June 23, 1885 (Tuesday)

Under the management of the Old Folks Committee, the aged people of Salt Lake County had an excursion to Garfield, on the southern shore of the Great Salt Lake.

June 24, 1885 (Wednesday)

Samuel Ensign, an eighty year old veteran, fell from the Temple walls, in Salt Lake City, and was instantly killed.

June 24, 1885 (Wednesday)

The polygamy case against Charles L. White was dismissed, and he was held under $500 bonds to answer to the charge of u.c. Miss Starkey was brought before the grand jury and Judge Zane, but as she still refused to answer certain questions, she was taken back to the Penitentiary.

June 25, 1885 (Thursday)

Frederik H. Hansen, of Pleasant Green, Salt Lake Co., was arrested on a charge of u.c. and placed under $500 bonds.

June 25, 1885 (Thursday)

Septimus W. Sears, of Salt Lake City, was arrested in Chicago, Ill., on a charge of u.c. He was released on $3,000 bonds.

June 27, 1885 (Saturday)

John Nicholson, Andrew Smith, Geo. Romney and John Connelly, all of Salt Lake City, were arrested, charged with u.c. They each gave bonds in $1,500, to appear for trial in September.

June 27, 1885 (Saturday)

The Supreme Court of Utah affirmed the decision of the Third District Court against Pres. Angus M. Cannon.

June 28, 1885 (Sunday)

Wm. W. Drummond, once associate justice of Utah, was sentenced to the House of Correction for stealing postage stamps, in Chicago, Ill.

June 29, 1885 (Monday)

Joseph W. McMurrin, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and gave bonds in $1,500, to await trial.

June 29, 1885 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Wm. D. Newsom, John Connelly, John Daynes, Geo. Romney and Andrew Smith plead not guilty to the charges against them, while John Nicholson refused to plead.

June 30, 1885 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Francis A. Brown, being on trial for u.c., read an able plea in his own defence.

July 1885

After three days' trial the jury in the Third District Court brought in a verdict of guilty against Brigham Y. Hampton for conspiracy.

December 30, 1885 (Wednesday)

In the Third District Court, Judge Zane sentenced Brigham Y. Hampton to one year's imprisonment in the Salt Lake County jail.

December 31, 1885 (Thursday)

After two days' trial in the First District Court, at Ogden, the jury returned a verdict of guilty against Lorenzo Snow for u.c. in 1885, notwithstanding the evidence introduced had proven him innocent.
