Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year new settlements were founded by the Saints on the Muddy, Arizona (now Nevada). A long and desperate war between the settlers in Sanpete and Sevier Valleys and the Indians under the chief Black Hawk was commenced. Many of the settlers were killed and wounded.

January 8, 1865 (Sunday)

The first Latter-day Saint settlers on the lower Muddy (now in Lincoln Co., Nev.) arrived there. Under the presidency of Thos. S. Smith they and other settlers, who followed, located St. Thomas.

January 18, 1865 (Wednesday)

Apostle Orson Pratt and Wm. W. Riter arrived as missionaries in Vienna, Austria, to open up the gospel door in that country.

January 20, 1865 (Friday)

The Utah legislature adjourned.

January 23, 1865 (Monday)

The legislature of the State of Deseret met in G.S.L. City, attended to some business and adjourned.

January 28, 1865 (Saturday)

The Church, through its agent, Francis A. Hammond, purchased the Laie plantation, consisting of 6,500 acres of land, on the island of Oahu, Sandwich Islands, for $14,000, of T. Dougherty. This place, which has since been a gathering place and mission headquarters for the natives of the Hawaiian Islands, is about thirty-two miles from Honolulu, the capital of the islands.

February 1865

A warehouse was erected at Call's Landing, on the Colorado river.

February 4, 1865 (Saturday)

A company for building a canal from the Jordan river, near the Point of the Mountain south, to G.S.L. City was partly organized.

February 13, 1865 (Monday)

The 69th quorum of Seventy was partly organized at Parowan, Iron Co., with Silas S. Smith, sen., Zachariah B. Decker, Abraham Smith, Joseph K. Parramore, Horace Thornton, Edward Dalton and Wm. E. McGregor as presidents. Most of the ordinations took place Feb. 22nd.

February 15, 1865 (Wednesday)

James Lindley, James Wiles, John Mullcaron and Robert Nicholson were killed by a snowslide, while asleep in their tent in City Creek Canyon.

February 16, 1865 (Thursday)

Luther William Glazier, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Provo, Utah Co.

February 22, 1865 (Wednesday)

Geo. Barzee and John Boice, jun., perished in the snow, near Franklin, Cache Valley.

March 1865

The people in the Utah Dixie settlements suffered much for the want of breadstuff, and several of the northern counties were infested with the measles.

March 4, 1865 (Saturday)

A grand celebration on the occasion of the re-inauguration of President Abraham Lincoln, was held in G.S.L. City.

March 13, 1865 (Monday)

Wm. Millard's house, in the 20th Ward, G.S.L. City, was destroyed by fire, and a little girl burned to death and buried in the ruins.

April 6, 1865 (Thursday)

The 35th annual conference of the Church convened in the Tabernacle, G.S.L. City, and continued until the 9th.

April 9, 1865 (Sunday)

John Lowry had a quarrel with the Indian chief Jake, In Manti, Sanpete Co., Utah, the Indians boasting of having killed stock belonging to the citizens.

April 10, 1865 (Monday)

A small party of men from Manti was fired upon by the Indians, near Twelve Mile Creek, Sanpete Co., and young Peter Ludvigsen killed and mutilated by the savages. The same evening Elijah B. Ward and James Anderson were killed and scalped by the Indians, in Salina Canyon, Sevier Co., who also drove away considerable stock.

April 10, 1865 (Monday)

A special conference, held in G.S.L. City, voted to erect a telegraph line through the settlements of Utah.

April 12, 1865 (Wednesday)

Col. Reddick N. Allred, with 84 men, who pursued the Indians, had a dangerous encounter with them in the mountains, about fifteen miles east of Salina, during which Jens Sorenson, of Ephraim, and William Kearns, of Gunnison, were killed. The company retreated to Salina.

April 12, 1865 (Wednesday)

The brig Mexicano sailed from Port Elizabeth, South Africa, with 47 Saints on board, under the presidency of Miner G. Atwood, bound for Utah. The company arrived in New York, June 18th.

April 15, 1865 (Saturday)

News having reached G.S.L. City of the assassination of President Abraham Lincoln, all business houses in the city were closed, and the whole municipality was placed in a state of mourning.

April 17, 1865 (Monday)

The dead bodies of Sorensen and Kearns were secured and brought to Salina.

April 29, 1865 (Saturday)

The ship Belle Wood sailed from Liverpool with 636 Saints on board, bound for Utah, under the direction of Wm. H. Shearman. The company landed at New York June 1st, and arrived at Wyoming, Neb., on the 15th.

May 1, 1865 (Monday)

About sixty missionaries, called at the late conference, were set apart at the Historian's Office, G.S.L. City.

May 3, 1865 (Wednesday)

Pres. Brigham Young, accompanied by five of the Twelve and other brethren, left G.S.L. City on a trip to Cache Valley, from which they returned on the 11th.

May 8, 1865 (Monday)

The packet ship B.S. Kimball sailed from Hamburg, Germany, with 557 Saints, under the direction of Anders. W. Winberg. The company landed in New York June 15th and arrived at Wyoming June 26th.

May 10, 1865 (Wednesday)

A company of 24 Saints, under the direction of Wm. Underwood, sailed from Liverpool on board the ship David Hoadley, bound for Utah.

May 18, 1865 (Thursday)

A company, consisting of nine missionaries, eleven women, eighteen children and six teamsters, left G.S.L. City, bound for the Hawaiian Islands. Geo. Nebeker was appointed captain; the company had ten wagons.

May 20, 1865 (Saturday)

Elder Jesse Yelton Cherry, of Centreville, Davis Co., Utah, died of small pox in Nottingham, England, where he labored as a missionary.

May 22, 1865 (Monday)

The missionaries bound for the East were organized, with Bishop Wm. B. Preston as captain.

May 25, 1865 (Thursday)

Jens Larsen, a sheep herder, was killed by Indians about four miles north of Fairview, Sanpete Co.

May 26, 1865 (Friday)

John Given, his wife and four children, were murdered and their bodies fearfully mangled by Indians, near Thistle Valley, about twelve miles north of Fairview, Sanpete Co.

May 28, 1865 (Sunday)

The Saints who were settling St. Joseph, on the Muddy (now in Nevada), were organized as a branch of the Church, with Warren, Foote as president.

May 29, 1865 (Monday)

David Hadlock Jones, a member of the Mormon Battalion, was killed by Indians, about three miles northwest of Fairview, Sanpete Co.

June 8, 1865 (Thursday)

Col. O.H. Irish, superintendent of Indian affairs, made a treaty with the principal Indian chiefs in Utah, at Spanish Fork Reservation farm, in the presence of Brigham Young and other leading men.

June 11, 1865 (Sunday)

Hon. Schuyler Colfax, speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives, and party, arrived in G.S.L. City. He remained until the 19th.

June 12, 1865 (Monday)

Schuyler Colfax and two of his companions, Lieut.-Gov. Bross, of Illinois, and Albert D. Richardson, war correspondent of the Tribune, addressed the citizens in front of the Salt Lake House.

June 13, 1865 (Tuesday)

Gov. James D. Doty died in G.S.L. City.

June 17, 1865 (Saturday)

The Saints who had settled on Chalk creek, above Coalville, Summit Co., Utah, were organized as a branch of the Church, named Upton; Joseph Huff, president.

June 18, 1865 (Sunday)

A petition was drafted to Andrew Johnson, President of the United States, asking for the appointment of Col. O.H. Irish to the governorship of Utah. It was signed by 250 leading citizens.

June 24, 1865 (Saturday)

Patriarch Isaac Morley died at Fairview, Sanpete Co.

June 29, 1865 (Thursday)

Lars Petersen, a benevolent young man, who had assisted some twenty Danish Saints to emigrate, was drowned in a small stream, called the Weeping Waters, near Wyoming, Neb.

July 1, 1865 (Saturday)

The 70th quorum of Seventy was organized in Davis Co., Utah, with Wm. H. Lee, L.S. Burnham, Samuel Bryson, sen., Andrew Dalrymple, A.D. Boynton, Henry Tingey and Israel Barlow, jun., as presidents. Nearly all the brethren who became members of the quorum resided in Bountiful and Centreville.

July 4, 1865 (Tuesday)

The national holiday was remembered in G.S.L. City, by a grand celebration.

July 4, 1865 (Tuesday)

Hon. J.M. Ashley, of Ohio, chairman of the Committee on Territories, in Congress, arrived in G.S.L. City, on a visit.

July 6, 1865 (Thursday)

Francis A. Hammond and George Nebeker arrived at the Laie plantation, Hawaiian Islands.

July 7, 1865 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young, several of the Twelve and others left G.S.L. City on a missionary trip to Sanpete County, from which they returned on the 19th, having traveled about three hundred miles and held eighteen meetings.

July 14, 1865 (Friday)

Robert Gillespie and Anthony Robinson were killed by Indians, near Salina, Sevier Co.

July 15, 1865 (Saturday)

Chas. Durkee, of Wisconsin, was appointed governor of Utah.

July 18, 1865 (Tuesday)

The militia under Warren S. Snow surprised a party of hostile Indians, killed twelve and routed the rest, in Grass Valley. The command then went east to Green river and suffered much by long marches and for want of supplies.

July 24, 1865 (Monday)

The people of southern Utah celebrated the day in the pines, 2 1/2 miles above Pine Valley, Washington Co.

July 24, 1865 (Monday)

Hon. J.M. Ashley addressed an audience in the Bowery, G.S.L. City, at the celebration of the Territorial anniversary.

July 26, 1865 (Wednesday)

The Indians attacked Glenwood, Sevier Co., Utah, wounded a man and drove off nearly all the stock belonging to the settlement.

July 31, 1865 (Monday)

A provost guard of soldiers, who for some time had been stationed in front of the Tabernacle, on the south side of South Temple Street, in G.S.L. City, was removed.

August 1865

Brigham Young, jun., succeeded Daniel H. Wells as president of the European mission.

August 1, 1865 (Tuesday)

Pres. Brigham Young and a party of brethren left G.S.L. City on a missionary trip to Cache Valley, returning on the 10th.

August 1, 1865 (Tuesday)

After seven months' unsuccessful labors, Apostle Orson Pratt and William W. Riter left Vienna, Austria, on their return to England. They arrived at Liverpool Aug. 6th.

August 3, 1865 (Thursday)

James Davis, one of the Mormon Battalion, died suddenly in G.S.L. City.

August 23, 1865 (Wednesday)

A book, entitled "Joseph Smith the Prophet," by Lucy Smith, the Prophet's mother, published by Orson Pratt and Samuel W. Richards, in England, was condemned for its inaccuracy, by the First Presidency and Twelve Apostles.

August 25, 1865 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young and others left G.S.L. City on a missionary trip to Tooele County, from which they returned on the 27th.

September 4, 1865 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young and others left G.S.L. City on a trip to southern Utah. After holding a number of meetings in the intervening settlements, the company arrived in St. George on the 15th, and on the 29th returned to G.S.L. City.

September 15, 1865 (Friday)

An agricultural fair was held in St. George, Utah.

September 18, 1865 (Monday)

Col. O.H. Irish made a treaty with the Piede Indians, at Pinto, Washington Co., Utah.

September 21, 1865 (Thursday)

General Warren S. Snow had an engagement with the Indians, near Fish lake, 80 miles east of Circleville. Seven Indians were killed, and Snow and two of his men wounded.

September 22, 1865 (Friday)

Captain Miner G, Atwood's company of immigrating Saints was attacked by Indians west of Fort Laramie. Several of the brethren were wounded and one woman (Mrs. Grundtvig) carried away by the savages.

September 30, 1865 (Saturday)

Charles Durkee, Utah's sixth governor, arrived in G.S.L. City. He took the oath of office Oct. 3rd.

October 1, 1865 (Sunday)

The 71st quorum of Seventy was organized at Nephi, Juab Co., with Edward Oakey, Samuel Claridge, Edwin Harley, Daniel Miller, John Kienke, Charles Sperry and Benjamin Riches as presidents.

October 7, 1865 (Saturday)

Pres. Daniel H. Wells, who had presided over the European mission, arrived in G.S.L. City.

October 8, 1865 (Sunday)

The semi-weekly Deseret News was first issued.

October 10, 1865 (Tuesday)

The surviving members of Zion's Camp had a feast in the Social Hall, G.S.L. City.

October 17, 1865 (Tuesday)

Morten Pedersen Kuhr and wife, Elizabeth Petersen, Wm. Thorpe, Soren N. Jespersen, Benj. J. Black and Wm. T. Hite were killed by Indians, under the chief Black Hawk, near Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

October 17, 1865 (Tuesday)

A company of Saints, bound for Utah, sailed from Melbourne, Australia, on board the barque Albert.

October 22, 1865 (Sunday)

Foster Gordon and wife were found murdered in Skull Valley. Four discharged soldiers were suspected of the crime.

November 1865

The first Hebrew marriage in G.S.L. City was celebrated.

November 1, 1865 (Wednesday)

Elder Geo. Simms, of G.S.L. City, returning home from a mission to England, was drowned in the Platte river.

November 8, 1865 (Wednesday)

Capt. Miner G. Atwood's company of immigrants, which had left Wyoming, July 31st, with 45 wagons and about four hundred souls, arrived at G.S.L. City.

November 9, 1865 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young issued a circular to the Bishops and presiding Elders in the Church, calling upon them to assist in the erection of a telegraph line through the settlements.

November 9, 1865 (Thursday)

Capt. Henson Walker's company of immigrating Saints, which had started from Wyoming, Aug. 12th, arrived at G.S.L. City.

November 23, 1865 (Thursday)

Faust's livery stables, on 2nd South Street, G.S.L. City, were destroyed by fire.

November 29, 1865 (Wednesday)

Capt. Wm. S.S. Willis' ox train of immigrating Saints, which started from Wyoming, Aug. 15th, arrived at G.S.L. City. The women and children had arrived previously with relief teams, sent out about three hundred and fifty miles to meet the immigrants.

December 11, 1865 (Monday)

The fifteenth session of the Utah legislature convened in the State House, G.S.L. City, and organized by appointing Geo. A. Smith president of the Council, and John Taylor speaker of the House.

December 14, 1865 (Thursday)

Hon. Wm. H. Hooper, Utah's delegate to Congress, left G.S.L. City for Washington, D.C.

December 18, 1865 (Monday)

A number of Piede Indians, made a break on Kanab, Kane Co., Utah, and stole some horses.

December 23, 1865 (Saturday)

Wm. Naylor, of West Jordan, Salt Lake Co., froze to death, while returning from Cottonwood mill.

December 24, 1865 (Sunday)

John Singleton, of American Fork, Utah Co., froze to death, near Lehi.

December 24, 1865 (Sunday)

The winter of 1865-66 was very cold and severe in Utah.
