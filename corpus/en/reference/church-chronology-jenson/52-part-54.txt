Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

In the forepart of this year there was great scarcity of provisions in Utah. Many domestic animals died from starvation. Beaver County, Utah, was settled by pioneers from Parowan. A general reformation took place throughout the Church, most of the Saints renewing their covenants by baptism. This reformation extended to the several missionary fields in different parts of the world. Many of the Saints from Europe suffered severely in crossing the plains and mountains with handcarts. The practice of paying tithing was generally introduced among the Saints in Europe.

January 5, 1856 (Saturday)

Box Elder, Cache, Greasewood, Humboldt, St. Mary's, Malad and Cedar Counties, Utah, were created by legislative acts, approved by Gov. Brigham Young.

January 12, 1856 (Saturday)

An act, passed by the Utah legislature, creating Shambip County, Utah, was approved.

January 18, 1856 (Friday)

The Utah legislature adjourned.

January 26, 1856 (Saturday)

At a mass meeting held in G.S.L. City, steps were taken for organizing the B.Y. Express Carrying Company, to carry a daily express from the Missouri river to California. In subsequent meeting shares were taken to stock a thousand miles of the road.

February 1856

Beaver County, recently created by legislative act, was settled by Simeon F. Howd and thirteen others from Parowan, who located Beaver City. The townsite was laid out April 17, 1856.

February 1856

The Indians stole many cattle and horses in Utah and Cedar Valleys. On Feb. 21st they killed two herdsmen west of Utah Lake, and on the 22nd a posse of ten men with legal writs called at an Indian camp in Cedar Valley to arrest the murderers. A fight ensued, in which one Indian and a squaw were killed and Geo. Carson, one of the posse, mortally wounded. He died on the 23rd. On that day (the 23rd) Gov. Brigham Young, by proclamation, ordered out part of the Utah militia to fight the Indians. This difficulty with the natives is known in history as the "Tintic War."

February 6, 1856 (Wednesday)

Elder Robert C. Petty, of Herriman, Utah, died on Grand river, Ind. Ter., where he labored as a missionary.

February 8, 1856 (Friday)

The Saints who were settling on Beaver creek, Beaver Co., Utah, were organized into a branch of the Church by Apostle Geo. A. Smith, with Simeon F. Howd as president.

February 12, 1856 (Tuesday)

The Seventies, now numbering 40 quorums, commenced a jubilee in G.S.L. City, which lasted five days. Their hall, which had undergone a thorough improvement, was again dedicated.

February 18, 1856 (Monday)

The ship Caravan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 454 Saints, under the direction of Daniel Tyler. The company arrived at New York March 27th.

February 23, 1856 (Saturday)

The first number of the Western Standard, a weekly paper published in the interest of the Church, was issued at San Francisco, Cal.; Geo. Q. Cannon, editor.

February 26, 1856 (Tuesday)

John Catlin and another man were killed, and Geo. Winn was mortally wounded, by Indians, near Kimball's creek, southwest of Utah lake. Capt. Peter Connover, with eighty men, soon afterwards crossed Utah lake on the ice and pursued the hostile tribe into Tintic Valley, where he recovered some of the stock stolen by the savages.

February 27, 1856 (Wednesday)

Elder Robert W. Wolcott, of G.S.L. city, died of small pox at Northampton, England, where he labored as a missionary.

March 3, 1856 (Monday)

Elder A. Milton Musser sailed from Calcutta, for London, England, where he arrived July 19, 1856, after being 138 days at sea. He came by way of the Cape of Good Hope. Capt. Winsor, of the Viking, gave Elder Musser a free first-class passage.

March 17, 1856 (Monday)

A convention met in G.S.L. City to prepare a State constitution and memorialize Congress for the admission of Utah into the Union as the State of Deseret. The constitution and memorial were adopted on the 27th, and Apostles Geo. A. Smith and John Taylor were elected delegates to present the same to Congress.

March 23, 1856 (Sunday)

The ship Enoch train sailed from Liverpool, England, with 534 Saints, under the direction of James Ferguson. It arrived at Boston May 1st. From that city the emigrants traveled by rail via New York to Iowa City, Iowa, whence the journey across the plains this year was commenced by wagons and handcarts. Daniel Spencer acted as general superintendent of emigration on the borders, assisted by Geo. D. Grant, Wm. H. Kimball, James H. Hart and others.

March 28, 1856 (Friday)

Elder Hector C. Haight, president of the Scandinavian mission, was arrested and a conference meeting broken up by the police, at Malmo, Sweden.

April 6, 1856 (Sunday)

On this and the following day the 26th annual conference of the Church was held in G.S.L. City. About two hundred Elders were called on foreign missions.

April 19, 1856 (Saturday)

The ship Samuel Curling sailed from Liverpool with 707 Saints, under the direction of Dan Jones; it arrived at Boston May 23rd. From that city the emigrants traveled by rail to Iowa City.

April 21, 1856 (Monday)

Jacob Whitmer, one of the Eight Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, died near Richmond, Ray Co., Mo.

April 22, 1856 (Tuesday)

A large company of missionaries, including Apostles Orson Pratt, Geo. A. Smith, Ezra T. Benson and Erastus Snow, Elder Abraham O. Smoot and many other prominent men, left G.S.L. City, on missions to the States and Europe. They arrived at St. Louis, Mo., June 12th.

May 2, 1856 (Friday)

Elder Robert Skelton, after appointing James Patrick Meik to preside over the Saints in India, sailed from Calcutta, homeward bound. He was the last of the American Elders to leave India, which was now abandoned for the time being as a missionary field.

May 4, 1856 (Sunday)

The ship Thornton sailed from Liverpool, England, with 764 Saints, under the direction of James G. Willie. It arrived at New York June 14th, and the emigrants, continuing the journey by rail, arrived at Iowa City, June 26th.

May 25, 1856 (Sunday)

The ship Horizon sailed from Liverpool with 856 Saints, under the direction of Edward Martin. The company arrived safely at Boston, and reached Iowa City by rail July 8th.

May 28, 1856 (Wednesday)

A small company of Australian Saints, under the direction of Augustus Farnham, sailed from Port Jackson, New South Wales, bound for Utah. The ship touched at Tahiti, Society Islands, June 22nd, Honolulu, Hawaii, July 16th, and arrived at San Pedro, Cal., Aug. 15th. From the latter place the emigrants traveled by teams to San Bernardino.

June 1, 1856 (Sunday)

Weber County, Utah, was divided into four Bishop's Wards, and Erastus Bingham appointed Bishop of the First, James G. Browning of the Second, Chauncey W. West of the Third and Thos. Dunn of the Fourth Ward.

June 1, 1856 (Sunday)

The ship Wellfleet sailed from Liverpool, England, with 146 Saints, under the direction of John Aubray. It arrived at Boston July 13th. The emigrants remained in the States until the following season.

July 5, 1856 (Saturday)

The ship Lucy Thompson sailed from Liverpool with fourteen Saints, under the direction of James Thompson. It arrived at New York Aug. 8th.

July 19, 1856 (Saturday)

Six families from Mississippi, under the direction of Benjamin Matthews, arrived at G.S.L. City, as the first immigrants of the season. They brought small pox with them into the Valley.

July 24, 1856 (Thursday)

Pioneer day was celebrated on the headwaters of Big Cottonwood creek, where a temporary bowery had been erected for the occasion.

August 1856

Apostle Orson Pratt succeeded Apostle Franklin D. Richards in the presidency of the European Mission.

August 18, 1856 (Monday)

The last of Capt. Philemon C. Merril's company of Saints arrived at G.S.L. City.

August 23, 1856 (Saturday)

Bishop Isaac Houston, of Alpine, Utah Co., died.

August 25, 1856 (Monday)

Col. Almon W. Babbitt's train loaded with government property and traveling west, was plundered by Cheyenne Indians, near Wood river, Neb. A. Nichols and two others were killed, and a Mrs. Wilson was carried away by the savages.

September 1856

Cache County was settled by Peter Maughan and others, who located what is now the town of Wellsville.

September 1856

Col. Almon W. Babbitt, Thos. Margetts and child, James Cowdy and wife and others were killed, and Mrs. Margetts carried away by Cheyenne Indians, east of Fort Laramie.

September 2, 1856 (Tuesday)

Capt. John A. Hunt's company of Saints, the last wagon train of the season, left Florence, Neb., for G.S.L. Valley, having commenced the journey from Iowa City a few months previous.

September 11, 1856 (Thursday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt, accompanied by other Elders, left G.S.L. City on a mission to the States, from which he never returned.

September 17, 1856 (Wednesday)

A Female Relief Society was organized in the 14th Ward, G.S.L. City, with Phoebe Woodruff as president.

September 20, 1856 (Saturday)

Elder Knud Peterson's wagon company of immigrants (mostly Scandinavians) arrived in G.S.L. City. This was called the second company of the season.

September 26, 1856 (Friday)

The first two companies of immigrating Saints, which crossed the plains with handcarts, arrived at G.S.L. City, in charge of Capt. Edmund Ellsworth and Daniel D. McArthur. They were met and welcomed by the First Presidency of the Church, a brass band, a company of lancers, and a large concourse of citizens. Capt. Ellsworth's company had left Iowa City June 9th, and McArthur's June 11th. When they started, both contained 497 souls, with 100 handcarts, 5 wagons, 24 oxen, 4 mules and 25 tents.

October 2, 1856 (Thursday)

Capt. John Banks' wagon company of immigrating Saints, and Capt. Edward Bunker's handcart company, which had left Iowa City June 23rd, arrived in G.S.L. City. The immigrants in the latter were mostly from Wales.

October 2, 1856 (Thursday)

The Deseret Agricultural and Manufacturing Society commenced its first exhibition in G.S.L. City, called the "Deseret State Fair."

October 3, 1856 (Friday)

W. M. F. Magraw, formerly mail contractor, wrote a defamatory letter to the President of the United States, about Utah affairs.

October 4, 1856 (Saturday)

Apostle Franklin D. Richards, Daniel Spencer, John Van Cott, Wm. C. Dunbar, John D.T. McAllister, Nathaniel H. Felt, and a number of other missionaries, arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence Sept. 3rd.

October 6, 1856 (Monday)

The general semi-annual conference of the Church was commenced in G.S.L. City. It continued three days; 177 Elders were called to go on missions.

October 7, 1856 (Tuesday)

The Twentieth Ward, G.S.L. City, was organized with John Sharp as Bishop.

October 7, 1856 (Tuesday)

Capt. Geo. D. Grant left G.S.L. City with a relief company to meet the immigration.

October 11, 1856 (Saturday)

Capt. Croft's company of emigrants from Texas and the Cherokee Nation arrived in G.S.L. City.

October 17, 1856 (Friday)

An ordinance was passed by the G.S.L. City council, organizing a Fire Department. Jesse C. Little was appointed chief engineer.

October 28, 1856 (Tuesday)

Capt. Edward Martin's handcart company, detained by the unusual early snow storms of the season, was met by Joseph A. Young, Daniel W. Jones and Abel Garr, at a point sixteen miles above the Platte bridge. Three days later the company arrived at Greasewood creek, where four wagons of the relief company, in charge of Geo. D. Grant, loaded with provisions and some clothing for the suffering emigrants were awaiting them.

November 9, 1856 (Sunday)

Capt. James G. Willie's handcart company arrived in G.S.L. City, after great sufferings from scarcity of provisions, cold and over-exertion in the mountains. It left Iowa City, Iowa, July 15th, with 120 handcarts and six wagons, numbering about five hundred souls, of whom 66 died on the journey. Captain Abraham O. Smoot's wagon train arrived the same day.

November 13, 1856 (Thursday)

Joseph A. Young and Abel Garr arrived in G.S.L. City with the news that the last companies of emigrants were perishing in the mountains. More teams and provisions were immediately forwarded to help them in.

November 18, 1856 (Tuesday)

The ship Columbia sailed from Liverpool with 223 Saints, under the direction of J. Williams. It arrived at New York Jan. 1, 1857.

November 20, 1856 (Thursday)

The ladies of Cedar City, Iron Co., organized a Female Benevolent Society, with Mrs. Lydia Hopkins as president.

November 22, 1856 (Saturday)

Heber Jeddie Grant was born in G.S.L. City.

November 30, 1856 (Sunday)

Edward Martin's handcart company arrived in G.S.L. City, after extreme suffering. Many of the emigrants had died in the mountains, and the handcarts had to be gradually abandoned as the relief teams from the Valley were met. When the company passed Florence, Neb., Aug. 25th, it consisted of 576 persons, 146 handcarts, 7 wagons, etc.

December 1, 1856 (Monday)

Jedediah M. Grant, second Counselor to Pres. Brigham Young, died in G.S.L. City.

December 2, 1856 (Tuesday)

About sixty mule and horse teams started from G.S.L. City to meet Capts. Hodgett's and Hunt's wagon companies.

December 5, 1856 (Friday)

David S. Laughlin, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Cedar Valley, Utah.

December 8, 1856 (Monday)

The Utah legislature (sixth annual session) convened at Fillmore and organized by electing Heber C. Kimball president of the Council, and Hosea Stout speaker of the House. It then adjourned to G.S.L. City.

December 10, 1856 (Wednesday)

The First Presidency issued their "Fourteenth General Epistle" to the Church.

December 10, 1856 (Wednesday)

On this and the following six days Capts. Wm. B. Hodgett's and John A. Hunt's companies of emigrants arrived in G.S.L. City, after much suffering, being helped in by the relief trains sent out from the Valley.

December 11, 1856 (Thursday)

Contractor Magraw failing to carry the mails through, Feramorz Little and Eph. K. Hanks left G.S.L. City with the mail, for the East.

December 18, 1856 (Thursday)

The Utah legislature convened in the Social Hall, G.S.L. City.

December 24, 1856 (Wednesday)

Pres. Brigham Young gave an entertainment in the "Lion House" to a large number of Elders, lately returned from foreign missions.
