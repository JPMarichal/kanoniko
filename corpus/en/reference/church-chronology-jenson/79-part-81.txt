Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Two Stakes of Zion were organized in Idaho. The Logan Temple was dedicated. Several new Quorums of Seventy were organized. The prosecutions under the Edmunds law were commenced.

January 1, 1884 (Tuesday)

A fire broke out in the Utah Central coal mines, at Pleasant Valley, Emery Co., whereby John McLean and his son were suffocated.

January 14, 1884 (Monday)

The 26th session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City, and organized by electing Wm. W. Cluff president of the council, and James Sharp speaker of the House.

January 25, 1884 (Friday)

Elders Wm. H. Crandall and John W. Galley were mobbed in Jasper County, Mississippi.

January 27, 1884 (Sunday)

The Brigham Young Academy in Provo was destroyed by fire.

January 27, 1884 (Sunday)

Plymouth Ward, Box Elder Co., Utah, was organized; Myron J. Richards, Bishop.

January 28, 1884 (Monday)

Senator Hoar introduced another anti-Mormon bill in the U.S. Senate, which was passed June 18th.

February 4, 1884 (Monday)

The Bannock Ward, Snake River Valley, Idaho, was organized as the Bannock Stake of Zion; Thos. E. Ricks, president.

February 6, 1884 (Wednesday)

Peter Carlson and son were killed in a snowslide, near Logan.

February 11, 1884 (Monday)

At the municipal election in Salt Lake City, James Sharp was elected mayor.

February 13, 1884 (Wednesday)

The members of the Wyoming legislature and a number of others from that Territory arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit. They remained two days, during which they visited the Utah legislature than in session; speeches were made by both bodies.

February 18, 1884 (Monday)

Elders Wm. H. Crandall and Thomas Davis were shot at by a mob in Jones County, Miss.

February 24, 1884 (Sunday)

Portions of the North Jordan and Brighton Wards, Salt Lake Stake, were organized as Granger Ward; Daniel McRae, Bishop.

February 25, 1884 (Monday)

Elder Henry C. Fowler died in Salt Lake City.

March 2, 1884 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled near Safford, Graham Co., Ariz, were organized as Layton branch; John Walker, presiding Elder.

March 7, 1884 (Friday)

Ten men and two women were killed in a snowslide, at the Emma mine, Little Cottonwood Canyon.

March 14, 1884 (Friday)

The Utah legislature adjourned.

March 17, 1884 (Monday)

Bishop Silas Richards died at Union, Salt Lake Co.

March 22, 1884 (Saturday)

Daniel Mathison died at Parowan, Iron Co.

March 30, 1884 (Sunday)

Elder Thomas Biesinger was arrested and imprisoned at Prague, Bohemia, for preaching the gospel.

April 1, 1884 (Tuesday)

The world-renowned Adelina Patti, assisted by an excellent company, sang in the Tabernacle, in Salt Lake City.

April 4, 1884 (Friday)

Ebenezer Hanks, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Graves' Village, Piute Co.

April 4, 1884 (Friday)

Patriarch John Rowberry died at Tooele, Tooele Co.

April 4, 1884 (Friday)

On this and the three following days the 54th annual conference of the Church was held in Salt Lake City.

April 9, 1884 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 319 Saints, including 17 returning missionaries, in charge of Christian D. Fjeldsted. It arrived in New York harbor April 19th, and the company reached Ogden, Utah, April 27th.

April 15, 1884 (Tuesday)

A large company of missionaries left Salt Lake City for the United States and Europe.

April 17, 1884 (Thursday)

Presidents John Taylor and Geo. Q. Cannon, accompanied by a number of others, left Salt Lake City, to visit the Iron Works in southern Utah.

April 20, 1884 (Sunday)

The 77th quorum of Seventy was organized by Wm. W. Taylor at Ogden, with John Crawford, Louis F. Monch, Fred. Foulger, Chas. C. Brown and Henry W. Gwilliams as presidents. Ludvig Ehrnstrom and Ephraim H. Nye were afterwards added to the council.

April 24, 1884 (Thursday)

Rudger Clawson was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of polygamy, and placed under $3,000 bonds.

April 27, 1884 (Sunday)

At the first quarterly conference of the Bannock Stake of Zion, held at Rexburg, Idaho, the Saints at Rexburg were organized as a Ward; Thos. E. Ricks, jun., Bishop.

April 27, 1884 (Sunday)

The 78th quorum of Seventy was organized by Wm. W. Taylor, at Oakley, Cassia Co., Idaho; Robert Wilson, John Alexander, Moroni F. Fairchilds, George S. Grant, John J. Millard, Thomas Taylor and Edward D. Hoagland, presidents. The two last named were ordained a day or two later.

April 27, 1884 (Sunday)

Pere Hyacinthe, renowned French orator, who was in Salt Lake City on a visit, attended the services in the Tabernacle.

April 27, 1884 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Eureka, Juab Co.; John Beck, president.

April 28, 1884 (Monday)

Christian D. Fjeldsted, of Logan, was ordained one of the First Seven Presidents of Seventies, to fill the vacancy caused by the death of John Van Cott.

April 29, 1884 (Tuesday)

A third trial of the murderer Fred. Hopt was commenced in the Third District Court, Salt Lake City. He was convicted May 5th, and sentenced on the 9th to be shot June 13th.

May 12, 1884 (Monday)

The 79th quorum of Seventy was organized by Abraham H. Cannon, in Bear Lake County, Idaho; Charles H. Bridges, John Bunney, Christian Hogansen, Carl F. Hellstrom, Herbert Horsley, Charles R. Clark and Brigham L. Tippetts, presidents.

May 13, 1884 (Tuesday)

David Gallifant, of Salt Lake City, was sentenced to five years' imprisonment for killing Richard Fowler on May 28, 1883.

May 13, 1884 (Tuesday)

Ole U.C. Monster, the first person baptized by Erastus Snow, in Denmark, died at Pettyville, Sanpete Co.

May 17, 1884 (Saturday)

The Logan Temple was dedicated, under the direction of Pres. John Taylor.

May 17, 1884 (Saturday)

The steamship Arizona sailed from Liverpool, England, with 287 Saints, including 13 returning missionaries, in charge of Ephraim H. Williams. On the 26th they arrived in New York, and reached Salt Lake City June 1st.

May 21, 1884 (Wednesday)

The first marriages in the Logan Temple were solemnized. E.Y. Taylor and Rida Colebrook, Frank Y. Taylor and Elizabeth Campbell, Matthias F. Cowley and Abbie Hyde were the contracting parties.

May 21, 1884 (Wednesday)

Ground was broken for a new building for the B.Y. Academy at Provo.

May 22, 1884 (Thursday)

Nellie White, who refused to answer certain questions in the Third District Court, was sent to the Penitentiary.

May 29, 1884 (Thursday)

Edward Wallace East, a prominent Elder, died at Pima, Arizona.

May 29, 1884 (Thursday)

Elders Wm. C.A. Smoot, jun., and James E. Jennings, who labored as missionaries in Bavaria, were expelled from that country.

May 30, 1884 (Friday)

In Christiania, Norway, Elders N.C. Skaugaard and Peter Olsen were sentenced to pay a fine of 40 "Kroner" and costs of suit, each, for performing the ordinance of baptism.

June 1884

A missionary field was opened in Ireland by Elders Robert Marshall and Geo. Wilson. At the end of the year 47 had been baptized.

June 1, 1884 (Sunday)

The Oneida Stake of Zion was organized by Apostle Moses Thatcher; Wm. D. Hendricks, president; Solomon H. Hale and Geo. C. Parkinson, counselors. The Wards and branches embraced in the new organization and formerly belonged to Cache and Box Elder Stakes.

June 5, 1884 (Thursday)

The Chesterfield branch Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized as a Ward; Parley P. Willey, Bishop.

June 5, 1884 (Thursday)

The Saints at Lyman, Snake River Valley, Idaho, were organized as a Ward; Sidney Weeks, Bishop.

June 5, 1884 (Thursday)

The Logan 6th and 7th Wards, Cache Co., were organized; Anthon L. Skanchey and Isaac Smith, Bishops.

June 8, 1884 (Sunday)

Elder Christian H. Steffensen was released from prison in Drammen, Norway, where he had been confined five days for administering the Sacrament.

June 9, 1884 (Monday)

The Saints at Wilford, Snake River Valley, Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church.

June 9, 1884 (Monday)

The building known as the "Cock Pit," at Preston, England, in which the first "Mormon" missionaries to England held meetings in 1847, tumbled down.

June 10, 1884 (Tuesday)

The Saints at Teton, Snake River Valley, Idaho, were organized as a Ward; John Donaldson, Bishop.

June 10, 1884 (Tuesday)

Elders Wm. Willes, Henry F. McCune, Milson R. Pratt and Geo. H. Booth, left Salt Lake City, on a mission to India.

June 11, 1884 (Wednesday)

The Saints at Parker, Snake River Valley, Idaho, were organized as a Ward; Wyman M. Parker, Bishop.

June 13, 1884 (Friday)

By a reprieve, issued by Acting-Governor Arthur L. Thomas, the execution of the murderer Fred Hopt (Welcome) was postponed.

June 14, 1884 (Saturday)

The steamship Arizona sailed from Liverpool, with 531 Saints, including 25 returning missionaries, in charge of Ephraim H. Nye. They arrived in New York June 23rd and at Ogden June 29th.

June 15, 1884 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held at Mesa, Maricopa Co., Ariz., Tempe branch was organized as a Ward, Samuel Openshaw Bishop; and Alma Ward was organized, with Oscar M. Stewart as Bishop.

June 17, 1884 (Tuesday)

Martin H. Peck died in Salt Lake City.

June 29, 1884 (Sunday)

Nicholas Groesbeck died in Salt Lake City.

July 3, 1884 (Thursday)

David O. Calder, Coun. in the Salt Lake Stake presidency, died at Lake Point, Tooele, Co.

July 6, 1884 (Sunday)

A fearful tornado visited Summit County, Utah, doing much damage and causing the death of a little girl.

July 7, 1884 (Monday)

Nellie White, who had been confined in the Penitentiary since May 22nd, was restored to liberty.

July 17, 1884 (Thursday)

The Deseret Hospital was removed from the 12th to the 17th Ward, Salt Lake City.

July 20, 1884 (Sunday)

The Fairview branch of the Church, near Franklin, Idaho., was organized as a Ward; Heman Hyde, Bishop.

July 22, 1884 (Tuesday)

About five hundred of the Old Folks of Salt Lake County had their annual excursion, going to American Fork, Utah Co.

July 31, 1884 (Thursday)

Leonard W. Hardy, first Counselor to the Presiding Bishop of the Church, died in Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co., and Orson K. Whitney, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

August 1, 1884 (Friday)

Wm. W. Taylor, son of Pres. John Taylor, and one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies, died in Salt Lake City.

August 1, 1884 (Friday)

Elders Wm. Willes, Henry F. McCune, Milson R. Pratt and Geo. H. Booth, arrived, as missionaries, in Calcutta, India.

August 2, 1884 (Saturday)

Fourteen Saints sailed from Liverpool, England, in charge of H.W. Attley, on the steamship Nevada. They landed in New York Aug. 13th, and arrived at Salt Lake City Aug. 18th.

August 7, 1884 (Thursday)

The 80th quorum of Seventy was organized by Seymour B. Young, at Spring City, Sanpete Co., Thos. B. Allred, Jos. F. Ellis, John Larsen, James Christensen, Mads Nielsen, Lauritz Rasmussen and Joseph Downard, presidents.

August 8, 1884 (Friday)

James Roskelley, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, was shot and wounded in the arm by a negro, in Lee Valley, Tenn.

August 9, 1884 (Saturday)

J.R. Henson and family, because of their being "Mormons," were fired upon by a mob in Decatur County, Tennessee, and shortly after compelled to leave their homes, to escape mob violence.

August 10, 1884 (Sunday)

Elders Wm. S. Berry, of Kanarra and John H. Biggs, of Paradise, and Martin Condor and John Riley Hudson, of Tennessee, were murdered by a mob on Cane Creek, Lewis Co., Tenn., while holding religious services.

August 15, 1884 (Friday)

The first number of the Sevier Valley Echo, a weekly newspaper, was published in Richfield, Sevier Co., by James T. Jakeman. It was continued until May 1, 1885, when it was superseded by the Home Sentinel, published in Manti, Sanpete Co.

August 15, 1884 (Friday)

Joseph H. Coult, of Salt Lake City, was drowned at Calder's Farm.

August 16, 1884 (Saturday)

Bishop Wm. H. Dame died suddenly at Paragoonah, Iron Co.

August 17, 1884 (Sunday)

At a quarterly conference held at Rexburg, Idaho, the Menan (Cedar Buttes) and Louisville Wards were partly organized, the former with Robert L. Bybee and the latter with Richard F. Jardine as Bishop.

August 17, 1884 (Sunday)

Elder Jesse J. Fuller, an elderly missionary, was whipped by a mob in Lauderdale County, Ala.

August 22, 1884 (Friday)

The remains of Elders Wm. S. Berry and Geo. H. Gibbs arrived in Salt Lake City from Tennessee.

August 23, 1884 (Saturday)

Under the direction of the Old Folks' Committee, the orphan children of Salt Lake County had a free excursion to Black Rock.

August 27, 1884 (Wednesday)

The St. John Ward (formerly a part of the Malad Ward), Oneida Co, Idaho, was organized; James Harrison, Bishop.

August 30, 1884 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 496 Saints, including 31 returning missionaries, under the direction of Benjamin Bennett. They arrived at New York Sept. 9th, and at Ogden Sept. 16th.

August 31, 1884 (Sunday)

The Saints residing on Ferron creek, east of Ferron, Emery Co., were separated from Ferron Ward and organized as Molen Ward; Lyman S. Beach, Bishop.

September 1884

The 81st quorum of Seventy was organized by Seymour B. Young in Emery County; Noah T. Guyman, Wm. H. Branch, J.P. Wimmer, Peter R. Petersen, Abner Buckley and James C. Jensen, presidents.

September 1, 1884 (Monday)

Margaret T. Smoot, wife of Abraham O. Smoot, died in Provo, Utah, Co.

September 1, 1884 (Monday)

Charles S. Zane, recently appointed chief justice of Utah, took the oath of office and was assigned, by proclamation of Gov. Eli H. Murray, to the Third District Court.

September 2, 1884 (Tuesday)

Piute County, Utah, was visited by a fearful hail storm.

September 3, 1884 (Wednesday)

The Saints who had settled on Rock Creek, Oneida Co., Idaho, were organized as Rockland Ward, of the Box Elder Stake; Isaac Thorn, Bishop.

September 6, 1884 (Saturday)

Wilford branch, Snake River Valley, Idaho, was organized as a Ward; Thos. S. Smith, Bishop.

September 9, 1884 (Tuesday)

The Saints who had settled on the Mancos river, Colo., were organized as a branch; James H. Duncan, presiding Elder.

September 15, 1884 (Monday)

Ashley Ward, Uintah Co., was divided, and two new districts, Merrill and Glines, organized, with Geo. A. Davis and James H. Glines as their respective acting Bishops.

September 15, 1884 (Monday)

Wm. C.A. Smoot, jun., missionary in Germany, was arrested in Kiel, for baptising a woman. He was held a prisoner until Oct. 7th, when he was acquitted, but nevertheless banished from the city.

September 22, 1884 (Monday)

Elder John Nicholson delivered a lecture in the Salt Lake Theatre on the subject: The Tennessee massacre and its causes; showing that it was the result of inflammatory articles in the Salt Lake Tribune, circulated by sectarian ministers in Tennessee.

September 27, 1884 (Saturday)

The first open venire grand jury in Salt Lake City was impaneled by Judge Zane.

October 1884

Severe persecutions continued against the Elders in the Southern States, and also in Indiana, Michigan and other places.

October 1, 1884 (Wednesday)

The 82nd quorum of Seventy was organized in Grass Valley, Piute Co, Utah, by Seymour B. Young; Geo. A. Burr and Ole E. Olsen, presidents. Chapman Duncan, Wm. E. Stringham and Geo. A. Hatch were set apart as presidents afterwards.

October 7, 1884 (Tuesday)

John Morgan was ordained one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies, to fill the vacancy caused by the death of Wm. W. Taylor.

October 14, 1884 (Tuesday)

Bishop Marius Ensign died at Santa Clara, Washington Co.

October 15, 1884 (Wednesday)

The trial of Rudger Clawson for polygamy was commenced in the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, and continued several days, during which Presidents John Taylor, Geo. Q. Cannon and other prominent men were subpoenaed as witnesses.

October 21, 1884 (Tuesday)

The jury, which could not agree on a verdict in Rudger Clawson's case, was discharged, and preparations were made for a new trial.

October 23, 1884 (Thursday)

The steamship City of Berlin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 93 Saints, including nine returning Elders in charge of Carl August Ek. the company arrived at New York Nov. 2nd, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 9th.

October 24, 1884 (Friday)

Lydia Spencer, Rudger Clawson's alleged second wife, having been arrested, a new trial was commenced, and Lydia Spencer, who refused to testify, was sent to the Penitentiary.

October 25, 1884 (Saturday)

In the Third District Court, Lydia Spencer, by her husband's consent, acknowledged that she was Rudger Clawson's wife, and the jury, after 17 minutes' consultation, returned a verdict of guilty against Clawson.

October 31, 1884 (Friday)

After several days' trial in the Third District Court, John Connelly was acquitted on the charge of polygamy, his case being barred by the statute of limitations.

November 1, 1884 (Saturday)

The steamship Arizona sailed from Liverpool, England, with 163 Saints, including 20 returning missionaries, in charge of Joseph Alma Smith. the company arrived at New York Nov. 11th, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 19th.

November 3, 1884 (Monday)

Hans Ottesen was murdered in Manti, Sanpete Co.

November 3, 1884 (Monday)

In the Third District Court (Judge Zane), Rudger Clawson was sentenced to four years' imprisonment and $800 fine for polygamy and unlawful cohabitation. The case was appealed, but bail was refused and Clawson taken to the Penitentiary.

November 3, 1884 (Monday)

Paul A. Schettler, treasurer of Salt Lake City, died.

November 4, 1884 (Tuesday)

At the general election, John T. Caine, the People's Party candidate for delegate to Congress, received 22,120 votes and Ransford Smith, the Liberal Party candidate, 2,215.

November 4, 1884 (Tuesday)

Layton branch, Graham Co., Ariz., was organized as a Ward; John Welker, Bishop.

November 5, 1884 (Wednesday)

The trial of Joseph H. Evans, indicted for polygamy and unlawful cohabitation, was commenced in the Third District Court, and the following day the jury returned a verdict of guilty.

November 8, 1884 (Saturday)

The news of the Democratic victory in the election of Grover Cleveland for president of the United States having reached Salt Lake City, a grand jollification meeting was held by the Salt Lake City Democrats, in front of the City Hall.

November 8, 1884 (Saturday)

In the Third District Court (Judge Zane), Joseph H. Evans was sentenced to three and a half years' imprisonment in the Penitentiary and $250 fine.

November 13, 1884 (Thursday)

Eighty Latter-day Saint emigrants from the Southern States mission, and nine returning Elders, left Chattanooga, Tenn., bound for Colorado and Utah.

November 14, 1884 (Friday)

Rudger Clawson was brought before the Supreme Court of Utah on a writ of habeas corpus, and on the following day the decision of the lower court, in refusing him and Joseph H. Evans bail, pending an appeal to the higher courts, was affirmed.

November 19, 1884 (Wednesday)

Frederick W. Schoenfeld and Rudolph Hochstrasser, in the District Court of Zofingen, Canton Aargau, Switzerland, were sentenced to pay a fine of 100 francs each, and banished from the canton, for preaching the gospel in Niederwyl.

November 22, 1884 (Saturday)

Horace Kimball Whitney, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

November 23, 1884 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held in Rexburg, Idaho, the Saints who had settled on the Teton island, near Rexburg, were organized as Salem Ward; Geo. H. B. Harris, Bishop.

November 25, 1884 (Tuesday)

John Aird, jun., of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of unlawful cohabitation. The following day he was placed under $3,000 bonds, to await action of the grand jury.

December 4, 1884 (Thursday)

Ole L. Hansen, of Brighton, Salt Lake Co., was arraigned before U.S. Commissioner Wm. McKay, Salt Lake City, charged with polygamy.

December 5, 1884 (Friday)

Ammon M. Tenney, Peter J. Christoffersen, and Christopher J. Kempe, tried and convicted of polygamy, were each sentenced by Judge Howard, at Prescott, Ariz., to three years, and six months' imprisonment in the House of Correction at Detroit, Mich., and $5.00 fine. Their offence was unlawful cohabitation, but this was construed by the court as polygamy. Wm. J. Flake and Jens N. Skousen, who plead guilty to u.c. (unlawful cohabitation), were each sentenced to six months' imprisonment at Yuma, Ariz., and $500 fine.

December 7, 1884 (Sunday)

Ammon M. Tenney, Peter J. Christoffersen and Christopher J. Kempe left Prescott, Ariz., for the prison at Detroit, Mich., and Wm. J. Flake and Jens. N. Skousen for Yuma prison.

December 13, 1884 (Saturday)

John Olsen, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of u.c. and placed under $1,000 bonds.

December 14, 1884 (Sunday)

The 83rd quorum of Seventy was partly organized by Seymour B. Young, at St. Johns, Apache Co., Ariz.

December 31, 1884 (Wednesday)

Elder Jacob Spori arrived as a Latter-day Saint missionary at Constantinople, Turkey.
