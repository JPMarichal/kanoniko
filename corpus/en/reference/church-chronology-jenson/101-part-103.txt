Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

During this year three new Apostles were chosen; the President of the Church made a trip to Europe, and a number of prominent Elders and pioneers died.

January 1, 1906 (Monday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith, and party returned to Salt Lake City from their visit to Vermont and other eastern States.

January 1, 1906 (Monday)

George Cook, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died at Willard, Box Elder Co., Utah.

January 1, 1906 (Monday)

William Mutch, a Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Mona, Juab Co., Utah.

January 2, 1906 (Tuesday)

The American Party went into power in Salt Lake City for the first time.

January 3, 1906 (Wednesday)

Martha E. Christiansen, a Utah pioneer of 1865, died at Hyrum, Cache Co., Utah.

January 4, 1906 (Thursday)

Thomas Barratt, a prominent citizen of American Fork, Utah Co., died at that place.

January 5, 1906 (Friday)

Mrs. C.O. Conover, matron of the Manti Temple, died in Manti, Utah.

January 6, 1906 (Saturday)

Wm. O. Gibbons died at St. Johns, Ariz.

January 7, 1906 (Sunday)

Lemuel H. Redd succeeded Jens Nielsen as Bishop of Bluff Ward, San Juan Co., Utah. He was ordained Feb. 1st.

January 9, 1906 (Tuesday)

Mary M. Neal died in Salt Lake City.

January 10, 1906 (Wednesday)

Alfred Newby died at Monroe, Sevier Co., Utah.

January 11, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Diana Holbrook Drake, the last surviving member of Zion's Camp, died at Centerville, Davis Co., Utah.

January 11, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Alice Booth Robinson, the first woman settler of Plain City, Weber Co., Utah, died at that place.

January 12, 1906 (Friday)

American Fork and Lehi, Utah Co., celebrated in appreciation of securing the new State experiment farm.

January 14, 1906 (Sunday)

Franklin S. Tingey succeeded Walter J. Beatie as Bishop of the Seventeenth Ward, Salt Lake City.

January 14, 1906 (Sunday)

Anthon Nielson succeeded James W. Nixon as Bishop of Huntington, Emery Co., Utah. Henry G. Mills succeeded Manasseh J. Blackburn as Bishop of Desert Lake, and Henry M. Reid succeeded Jasper Robertson as Bishop of Orangeville, Emery Co., Utah.

January 14, 1906 (Sunday)

Frank C. Parkinson was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Blackfoot Ward; Wilford N. Christensen was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Riverside Ward; Joseph Holland was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Shelley Ward, and John M. Rider was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Woodville Ward, all in the Blackfoot Stake, Bingham Co., Ida.

January 15, 1906 (Monday)

Thos. Lewis Griffiths succeeded Fred T. Gunn as Bishop of Adamsville, Beaver, Co., Utah.

January 15, 1906 (Monday)

John T. Rowley was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Spring Glen Ward, Emery Stake, Carbon Co., Utah.

January 15, 1906 (Monday)

Ambrose Shaw, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Ogden, Utah.

January 16, 1906 (Tuesday)

Samuel Dugmore succeeded John Potter as Bishop of Sunnyside Ward, Emery Stake, Carbon Co., Utah.

January 20, 1906 (Saturday)

August K. Anderson was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Grantsville Ward, Tooele Co., Utah.

January 21, 1906 (Sunday)

The Oakley First Ward Cassia Co., Idaho, was divided and a new Ward called the Oakley Fourth Ward organized with David P. Thomas as Bishop; he was ordained Jan. 27th.

January 22, 1906 (Monday)

John Hawkins, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Payson, Utah Co., Utah.

January 24, 1906 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Elizabeth Pettit Willie, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Mendon, Cache Co., Utah.

January 24, 1906 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Sarah C. Sessions, widow of the late Perrigrine Sessions, died at Bountiful, Davis Co., Utah.

January 25, 1906 (Thursday)

The "See America First" movement held its first conference in Salt Lake City.

January 26, 1906 (Friday)

The Swedish saints of the Ensign Stake were organized for holding meetings; Hugo D. E. Peterson, president.

January 26, 1906 (Friday)

Lucinda Petty, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Deseret, Millard Co., Utah.

January 28, 1906 (Sunday)

Henry J. Garner was appointed Bishop of Plain City Ward, Weber Co., Utah, succeeding Geo. W. Bramwell; he was ordained Jan. 31st.

January 30, 1906 (Tuesday)

Wm. Spry was confirmed U.S. marshal for the District of Utah.

January 30, 1906 (Tuesday)

Hon. Robert C. Lund died at St. George, Utah.

January 31, 1906 (Wednesday)

Professor Albert Miller, of the B.Y. University, died at Provo, Utah.

February 1906

The Book of Mormon was published in the Turkish language, under the direction of Ferdinand F. Hintze, in Boston, Mass.

February 2, 1906 (Friday)

Nancy Margaret Nielsen, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Hyrum, Cache Co., Utah.

February 3, 1906 (Saturday)

Henry Dalton, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Kanosh, Millard Co., Utah.

February 3, 1906 (Saturday)

A new Ward named Coltman was organized in the Bingham Stake, Idaho, with Eberhard Bauer as Bishop.

February 3, 1906 (Saturday)

Robert Gardner, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Pine Valley, Washington Co., Utah.

February 4, 1906 (Sunday)

The Nineteenth Ward, Salt Lake City, was divided and that portion of the same lying north of Fifth North Street and Pear Street was organized as the Twenty-fourth Ward of Salt Lake City, with Wm. Wood, jun., as Bishop.

February 4, 1906 (Sunday)

Eberhard Bauer was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Coltman Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho.

February 6, 1906 (Tuesday)

Apostle Marriner W. Merrill died at Richmond, Cache Co., Utah.

February 7, 1906 (Wednesday)

The case of Senator Reed Smoot was reopened before the Senate Committee in Washington, D.C.

February 7, 1906 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Morilla Spink Bates, a former resident of Nauvoo, died at Payson, Utah Co., Utah.

February 8, 1906 (Thursday)

The Society Islands were visited by a terrific cyclone. "Mormon" Elders rendered efficient aid in saving the archives of the American consulate at Papeete, Tahiti, from destruction.

February 9, 1906 (Friday)

The first annual gathering of the Utah war veterans took place in Salt Lake City. Orson P. Arnold was chosen commander.

February 9, 1906 (Friday)

Ira N. Jacob, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Loma, Colo.

February 10, 1906 (Saturday)

Geo. Murdock, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Lehi, Utah.

February 11, 1906 (Sunday)

The Turner Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, was organized with Jos. P. Greene as Bishop.

February 13, 1906 (Tuesday)

Nephi Parker, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Layton, Arizona.

February 16, 1906 (Friday)

John W. Robertson, recently returned from a mission to Great Britain, died at Salt Lake City.

February 17, 1906 (Saturday)

Gearsen S. Bastian succeeded Willis E. Robison as President of the Wayne Stake, Wayne Co., Utah.

February 17, 1906 (Saturday)

Johnson B.B. Young, an early pioneer of Utah, and Wm. Farrer, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Provo.

February 17, 1906 (Saturday)

Andrew D. Mortensen was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Glendale Ward, Oneida Co., Idaho.

February 18, 1906 (Sunday)

The Wayne Stake presidency was reorganized; Gearsen S. Bastian was chosen president with Joseph Eckersley as first and John R. Stewart as second counselor.

February 19, 1906 (Monday)

Wm. W. Woodland, a Utah pioneer of 1838, died in Salt Lake City.

February 20, 1906 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Mary Pitt Oakey, one of the oldest citizens of American Fork, Utah, died.

February 21, 1906 (Wednesday)

Elder Thos. H. Clark died at Grantsville, Utah.

February 21, 1906 (Wednesday)

Christina Erika Forsgren Davis, the first woman baptized in Sweden, died in Brigham City, Utah.

February 21, 1906 (Wednesday)

Wilford Paxman, a recently returned missionary from Great Britain, died at American Fork, Utah.

February 22, 1906 (Thursday)

John S. Lindsay, a pioneer actor of Utah, died in Salt Lake City.

February 22, 1906 (Thursday)

Joshua Adams, a Utah pioneer of 1849, and one of the first settlers of American Fork, Utah, died.

February 24, 1906 (Saturday)

Dudley W. Chase, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at St. Anthony, Ida.

February 24, 1906 (Saturday)

Elizabeth Crosby Brown, widow of the late Bishop John Brown, and a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah.

February 25, 1906 (Sunday)

Moses Franklin Farnsworth, recorder in the Manti Temple, died at Manti, Utah.

February 25, 1906 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in Sugar City, Salem, Hibbard and Plano, Fremont Co., Idaho, were organized by Joseph W. McMurrin as the 138th quorum of Seventy with Daniel W. Grover as senior president. About two months prior to this the 147th quorum of Seventy had been organized in the Blackfoot Stake with Burton S. Rupp as senior president.

February 26, 1906 (Monday)

Geo. Evans, city marshal of Lehi, Utah, died at Provo.

February 28, 1906 (Wednesday)

Hugh McDougall died at Springville, Utah.

March 1, 1906 (Thursday)

James Eli Ashcroft, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Mapleton, Utah Co., Utah.

March 2, 1906 (Friday)

Peter Lyman Lott, a Utah pioneer of 1848, and one of the first settlers of Utah County, died at Lehi.

March 3, 1906 (Saturday)

Mrs. Margaret Thomas, one of the first settlers of Brigham City, Utah, died at Oakley, Idaho.

March 3, 1906 (Saturday)

Henry E. Bowring, a veteran actor, died at Brigham City, Utah.

March 4, 1906 (Sunday)

The Murray Ward, Salt Lake Co., Utah, was divided and the west part of the same organized as the Murray Second Ward with Emil Erickson as Bishop; he was ordained on the 7th.

March 6, 1906 (Tuesday)

Samuel Peterson, formerly Bishop of the Second Ward, Salt Lake City, died.

March 8, 1906 (Thursday)

Sarah Almyra Marsh Taylor, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Payson, Utah.

March 8, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Mary Ann Holden Cook, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Lyman, Wayne Co., Utah.

March 9, 1906 (Friday)

Mrs. Elizabeth Ann Taylor Criddle, a Utah pioneer of 1850, died at Kaysville, Utah.

March 10, 1906 (Saturday)

Thos. Howard, a pioneer papermaker, died in Salt Lake City.

March 11, 1906 (Sunday)

Arnold G. Giauque succeeded James C. Watson as Bishop of the Sixth Ward, Salt Lake City.

March 11, 1906 (Sunday)

Jos. W. Lee was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Mercur Ward, and Ormus A. Bates ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Batesville Ward, Tooele Co., Utah.

March 13, 1906 (Tuesday)

Patriarch Ole C. Olsen died at Mayfield, Sanpete Co., Utah.

A new Ward named Inkom was organized in the Pocatello Stake with Edward M. Webb as Bishop.

James Anderson Ivie, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Salina, Utah.

Richard Matthews, an old actor and employee of the "Deseret News," died in Salt Lake City.

Elder John C. Graham and Maria A. Loveless Jones died at Provo, Utah.

March 18, 1906 (Sunday)

Phares Wells, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at St. George, Utah, 92 years old.

March 18, 1906 (Sunday)

A new Ward named Lebanon was organized in the St. Joseph Stake, Ariz.

March 20, 1906 (Tuesday)

Professor Ebenezer Beesley, once conductor of the Salt Lake Tabernacle Choir, died in Salt Lake City

March 21, 1906 (Wednesday)

Col. Willard Young was chosen president of the Latter-day Saints' University in Salt Lake City, succeeding Prof. Joshua H. Paul; Elder Young took charge of the university March 30th.

March 22, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Margaret Sharp Cahoon, a Utah pioneer of 1850, died in Salt Lake City.

March 23, 1906 (Friday)

Homer Duncan, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died in Salt Lake City.

March 24, 1906 (Saturday)

Richard Bentley, a former employee of the Historian's Office, died at St. George, Utah.

March 25, 1906 (Sunday)

Roscoe W. Eardley succeeded Oliver Hodgson as Bishop of the Third Ward, and Chas. H. Worthen succeeded Harrison Sperry as Bishop of the Fourth Ward, Salt Lake City.

March 25, 1906 (Sunday)

Ezra Parrish became Bishop of the Peterson Ward, Morgan Co., Utah.

March 25, 1906 (Sunday)

Prof. Jacob Franklin Miller, of the B.Y. College, died at Logan, Utah.

March 27, 1906 (Tuesday)

Wm. X. Jones, a hand-cart pioneer and prominent member of the Tabernacle Choir, died in Salt Lake City.

March 28, 1906 (Wednesday)

Reuben Carter, Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Fountain Green, Sanpete Co., and Wm. H. Stott, a Utah pioneer of 1849, died in Meadow, Millard Co., Utah.

March 29, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Cordelia Barlow, one of the early members of the Church, died at Lewiston, Cache Co., Utah.

March 31, 1906 (Saturday)

Mrs. Mary Houtz Snow, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Brigham City, Utah.

April 1, 1906 (Sunday)

Elizabeth Derr, a Utah pioneer of 1864, and Mary Jane Phippen, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died in Salt Lake City.

April 4, 1906 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Harriet Ingram Gardner Hoyt, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died in Kane County, Utah.

April 5, 1906 (Thursday)

Chas. H. Rannel, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Hayden, Idaho.

April 6, 1906 (Friday)

The seventy-sixth annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City; it was continued on the 7th and 8th.

April 7, 1906 (Saturday)

Hyrum Christensen was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Mayfield Ward, Sanpete Co., Utah, succeeding Parley Christiansen.

April 7, 1906 (Saturday)

Patriarch Edward Cliff died at Mt. Pleasant, Sanpete Co., and Bishop Robert Calder died at Garden City, Rich Co., Utah.

April 8, 1906 (Sunday)

In the final session of the general conference in Salt Lake City, Pres. Francis M. Lyman announced the resignation of Apostles John W. Taylor and Matthias F. Cowley. Geo. Franklin Richards, Orson Ferguson Whitney and David O. McKay were selected to fill vacancies in the council of the Twelve Apostles. Chas. H. Hart was selected to fill the vacancy in the First Council of Seventies caused by the death of Christian D. Fjeldsted. Joseph F. Smith, Jr., was sustained as an Assistant Church Historian.

April 8, 1906 (Sunday)

Hans Christiansen Holdensgaard died at Richfield, Utah.

April 9, 1906 (Monday)

Newel K. Beal was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Ferron Ward, Emery Co., Utah.

April 9, 1906 (Monday)

David Murray was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Mt. Sterling Ward, Cache Co., Utah.

April 9, 1906 (Monday)

Geo. Anderson, a Utah pioneer of 1855, died at Springville, Utah.

April 12, 1906 (Thursday)

The Archaeological Society of Utah was organized with Col. E.A. Wall as president, and Mathonihah Thomas secretary.

April 14, 1906 (Saturday)

Mrs. Sarah Everard, a former resident of Utah, died in London, England.

April 16, 1906 (Monday)

Elizabeth Brook Fox, a Utah pioneer of 1861, and the first Stake president of the Oneida Stake Relief Societies, died at Franklin, Idaho.

April 17, 1906 (Tuesday)

Louisa Ann Eddins Hales, a Utah pioneer of 1858 died at Beaver, Utah. Warren G. Childs, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Ogden, Utah.

April 18, 1906 (Wednesday)

The greatest and most desolating earthquake and fire in the history of modern America occurred in San Francisco. Among the sufferers were several members of the Church.

April 19, 1906 (Thursday)

The Church authorities gave $10,000 to the Relief Fund for the California earthquake sufferers. It was the first donation tendered from any outside State.

April 19, 1906 (Thursday)

Evan Owen, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Wellsville, Cache Co., Utah.

April 19, 1906 (Sunday)

Thos. Alfred Clawson was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Eighteenth Ward, Salt Lake City, succeeding Orson F. Whitney, who had been ordained an Apostle.

April 19, 1906 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in Emery, Ferron and Molen, Emery c Utah, were organized by Charles H. Hart as the 149th quorum of Seventy, with John B. Broderick as senior president.

April 23, 1906 (Monday)

John Leishman, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Wellsville, Cache Co., Utah.

April 24, 1906 (Tuesday)

patriarch Ola N. Liljenquist died at Rexburg, Idaho.

April 24, 1906 (Tuesday)

Bishop Jens Nielsen died at Bluff, San Juan Co., Utah, and Niels M. Skougaard died at Richfield, Utah.

April 24, 1906 (Tuesday)

Elder James W. Bush, who labored as a missionary in the Hawaiian Islands, died at Honolulu.

April 25, 1906 (Wednesday)

Elizabeth J. Stevenson, wife of the late Edward Stevenson, and a Utah pioneer of 1854, died at Emery, Emery Co., Utah.

April 27, 1906 (Friday)

Prof. Horace H. Cummings was appointed general superintendent of Church schools, succeeding Joseph M. Tanner.

April 27, 1906 (Friday)

Robert Jones, a pioneer of Summit Co., Utah, died at Henefer, Utah.

April 29, 1906 (Sunday)

A new Ward Assembly Hall was dedicated by President Joseph F. Smith at South Bountiful, Davis Co., Utah.

May 1, 1906 (Tuesday)

Bishop Samuel Frink Atwood, a Utah pioneer of 1851, died in Salt Lake City.

May 1, 1906 (Tuesday)

Elder Hugh Melvin Welker of Bennington, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, died of smallpox in Holland, where he labored as a missionary.

May 3, 1906 (Thursday)

Mrs. Louisa Maria Tanner Lyman died in Salt Lake City.

May 5, 1906 (Saturday)

Ann Matilda Little, wife of the late James A. Little, and a Utah pioneer of 1856, died at Colonia Diaz, Mexico.

May 6, 1906 (Sunday)

Patriarch John Davidson Burt died at Brigham City, and Mrs. Emeline M. Curday, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Springville, Utah.

May 7, 1906 (Monday)

Henry T. Coleman was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Midway First Ward, Wasatch Co., Utah.

May 7, 1906 (Monday)

Mrs. Mary Bright Peek, a former resident of Nauvoo, Ill., died at South Weber, Davis Co., Utah.

May 9, 1906 (Wednesday)

Elizabeth Stallings, an early pioneer of Utah, died at Lewisville, Idaho.

May 11, 1906 (Thursday)

Edwin Booth, a Utah pioneer of 1851, died at Nephi, Juab Co., Utah, and Nicholas T. Silcock, a Utah pioneer of 1850, died at Wellington, Carbon Co., Utah.

May 11, 1906 (Thursday)

The Seventies residing in Magrath, Canada, were organized by Joseph W. McMurrin as the 150th quorum of Seventy, with Willard T. Fletcher as senior president.

May 12, 1906 (Saturday)

Abraham Holladay, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Provo, and Patriarch Francis Webster died at Cedar City, Utah.

May 12, 1906 (Saturday)

The Seventies residing in Sterling, Tabor and Welling, Canada, were organized by Joseph W. McMurrin as the 151st quorum of Seventy, with Hjalderman Astlund as senior president.

May 13, 1906 (Sunday)

Jonathan W. Cameron was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Torrey Ward, Wayne Co., Utah.

May 14, 1906 (Monday)

Elder Abraham John Gold of Salt Lake City, who labored as a missionary in the Netherlands, died of smallpox at Groningen, Holland.

May 15, 1906 (Tuesday)

Walter Hamilton Grow, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Woodville, Bingham Co., Idaho.

May 17, 1906 (Thursday)

The Salt Lake and Los Angeles Railroad, Saltair Beach pavilion and other properties were sold to a local syndicate, comprising Col. Nephi W. Clayton, Chas. W. Nibley, Jeremiah E. Langford and Joseph Nelson.

May 18, 1906 (Friday)

Judge Morse, in the Sixth Judicial District Court sitting at Richfield, Utah, signed an important decree, establishing certain rights to the Sevier River and its tributaries.

May 21, 1906 (Monday)

Madame Sarah Bernhardt, the famous French actress, arrived in Salt Lake City on a visit. She refused to enter the Tabernacle or other "Mormon" buildings.

May 22, 1906 (Tuesday)

A most disastrous fire destroyed a number of buildings in Ogden, Utah.

May 22, 1906 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Mary Willis, widow of the late Wm. Wesley Willis, of Mormon Battalion fame died in Salt Lake City.

May 23, 1906 (Wednesday)

Wm. M. Frampton, one of the earliest settlers of Pleasant Grove, and a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah.

May 24, 1906 (Thursday)

Slight shocks of earthquake were felt in Ogden and vicinity.

May 24, 1906 (Thursday)

Wm. R. Jones, a Utah pioneer, died in Salt Lake City.

May 24, 1906 (Thursday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Douglas, Arizona, with J. Parley Biglow as presiding Elder.

May 25, 1906 (Friday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith purchased the old Washington press, upon which the first edition of the Book of Mormon was printed in 1820.

May 25, 1906 (Friday)

Thos. Henry Bullock, a Utah pioneer, died at Warren, Weber Co., Utah.

May 25, 1906 (Friday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Tombstone, Arizona, with Levi Nelson as Presiding Elder.

May 26, 1906 (Saturday)

Patriarch Samuel Francis, a Utah pioneer of 1855, died at Morgan, Morgan Co., Utah.

May 27, 1906 (Sunday)

Collins R. Hakes, jun., was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Bluewater Ward (St. Johns Stake), Valencia Co., New Mexico.

May 28, 1906 (Monday)

Sarah Delight Stocking Woodruff, widow of the late Pres. Wilford Woodruff and a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co, Utah.

May 30, 1906 (Wednesday)

Jesse J. Fuller, second cousin of the Prophet Joseph Smith, died at Provo, and Joseph Morrell, president of the Cache Stake, died at Logan, Cache Co., Utah.

June 1, 1906 (Friday)

The Senate Committee on Privileges and Elections by a vote of 7 to 5 declared that Senator Reed Smoot, of Utah, was not entitled to his seat in the U.S. Senate.

June 1, 1906 (Friday)

Peter Stubbs, a Utah pioneer of 1853, and James Chislett, a Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Provo, Utah.

June 2, 1906 (Saturday)

John Aldridge Evans, cashier of the "Deseret News," died in Salt Lake City, as a result of a street car accident.

June 3, 1906 (Sunday)

At the quarterly conference of the Uintah Stake, held at Vernal, Utah, Samuel R. Bennion was released from his presidency of the Uintah Stake, together with his counselors, Reuben S. Collett, and James Hacking, and Wm. H. Smart was chosen as president of the Stake.

June 3, 1906 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at Burley, Ida., were organized as a Ward with Lorenzo W. Robbins as Bishop.

June 3, 1906 (Sunday)

Patriarch Wm. Mendenhall died at Springville, Utah.

June 4, 1906 (Monday)

Edgar Hunsaker was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Alma Ward, Maricopa Stake, Arizona.

June 4, 1906 (Monday)

Diana Lovina Rogers, a Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Provo, and Randina Olson, a Utah pioneer of 1866, died at Santaquin, Utah Co., Utah.

June 5, 1906 (Tuesday)

Geo. Openshaw, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died in Salt Lake City, 89 years of age.

June 5, 1906 (Tuesday)

Jesse Nathaniel Smith, president of the Snowflake Stake of Zion, and a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Snowflake, Arizona.

June 8, 1906 (Friday)

Lorenzo W. Robbins was ordained a Bishop by Francis M. Lyman in Salt Lake City and set apart to preside over the Burley Ward, Cassia Co., Idaho.

June 8, 1906 (Friday)

John S. Warren was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Bisbee Ward, Cochise Co., Arizona, and Thos. G. Alder was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Lebanon Ward, St. Joseph Stake, Graham Co., Arizona.

June 10, 1906 (Sunday)

At a conference of the Bear Lake Stake of Zion held at Paris, Idaho, Wm. Budge was honorably released from his position as president of the Stake, having been called to preside over the Logan Temple. Jos. R. Shepherd was chosen as his successor, with Wm. L. Rich and Wilford W. Richards as his counselors. Alma Hayes was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Georgetown Ward, succeeding Wilford W. Richards.

June 10, 1906 (Sunday)

The Senate Committee on Privileges and Elections at Washington, D.C., filed two reports in the Senate in the cases of Senator Reed Smoot.

June 12, 1906 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Olivia Pratt Driggs, a Utah pioneer of 1852, and daughter of the late Apostle Parley P. Pratt, died in Pleasant Grove, Utah.

June 14, 1906 (Thursday)

Niels C. Rasmussen, an old resident of Manti, Utah, was accidentally drowned in a creek in Manti Canyon.

June 14, 1906 (Thursday)

Joseph Biddlecomb, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Bowling Green, Missouri.

June 17, 1906 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at and near Bluewater, Valencia Co., New Mexico, were more fully organized as a Ward with Collins R. Hakes, jun., as Bishop.

June 18, 1906 (Monday)

Geo. Biddlecomb and his 14-year-old daughter were accidentally drowned in Ferron Creek, Emery Co., Utah.

June 20, 1906 (Wednesday)

July 13, 1906 (Friday)
