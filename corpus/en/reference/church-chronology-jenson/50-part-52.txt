Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year the crops in Utah were partly destroyed by grasshoppers; the so-called Walker war was terminated and the Deseret alphabet was formulated. Hundreds of emigrating Saints from Europe died from cholera while sailing up the Mississippi and Missouri rivers and crossing the plains. A Stake of Zion was organized at St. Louis, Mo., and a mission opened up in New Zealand.

January 1854

The Deseret News was changed from a semi-monthly to a weekly paper.

January 3, 1854 (Tuesday)

The ship Jesse Munn sailed from Liverpool, England, with 300 Scandinavian and 33 German Saints, under the direction of Christian Larsen. It arrived at New Orleans Feb. 10th, and the emigrants continued up the rivers to Kansas City, Mo., which this year was selected as the outfitting place for the Saints crossing the plains.

January 6, 1854 (Friday)

Allred's Settlement (Spring City), Sanpete Co., which had been deserted by its inhabitants the previous summer, because of Indian troubles, was burned to the ground.

January 12, 1854 (Thursday)

The 37th quorum of Seventy was organized in G.S.L. City, with Cyrus H. Wheelock, John Lyon, Jesse W. Crosby, Jonathan Midgley, David J. Ross, George Halliday and Claudius V. Spencer as presidents.

January 12, 1854 (Thursday)

Some time previous the 36th quorum had been organized, with Jesse W. Fox as one of the presidents.

January 18, 1854 (Wednesday)

Elders Samuel A. Woolley and Wm. Fotheringham arrived, as missionaries, at Agra, Hindostan.

January 20, 1854 (Friday)

The legislative assembly of Utah adjourned. Among the acts passed and approved were those providing for the organization of Summit, Green River and Carson Counties, and defining the boundaries of Davis County.

January 22, 1854 (Sunday)

The ship Benjamin Adams sailed from Liverpool, England, with 378 Scandinavian and 6 British Saints, under the direction of Hans Peter Olsen. The company arrived at New Orleans, March 22nd, and at Kansas City in the beginning of April.

January 23, 1854 (Monday)

A branch of the Church was organized by Edward Stevenson, at Gibraltar, Spain, with 10 members.

January 31, 1854 (Tuesday)

Bishop Isaac Clark died at Ogden.

January 31, 1854 (Tuesday)

A mass meeting was held in G.S.L. City for the purpose of taking steps towards memorializing Congress to construct a national railroad from the Missouri river, via the South Pass and G.S.L. City, to the Pacific.

February 4, 1854 (Saturday)

The ship Golconda sailed from Liverpool, England, with 464 Saints, under the direction of Door P. Curtis; it arrived at New Orleans March 18th.

February 5, 1854 (Sunday)

At a Seventies' quarterly conference held in G.S.L. City, the 38th and 39th quorums of Seventy were organized with Benjamin F. Cummings and Daniel McIntosh as senior presidents.

February 7, 1854 (Tuesday)

John C. Fremont, with a company of nine whites and twelve Delaware Indians, arrived at Parowan, Iron Co., in a state of starvation. One man had fallen dead from his horse near the settlement, and others were nearly dead. Animals and provisions were supplied by the Saints, and, after resting until the 20th, Fremont and company continued their journey to California.

February 14, 1854 (Tuesday)

Clarissa Smith, Patriarch John Smith's wife, died in G.S.L. City.

February 22, 1854 (Wednesday)

The ship Windermere sailed from Liverpool, England, with 484 Saints, under Daniel Garn's direction; it arrived at New Orleans April 23rd. Many died on board from the small pox.

February 22, 1854 (Wednesday)

Elders A. Milton Musser and Truman Leonard sailed from Bombay, India, for Kurrachee, Scinde, which place they reached Feb. 26th. Kurrachee is about 900 miles northwest of Bombay.

March 1854

Elias Smith succeeded the late Willard Richards as postmaster of Great Salt Lake City.

March 1854

Ephraim, Sanpete Co., was first settled.

March 1854

The first number of the Latter-day Saints' Millennial Star and Monthly Visitor, an eight-page periodical (octavo size), was published at Madras, Hindostan; Elder Richard Ballantyne editor and publisher.

March 5, 1854 (Sunday)

The ship Old England sailed from Liverpool, England, with 45 Saints, under the direction of John O. Angus. It arrived at the mouth of the Mississippi river April 24th.

March 6, 1854 (Monday)

Elders Samuel A. Woolley and Wm. Fotheringham returned to Calcutta from an unsuccessful mission to the interior, on which they visited Benares, Belaspore, Marat, Delhi, Kurnaul, Agra, Cawnpore, Allahabad, etc.

March 11, 1854 (Saturday)

Dr. Willard Richards, second Counselor to Pres. Brigham Young, and editor of the Deseret News, died in G.S.L. City, of dropsy.

March 12, 1854 (Sunday)

The ship John M. Wood sailed from Liverpool, with 383 Saints, including 58 from Switzerland and Italy, under the direction of Robert L. Campbell. It arrived at New Orleans May 2nd.

March 22, 1854 (Wednesday)

The ship Julia Ann sailed from Sydney, Australia, with about seventy Saints, bound for Utah, under the direction of Wm. Hyde. The company landed at San Pedro, Cal., June 12th.

March 24, 1854 (Friday)

Geo. E. Ashburner, captain of the police, peremptorily ordered Elders A. Milton Musser and Truman Leonard out of the cantonment of Camp Kurrachee, India, and admonished them not to return.

March 29, 1854 (Wednesday)

Under the administration of Elders John S. Fulmer and David B. Dille, Halsden Marsden, 18 years old, who was born deaf and dumb, was miraculously healed from his deafness, at Rochdale, England.

April 1854

A number of Elders were called on a mission to the Indians in southern Utah. This more directly resulted in opening up that part of Utah south of the Great Basin to settlement.

April 4, 1854 (Tuesday)

The ship Germanicus sailed from Liverpool, England, with 220 Saints, under the direction of Richard Cook. The company arrived at New Orleans June 12th.

April 6, 1854 (Thursday)

The 24th annual conference of the Church was commenced in G.S.L. City; it was continued till the 9th. On the 7th, Jedediah M. Grant was chosen second Counselor to Pres. Brigham Young, in place of Willard Richards, deceased. Bro. Grant was set apart on the 9th. Geo. A. Smith was sustained as Church Historian. A number of missionaries were called to Great Britain, the United States, Canada and the Pacific Islands.

April 8, 1854 (Saturday)

The ship Marshfield sailed from Liverpool, with 366 Saints, including about forty from the French mission, under the direction of Wm. Taylor. The company arrived at New Orleans May 29th.

April 10, 1854 (Monday)

The First Presidency issued its "Eleventh General Epistle" to the whole Church.

April 23, 1854 (Sunday)

The Sugar House Ward, G.S.L. Co., Utah, was organized with Abraham O. Smoot as Bishop.

April 24, 1854 (Monday)

Twenty-nine Saints sailed from England on the ship Clara Wheeler, bound for Utah.

May 4, 1854 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young left G.S.L. City, accompanied by many leading men, on a tour through the southern settlements, from which he returned on the 30th.

May 5, 1854 (Friday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt left G.S.L. City on his second mission to California. He arrived at San Bernardino June 9th, and in San Francisco July 2nd.

May 8, 1854 (Monday)

A branch of the Church, consisting of 19 members, was organized in Piedmont, Italy, where considerable persecution had raged.

May 23, 1854 (Tuesday)

Patriarch John Smith died in G.S.L. City, and on June 28th John Smith, son of Hyrum Smith, was chosen Patriarch to the Church in place of the deceased.

May 23, 1854 (Tuesday)

Late in May, (after a "talk" with Pres. Brigham Young), the Indian chief Walker, surrounded by his braves, and Kanosh, chief of the Pauvan Indians, entered into a formal treaty of peace at Chicken Creek, Juab Co. This ended the Ute war, during which 19 white persons and many Indians had been killed, a number of the smaller settlements had been broken up, and their inhabitants moved to the larger towns.

June 1854

Apostle Franklin D. Richards succeeded Samuel W. Richards as president of the British mission. His letter of appointment authorized him "to preside over all the conferences and all the affairs of the Church in the British Islands and adjacent countries." This was the beginning of what has since been called the European mission, which embraces all the missions in Europe, and at one time also the Church organizations in Africa, Australia, India, etc.

June 2, 1854 (Friday)

The first company of emigrants of the season, bound for California, passed through G.S.L. City, having left Council Bluffs April 12th.

June 16, 1854 (Friday)

The workmen began at the south east corner to lay the foundation of the Temple, in G.S.L. City.

July 1854

The grasshoppers made their appearance in the fields of some of the settlements in Utah and did much damage.

July 8, 1854 (Saturday)

Apostle Erastus Snow, accompanied by other Elders, left G.S.L. City for the East, to take charge of the Church in St. Louis and the Western States.

July 13, 1854 (Thursday)

The Jordan river bridge, west of G.S.L. City, was crossed by teams and herds for the first time.

July 20, 1854 (Thursday)

Elder Gudmund Gudmundsen left Iceland, where he had labored upwards of three years preaching the gospel, and had baptized nine persons. He returned to Denmark.

July 25, 1854 (Tuesday)

Elder Richard Ballantyne sailed from Madras, India, bound for London, where he arrived Dec. 6, 1854.

August 1854

The native Saints in the Hawaiian mission commenced to gather to the island of Lanai, which had been selected as a gathering place for them, and the building of a city was commenced in the valley or basin known as Palawai.

August 2, 1854 (Wednesday)

Pres. Brigham Young advised the presidency of the British mission to ship the emigrating Saints from Europe to a more northern port than New Orleans, as the latter place was very unhealthful.

August 8, 1854 (Tuesday)

Wm. and Warren Weeks, sons of Bishop Allen Weeks, were killed by Goshute Indians, in Cedar Valley.

August 12, 1854 (Saturday)

Peter Whitmer, sen., died in Richmond, Ray Co., Mo. He was born April 14, 1773.

August 13, 1854 (Sunday)

Elders Geo. C. Riser and Jens C. Nielsen, who labored as missionaries in Hamburg, Germany, were arrested and imprisoned for preaching the gospel and baptizing a few persons.

August 15, 1854 (Tuesday)

The wall around the Temple Block, in G.S.L. City, was completed.

August 22, 1854 (Tuesday)

Elder Truman Leonard left Kurrachee, India, with a Masonic friend for Kotree, about one hundred miles inland.

August 24, 1854 (Thursday)

John F. Kinney, of Iowa, succeeded Lazarus H. Reed as chief justice of Utah.

August 29, 1854 (Tuesday)

Geo. Mills, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died in G.S.L. City.

August 31, 1854 (Thursday)

Col. E. J. Steptoe, who had been appointed governor of Utah, arrived in G.S.L. City with about one hundred and seventy-five soldiers.

September 5, 1854 (Tuesday)

After 23 days' imprisonment, Elders Geo. C. Riser and Jens C. Nielsen, through the influence of Mr. Bromberg, the American consul, were liberated from prison, in Hamburg, on conditions that they should leave the country forthwith.

September 29, 1854 (Friday)

Capt. James Brown's company of immigrating Saints (with 42 wagons) arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 30, 1854 (Saturday)

Capt. Darwin Richardson's company of immigrating Saints (40 wagons) arrived in G.S.L. City.

October 1, 1854 (Sunday)

Daniel Garn's company of immigrating Saints, including the Germans, arrived in G.S.L. City.

October 2, 1854 (Monday)

Elder Wm. W. Major, of G.S.L. City, died in London, England, where he labored as a missionary.

October 5, 1854 (Thursday)

Elder Hans Peter Olsen's company of immigrating Saints, including the Scandinavians, arrived in G.S.L. City. Many had died from cholera while crossing the plains.

October 8, 1854 (Sunday)

A printing press and the necessary material for printing the Book of Mormon in the Hawaiian language arrived at Honolulu, Hawaii, but subsequently it was shipped to San Francisco, Cal., and the printing done there.

October 24, 1854 (Tuesday)

Wm. A. Empey's company of immigrating Saints (with 43 wagons) arrived in G.S.L. City.

October 27, 1854 (Friday)

Augustus Farnham, president of the Australasian Mission, and Wm. Cooke arrived at Auckland, as the first missionaries to New Zealand.

October 28, 1854 (Saturday)

Robert L. Campbell's company of immigrating Saints, the last of the season, arrived in G.S.L. City.

November 4, 1854 (Saturday)

Apostle Erastus Snow organized a Stake of Zion in St. Louis, Mo., with Milo Andrus as president and Charles Edwards and George Gardiner as counselors. A High Council was also organized, consisting of James H. Hart, Andrew Sproule, John Evans, Wm. Morrison, James S. Cantwell, Wm. Lowe, Samuel J. Lees, Edward Cook, James S. Brooks. William Gore, John Clegg and Charles Chard.

November 11, 1854 (Saturday)

Professor Orson Pratt discovered "a new and easy method of solution of the cubic and biquadratic equations."

November 22, 1854 (Wednesday)

The first number of the St. Louis Luminary was published by Erastus Snow, in St. Louis, Mo.

November 27, 1854 (Monday)

The ship Clara Wheeler sailed from Liverpool, England, with 422 Saints, under the direction of Henry E. Phelps. The company arrived at New Orleans Jan. 11, 1855, and at St. Louis Jan. 22nd.

November 28, 1854 (Tuesday)

Apostle Charles C. Rich arrived in G.S.L. City from San Bernardino, and Geo. Q. Cannon and others from the Sandwich Islands.

December 11, 1854 (Monday)

The Utah legislature (4th annual session) convened in the Council House, in G.S.L. City, and organized by electing Heber C. Kimball president of the Council, and Jedediah M. Grant speaker of the House.

December 24, 1854 (Sunday)

Patriarch William Draper died at Draperville, Salt Lake Co.,

December 25, 1854 (Monday)

The Seventies' Council Hall, in G.S.L. City, was dedicated. It was 53 x 25 feet, and had cost $3,500.

December 30, 1854 (Saturday)

A petition praying for the reappointment of Brigham Young to the governorship of Utah, and signed by Col. Steptoe and the leading officials and business men of G.S.L. City, was sent to Washington, D.C.

December 31, 1854 (Sunday)

The European mission, consisted of 67 conferences, 788 branches and 32,627 members. Of these 29,441 were in Great Britain, 2,447 in Scandinavia, 299 in Switzerland and Italy, 326 in the French mission, 56 in the German mission, 40 on the island of Malta and 18 at Gibraltar.
