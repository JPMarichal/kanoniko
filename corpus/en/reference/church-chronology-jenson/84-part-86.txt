Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

By the Saints in Utah 1889 will be remembered as the year when the question whether or not the Church to which they belonged could be robbed of its property by the government was laid before the supreme tribunal of their country; and also the question whether they could be robbed of civil and political rights as individual citizens, because of their religious belief. Abroad the missionaries met with considerable success, and a few were baptized in Asia Minor and Palestine. In New Zealand large numbers of natives embraced the gospel, and the progress in Europe is shown by the fact that, during the year, 321 were baptized in Great Britain, 572 in Scandinavia, 219 in Switzerland and Germany, and 77 in Holland. The Book of Mormon was translated into the modern Jewish and Maori languages. In Salt Lake City, Ogden, and other places in Utah the political developments toward the close of the year will always be remembered as being very significant and demonstrative.

Apostles Joseph F. Smith and Franklin S. Richards arrived at Washington, D.C., where they spent several weeks working in the interest of Statehood for Utah, assisted by John W. Young, Charles W. Penrose and others. Franklin S. Richards and Charles W. Penrose visited nearly all the Congressmen in the capitol and had several interviews with Pres. Cleveland; Isador Morris, a non-Mormon, from Salt Lake City, who went to Washington at his own expense, also used his influence with the President and other leading men in behalf of the "Mormon" people.

Among those who opposed Statehood for Utah, before the Committee, were Robert N. Baskin, John R. McBride and E.P. Ferry.

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 161 Saints, namely 116 from Scandinavia, 6 from Holland, 24 from Great Britain, 12 returning Elders and three returning visitors, in charge of A.L. Skanchy. The company arrived in New York in due course of time and in Salt Lake City Nov. 13th.

October 28, 1889 (Monday)

Jens P. Holm was arrested in Salt Lake City, and Edward A. Bagley at Greenwich, Piute Co., for u.c.

October 30, 1889 (Wednesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, William C. Dunbar was refused citizenship because he believed in polygamy. Elder Dunbar had lost his citizen's papers, which he obtained many years before.

October 31, 1889 (Thursday)

The Reform School, at Ogden, was opened.

October 31, 1889 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Richard M. Humphreys, of Salina, was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to 90 days' imprisonment and $300 fine, for u.c.

November 1889

The Endowment House on the Temple Block, Salt Lake City, was taken down. This historic building was erected in 1855.

November 2, 1889 (Saturday)

A Liberal torchlight parade took place in Salt Lake City; 2000 men were in line.

November 2, 1889 (Saturday)

Truman O. Angell was arrested in Salt Lake City, for u.c.

November 2, 1889 (Saturday)

George C. Wood, of Bountiful, Davis Co., having been pardoned by Pres. Harrison, was released from the Penitentiary.

November 4, 1889 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Robert Allen, who promised to obey the law, was fined $100, for u.c.

November 4, 1889 (Monday)

George W. Hancock was arrested in Payson on a charge of murder, committed 32 years ago.

November 6, 1889 (Wednesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, John T.R. Hicks, of South Cottonwood, was sentenced by Judge Anderson to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine; and in the First District Court, Oluf A. Andelin, of Dover, Sanpete Co., was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to four months' imprisonment and $300 fine; both for u.c.

November 6, 1889 (Wednesday)

Samuel G. Spencer, of Pleasant Green, wanted on a charge of u.c., gave himself up and was admitted to bonds.

November 8, 1889 (Friday)

Albert Jones, of Provo, and Michael Vaughan, of Lehi, were discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 8, 1889 (Friday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Judge Henderson rendered a decision which gave the Latter-day Saint meeting house at Woodruff, Rich Co., to the district school trustees.

November 11, 1889 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Judge Anderson refused to admit a number of applicants to citizenship because they were "Mormons."

November 11, 1889 (Monday)

Spring Glen Ward, near Price, Emery Co., was organized; Heber J. Stowell, Bishop. The Saints who resided at Castle Gate were organized into a branch of the Church; Wm T. Lamph, presiding Elder.

November 14, 1889 (Thursday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, the extraordinary proceedings, in which John Moore and other "Mormons" were refused citizenship, on the pretence that they had subscribed to a certain secret and disloyal oath in the Endowment House, was commenced before Judge Thos. J. Anderson. Proceedings were continued day by day until the 25th.

November 14, 1889 (Saturday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 11 Saints, including four returning Elders and one visitor, in charge of Richard Morse.

November 17, 1889 (Sunday)

Patriarch Gardner Snow died in Manti.

November 20, 1889 (Wednesday)

Charles W. Penrose, who had testified as a witness in the naturalization case before Judge Anderson in the Third District Court, was committed to the Penitentiary for refusing to answer an impertinent question with reference to his family affairs.

November 25, 1889 (Monday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Goudy Hogan, of Richmond, Cache Co., was sentenced by Judge Henderson to 30 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

November 25, 1889 (Monday)

Andrew Stratford and Knud Emmertsen were discharged from the Penitentiary. On the same day Charles W. Penrose was released from the Penitentiary. The testimony and arguments in the naturalization cases before Judge Anderson having been completed and the case submitted, there was no excuse for his further detention.

November 26, 1889 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Fred. Jensen, of Logan, was sentenced by Judge Henderson to six months' imprisonment and $100 fine, for u.c.

November 28, 1889 (Thursday)

Elder John Lyon died in Salt Lake City.

November 28, 1889 (Thursday)

James Bywater was discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 29, 1889 (Friday)

The second grand parade of the Liberals occurred in Salt Lake City; it was the greatest affair of the kind ever witnessed in Utah.

November 29, 1889 (Friday)

Jesse B. Martin, of Scipio, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 30, 1889 (Saturday)

Judge Anderson, in the Third District Court, rendered a decision to the effect, that "Mormon" aliens could not be admitted to citizenship.

November 30, 1889 (Saturday)

Joseph Clark, of Provo, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 1889

In the Third District Court, Charles L. White, of Salt Lake City, was sentenced by Judge Anderson to six months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 1889

Thos. Griffin was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 2, 1889 (Monday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Sidney B. Kent, of Lewiston, Cache Co., was sentenced by Judge Henderson to two months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 3, 1889 (Tuesday)

John Jacobs was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 5, 1889 (Thursday)

The second grand Liberal rally in the season's municipal campaign was held in the Opera House, Salt Lake City.

December 6, 1889 (Friday)

Elder David Williams died at Price, Emery Co.

December 7, 1889 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Charles Frank, of Logan, was sentenced by Judge Henderson to one year's imprisonment, for adultery, and Bishop Anthon L. Skanchy, of Logan, to four months and $100 fine, for u.c.

December 7, 1889 (Saturday)

Peter Ahlstrom, Niels Andersen and Frank, W. Young were discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 8, 1889 (Sunday)

George Curtis was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 9, 1889 (Monday)

The sale of city lots by auction was commenced in Salt Lake City.

December 9, 1889 (Monday)

On this and the following day the case of Samuel D. Davis, who had been sentenced by an Idaho court for voting at an election, being a "Mormon," was argued in the U.S. Supreme Court.

December 9, 1889 (Monday)

Elder Benjamin Franklin Taylor died at Levan, Juab Co.

December 10, 1889 (Tuesday)

In the Second District Court, at Beaver, Christina L. Christensen, of Teasdale, was sentenced to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine, for u.c.

December 10, 1889 (Tuesday)

The Idaho test oath case was argued before the U.S. Supreme Court.

December 11, 1889 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Hyrum Petersen was sentenced to six months' imprisonment and $100 fine, for alleged fornication.

December 12, 1889 (Thursday)

Joseph E. Taylor was arrested in Salt Lake City, for u.c.

December 12, 1889 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, John Christophersen, of Richmond, was sentenced by Judge Henderson to three months' imprisonment for breaking the Edmunds law.

December 13, 1889 (Friday)

John Groves, of Sugar House Ward, was arrested for u.c.

December 13, 1889 (Friday)

Benjamin Barney was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 14, 1889 (Saturday)

In the Second District Court, at Beaver, William Robinson, of Beaver, was sentenced to one year's imprisonment, for alleged adultery, and Thomas J. Jones, of Cedar City, to six months and $300 fine, for u.c. Hans Thurgesen, of Koosharem, was sentenced to $50 fine, and Joseph P. Barton, of Paragoonah, to $300 fine, both for u.c. Not being able to pay, they were sent to the Penitentiary. Edward A. Bagley, who promised to obey the law, was fined $100, for u.c.

December 14, 1889 (Saturday)

John Durrant, of American Fork, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 16, 1889 (Monday)

Mayor Francis Armstrong and other municipal officials were arrested, in Salt Lake City, on a trumped-up charge of misappropriation of public funds and conspiracy. It was done for political effect.

December 17, 1889 (Tuesday)

Arthur L. Thomas was confirmed as governor, and Elijah Sells as secretary of Utah, by the U.S. Senate.

December 18, 1889 (Wednesday)

Judge Zane denied the People's Party mandamus against the Salt Lake City registrars, some of whom were charged with crooked work in their official capacity, calculated to harm the People's Party and favor the Liberals at the approaching election.

December 18, 1889 (Wednesday)

Elder Henry G. Bywater died in Salt Lake City.

December 18, 1889 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, William Gee, of Dover, Sanpete Co., was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to six months' imprisonment and costs.

December 18, 1889 (Wednesday)

Jens Jorgensen, of Redmond, Sevier Co., was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 19, 1889 (Thursday)

The Utah Commission sustained the registrars in their discrimination against "Mormon voters" and adjourned.

December 20, 1889 (Friday)

In the Second District Court, at Beaver, Joseph W. McAllister was sentenced to six months' imprisonment and $300 fine, for u.c.

December 23, 1889 (Monday)

This day was observed as a day of fasting and prayer by the Latter-day Saints generally, agreeable to a circular issued by the First Presidency a few days before.

December 23, 1889 (Monday)

Bishop Joseph S. Black and Stephen H. Allred were discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 24, 1889 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Jens P. Jensen, of Logan, was sentenced by Judge Henderson to 18 months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery, and Anton A. Janson, of Brigham City, to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 25, 1889 (Wednesday)

Goudy Hogan, of Richmond, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 26, 1889 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Andrew Madsen, of Brigham City, was sentenced by Judge Henderson to eight months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

December 26, 1889 (Thursday)

The Liberals raised a 110 foot flag pole and had a grand rally in Salt Lake City.

December 27, 1889 (Friday)

James Leatham was arrested in Salt Lake City, for u.c.

December 28, 1889 (Saturday)

William Hutchings, of Lehi, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 29, 1889 (Sunday)

Elder Francis Cope died in Salt Lake City.

December 30, 1889 (Monday)

James P. Park, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Fairfield, Utah Co.

December 30, 1889 (Monday)

Jens L. Jensen, of Central, Sevier Co., was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 31, 1889 (Tuesday)

Joseph L. Jolly, of Moroni, was discharged from the Penitentiary.
