Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

President Wilford Woodruff died, and the First Presidency of the Church was reorganized; Lorenzo Snow, President. Three new Stakes of Zion were organized.

January 1, 1898 (Saturday)

The Swiss and German Mission was divided into two, namely: the Swiss Mission, with H.E. Bowman as president, and headquarters in Berne, Switzerland; and the German Mission, under the presidency of Peter Loutensock, with headquarters at Hamburg.

January 3, 1898 (Monday)

John Clark assumed the mayorship of Salt Lake City.

January 3, 1898 (Monday)

A contract was closed at Los Angeles, Cal., for the erection of a beet sugar factory at Ogden, Utah, to be completed in time to handle the beet crop of 1898.

January 4, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder Geo. Alfred Alder died in the Second Ward, Salt Lake City.

January 5, 1898 (Wednesday)

Ex-Gov. Arthur L. Thomas was appointed postmaster of Salt Lake City, and Geo. A. Smith receiver of the land office.

January 8, 1898 (Saturday)

Elder Joseph Crook died at Payson, Utah Co.

January 8, 1898 (Saturday)

A convention of the presidency and conference presidents of the British mission was commenced at Birmingham, England. It was continued four days. Several matters of interest in connection with missionary labors were considered, and measures were adopted to bring about a greater uniformity of method in conducting the spiritual and financial affairs of the mission.

January 9, 1898 (Sunday)

Wm. Smith, an old and faithful Elder in the Church, died at Providence, Cache Co.

January 9, 1898 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Omaha, Neb.; L.S. Mecham, president.

January 9, 1898 (Sunday)

Congressman William H. King, of Utah, arrived in Tampa, Fla., from his trip of investigation in Cuba. He found the condition of the reconcentradoes most terrible, many of them naked and emaciated and dying in the streets.

January 9, 1898 (Sunday)

January 9, 1898 (Sunday)

Madam Mountford, on her way from California to her home in Jerusalem, Palestine, addressed the congregation in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

January 10, 1898 (Monday)

Elder John Edwards, one of Utah's early settlers, died at Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co.

January 14, 1898 (Friday)

At an Elders' council meeting, held at Anaconda, Mont., Pres. F.S. Bramwell presiding, the Montana mission, recently established, was divided into four conferences, named, respectively, Anaconda, Butte, Missoula, and Sheridan.

January 15, 1898 (Saturday)

Col. Miguel Ahumada, governor of Chihuahua, and other prominent officials, visited Colonia Juarez, Mexico.

January 16, 1898 (Sunday)

Dr. J.H. Reiner, a prominent Roman Catholic minister, addressed the congregation in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City. He was followed by Elder Chas. W. Penrose and Pres. Wilford Woodruff.

January 19, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder Geo. A. Wadsworth died at Panaca, Nev.

January 21, 1898 (Friday)

General Nathan Kimball died at Ogden, Weber Co.

January 24, 1898 (Monday)

A grand celebration, in honor of the discovery of gold in California, fifty years before, was held in San Francisco, Cal. Among the specially invited guests, in attendance, were Elders Jas. S. Brown, of Salt Lake City, Henry W. Bigler, of St. George, Azariah Smith, of Manti, and Wm. Johnston, who, as members of the Mormon Battalion, were all present when Mr. Marshall first discovered the precious metal at Sutter's Mill, in 1848.

January 25, 1898 (Tuesday)

Ann Dilworth Bringhurst, a Utah pioneer woman, died at Springville, Utah Co.

January 26, 1898 (Wednesday)

A remarkably cold wave prevailed throughout Utah. At St. George, the coldest weather ever known there was experienced.

January 27, 1898 (Thursday)

The 115th quorum of Seventy was organized at North Jordan, Salt Lake Co., Utah, by Seymore B. Young; Hyrum Bennion, Geo. M. Spencer, Wm. O. Newbold, Henry Harker and Louis Bringhurst, presidents.

January 28, 1898 (Friday)

Jarvis Johnson, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Beaver Dams, Box Elder Co., Utah.

January 29, 1898 (Saturday)

Andrew Kimball succeeded Christopher Layton as president of the St. Joseph Stake of Zion, Ariz.

January 30, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder Goudy Hogan died at Richmond, Cache Co.

February 1898

Wm. R. Kingsford, one of Ogden's early settlers, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

February 3, 1898 (Thursday)

Zacharias W. Derrick, a president of the second quorum of Seventy died in the Twelfth Ward, Salt Lake City.

February 5, 1898 (Saturday)

On this and the following day, the first general conference of the Eastern States mission (since the reopening of the mission) was held in Brooklyn, N.Y. Apostles Francis M. Lyman and Matthias F. Cowley were present. Elder Alonzo P. Kesler, president of the Eastern States mission, presided.

February 5, 1898 (Saturday)

The First Presidency published in the Deseret Evening News a letter vindicating the course of Col. Isaac Trumbo.

February 6, 1898 (Sunday)

At the conference of the Eastern States mission, Apostle Francis M. Lyman and Congressman Wm. H. King addressed the congregation.

February 7, 1898 (Monday)

Caleb Robinson Barratt, formerly postmaster of Salt Lake City, died here.

February 7, 1898 (Monday)

Elder Robert R. Anderson was appointed State bank examiner for Utah.

February 12, 1898 (Saturday)

The Salt Lake Tribune published a statement, showing the bonded indebtedness of the State of Utah to be $900,000; that of Salt Lake County, $470,000; Salt Lake city, $2,548,000; Board of Education, Salt Lake City, $825,000; total, $4,743,00, over 70 per cent of which was owing by Salt Lake City and its Board of Education. When the Liberal Party took possession of the city in 1890 the municipality had about $500,000 on hand, mostly in cash, besides other available means.

February 15, 1898 (Tuesday)

The news of the destruction of the battleship Maine in Havana harbor, Cuba, this day, caused great excitement in Salt Lake City and the State. The vessel was entirely destroyed and about 260 of her officers and crew were killed. The explosion shook the whole city of Havana.

February 18, 1898 (Friday)

The Public Library in the Joint City and County Building, Salt Lake City, was formally opened and dedicated.

February 21, 1898 (Monday)

Patriarch John L. Smith, an aged Church veteran, and a cousin of the Prophet Joseph Smith, died at St. George, Washington Co.

February 23, 1898 (Wednesday)

Agnes Macdonald, wife of Alexander F. Macdonald, was murdered by Mexicans at Colonia Garcia, Chihuahua, Mexico.

February 27, 1898 (Sunday)

A new Latter-day Saint meeting house was dedicated in the Golansville branch, Caroline Co., Va.

February 28, 1898 (Monday)

James T. Little, president of the Deseret Savings Bank, died in Salt Lake City.

February 28, 1898 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court handed down a decision, affirming the constitutionality of Utah's eight-hour law.

March 1898

The name of the Indian Territory mission was changed by the First Presidency to the Southwestern States mission, and was to include the States of Kansas, Arkansas and Texas.

March 2, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder John Borrowman, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Nephi Juab Co.

March 4, 1898 (Friday)

David Jenkins, the first surveyor of Ogden and a Weber County pioneer, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

March 6, 1898 (Sunday)

The Arcadia branch, Fremont Co., Idaho, was organized as the Ora Ward; M. Joseph Kerr, Bishop.

March 6, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder Charles Hayes, an early convert to "Mormonism" from the "United Brethren," in Herefordshire, England, died at Bountiful, Davis Co.

March 7, 1898 (Monday)

Zaccheus Cheney, a member of the Mormon Battalion and one of the pioneers of California, died at Centerville, Davis Co.

March 8, 1898 (Tuesday)

Several Elders, including Hirani Whaanga, the Maori chief (70 years old), who had been a resident of Utah since 1894, left Salt Lake City, on missions to New Zealand; Elder Ezra T. Stevenson in charge of the company.

March 8, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder Joseph Hyrum Jenson, of Union, Salt Lake Co., Utah, died at Gothenburg, Sweden, where he labored as a missionary.

March 17, 1898 (Thursday)

Bishop Elias Morris died in Salt Lake City, from the effects of an accident, sustained by him the previous Monday (March 14th).

March 23, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder George Lake, one of the first Latter-day Saint settlers in Mexico, and a Church veteran, died at Colonia Dublan, Chihuahua, Mexico.

March 27, 1898 (Sunday)

Henry W. Brizzee, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Mesa, Ariz. Elder Walter Henry Huish died at Payson, Utah Co.

April 3, 1898 (Sunday)

The Fifteenth Ward, Salt Lake City, was divided, and all that part of it lying west of Sixth West Street was organized as the Twenty-fourth Ward; Edward T. Ashton, Bishop.

April 6, 1898 (Wednesday)

The sixty-eight annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City, continuing four days. When the general authorities were voted for, on the 10th, Andrew Jenson was sustained as an assistant Church Historian.

April 7, 1898 (Thursday)

Zion's Maori association was reorganized in consequence of the death of Wm. Paxman, the former president. Benjamin Goddard was chosen president; Wm. Gardner vice-president, and Heber S. Cutler secretary of the new organization.

April 7, 1898 (Thursday)

Samuel Allen Wilcox, sen., an aged Church veteran, died at Cedar Fork, Utah Co.

April 7, 1898 (Thursday)

Elders Brigham F. Duffin and Thos. H. Chambers arrived at Kingston, Caldwell Co., Mo., to open a mission in Caldwell and Daviess Counties. The next day (April 8), they held their first meeting at Kingston in the Josephite meeting house. This is believed to be the first public meeting held in that part of the State by any Elders in the Church since the Saints were expelled from Missouri in 1838.

April 9, 1898 (Saturday)

The foundation of the sugar factory, near Ogden, Weber Co., was completed.

April 10, 1898 (Sunday)

At the annual conference meeting of the Deseret Sunday School Union, held in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, Elder Geo. Goddard, assistant general Sunday School superintendent, was tendered an ovation, in appreciation of his long and faithful services in the Sunday School cause.

April 10, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder Thomas Alfred Jeffery died at Loa, Wayne Co.

April 17, 1898 (Sunday)

Elders Brigham F. Duffin and Thos. H. Chambers held a meeting in a school house, near Lock Springs, Daviess Co., Mo.

April 17, 1898 (Sunday)

The new Utah Stake tabernacle at Provo, Utah Co., was dedicated, Pres. Geo. Q. Cannon offering the dedicatory prayer.

April 20, 1898 (Wednesday)

The 24th Infantry (colored troops, who had been stationed at Fort Douglas) left Salt Lake City for Cuba, to participate in the war with Spain.

April 21, 1898 (Thursday)

Sisters Lucy Jane Brimhall and Inez Knight, the two first Latter-day Saint lady missionaries sent from Zion to Great Britain, arrived in Liverpool, England.

April 22, 1898 (Friday)

Elder Thomas Wm. Brewerton died at Willard City, Box Elder Co.

April 23, 1898 (Saturday)

The Sixteenth U.S. Infantry passed through Salt Lake City en route for Cuba, and was accorded a rousing reception.

April 24, 1898 (Sunday)

Sermons on the war were delivered in the Tabernacle and in all the churches in Salt Lake City.

April 26, 1898 (Tuesday)

In response to the call of the Federal government, Gov. Heber M. Wells issued a proclamation, calling for volunteers to serve in the army of the United States. Five hundred men were called for from Utah.

April 27, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder Wm. Robinson died at Fayette, Sanpete Co.

April 27, 1898 (Thursday)

The First Presidency of the Church, in a letter addressed to Gov. Heber M. Wells, encouraged the young men of the Church to respond to the call for volunteers for the army.

May 1898

Elder LeRoy C. Snow was arrested and imprisoned at Munich, Bavaria, for preaching the gospel. After spending some time in prison, he was banished from the country.

May 1, 1898 (Sunday)

Governor Heber M. Wells telegraphed the War Department that Utah's quota of volunteers was filled, and that the men would be at Fort Douglas, ready to be mustered into the service, on May 5th.

May 2, 1898 (Monday)

The Utah volunteers pitched camp at Fort Douglas.

May 4, 1898 (Wednesday)

Govern Wells appointed Richard W. Young, grandson of President Brigham Young, and Frank A. Grant captains of Companies A and B, respectively, Utah (volunteer) Light Artillery.

May 7, 1898 (Saturday)

The news of the destruction of the Spanish fleet at Manila, Philippine Islands, by the American fleet under Commodore George Dewey, May 1st, having reached Salt Lake City, an enthusiastic celebration in honor of the event was held.

May 8, 1898 (Sunday)

Governor Wells named Joseph E. Caine for captain of the troop of cavalry included in Utah's quota of volunteers. Other officers were also appointed.

May 9, 1898 (Monday)

The two batteries (A and B) of Utah's volunteer artillery were mustered into service at Fort Douglas.

May 9, 1898 (Monday)

The first baptisms by divine authority in this dispensation at Jerusalem, Palestine, took place in Mary's Well, where Elder Ferdinand F. Hintze baptized Geo. Vezerian and Geo. Nadgarian, two Armenians from Asia Minor. They were both confirmed the same day, the first-named by Apostle Anthon H. Lund and the latter by Elder Hintze.

May 11, 1898 (Wednesday)

Mary E. Fenton Young, wife of Apostle Brigham Young, died in Salt Lake City.

May 15, 1898 (Sunday)

A troop of volunteer cavalry, subsequently known as Troop I, of the Second United States Cavalry (Torrey's Rough Riders), was organized in Salt Lake City, and left for Cheyenne, Wyo.

May 16, 1898 (Monday)

George Gwilliam Bywater, a prominent and talented Elder in the Church, died suddenly from apoplexy in Salt Lake City.

May 17, 1898 (Tuesday)

John Q. Cannon was elected captain of Rough Riders, with J. Wash Young and Andrew J. Burt first and second lieutenants respectively.

May 18, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder Theodore Nystrom arrived in Paris, France, as a Latter-day Saint missionary, having been called by Pres. Bowman, of the Swiss mission, to introduce the gospel in the French capital.

May 20, 1898 (Friday)

Batteries A and B, Utah volunteers, under Captains Richard W. Young and Frank A. Grant, left Salt Lake City for San Francisco, Cal., on their way to the Philippine Islands.

May 21, 1898 (Saturday)

Charles Shumway, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Shumway, near Snowflake, Ariz.

May 24, 1898 (Tuesday)

Joseph Egbert, a Pioneer of 1847, died at Ogden, and Wm. Bond died at Henefer, Summit Co.

May 24, 1898 (Tuesday)

The Utah volunteer cavalry left Salt Lake City for San Francisco.

May 30, 1898 (Monday)

Ole Christian Jensen, of Mayfield, Sanpete Co., Utah, died at Randers, Denmark, where he labored as a missionary.

May 31, 1898 (Tuesday)

Willard Young, son of Pres. Brigham Young, was nominated, by Pres. Wm. McKinley, colonel of the Second regiment of U.S. volunteer engineers.

June 1, 1898 (Wednesday)

Hon. Wm. H. King delivered an eloquent address in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, on Cuba and the War.

June 3, 1898 (Friday)

Elder Hyrum Maughan, of Weston, Oneida Co., Idaho, died at Anderson, Madison Co., Ind., where he labored as a missionary.

June 5, 1898 (Sunday)

At a conference held on this and the following day at Almy, Wyo., Woodruff Stake of Zion was organized by Apostles John Henry Smith, Heber J. Grant, Matthias F. Cowley and Abraham O. Woodruff; John M. Baxter, president; Byron Sessions and Charles Kingston counselors. The new Stake included the Wards of Evanston, Almy Rock Springs and Bridger, which had formerly belonged to the Summit Stake, and Randolph, Woodruff, Argyle and Diamondville, which formerly constituted a part of Bear Lake Stake.

June 5, 1898 (Sunday)

Geo. P. Canova, a local Elder and president of the Sanderson branch of the Church, was assassinated at Juncture, Florida; Brother T. Hill, his companion, escaped.

June 6, 1898 (Monday)

The International Mining Congress was opened in the Arsenal Hall, Salt Lake City, continuing several days.

June 14, 1898 (Tuesday)

James Ballard, alias "Schooner Jim," died in Salt Lake City.

June 15, 1898 (Wednesday)

The second Manila expedition, consisting of four large transports, carrying nearly 3,500 soldiers, sailed from San Francisco, Cal., for the Philippine Islands. Battery A, of the Utah Artillery (120 men), together with two companies of the regular U.S. army, sailed on the steamer Colon; one-half of Battery B, of Utah volunteers, sailed on the steamer China, and the other half on the steamer Zealandia.

June 19, 1898 (Sunday)

Park City, Summit Co., was partly destroyed by fire. Nearly all the principal stores and many private residences were reduced to ashes. The loss was estimated at about $1,000,000. Among the buildings consumed was a Latter-day Saint meeting house, which has just been completed at a cost of about $5,000. This was considered the most disastrous fire that ever happened in Utah.

June 20, 1898 (Monday)

The enlistment of volunteer engineers for U.S. service was commenced in Utah.

June 21, 1898 (Tuesday)

Horace Burr Owens, a Church veteran, died at Woodruff, Navajo Co., Ariz.

June 21, 1898 (Tuesday)

Bishop Samuel Carter died at Porterville, Morgan Co.

June 21, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder James Wareham, a Church veteran and an early settler of Manti, Sanpete Co., died there.

June 23, 1898 (Thursday)

A terrific storm did considerable damage to property at Bunkerville and Mesquit, Lincoln Co., Nev.

June 25, 1898 (Saturday)

Elder Andrew Hendriksen died at Levan, Juab Co.

July 1898

The fulness of the gospel was first introduced in North Dakota by Elders David J. Hammon, Charles A. Haacke, Elias Nielsen and Lorenzo Day.

July 1898

Elder James E. Talmage delivered his illustrated lectures on Utah and its People in some of the principal cities of Great Britain.

July 1, 1898 (Friday)

Elder Ben. E. Rich succeeded Elder Elias S. Kimball in the presidency of the Southern States mission. Elder Kimball had been appointed chaplain in the Second regiment of U.S. volunteer engineers.

July 3, 1898 (Sunday)

News reached Utah that the Spanish Cape de Verde fleet, under command of Admiral Cervera, had been entirely destroyed by the American squadron, off the harbor of Santiago de Cuba. News of the battle of San Juan hill, before Santiago de Cuba, brought grief to the soldiers' families at Camp Douglas, as many of the Twenty-fourth infantry were killed in the battle.

July 4, 1898 (Monday)

Elder Thos. Cox, sen., died at Castle Dale, Emery Co.

July 6, 1898 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County, in being treated to their regular annual excursion under the direction of the Old Folks Committee, spent a pleasant day at the Lagoon, Farmington, Davis Co.

July 6, 1898 (Wednesday)

The first of these popular Old Folks excursions took place May 14, 1875, through the special kindness of John W. Young, who arranged a free ride to the Great Salt Lake for the old people of Salt Lake County. The original committee of arrangements consisted of Bishop Edward Hunter, George Goddard and Charles R. Savage. In 1879, Wm. Eddington, Wm. Naylor, John Kirkham and Wm. L. Binder were added to the committee, which was further augmented in 1883 by the addition of Andrew Jenson, in 1885 by Nelson A. Empey, and in 1896 by Brigham S. Young. After the death of Bishop Edward Hunter, in 1883, Presiding Bishop Wm. B. Preston has acted as chairman of the committee.

July 6, 1898 (Wednesday)

The first International Mining Congress was opened in Salt Lake City.

July 7, 1898 (Thursday)

Gov. Wells announced the appointment of Frank W. Jennings as captain of Battery C, Utah volunteer artillery.

July 10, 1898 (Sunday)

The Utah U.S. volunteer engineers left Salt Lake City for San Francisco.

July 11, 1898 (Monday)

Hadley D. Johnson, a prominent Utah politician and editor, died in Salt Lake City.

July 13, 1898 (Wednesday)

Sister Melissa Lott Smith Willis, a widow of the Prophet Joseph Smith, died at Lehi, Utah Co.

July 13, 1898 (Wednesday)

The troop of Utah cavalry volunteers, which had been stationed at San Francisco, were relieved from duty with the Philippine expedition forces and ordered to perform home duty.

July 14, 1898 (Thursday)

Battery C (Utah volunteers) were sworn into U.S. service.

July 17, 1898 (Sunday)

The second Philippine expedition, which included the Utah batteries, arrived at Cavite, near Manila, Philippine Islands.

July 19, 1898 (Tuesday)

Four young ladies, Lucy, Emma and Susie Keele, of Payson, Utah Co., and Miss Bauer, of Homansville, Juab Co., were accidentally drowned in the Utah Lake, near Benjamin, Utah Co.

July 21, 1898 (Thursday)

Elder August L. Hedberg, a late resident of Utah, died at Los Angeles, Cal., where he acted as a counselor in the presidency of the branch.

July 21, 1898 (Thursday)

In a council of the First Presidency and Apostles, held in Salt Lake City, it was decided to change the name of Bannock Stake, Idaho, to Fremont.

July 23, 1898 (Saturday)

Elder Brigham F. Duffin and Leonidus S. Mecham, arrived at Watertown, South Dakota, having been called by Louis A. Kelsch, pres. of the Northern States mission, to open a missionary field in that State. On the night of July 27th, they held their first open air meeting, near the post office, at Watertown.

July 24, 1898 (Sunday)

Memorial services were held in the different settlements of the Saints and collections taken up for a monument, to be raised in honor of the American sailors who lost their lives, Feb. 15, 1898, by the explosion of the Maine, in the harbor of Havana, Cuba.

July 24, 1898 (Sunday)

Pioneer Square, Salt Lake City, was dedicated and set apart as a public park (Pioneer Park). Pres. Geo. Q. Cannon offered the dedicatory prayer; and addresses were made by Mayor John Clark, Pres. Wilford Woodruff, Congressman Wm. H. King and others.

July 24, 1898 (Sunday)

At a quarterly conference of the Oneida Stake, held in Gentile Valley, Idaho, the settlements of the Saints in that valley and neighborhood were detached from Oneida Stake and organized as the Bannock Stake of Zion, by Apostles Heber J. Grant, Marriner W. Merrill and Matthias F. Cowley; Lewis S. Pond, president; Denmark Jenson and Clarence Eldredge, counselors.

July 25, 1898 (Monday)

Wm. Tunbridge, a prominent citizen of Levan, Juab Co., was shot and killed by Andrew Hendriksen, who was supposed to be insane.

July 27, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder Alexander Hood Hill died at Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

July 31, 1898 (Sunday)

Elisha Hildebrand Davis, a prominent Elder and Church veteran, died at Lehi, Utah Co.

July 31, 1898 (Sunday)

The Utah artillery took a very active and brave part in the defence of the American position before Manila, Philippine Islands.

July 31, 1898 (Sunday)

Battery C, of the Utah Volunteer artillery (about one Hundred men), under command of Capt. Frank W. Jennings, left Salt Lake City, for California. This battery was Utah's contribution under the second government call for troops.

August 6, 1898 (Saturday)

Lyman Curtis, a member of Zion's Camp and a Utah Pioneer of 1847, died at Salem, Utah Co.

August 7, 1898 (Sunday)

A number of settlements, formerly belonging to the Oneida and Malad Stakes, were organized by Apostles Heber J. Grant and Matthias F. Cowley, as the Pocatello Stake of Zion. Wm. C. Parkinson (Bishop of Preston, Idaho) was appointed president of the new Stake.

August 7, 1898 (Sunday)

Patriarch Christopher Layton, late president of the St. Joseph Stake, Ariz., and a prominent Elder in the Church, died at Kaysville, Davis Co.

August 7, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder Theodore Nystrom, who had been appointed to re-open a mission in France, held his first and only meeting in Paris. Soon afterwards he left the city, without making any converts.

August 12, 1898 (Friday)

Elder Christian Jensen, father of Historian Andrew Jenson, died at Richfield, Sevier Co.

August 12, 1898 (Friday)

The American flag was raised over Hawaii, at Honolulu.

August 12, 1898 (Friday)

The peace protocol, terminating the war between the United States and Spain, was signed at Washington, D.C.

August 13, 1898 (Saturday)

Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Geo. Q. Cannon left Salt Lake City on a visit to California.

August 13, 1898 (Saturday)

Elder Cadwallader Owens died at Iona, Bingham Co., Idaho.

August 13, 1898 (Saturday)

The Utah cavalry, which had been stationed at San Francisco, Cal., left that city for Yosemite and Sequoia Parks.

August 13, 1898 (Saturday)

In the capture of Manila, the capital of the Philippine Islands, by the American troops, the Utah volunteers rendered effectual service.

August 15, 1898 (Monday)

Trooper Albert W. Luff Naisbitt died at San Francisco, Cal.

August 16, 1898 (Tuesday)

A contract between the Oregon Short Line Railroad and the promoters of a railroad from Milford to the State line was signed in Salt Lake City. The signers were officials of the Oregon Short Line, on one side, and A.W. McCune, David Eccles, Charles W. Nibley and others, on the other side.

August 19, 1898 (Friday)

Patriarch David M. Stewart, a prominent Elder in the Church, and, in his younger days, a successful missionary, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

August 26, 1898 (Friday)

In an altercation between natives and United States soldiers at Cavite, near Manila, Philippine Islands, Geo. H. Hudson, of Baker City, Ore., was killed and Wm. Q. Anderson, of Logan, Utah, severely wounded. Both men belonged to Battery B, Utah volunteers.

August 27, 1898 (Saturday)

President McKinley promoted Captains Richard W. Young and Frank A. Grant to be brevet-majors of volunteers.

August 30, 1898 (Tuesday)

The sessions of the Utah Presbytery of the Presbyterian Church closed at Manti, Sanpete Co. Before adjourning, resolutions were passed, replete with "garbled statements and absolute falsehoods concerning the 'Mormon' Church."

September 2, 1898 (Friday)

Pres. Wilford Woodruff died at the house of Col. Isaac Trumbo, San Francisco, Cal., after an illness of only a few hours.

September 4, 1898 (Monday)

The train bearing the remains of Pres. Wilford Woodruff arrived in Salt Lake City, over the Rio Grande Western Ry.

September 8, 1898 (Thursday)

The funeral services over the remains of Pres. Wilford Woodruff were held in Salt Lake City. All of the Twelve Apostles were present and addresses were made by Joseph F. Smith, Lorenzo Snow, George Q. Cannon and Franklin D. Richards.

September 9, 1898 (Friday)

The Republican State convention nominated Alma Eldredge for Congress and Charles S. Zane for Supreme Judge in Utah.

September 10, 1898 (Saturday)

Stephen H. Goddard, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at San Bernardino, Cal.

September 13, 1898 (Tuesday)

At a special meeting of the Apostles held in Salt Lake City, at which Lorenzo Snow, Franklin D. Richards, Geo. Q. Cannon, Joseph F. Smith, Brigham Young, Francis M. Lyman, John H. Smith, Geo. Teasdale, Heber J. Grant, John W. Taylor, Marriner W. Merrill, Anthon H. Lund, Matthias F. Cowley and Abraham O. Woodruff were present, Lorenzo Snow was nominated and sustained as President of the Church. President Snow then selected, with the approval of the council, Geo. Q. Cannon as his first and Joseph F. Smith as his second Counselor. Pres. Snow was also sustained as Trustee-in-Trust for the Church, and Franklin D. Richards as President of the Twelve Apostles. All the proceedings were unanimous.

September 14, 1898 (Wednesday)

The State Democratic convention held in Salt Lake City nominated Brigham H. Roberts for Congress and Robert N. Baskin for Supreme Judge.

September 17, 1898 (Saturday)

Elder Andreas Peterson, of Logan, Cache Co., Utah, succeeded Elder George Christensen as president of the Scandinavian Mission.

September 17, 1898 (Saturday)

This mission was founded in 1850 by Apostle Erastus Snow, who was its first president. He was succeeded by the following Elders: John E. Forsgren, March 4, 1852; Willard Snow, Dec. 20, 1852; John Van Cott, Aug., 1853; Hector C. Haight, Jan. 1, 1856; Carl Widerborg, Feb. 1, 1858; John Van Cott (second term), Jan. 1, 1860; Jesse N. Smith, May, 1862; Samuel L. Sprague pro tem, April 13, 1864; Carl Widerborg (second term), Aug. 1, 1864; Jesse N. Smith (second term), Sept., 1868; William W. Cluff, July 15, 1870; Canute Peterson, June 23, 1871; Christen G. Larsen, June 27, 1873; Nils C. Flygare, June 25, 1875; Ola N. Liljenquist, June 22, 1876; August W. Carlson pro tem, Nov., 1877; Nils C. Flygare (second term), Jan., 1878; Niels Wilhelmsen, Aug. 30, 1879; Andrew Jenson pro tem, Aug. 1, 1881; Anthon H. Lund, April 4, 1884; Nils C. Flygare (third term) Oct. 19, 1885; Christian D. Fjeldsted, (second term) October, 1888; Edward H. Anderson, September 29, 1890; Joseph Christiansen, September, 1892; Carl A. Carlquist, May 1893; Peter Sundwall, April, 1894; Christian N. Lund, June 11, 1896; and George Christensen, May 19, 1898.

September 25, 1898 (Sunday)

The hotel and buildings at Beck's Hot Springs, near Salt Lake City, were destroyed by fire.

September 25, 1898 (Sunday)

The first Latter-day Saint Sunday School in Providence, Rhode Island, was organized by Elder John M. Whitaker; Peter C. Cornia, supt.

September 26, 1898 (Monday)

Elder Joseph T. Ellis died at Spring City, Sanpete Co.

September 30, 1898 (Friday)

The Twenty-fourth Infantry returned to Fort Douglas, from their expedition to Cuba, having done gallant service at the battle of Santiago.

October 1898

The sugar factory, near Ogden, commenced running.

October 2, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder Wallace Damron died at Thatcher, Graham Co., Ariz.

October 4, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder Albert Spencer died at Taylorsville, Salt Lake Co.

October 5, 1898 (Wednesday)

Elder James C. Olsen died at Salina, Sevier Co.

October 6, 1898 (Thursday)

The 69th semi-annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City, continuing four days.

October 9, 1898 (Sunday)

At the afternoon session of the general conference of the Church, Lorenzo Snow was sustained as Prophet, Seer, and Revelator, and President of the Church, and Geo. Q. Cannon as his first and Joseph F. Smith as his second Counselor. Rudger Clawson was chosen to fill the vacancy which had occurred in the Council of the Twelve Apostles by the reorganization of the First Presidency.

October 10, 1898 (Monday)

Rudger Clawson was ordained an Apostle, in Salt Lake City, by Pres. Lorenzo Snow.

October 14, 1898 (Friday)

The new Stake Tabernacle, which was nearing completion, at Richfield, Sevier Co., was destroyed by an incendiary fire,; loss, about $30,000.

October 15, 1898 (Saturday)

Joseph Cordon Kingsbury, an old and faithful Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

October 17, 1898 (Monday)

The Richfield Advocate office was burned to the ground.

October 17, 1898 (Monday)

The Presbyterian synod of Utah, at its closing session in Ogden, passed resolutions declaring that polygamy was still practiced by the "Mormons" in Utah.

October 17, 1898 (Monday)

William Andrew Parker (a native of Utah), of Troop H, Torrey's Rough Riders, died near Jacksonville, Florida.

October 18, 1898 (Tuesday)

Presidents Lorenzo Snow and Joseph F. Smith, Apostle Franklin D. Richards, Gov. Heber M. Wells and others left Salt Lake City, for Omaha, Neb., to visit the Trans-Mississippi Exposition.

October 18, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder Martin Lenzi, a Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

October 18, 1898 (Tuesday)

The American flag was raised over San Juan, and the island of Porto Rico was formally ceded to the United States.

October 20, 1898 (Thursday)

Elder David M. Muir died of pneumonia, at Lochgelly, Scotland, where he labored as a missionary.

October 20, 1898 (Thursday)

This was Utah day at the Trans-Mississippi Exposition at Omaha, Neb. A speech of welcome was made by Wattles, president of the Exposition, and responses were made by Gov. Heber M. Wells and Presidents Lorenzo Snow, Geo. Q. Cannon and Joseph F. Smith.

October 23, 1898 (Sunday)

Troop I. (Utah's Rough Riders) were mustered out of service at Camp Cuba Libre, Panama Park, Jacksonville, Florida.

October 26, 1898 (Wednesday)

Shadrach Ford Driggs, an early settler of Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., died there.

October 30, 1898 (Sunday)

Elders B.F. Duffin and Chas. A. Haacke were taken out of the Methodist Episcopal church at Henry, Coddington Co, S.D. (where they were sleeping during the night), by a mob of five men, led by Quin McMullen. The Elders were followed out of town, threatened with tar and feathers and otherwise abused. The night was a bitterly cold one, and the Elders nearly perished through exposure.

November 8, 1898 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, Brigham H. Roberts (Democrat) was elected as Utah's representative to Congress, and Robert N. Baskin was elected to the Utah Supreme bench.

November 8, 1898 (Tuesday)

Elder Richard Ballantyne, a Church veteran, organizer of the first Sabbath School and a faithful Sunday School worker, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

November 8, 1898 (Tuesday)

The southeast part of Chesterfield Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, was organized as Hatch Ward by Pres. Lewis S. Pond; Peter J. Williams, Bishop.

November 28, 1898 (Monday)

An important Latter-day Saint Sunday School convention was commenced in Salt Lake City. It was continued three days.

December 1, 1898 (Thursday)

Sister Sarah M. Kimball, an old and respected member of the Church, and a faithful Relief Society worker, died in Salt Lake City.

December 3, 1898 (Saturday)

The first number of the Latter-day Saints' Southern Star, a weekly periodical published in the interest of the Church in the Southern States, was issued at Chattanooga, Tenn.

December 6, 1898 (Tuesday)

Twenty-four "ministers of the gospel," mostly residents of Salt Lake City, met and adopted an address, prepared by a committee of the so-called ministerial union, in which they "most earnestly" called upon the people of the United States to join them in a strong protest to Congress against the seating of Brigham H. Roberts, member elect from Utah.

December 8, 1898 (Thursday)

A terrific wind storm swept over the northern part of Utah, doing considerable damage in Weber and Davis counties. In Salt Lake City the greatest velocity the wind reached was 48 miles an hour.

December 8, 1898 (Thursday)

Presidents Rulon S. Wells and Joseph W. McMurrin sailed from Liverpool, England, homeward bound, leaving Elder Platte D. Lyman, with Henry W. Naisbitt as second counselor, in charge of the European mission.

December 10, 1898 (Saturday)

The last number (No. 26, Vol. 57) of the Deseret Weekly was issued.

December 10, 1898 (Saturday)

Col. Willard Young, of Utah, commanding the battalion of engineers at Marinoa, Cuba, unfurled the stars and stripes over the city hall at that place.

December 15, 1898 (Thursday)

Pres. Lorenzo Snow, as Trustee-in-Trust for the Church, announced in the Deseret Evening News that the Church would issue bonds to the amount of $500,000, running for a period of eleven years, and bearing interest at the rate of 6 per cent per annum. This method of raising means was resorted to in order to lighten the burthen of the Church indebtedness.

December 17, 1898 (Saturday)

Pres. Lorenzo Snow, as Trustee-in-Trust, announced in the Deseret Evening News, that that paper, with all its properties and appurtenances, had reverted to the Church, and that on and after Jan. 1, 1889, a complete change in the management would be inaugurated, with Charles W. Penrose as editor and Horace G. Whitney as business manager.

December 18, 1898 (Sunday)

Certificate of election (dated Dec. 10, 1898) was given to Brigham H. Roberts.

December 21, 1898 (Wednesday)

Battery C, Utah volunteers, were mustered out of U.S. service.

December 22, 1898 (Thursday)

Lydia Snow Pierce, wife of Elder Eli H. Pierce and daughter of Pres. Lorenzo Snow, died in Salt Lake City.

December 23, 1898 (Friday)

The ninety-third anniversary of the birth of the Prophet Joseph Smith was celebrated in the Sixteenth Ward meeting house, Salt Lake City.

December 23, 1898 (Friday)

Troop A, Utah cavalry, was mustered out of service at the Presidio, San Francisco, Cal.

December 24, 1898 (Saturday)

The new B.Y. College building at Logan, Cache Co., was dedicated.

December 25, 1898 (Sunday)

Elder John Ritter, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Eden, Weber Co.

December 28, 1898 (Wednesday)

Mary Adaline Snow, wife of Pres. Lorenzo Snow, died at Brigham City.

December 29, 1898 (Thursday)

Pres. Lorenzo Snow, in a telegraphic communication to the New York World, declared officially that polygamous or plural marriages ceased in Utah with the issuance of the manifesto by the late Pres. Wilford Woodruff in 1890, and that the election of Brigham H. Roberts to Congress was an entirely secular affair, with which the Church had nothing to do.

December 30, 1898 (Friday)

Elder Albert Peterson, of Murray, Salt Lake Co., died at Upsala, Sweden, where he labored as a missionary.
