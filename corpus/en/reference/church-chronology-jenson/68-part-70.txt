Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year there was considerable railroad building in Utah. An unsuccessful attempt was made by a company of Saints to settle Arizona Territory. Pres. Geo. A. Smith and party visited Palestine and other countries.

January 1, 1873 (Wednesday)

Elder Stephen Winchester died in Salt Lake City.

January 17, 1873 (Friday)

Professor John Tullidge died in Salt Lake City.

January 23, 1873 (Thursday)

David R. Allen, a prominent citizen, died at Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co.

January 28, 1873 (Tuesday)

Associate Justice C.M. Hawley liberated a number of criminals held by the Box Elder County officers for cattle stealing, at Corinne.

January 31, 1873 (Friday)

The Utah Northern Railway was completed to Logan.

February 1873

A daily anti-Mormon paper, called the New Endowment, was published in Salt Lake City, by W.J. Forbes.

February 6, 1873 (Thursday)

Pres. Geo. A. Smith and party arrived at Alexandria, Egypt. Since leaving Paris the party had visited Lyons, Marseilles, Genoa, Rome, Naples, Corfu and other large cities.

February 21, 1873 (Friday)

Major Wm. Pitt, the famous leader of the Nauvoo brass band, died in Salt Lake City.

February 23, 1873 (Sunday)

Wm. W. Player, a respected veteran of the Church, died in Salt Lake City.

February 25, 1873 (Tuesday)

The Frelinghuysen anti-Mormon bill was passed in the U.S. Senate, but failed to come up before the House.

February 25, 1873 (Tuesday)

The Geo. A. Smith Palestine party arrived at Jerusalem.

February 27, 1873 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young returned to Salt Lake City, from St. George, where he had spent the winter. General Thos. L. Kane and family also returned from a trip to southern Utah.

February 28, 1873 (Friday)

The Palestine party visited the Dead Sea; they returned to Jerusalem on the 1st of March.

March 2, 1873 (Sunday)

The Palestine party held solemn worship on the Mount of Olives. After having visited all the noted places in Jerusalem and vicinity, the party left that city March 5th, and journeyed northward, visiting the ancient sites of Shiloh, Shechem (now Nablous), Samaria, Nazareth, Cana, Tiberias, by the sea of Galilee, Bethsaida, Capernaum, Dan and Cesarea Philippi, at the foot of Mount Hermon, and arrived in Damascus, Syria, March 15th. From that city the journey was continued over the mountains of Lebanon to Beyrout, where they embarked on a steamer for Constantinople, Turkey, arriving there April 1st.

March 5, 1873 (Wednesday)

Elder Wm. C. Staines was set apart for his mission to attend to the emigration of the Saints in New York. He labored efficiently in that business until his death in 1881.

March 6, 1873 (Thursday)

Apostle Erastus Snow and others left Salt Lake City for Europe. They arrived in Liverpool, England, April 1st.

March 8, 1873 (Saturday)

Quite a large number having been called by the authorities of the Church to plant colonies in Arizona, a general meeting was held in the Old Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, where they were instructed by Pres. Brigham Young and others concerning their mission.

March 10, 1873 (Monday)

The mason work was commenced on the St. George Temple.

March 15, 1873 (Saturday)

Hon. Wm. H. Hooper arrived in Utah from Washington, D.C. He had served the Territory faithfully for ten years, as its delegate to Congress.

April 6, 1873 (Sunday)

The 43rd annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City; it was continued for three days. Owing to infirmities incident to old age, Pres. Brigham Young resigned several minor official positions, and chose five additional Counselors, namely Lorenzo Snow, Brigham Young, jun., Albert Carrington, John W. Young, and George Q. Cannon.

April 14, 1873 (Monday)

Ground was broken for the Salt Lake, Sevier Valley and Pioche Railroad (afterwards the Utah and Nevada), in Salt Lake City.

April 20, 1873 (Sunday)

A society for young men's mutual improvement was organized by Apostle Franklin D. Richards and others, at Ogden.

April 21, 1873 (Monday)

Elder Calvin C. Pendleton died at Parowan, Iron Co.

May 3, 1873 (Saturday)

The Wasatch and Jordan Valley Railway was completed to Granite, at the mouth of Little Cottonwood Canyon.

May 3, 1873 (Saturday)

The Alta Daily Independent, a newspaper, was first published at Alta, Little Cottonwood Canyon. It only lived a short time.

May 3, 1873 (Saturday)

On this and the following day an adjourned session of the 43rd annual conference of the Church was held in Salt Lake City.

May 7, 1873 (Wednesday)

John S. Eldredge, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died at Charleston, Wasatch Co., Utah.

May 10, 1873 (Saturday)

Elder James D. McCullough died at Panacca (Nevada).

May 13, 1873 (Tuesday)

James Edwards, a desperado, was killed at Sandy, Utah, after threatening the lives of several citizens.

May 14, 1873 (Wednesday)

The first car-load of coal was shipped from Coalville, over the Summit County Railway.

May 15, 1873 (Thursday)

Apostle Erastus Snow and son (Erastus W.) arrived in Copenhagen, Denmark, on a visit.

May 18, 1873 (Sunday)

Pres. Geo. A. Smith and part of the Palestine party arrived in London, England.

May 23, 1873 (Friday)

James G. Blaine, speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 23, 1873 (Friday)

Cyril Call, an aged veteran, died at Bountiful, Davis Co.

May 25, 1873 (Sunday)

Feramorz Little and daughter, of the Palestine party, returned to Salt Lake City.

June 2, 1873 (Monday)

Lieut. Geo. M. Wheeler, of the U.S. Army, arrived in Ogden, for the purpose of erecting a military observatory at that place.

June 4, 1873 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 246 Saints, in charge of Charles H. Wilcken. The company arrived at New York June 16th, and at Salt Lake City June 26th.

June 7, 1873 (Saturday)

Elder Joseph W. Young died at Harrisburg, Washington Co., Utah.

June 9, 1873 (Monday)

A branch of four miles of the Utah Northern Railway was completed to Corinne, from Brigham City Junction.

June 18, 1873 (Wednesday)

Pres. Geo. A. Smith returned to Salt Lake City, from his trip to Palestine.

June 30, 1873 (Monday)

Salt Lake City was first lighted with gas.

July 2, 1873 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 976 Saints, in charge of David O. Calder. The company arrived at New York July 15th, and at Salt Lake City July 24th.

July 5, 1873 (Saturday)

Zion's Savings' Bank and Trust Company was organized; Brigham Young, president.

July 10, 1873 (Thursday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 283 Saints, in charge of Elijah A. Box. The company landed in New York July 23rd, and at Salt Lake City Aug. 1st.

July 22, 1873 (Tuesday)

The Arizona missionaries reached the Little Colorado river. A company of explorers, which was sent out, brought back a discouraging report of the country, whereby the company became disheartened, and returned home.

July 23, 1873 (Wednesday)

Sylvester H. Earl, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died at St. George, Utah.

July 24, 1873 (Thursday)

Gabriel L. Cotton and his two sons were killed by S.M. Butcher, near the mouth of Bingham Canyon, Salt Lake Co.

July 30, 1873 (Wednesday)

Sever shocks of earthquake were felt at Beaver.

August 1, 1873 (Friday)

The first number of the Provo Daily Times was issued at Provo, Utah Co. The following year it was changed to a tri-weekly publication called the Utah County Times. In 1876 it was discontinued, and the Advertiser, a semi-weekly paper, published in its place.

August 5, 1873 (Tuesday)

A small company of immigrants arrived in Salt Lake City from Australia.

September 1873

A military post, afterwards known as Fort Cameron, was established near Beaver, Utah.

September 3, 1873 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 510 Saints (291 British and 219 Scandinavian), in charge of John B. Fairbanks. the company, after barely escaping shipwreck near Sable Island, landed in New York, Sept. 20th, and arrived at Salt Lake City, Sept. 29th.

September 4, 1873 (Thursday)

Sarah Ann Kimball, widow of Heber C. Kimball, and daughter of the late Bishop Newel K. Whitney, died in Salt Lake City.

September 23, 1873 (Tuesday)

The Utah Southern Railway was opened for traffic to American Fork.

September 28, 1873 (Sunday)

The Wasatch and Jordan Valley Railway made its terminus at Fairfield Flat, in Little Cottonwood Canyon.

October 1873

Lester J. Herrick succeeded Apostle Albert Carrington as president of the European mission.

October 1, 1873 (Wednesday)

Zion's Savings Bank and Trust Company commenced business in Salt Lake City. The sum of $6,000 was deposited the first day.

October 15, 1873 (Wednesday)

A.H. Brown, chief of police in Provo, was shot by Harrison Carter, a notorious renegade, who escaped, but was afterwards caught in Nevada, brought to Salt Lake City and imprisoned.

October 16, 1873 (Thursday)

The Bingham Canyon Railway was opened for traffic.

October 22, 1873 (Wednesday)

The steamship Idaho sailed from Liverpool, England, with 522 Saints, in charge of John I. Hart. The company arrived at New York, Nov. 4th, and at Salt Lake City, Nov. 14th.

October 24, 1873 (Friday)

The Clift House in Salt Lake City was burned. Estimated loss: $70,000.

October 28, 1873 (Tuesday)

In the Supreme Court of Utah, Judge McKean reversed his former decision in the Third District Court against Thos. Hawkins.

October 31, 1873 (Friday)

Elder Isaac Laney, a respected citizen, who was wounded in the Haun's Mill massacre, died in the 10th Ward, Salt Lake City.

November 6, 1873 (Thursday)

John Mullett, of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City, was accidentally shot and killed, while hunting ducks on the Jordan river.

November 25, 1873 (Tuesday)

A grand celebration was held in Provo, on the event of the Utah Southern Railway being completed to that city.

November 26, 1873 (Wednesday)

Geo. White Pitkin, a respected Church veteran, died at Millville, Cache Co., Utah.

November 28, 1873 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young and Geo. A. Smith and others left Salt Lake City for St. George, where they arrived Dec. 15th. They spent the winter there.

November 29, 1873 (Saturday)

A man and woman were burned to death at Alta, Salt Lake Co.

December 1, 1873 (Monday)

Notwithstanding Geo. R. Maxwell's protest, Geo. Q. Cannon was permitted to take his seat in Congress.

December 20, 1873 (Saturday)

The first number of Utah Posten, a weekly newspaper in the Danish-Norwegian language, was published by Peter O. Thomassen, in Salt Lake City. This was the first paper published in a foreign language in Utah.
