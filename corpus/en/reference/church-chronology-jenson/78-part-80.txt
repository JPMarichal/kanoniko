Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Saints who had settled on the Gila river, Arizona, and vicinity, were organized as a Stake of Zion; and a Stake of Zion was organized in San Luis Valley, Colo. A number of settlements were founded by the Saints in Snake River Valley, Idaho. A successful missionary work was commenced among the Maoris in New Zealand.

January 9, 1883 (Tuesday)

The Saints at Leamington, Millard Co., Utah, were organized as a Ward; Lars N. Christiansen, Bishop.

January 17, 1883 (Wednesday)

Notwithstanding bitter opposition, John T. Caine was permitted to take a seat in Congress, to fill the unexpired term of the 47th Congress.

January 19, 1883 (Friday)

The thermometer stood about 35 degrees F. below zero, in Salt Lake City.

February 1, 1883 (Thursday)

Judge Jeremiah S. Black delivered a powerful speech before the Committee on the Judiciary, at Washington, D.C., pleading for Utah's constitutional rights.

February 17, 1883 (Saturday)

Bishop Wm. Bringhurst, of Springville, died.

February 18, 1883 (Sunday)

John VanCott, one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies, died at his residence, near Salt Lake City.

February 18, 1883 (Sunday)

A number of Saints who had founded a new settlement (north of Manassa, Conejos Co., Colo.), were organized as a branch of the Church, named Richfield, with Thos. N. Petersen as presiding Elder.

February 25, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints at St. David, Ariz., by their vote, accepted of Christopher Layton as president of the St. Joseph Stake of Zion (which had just been organized by the First Presidency) with David P. Kimball and James H. Martineau as counselors.

February 26, 1883 (Monday)

Phil Robinson, the noted litterateur, and Mr. Sergeant Ballantyne, the eminent English barrister, visited Salt Lake City.

March 4, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on Price river, Emery Co., were organized as a Ward; Geo. Frandsen, Bishop.

March 11, 1883 (Sunday)

A townsite was selected in Snake River Valley, Idaho, and named Rexburg, in honor of Thos. E. Ricks.

March 20, 1883 (Tuesday)

Wm. Holt and David Barney, two loggers, sleeping under an overhanging rock, near Parowan, Iron Co., were killed by the rock falling on them.

March 30, 1883 (Friday)

The Denver and Rio Grande Western Railway was completed, and communication established between Salt Lake City and Denver by this route.

April 2, 1883 (Monday)

A Ward organization was effected at Meadows, near St. Johns, Ariz.; Peter Isaacson, Bishop.

April 3, 1883 (Tuesday)

About fifty "Mormon" immigrants arrived in Salt Lake City from the Southern States.

April 6, 1883 (Friday)

The 53rd annual conference of the Church, which was continued three days, commenced in Salt Lake City.

April 8, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled near Savoia, Valencia Co., New Mexico, were organized as the Navajo (now Ramah) Ward; Ernest A. Tietjen, Bishop.

April 10, 1883 (Tuesday)

About ninety missionaries left Salt Lake City for the United States and Europe.

April 10, 1883 (Tuesday)

The constitutional convention met in Salt Lake City and received the report of the committee appointed to present the memorial and constitution, upon which was based Utah's application for admission as a State.

April 11, 1883 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 352 Saints, including 13 returning missionaries, under the direction of David McKay. The company arrived at New York April 22nd, and Salt Lake City April 30th.

April 15, 1883 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Marysvale, Piute Co., Utah, with Hugh D. Lisenbee as presiding Elder.

April 23, 1883 (Monday)

A terrible wind storm did much damage in Utah.

April 24, 1883 (Tuesday)

Wm. E. McLellin, formerly one of the Twelve Apostles, died at Independence, Jackson Co., Mo.

April 30, 1883 (Monday)

Fort Cameron Military Reservation buildings, near Beaver, were sold and the fort was abandoned as a military station.

May 1, 1883 (Tuesday)

O.F. Due, of Salt Lake City, was arrested on a charge of bigamy and placed under $2,500 bonds.

May 13, 1883 (Sunday)

At a two days' meeting, held at Pima, Graham Co., Ariz., the Saints who had settled on the Gila river, Graham Co., Ariz., were organized into four Wards, namely, Pima, Thatcher, Graham, and Curtis, with Joseph K. Roger, John M. Moody, Jorgen Jorgensen and Moses M. Curtis as their respective Bishops.

May 16, 1883 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 427 Saints, including 14 returning missionaries, under the direction of Ben. E. Rich. The company arrived in New York May 27th, and in Salt Lake City June 3rd.

May 18, 1883 (Friday)

Belle Harris was committed to the Utah Penitentiary for contempt of court, in refusing to answer questions before the grand jury of the Second District Court, at Beaver, in a supposed polygamy investigation.

May 19, 1883 (Saturday)

James W. Cummings, a prominent Elder, died in Salt Lake City.

May 22, 1883 (Tuesday)

The Empire grist mill, up City creek, near Salt Lake City, was burned to the ground. Loss; $23,500.

May 27, 1883 (Sunday)

Bishop Calvin Bingham was accidentally killed near St. David, Cochise Co., Ariz.

May 28, 1883 (Monday)

Richard Fowler was shot and fatally wounded by David Gallifant, in Salt Lake City. Fowler died May 30th, and the murderer was held under $5,000 bonds.

May 29, 1883 (Tuesday)

Elder Hans Peter Jensen, one of the early converts to "Mormonism" in Scandinavia, died in Brigham City.

May 29, 1883 (Tuesday)

Bishops Wm. B. Preston and Leonard W. Hardy arrived at Rexburg, Snake River Valley, Idaho. During the few following days they located the townsites of Teton, Wilford, Lyman, Burton and Parker.

May 31, 1883 (Thursday)

John T. Alexander, of Salt Lake City, who labored as a missionary in Georgia, was shot and severely wounded by three masked men, near Plainville, Gordon Co., Ga.

June 7, 1883 (Thursday)

Dr. J.B. Carrington, a non-Mormon, arrested for bigamy, was discharged by Commissioner Gilchrist, at Salt Lake City, notwithstanding the proof of his guilt.

June 10, 1883 (Sunday)

Five young persons, ranging from 12 to 23 years of age, were accidentally drowned, while boating on Utah lake, near Benjamin.

June 10, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled in San Luis Valley, Colo., were organized as the San Luis Stake of Zion, with Silas S. Smith as president and Richard C. Camp and Wm. M. Christensen as counselors. The settlements of Manassa and Richfield were organized as Wards, with John C. Dalton and Thos. N. Petersen as their respective Bishops.

June 15, 1883 (Friday)

Theodore Thomas, the celebrated orchestral leader, gave a concert in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

June 20, 1883 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 697 Saints, including 22 returning missionaries, under the direction of Hans O. Magleby. The company arrived in New York July 1st, and at Ogden July 7th.

June 20, 1883 (Wednesday)

Mary B. Newell was miraculously healed under the administration of the Elders, at Johnsonville, Warren Co., Ind.

June 21, 1883 (Thursday)

The Council House and adjacent buildings, in Salt Lake City, were destroyed by fire and the explosion of powder. Loss: about $100,000.

June 21, 1883 (Thursday)

Bishop Leonard E. Harrington died at his residence, at American Fork, Utah Co.

June 23, 1883 (Saturday)

David Evans, formerly Bishop of Lehi, Utah Co., died at Lehi.

June 24, 1883 (Sunday)

Elder Shadrach Jones, of Willard City, Box Elder Co., died at Swansea, Wales, where he labored as a missionary.

July 4, 1883 (Wednesday)

Robert Ritter and William Ayers were drowned in Silver lake, Big Cottonwood Canyon, while boating.

July 6, 1883 (Friday)

A fatal powder magazine explosion occurred in Ogden Canyon.

July 6, 1883 (Friday)

Joseph Toronto, once a missionary to Italy, died at Salt Lake City.

July 10, 1883 (Tuesday)

Gov. Thomas A. Hendricks, of Indiana, visited Salt Lake City.

July 10, 1883 (Tuesday)

D.C. Rich and Rudolph Smith made an unsuccessful attempt to rob Zion's Savings Bank, in Salt Lake City, assaulting B.H. Schettler, the assistant treasurer. They were both arrested.

July 11, 1883 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake City were treated to their annual excursion, this time going to Provo.

July 14, 1883 (Saturday)

A company of Icelandic Saints sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Wisconsin, in charge of John A. Sutton. The company arrived in Salt Lake City July 30th, and subsequently located at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

July 28, 1883 (Saturday)

R.S.W. Andrew, a street car driver, was accidentally shot and killed in Salt Lake City.

July 29, 1883 (Sunday)

A terrific flood at Kanab, Kane Co, removed masses of earth, trees, etc., which it carried down stream, and partly destroyed the settlement.

August 2, 1883 (Thursday)

The Salt Lake City Council decided that all houses within the city should be numbered, as a preparatory step toward the anticipated free mail delivery.

August 19, 1883 (Sunday)

Jeremiah S. Black, the celebrated statesman and lawyer, who defended the people of Utah so ably a few months previously, died in Washington, D.C.

August 20, 1883 (Monday)

Ellen G. Lewis, a young lady, was accidentally shot and killed in Provo, by a policeman on duty.

August 21, 1883 (Tuesday)

The notorious "Bill" Hickman died in Lander City, Sweetwater Co., Wyoming.

August 25, 1883 (Saturday)

Bishop Andrew Burt, captain of the Salt Lake City police force, was killed by a negro, whom he was trying to arrest. Half an hour later the negro was lynched by a mob, in the jail yard.

August 26, 1883 (Sunday)

Alvin Henson was accidentally shot and killed by his comrades, while hunting, near Tooele, Utah.

August 26, 1883 (Sunday)

Jack Murphy was taken out of the jail at Coalville and lynched at Park City, Summit Co., for the murder of M. Brennan, a few days previously.

August 26, 1883 (Sunday)

The first permanent branch of the Church among the Maoris was organized by Ira N. Hinkley, jun., at Papawai, Wairarapa Valley, North Island, New Zealand; Manihera, a native chief, was ordained a Priest and appointed president. This was the beginning of a great work among the Maoris.

August 26, 1883 (Sunday)

The first Maori who joined the Church was a sailor, baptized by Elder Richard G. Lambert, near Honolulu, Hawaiian Islands, early in 1874. Oct. 18, 1881, Elder Wm. John McDonald, baptized Ngataki, at Aukland. He was the first Maori to join the Church in New Zealand. Toward the close of 1883, and in the beginning of 1884, a number of Maoris were baptized in the Waikato district through the instrumentality of Pres. Wm. M. Bromley, Wm. J. McDonald and Thos. L. Cox. These were organized as the Wautu branch by Elder Thos. L. Cox, Feb. 25, 1883, with Hare Te Katere as president. This was the first Maori branch of the Church organized; but most of its members proved unfaithful to the cause.

August 29, 1883 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 682 Saints, including 29 returning missionaries, in charge of Peter F. Goss. The company arrived at New York Sept. 7th, and at Salt Lake City Sept. 17th.

August 31, 1883 (Friday)

Belle Harris was released from custody, having been imprisoned since May 18th.

September 2, 1883 (Sunday)

Muddy branch, Emery Co., Utah, was organized as a Ward, with Casper Christensen as Bishop. The name of the settlement was afterwards changed to Emery.

September 3, 1883 (Monday)

Washakie, a little Indian town in Box Elder Co., was visited by a fire, which destroyed grain to the value of $3,000.

September 6, 1883 (Thursday)

Wm. G. Phillips was appointed marshal of Salt Lake City, in place of the late Andrew Burt.

September 6, 1883 (Thursday)

Feramorz Little transferred a two story brick building, which he had erected by the 13th Ward Assembly Rooms for the benefit of the poor, to Bishop Millen Atwood.

September 11, 1883 (Tuesday)

The celebrated Henry Ward Beecher lectured in the Salt Lake City Theatre.

September 12, 1883 (Wednesday)

About midnight Elders Stephen R. Marks and David Franklin Davis, of Salt Lake City, were cruelly mobbed, near Laurel, Franklin Co., Indiana, where they labored as missionaries.

September 22, 1883 (Saturday)

General William T. Sherman arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

September 23, 1883 (Sunday)

At a conference held at Bluff, San Juan Co., Utah, the Saints who had located at Fruitland, near Farmington, San Juan Co., New Mexico, were organized as a Ward, named Burnham, with Luther C. Burnham as Bishop.

September 23, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints constituting the Wilmot branch, Piute Co., Utah, were organized as the Marion Ward; Culbert King, Bishop.

September 26, 1883 (Wednesday)

Bishop Charles E. Robison, of Montpelier, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, died at Whittaker, South Carolina, where he labored as a missionary.

October 5, 1883 (Friday)

The semi-annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City; it was continued three days; 92 missionaries were called; Apostle Wilford Woodruff was sustained as Church Historian.

October 6, 1883 (Saturday)

King David Kalakaua visited the plantation of Laie, on the Hawaiian Islands, and addressed a conference of assembled Saints there.

October 8, 1883 (Monday)

John S. Fullmer, a prominent Elder in the Church, died in Springville, Utah Co., and Elder Henry Maiben died in Salt Lake City.

October 16, 1883 (Tuesday)

Presiding Bishop Edward Hunter died in Salt Lake City.

October 27, 1883 (Saturday)

A fire destroyed Causey, Harkins & Co's. skating rink on West Temple Street, Salt Lake City. Loss: $10,000.

October 27, 1883 (Saturday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 369 Saints, including 20 returning missionaries, in charge of John Pickett. The company arrived at New York, Nov. 7th, and at Ogden, Nov. 14th.

October 28, 1883 (Sunday)

The Neeleyville Ward, Oneida County, Idaho, was organized; Wm. Neeley, Bishop.

November 1, 1883 (Thursday)

Lewis Robison, prominent in the early history of Utah, died in Salt Lake City, 67 years of age.

November 17, 1883 (Saturday)

Apostle Charles C. Rich died at Paris, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, 74 years of age.

November 18, 1883 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled in Luna Valley, Socorro Co., N.M., were organized as a Ward, with Geo. C. Williams as Bishop.

November 21, 1883 (Wednesday)

David Patten Kimball, counselor to Pres. Christopher Layton, of the St. Joseph Stake of Zion, Ariz., died.

November 22, 1883 (Thursday)

Bishop Wm. Davis died at Brigham City, Utah.

November 28, 1883 (Wednesday)

The Saints who had located on Portneuf creek, Bingham Co., Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church; Judson A. Tolman, presiding Elder.

December 13, 1883 (Thursday)

Marshal Wm. G. Phillips, of Salt Lake City, reported to the postmaster that all the public streets in the city had been named and all the houses numbered.

December 21, 1883 (Friday)

Elder Lorenzo M. Richards died in Ogden.

December 24, 1883 (Monday)

A monument was raised on the grave of the late Pres. Niels Wilhelmsen, on "Assistents Kirkegaard," Copenhagen, Denmark.

December 26, 1883 (Wednesday)

Gen. Thos. L. Kane, favorably known in Church history, died at his home, in Philadelphia, Pa.
