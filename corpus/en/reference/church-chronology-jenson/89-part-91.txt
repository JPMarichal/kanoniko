Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The first settlement of the Saints in Sonora, Mexico, was organized as a Ward. The first Maori Saints, from New Zealand, arrived in Utah. Pres. Cleveland pardoned all polygamists, and restored them to their civil rights.

January 2, 1894 (Tuesday)

Don Maguire was selected as Utah's representative at the Midwinter Fair, San Francisco, Cal.

January 5, 1894 (Friday)

Ex Judge John W. Blackburn died at Provo, Utah Co.

January 8, 1894 (Monday)

The thirty-first session of the legislative assembly of Utah met in Salt Lake City and organized by the election of M.A. Breeden as president of the Council and A.B. Emery as speaker of the House.

January 9, 1894 (Tuesday)

Col. S.A. Merritt was appointed chief justice of Utah; he qualified on the 17th.

January 10, 1894 (Wednesday)

Wm. Ashworth was arrested at Provo, Utah Co., for u.c., and placed under $300 bonds.

January 10, 1894 (Wednesday)

On report of special Master Bache the Utah Supreme Court ordered certain confiscated Church property valued at $438,174 to be turned over to the First Presidency.

January 16, 1894 (Tuesday)

Elder Wm. R. Smith, president of the Davis Stake of Zion, died at Centerville, Davis Co.

January 17, 1894 (Wednesday)

A bill was introduced in the Utah legislature to abolish the Utah Commission; also to create the county of Carbon out of a portion of Emery County.

January 18, 1894 (Thursday)

Jens L. Brunn was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 20, 1894 (Saturday)

Annie K. Smoot, president of the Utah Stake Primary associations, died at Provo, Utah Co.

January 28, 1894 (Sunday)

John Hopkin was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 1, 1894 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled on Snake river, near Riverside, Bingham Co., Idaho, were organized as the Grover branch of the Church; W.D. Grover, presiding Elder.

February 1, 1894 (Thursday)

Elder Thomas Emmet died at Ogden, Weber Co.

February 6, 1894 (Tuesday)

The legislative Council passed the eight-hour law; it was signed by the governor, Feb. 20th.

February 8, 1894 (Thursday)

Peter Svendsen and Wm. A. Stewart were discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 18, 1894 (Sunday)

Fred. W. Cook was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 18, 1894 (Sunday)

Elder Walter Herbert Barton, of Kaysville, Davis Co., died at McComb, Pike Co., Miss., where he labored as a missionary.

February 19, 1894 (Monday)

The House branch of the Utah legislature passed a bill to abolish the Utah Commission.

February 20, 1894 (Tuesday)

Wm. J. Orchard was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 20, 1894 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Parley Young, of Fairview, Sanpete Co., was sentenced to 60 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

February 20, 1894 (Saturday)

Elder Charles I. Robson, president of the Maricopa Stake of Zion, died at Mesa, Maricopa Co., Ariz.

February 26, 1894 (Monday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Wm. P. Sampson was sentenced to three months' imprisonment and Wm. Ashworth, of Provo, to 50 days', both for u.c.

February 27, 1894 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Nels Peter Thomson was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for polygamy.

February 27, 1894 (Tuesday)

Gov. Caleb W. West appointed John T. Caine Territorial auditor and J.N. Whitehead Territorial treasurer.

February 28, 1894 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, Frank Greenwell was sentenced to 90 days' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

March 1894

Benjamin F. Grouard, once an active Elder in the Church, and one of the first missionaries sent to the Society Islands, died at Santa Ana, Los Angeles Co., Cal.

March 1894

Elders John Vetterli and Julius Billeter, jun., were banished from Prussia, where they labored as Latter-day Saint missionaries.

March 1, 1894 (Thursday)

John M. Allen was arrested at Clifton, Garfield Co., for u.c

March 2, 1894 (Friday)

Elder Isaac Harrison, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Sandy, Salt Lake Co.

March 2, 1894 (Friday)

Bishop Benjamin M. Lewis was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 2, 1894 (Friday)

The Utah legislature passed bills for bounties on silk and sugar beets.

March 4, 1894 (Sunday)

Bishop Carl C.N. Dorius died at Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

March 9, 1894 (Friday)

The Utah legislature passed the bill for a bounty on canaigre root; also the mechanics' lien bill and free library bill. The governor vetoed the free library bill on the 10th.

March 11, 1894 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the Bavispe river, Sonora, Mexico, were organized by Apostle Brigham Young, John Henry Smith and George Teasdale as the Oaxaca Ward; Franklin Scott, Bishop.

March 12, 1894 (Monday)

Gov. West vetoed all the bounty bills, and the Utah legislature adjourned.

March 14, 1894 (Wednesday)

Charles A. Allen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 15, 1894 (Thursday)

Nabbie Young Clawson, daughter of Pres. Brigham Young and wife of Spencer Clawson, died in Salt Lake City.

March 16, 1894 (Friday)

Joseph F. Parker was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 30, 1894 (Friday)

The Salt Lake City council authorized the issue of $800,000 in municipal bonds.

March 30, 1894 (Friday)

A two days' discussion between Elder Willard W. Bean and a Campbellite minister was commenced at Sparta, White Co., Tenn. It resulted in victory to "Mormonism."

April 1, 1894 (Sunday)

Elder Jesse Williams Fox, surveyor, one of Utah's early Pioneers and a prominent citizen, died at Bountiful, Davis Co.

April 6, 1894 (Friday)

The 64th annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

April 7, 1894 (Saturday)

Gov. West ordered out the Utah militia to head off General Kelley's commonwealth army, coming from California.

April 8, 1894 (Sunday)

Kelley's commonwealers reached Ogden, 1200 strong, and were met and guarded by the militia. A squad of Salt Lake City police was sent to Ogden to help drive the "army" back. The municipality of Ogden fed the "wanderers."

April 9, 1894 (Monday)

Thos. C. Sharp, one of the main instigators of the murder of Joseph and Hyrum Smith, died at Carthage, Ill.

April 11, 1894 (Wednesday)

The "Industrial Army" of Kelley's commonwealers marched out of Ogden, boarded a train and were taken eastward.

April 17, 1894 (Tuesday)

Wm. Ashworth was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 18, 1894 (Wednesday)

The Utah "Industrial" army was organized with H.E. Carter as general.

April 21, 1894 (Saturday)

John H. Lutz, who had been pardoned by Pres. Cleveland, was released from the Penitentiary.

April 25, 1894 (Wednesday)

Parley Young was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 27, 1894 (Friday)

The new Utah Commission organized with Geo. W. Thatcher as president. The other members of the commission consisted of A.G. Norrell, J.R. Letcher, Hoyt Sherman, jun., and E.W. Tatlock.

April 27, 1894 (Friday)

The Utah Sugar Company issued bonds to the amount of $400,000.

April 28, 1894 (Saturday)

Elder Ransom M. Stevens, president of the Samoan mission, died at Fagalii, Upolu, Samoa.

May 1894

The first election for the new county of Carbon resulted in Republican success. Price won the county seat.

May 7, 1894 (Monday)

The Utah Commission was permanently organized, with J.R. Letcher as chairman.

May 8, 1894 (Tuesday)

Robert B. Dalley was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 11, 1894 (Friday)

Elder Edson Whipple, one of Utah's Pioneers of 1847, died at Colonia Juarez, Chihuahua, Mexico.

May 11, 1894 (Friday)

Wm. P. Sampson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 12, 1894 (Saturday)

General Carter's "Industrials" captured a Union Pacific train at Lehi and proceeded as far as Provo, where the engine was ditched. Gov. West called out the militia, and deputy marshals arrested 27 of the "Industrials," including General Carter, and took them to the Penitentiary.

May 12, 1894 (Saturday)

The Utah militia returned to Salt Lake City from Provo, and the "Industrial army" broke up.

May 17, 1894 (Thursday)

The Hot Springs' Railroad was completed to Centerville, Davis Co.

May 18, 1894 (Friday)

General Carter and nineteen of his "Industrials" were sent to the Penitentiary for contempt of court, in stealing a Union Pacific railway train.

May 19, 1894 (Saturday)

John H. Rumel, an old Pioneer of Utah, died at Farmers Ward, Salt Lake Co.

May 21, 1894 (Monday)

Edward W. Tullidge, one of Utah's literary men, died in Salt Lake City.

May 21, 1894 (Monday)

One hundred "Industrials" took possession of a freight train at Thistle Station, Utah Co. Subsequently sixteen of the leaders were arrested.

May 23, 1894 (Wednesday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, John M. Allen, of Clifton, Garfield Co., and James A. Smith were each sentenced by Judge Geo. W. Bartch to 60 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

May 24, 1894 (Thursday)

Davis County applied for an injunction to prevent "General" Smith's "Industrial army" from marching through the county.

May 25, 1894 (Friday)

A squad of Salt Lake City police went to Davis County to aid in stopping the "Industrial army." The "army" was halted at the county line.

May 26, 1894 (Saturday)

Judge Smith, at Provo, Utah Co., sentenced 23 "Industrialists" to the Penitentiary.

May 29, 1894 (Tuesday)

Judge Merritt dissolved the injunction against the "Industrials" crossing the Davis County line.

May 31, 1894 (Thursday)

Elder Samuel F. Lee, one of the Pioneers of Tooele Co., died at Tooele, Tooele Co.

June 1, 1894 (Saturday)

Smith's "Industrial" army marched into Salt Lake City.

June 9, 1894 (Saturday)

Elder Francis A. Brown died at Ogden, Weber Co.

June 16, 1894 (Saturday)

Hirini Whaanga, Mere Whaanga, Apikara Whaanga and four children sailed from Auckland, New Zealand, bound for Utah. these were the first Maori Saints who gathered to Zion. They emigrated in charge of Elders William Douglas, Wesley Gibson and Lars Christian Rasmussen, returning missionaries.

June 22, 1894 (Friday)

The workers in the Salt Lake Temple and a few of their friends left Salt Lake City on an excursion to Brigham City, Mantua and Willard, Box Elder Co.

July 1, 1894 (Sunday)

Salt Lake City railroad employees held a meeting and decided to join in the Pullman strike; the places of strikers were filled with non-union men; all trains were tied up at Ogden.

July 2, 1894 (Monday)

A complete tie-up of all trains by the strike existed; a stage line was established between Salt Lake City and Ogden.

July 3, 1894 (Tuesday)

A thousand strikers stopped a Union Pacific train at Ogden.

July 4, 1894 (Wednesday)

Strikers at Ogden overcame the United States marshall and deputies and prevented the running of trains.

July 5, 1894 (Thursday)

No railroad trains entered or left Salt Lake City.

July 6, 1894 (Friday)

Elder George M. Brown died at Colonia Diaz, Chihuahua, Mexico.

July 6, 1894 (Friday)

Federal officers in Utah sent a telegram to Attorney General Olney, asking that troops be called out to subdue the strikers.

July 8, 1894 (Sunday)

Incendiaries attempted to burn the city of Ogden, Utah. Seven fires were started in a little over an hour and property to the value of $135,000 was destroyed.

July 8, 1894 (Sunday)

John M. Allen and James A. Smith were discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 8, 1894 (Sunday)

The Sixteenth Infantry, stationed at Fort Douglas, was ordered out to protect Union Pacific and Southern Pacific trains.

July 9, 1894 (Monday)

The strike in Salt Lake City was broken, and train service was resumed.

July 10, 1894 (Tuesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County had their annual excursion, this time going to Saltair.

July 17, 1894 (Tuesday)

The Riverside branch, Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized as the Riverside Ward; Charles Erastus Liljenquist, Bishop.

July 17, 1894 (Tuesday)

The first train from San Francisco, Cal., since the strike was inaugurated, arrived in Salt Lake City.

July 17, 1894 (Tuesday)

President Cleveland signed the Enabling act or Utah Statehood bill.

July 18, 1894 (Wednesday)

A company was organized to build a railroad from Fairfield station, Utah Co., to Mercur, a mining camp.

July 23, 1894 (Monday)

In the First District Court held at Manti, Sanpete Co., Eliott Hudson was sentenced to 30 days' imprisonment, for "fornication."

August 1, 1894 (Wednesday)

Acting Governor Charles C. Richards issued a proclamation, ordering an election of delegates to the Constitutional Convention, under the Enabling Act.

August 2, 1894 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, Wm. A. Kirkwood was sentenced to 60 days' imprisonment, for "fornication."

August 3, 1894 (Friday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, John Welch was sentenced by Judge Miner to pay a fine of $25, for u.c.

August 6, 1894 (Monday)

Hon. Harvey W. Smith succeeded Judge James A. Miner on the bench of the Fourth District Court, at Ogden.

August 11, 1894 (Saturday)

Utah exhibits at the Midwinter Fair, at San Francisco, Cal., carried off fourteen rewards.

August 14, 1894 (Tuesday)

John Morgan, one of the first Seven Presidents of Seventies, died at Preston, Idaho.

August 22, 1894 (Wednesday)

Elder Peter C. Geertsen died at Huntsville, Weber County.

August 22, 1894 (Wednesday)

Elliott Hudson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 28, 1894 (Tuesday)

The Cannonville Ward, Garfield Co., was divided by Apostle Francis M. Lyman, into three Wards, namely Cannonville, Georgetown and Tropic. Wesley W. Willis was appointed Bishop of Cannonville and Geo. W. Johnson Bishop of Georgetown.

August 30, 1894 (Thursday)

Bishop Henry Clegg died at Heber City, Wasatch Co.

August 31, 1894 (Friday)

The Utah Supreme Court appointed John R. Winder Receiver of Church property, in place of Henry W. Lawrence; the new Receiver gave bonds in the sum of $100,000.

August 31, 1894 (Friday)

Prof. Theodore B. Lewis succeeded Judge Jacob S. Boreman as Territorial School Commissioner.

September 9, 1894 (Sunday)

Charles David Barnum, one of Utah's oldest Pioneers, died in Salt Lake City.

September 11, 1894 (Tuesday)

The Republican Territorial convention, at Provo, nominated Frank J. Cannon for Congress.

September 13, 1894 (Thursday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, James A. Stratton was sentenced to 18 months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

September 15, 1894 (Saturday)

The Democratic convention, held in Salt Lake City, nominated Joseph L. Rawlins for Congress.

September 18, 1894 (Tuesday)

Benjamin Goddard left Auckland, New Zealand, on a special missionary tour to Australia and Tasmania.

September 18, 1894 (Tuesday)

A new oath was framed by the Utah Commission for polygamists.

September 20, 1894 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Richard Crowther was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

September 27, 1894 (Thursday)

Pres. Grover Cleveland issued a proclamation granting pardon and restoring civil rights to all persons who were disfranchised by the anti-polygamy laws, excepting those who had not complied with Pres. Harrison's proclamation of Jan. 4, 1893.

September 29, 1894 (Saturday)

A new meeting house, erected by the Elders laboring in the Indian Territory mission, was dedicated in the Massy settlement, Choctaw Nation, Ind. Ter.

October 4, 1894 (Thursday)

Wm. A. Kirkwood was discharged from the Penitentiary.

October 4, 1894 (Thursday)

The Territorial Exposition was opened in Salt Lake City, with a grand military display.

October 5, 1894 (Friday)

The general semi-annual conference of the Church commenced in Salt Lake City. It was continued daily till the 7th. In voting for the general authorities of the Church, on the 7th, Edward Stevenson was sustained as one of the First Seven Presidents of Seventies, to fill the vacancy caused by the death of John Morgan.

October 7, 1894 (Sunday)

Elder Hyrum Judd, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Colonia Juarez, Chihuahua, Mexico.

October 9, 1894 (Tuesday)

Edward Stevenson was set apart as one of the First Seven Presidents of Seventies.

October 9, 1894 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Thomas Ogden was sentenced to 100 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

October 11, 1894 (Thursday)

Elder Cyrus H. Wheelock, a Church veteran, died at Mt. Pleasant, Sanpete Co.

October 11, 1894 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Poul Poulson was sentenced to 30 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

October 13, 1894 (Saturday)

In the Fourth District Court, Provo, Hyrum G. White was sentenced by Judge Wm. H. King to six months' imprisonment, and John McKellar to a longer term, both for "adultery."

October 22, 1894 (Monday)

Elder Hyrum Carter, of Porterville, Morgan Co., Utah died at Tooele, Tooele Co.

November 5, 1894 (Monday)

Lewis Barney, one of Utah's Pioneers of 1847, died at Mancos, Colo.

November 6, 1894 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah the Republicans elected Frank J. Cannon delegate to Congress. The Republicans also elected 60 of the 107 delegates to the constitutional convention.

November 9, 1894 (Friday)

Elder Wm. W. Casto, senior president of the 61st quorum of Seventy and once a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Big Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

November 10, 1894 (Saturday)

Poul Poulson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 21, 1894 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, David Bigelow, of Wallsburg, was sentenced to 50 days imprisonment, for u.c.

November 22, 1894 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Zeb. Barkdull was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for "fornication."

November 27, 1894 (Tuesday)

Southern Ute Indians from Colorado invaded southeastern Utah. Gov. Wells asked the Washington authorities to send them back.

November 28, 1894 (Wednesday)

James A. Stratton was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 3, 1894 (Monday)

A delegation of settlers from San Juan County arrived in Salt Lake City, and appealed to the governor for protection against the Utes.

December 4, 1894 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Magnus Erickson was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for "adultery;" he was incarcerated in the Penitentiary on the 9th.

December 5, 1894 (Wednesday)

Richard Crowther was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 5, 1894 (Wednesday)

Gov. West sent arms and ammunition to the San Juan settlers.

December 13, 1894 (Thursday)

General William Booth, of the Salvation Army, spoke in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

December 14, 1894 (Friday)

As a result of a conference between Gov. West, Col. Tatlock, Colorado officials and the Utes, held at Monticello, Utah, the Indians agreed to return to their reservation.

December 28, 1894 (Friday)

The new City and County building in Salt Lake City was dedicated.

December 31, 1894 (Monday)

Thomas Ogden was discharged from the Penitentiary.
