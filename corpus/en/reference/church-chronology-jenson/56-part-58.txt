Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

General Albert Sidney Johnston, left Utah with a part of the Federal army, which had been stationed at Camp Floyd, Cedar Valley, since 1858. A large immigration arrived in Utah from Europe.

January 25, 1860 (Wednesday)

John King was accidentally killed and buried in a snowslide, in Centreville, Canyon, Davis Co., Utah.

February 7, 1860 (Tuesday)

The Social Hall, G.S.L. City, was reopened for public amusements, which had been discontinued there for three years.

February 15, 1860 (Wednesday)

Wm. Price was ordained the first Bishop of Goshen, Utah Co.

March 1, 1860 (Thursday)

Gen. Albert Sidney Johnston, commander of the "Utah Army," left Camp Floyd for Washington, D.C. He had never visited G.S.L. City since he passed through with his army on June 26, 1858. Philip St. George Cooke, formerly commander of the Mormon Battalion, succeeded Johnston in the command.

March 4, 1860 (Sunday)

Levi Gifford, formerly a member of Zion's Camp, died at Moroni, Sanpete Co.

March 19, 1860 (Monday)

Dr. Wm. France died suddenly in G.S.L. City.

March 25, 1860 (Sunday)

Apostle Ezra T. Benson moved to Logan, Cache Co., having been called to preside over the Saints in Cache Valley.

March 30, 1860 (Friday)

The ship Underwriter sailed from Liverpool, England, with 594 British and Swiss Saints, under the presidency of James D. Ross. It arrived at New York May 1st, and the emigrants continued to Florence, where Geo. Q. Cannon was acting as Church emigration agent this year, to arrange for the journey across the plains.

April 1860

Hyrum, Cache Co., Utah, was first settled by about twenty families. In the following month Calvin Bingham was appointed Bishop. Paradise, Cache Co., was settled about the same time.

April 7, 1860 (Saturday)

The Saints who had settled on lower Beaver creek, Beaver Co, Utah, were organized into a Ward named Minersville, by Apostles Amasa M. Lyman and Charles C. Rich; James K. Rollins, Bishop.

April 7, 1860 (Saturday)

The first "Pony Express" from the West arrived at G.S.L. City, having left Sacramento, Cal., on the evening of April 3rd.

April 9, 1860 (Monday)

The first "Pony Express" from the East arrived at G.S.L. City, having left St. Joseph, Mo., on the evening of April 3rd.

April 9, 1860 (Monday)

The Union Academy was opened in the building known as the Union Hotel (afterwards Deseret Hospital), with Orson Pratt as principal.

April 13, 1860 (Friday)

Thos. Miles was attacked and wounded by Indians, between Ogden and Kaysville. The savages proceeded to Brigham City, where they stole horses and insulted the citizens.

April 16, 1860 (Monday)

Hyde Park, Cache Co., was settled by several families from Utah County.

April 27, 1860 (Friday)

Jack Cole, a horsethief and outlaw, was mortally wounded at Springville, Utah Co., while resisting the officers of the law.

May 1860

A large number of the troops stationed at Camp Floyd, Utah, left, according to orders, for New Mexico and Arizona Territories.

May 1860

Nathaniel V. Jones and Jacob Gates succeeded Asa Calkin in the presidency of the European mission.

May 3, 1860 (Thursday)

John W. Brown was accidentally killed by the falling of a rock, near Draper, G.S.L. Co.

May 4, 1860 (Saturday)

Niels Jensen, one of the early members of the Church in Denmark, died in G.S.L. City.

May 8, 1860 (Tuesday)

Jesse W. Johnson was accidentally killed at Snyder's Mill, in Parley's Park.

May 11, 1860 (Friday)

The ship William Tapscott sailed from Liverpool, England, with 731 Saints (including 312 Scandinavians), under the direction of Asa Calkin. During the voyage small pox broke out among the emigrants, who had to remain several days in quarantine after arriving at New York harbor. They finally landed June 20th and continued their journey to Florence, Neb., where they arrived July 1st.

May 12, 1860 (Saturday)

G.S.L. City was visited by a heavy snow storm.

May 28, 1860 (Monday)

The Indians attacked the mail station at Deep Creek, Tooele Co., shot a man and stole several horses.

May 31, 1860 (Thursday)

Rees Jones Williams was accidentally killed in a saw mill, in Little Cottonwood Canyon.

June 3, 1860 (Sunday)

The first train of merchandise from the East that season arrived in G.S.L. City.

July 22, 1860 (Sunday)

Smithfield, Cache Co., was attacked by Indians. A fight ensued; John Reed and Ira Merrill and two Indians were killed, and several others wounded on both sides.

July 24, 1860 (Tuesday)

The day was celebrated by the citizens of G.S.L. County near the head waters of Big Cottonwood.

July 28, 1860 (Saturday)

The remains of a woman, evidently killed by the departing soldiers, were found in Provo Valley, Wasatch Co.

August 1860

Apostles Amasa M. Lyman and Charles C. Rich succeeded Nathaniel V. Jones and Jacob Gates in the presidency of the European mission.

August 2, 1860 (Thursday)

Mrs. Ruth B. Clark, of the Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co., was bitten by a scorpion, while asleep, causing her death.

August 4, 1860 (Saturday)

A terrible hailstorm visited Davis County, doing a great deal of damage.

August 9, 1860 (Thursday)

Capt. Warren Walling's train, the first company of immigrating Saints of the season, arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence, May 30th, with 160 persons and 30 wagons, mostly drawn by oxen.

August 12, 1860 (Sunday)

The Indians made an attack upon the mail station at Egan Canyon, (Tooele Co.) and the following day on Shell Creek Station. A company of soldiers came to the rescue and killed 17 Indians.

August 26, 1860 (Sunday)

Geo. Q. Cannon was ordained one of the Twelve Apostles, in G.S.L. City.

August 27, 1860 (Monday)

Capt. Daniel Robinson's handcart company (the first of the season), consisting of 233 persons, 43 handcarts, 6 wagons, 38 oxen and 10 tents, arrived in G.S.L. City. Pres. Brigham Young had sent out wagons with 2,500 lbs. of flour and 500 lbs. of bacon to help the company.

August 30, 1860 (Thursday)

Capt. J.E. Murphy's immigrant company, consisting of 279 persons, 38 wagons, 164 oxen and 39 cows, arrived at G.S.L. City, having left Florence June 19th.

September 1, 1860 (Saturday)

Capt. John Smith's company of immigrants, consisting of 359 persons and 39 wagons, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 3, 1860 (Monday)

Capt. James D. Ross' company of immigrants, consisting of 259 persons, 36 wagons, 142 oxen and 54 cows, which left Florence June 17th, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 4, 1860 (Tuesday)

A portion of Capt. Franklin Brown's company of immigrants arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 14, 1860 (Friday)

Capt. Brigham H. Young's train of immigrants arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 17, 1860 (Monday)

Capt. John Taylor's company of immigrating Saints arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence July 3rd.

September 24, 1860 (Monday)

The second handcart company of the season, under Capt. Oscar O. Stoddard, arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence July 6th, with 126 persons and 22 handcarts. These were the last immigrants who crossed the plains with handcarts.

September 26, 1860 (Wednesday)

On this and the two following days a company of missionaries left G.S.L. City, among whom were Apostles Orson Pratt and Erastus Snow, for the United States and Geo. Q. Cannon on his way to England.

October 1860

Capt. Jacob Hamblin, left Santa Clara, southern Utah, with nine men, to visit the Moquis Indians.

October 4, 1860 (Thursday)

Hon. John F. Kinney arrived in G.S.L. City, having been reappointed chief justice of the Territory of Utah.

October 5, 1860 (Friday)

Capt. Wm. Budge's train, the last immigrant company of the season, arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence July 20th, with over four hundred persons, 55 wagons, 215 oxen and 77 cows.

October 21, 1860 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Mountain Green, Weber Valley, Utah.

November 2, 1860 (Friday)

Geo. A. Smith, jun., (a son of Pres. Geo. A. Smith), one of Jacob Hamblin's exploring party, was killed by Navajo Indians, in New Mexico. The rest of the company were obliged to return, and barely escaped with their lives.

November 12, 1860 (Monday)

An extra session of the Utah legislature convened in G.S.L. City, for the purpose of assigning the Federal judges to the various districts, in obedience to the proclamation of Gov. Cumming.

November 16, 1860 (Friday)

A terrible storm visited Great Salt Lake, Weber and surrounding Counties, destroying considerable property.

December 3, 1860 (Monday)

Starling Graves Driggs, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died in Parowan, Iron Co.

December 4, 1860 (Tuesday)

The Ute Indian Chief Arrapeen died in the mountains between Sevier Valley and Grass Valley, about sixty miles south of Manti.

December 10, 1860 (Monday)

The tenth annual session of the Utah legislature convened in G.S.L. City and organized by electing Daniel H. Wells president of the Council, and John Taylor speaker of the House.
