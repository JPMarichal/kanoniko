Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

A number of settlements in Utah, Idaho, Arizona and Nevada were organized as Bishops' Wards. Three new counties were created by the Utah legislature. The Church celebrated its fiftieth anniversary with a grand jubilee, and successful missionary work was carried on by the Elders in foreign lands.

January 7, 1880 (Wednesday)

The first number of Ungdommens Raadgiver, a small monthly periodical, published in the interest of the young Latter-day Saints in Scandinavia, was issued in Copenhagen, Denmark; Andrew Jenson, editor.

January 12, 1880 (Monday)

The 24th session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City, and organized by appointing Lorenzo Snow president of the Council, and Orson Pratt speaker of the House.

February 1880

An act was passed by the Utah legislature, authorizing the city council of Salt Lake City to borrow money for the completion of the Jordan River and Salt Lake City Canal. Emery, San Juan and Uintah Counties were created by legislative acts.

February 8, 1880 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on Otter creek, or the East Fork (of the Sevier river), Piute Co., Utah, were organized as a branch of the Church called Wilmot; John D. Wilcox, presiding Elder.

February 9, 1880 (Monday)

The trial of parties charged with the murder of Dr. J. King Robinson, in 1866, was called, and, on motion of the prosecuting attorney, dismissed, notwithstanding the defendants demanded a trial.

February 22, 1880 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the Mesquite Flat, near Bunkerville, Nev., were organized as the Mesquite Ward; Wm. H. Branch, Bishop.

February 29, 1880 (Sunday)

Eli H. Murray, the 11th governor of Utah, and successor of Gov. Geo. W. Emery, arrived in Salt Lake City.

March 3, 1880 (Wednesday)

James Whittaker, sen., died in Cedar City, Iron Co.,

March 4, 1880 (Thursday)

The Salt Lake weekly Herald was first issued.

March 14, 1880 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled at Concho, Apache Co., Ariz., were organized as a branch of the Church with B.H. Wilhelm as presiding Elder.

March 19, 1880 (Friday)

John D. Rees, one of the first settlers of Brigham City, died at Malad, Idaho.

April 2, 1880 (Friday)

Co. Peter Litz, the first member of the Church in Virginia, died in Burke's Garden, Tazewell Co., Va.

April 4, 1880 (Sunday)

Public meetings were held in the Salt Lake Assembly Hall for the first time.

April 5, 1880 (Monday)

Salt Lake City decided by vote, to build the Salt Lake and Jordan Canal.

April 5, 1880 (Monday)

The co-operative store at Monroe, Sevier Co., was destroyed by fire.

April 5, 1880 (Monday)

Bluff City, on the San Juan river, was settled by a company of Latter-day Saints from Iron County.

April 6, 1880 (Tuesday)

At a conference of the Y.M.M.I. Associations, held in the Salt Lake Assembly Hall, Wilford Woodruff was appointed general superintendent of all the associations in the Church, with Joseph F. Smith and Moses Thatcher as his counselors. Junius F. Wells, Milton H. Hardy and R.C. Badger were sustained as assistants to the general superintendency, Heber J. Grant as secretary, and Wm. S. Burton as treasurer.

April 6, 1880 (Tuesday)

On this and three following days the 50th annual conference of the Church was held in Salt Lake City. It was voted to permit $802,000 of the indebtedness to the P.E. Fund, in favor of the worthy poor, and to distribute 1,000 cows and 5,000 sheep among the needy. The Saints were advised to be charitable and liberal toward one another, and make this a jubilee year by forgiving the worthy poor their debts, and thus relieve them from bondage. Wm. W. Taylor was sustained as one of the seven presidents of the Seventies, to fill the vacancy caused by the death of Albert P. Rockwood.

April 10, 1880 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 120 Saints, in charge of James L. Bunting. The company arrived at New York April 21st, and at Ogden and Salt Lake City April 30th.

April 13, 1880 (Tuesday)

The Utah Central Railway depot and adjoining hotel, at Sandy, Salt Lake Co., was destroyed by fire.

April 18, 1880 (Sunday)

Elder Wm. C. Martindale was appointed to preside over the Saints who had settled in Goose Creek Valley and vicinity, Cassia Co., Idaho.

April 23, 1880 (Friday)

Mary Parker, an aged lady, was outraged and cruelly murdered, near Rockville, Kane Co. Jared Dalton was subsequently arrested, suspected of the crime.

May 3, 1880 (Monday)

The corner stone of St. Paul's Chapel (Episcopal), Salt Lake City, was laid by the Masonic fraternity.

May 4, 1880 (Tuesday)

Rosewell Stevens, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died at Bluff, San Juan Co., Utah.

May 9, 1880 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized in Spring Basin, Cassia Co., Idaho, where a few families of Saints had located.

May 15, 1880 (Saturday)

The Utah Southern Railway was opened to Milford, Beaver Co.

May 24, 1880 (Monday)

John Y. Greene, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

June 5, 1880 (Saturday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 332 Saints, in charge of John G. Jones. The company arrived at New York June 15th, and at Salt Lake City June 25th.

June 23, 1880 (Wednesday)

The Utah Southern Railway was opened to Frisco.

June 26, 1880 (Saturday)

A small company of Saints from Iceland sailed from Liverpool, England, bound for Utah.

July 3, 1880 (Saturday)

John F. Turner, son of Sheriff John Turner, of Provo, was killed by Fred. Hopt (Welcome), at Park City, Summit Co. The body, which the murderer conveyed to Echo Canyon, was found there July 10th.

July 8, 1880 (Thursday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County had a grand excursion to Black Rock, on the shore of Great Salt Lake.

July 10, 1880 (Saturday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 727 Saints, in charge of Niels P. Rasmussen. The company landed at New York July 21st, and arrived at Salt Lake City July 29th.

July 20, 1880 (Tuesday)

According to the census return, Utah had a population of 143,690, showing an increase of 56,904 since 1870.

July 25, 1880 (Sunday)

Fred. Hopt (Welcome), the murderer, arrived in Salt Lake City, in custody of Sheriff John Turner, who had arrested him at Cheyenne, Wyo., on the 23rd.

August 1880

Apostles Erastus Snow and Brigham Young and other prominent men visited Castle Valley, Emery Co., Utah, and appointed Christen G. Larsen to preside over the Saints who were locating in that part of the country.

August 5, 1880 (Thursday)

Under the administration of the Elders, Eliza Robinson, of the Birmingham branch, England, was instantly healed of ulcers, after fifteen years suffering.

August 17, 1880 (Tuesday)

A stately monument was erected on the grave of the martyred Joseph Standing, in the Salt Lake City cemetery.

August 18, 1880 (Wednesday)

Jonathan H. Holmes, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Farmington, Davis Co.

August 19, 1880 (Thursday)

Elders Serge L. Ballif, Morris D. Rosenbaum and John Kienke were arrested and imprisoned at Berlin, Germany, and on the following day ordered out of the country for preaching the gospel.

September 1880

The Saints who had settled on the San Juan river, southeastern Utah, were organized as Bluff Ward, by Apostles Erastus Snow and Brigham Young, Jens Nielsen, Bishop.

September 4, 1880 (Saturday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 337 Saints, in charge of John Rider. The company arrived at New York Sept. 15th, and at Salt Lake City Sept. 25th.

September 5, 1880 (Thursday)

Rutherford B. Hayes, President of the United States, Mrs. Hayes and party, visited Salt Lake City.

September 11, 1880 (Saturday)

An electric light exhibition was given in Salt Lake City, in front of Z.C.M.I.

September 18, 1880 (Saturday)

Bishop Jonathan Pugmire, a prominent Elder, died at St. Charles, Bear Lake Co., Idaho.

September 21, 1880 (Tuesday)

John Orson Angus, a prominent Elder, died at St. George, Utah.

September 26, 1880 (Sunday)

Dr. Thos. De Witt Talmage, in a sermon at the "Brooklyn Tabernacle," N.Y., suggested the annihilation of the "Mormons" by the Ft. Douglas artillery.

September 26, 1880 (Sunday)

At a priesthood meeting held at Snowflake, Ariz., the Saints who had settled on the Gila river were organized as Smithville Ward; Joseph K. Rogers, Bishop.

September 26, 1880 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held at Snowflake, Apache Co., Ariz., Bush Valley branch was organized as Alpine Ward; Edward A. Noble, Bishop. The Saints who had located in Round Valley, Apache Co., Ariz., were organized as a Ward; Peter C. Christoffersen, Bishop. The Saints composing the Concho branch, Apache Co., Ariz., were organized as the Erastus Ward; Sixtus E. Johnson, Bishop. The Saints constituting the settlement of Woodruff, Apache Co., Ariz., were organized as the Woodruff Ward, with James C. Owens as Bishop; and the Saints who had settled above Snowflake were organized as the Walker (now Taylor) Ward; Henry Standiffird, Bishop.

October 10, 1880 (Sunday)

At the general conference held in Salt Lake City, the First Presidency of the Church was reorganized, with John Taylor as President, and Geo. Q. Cannon and Joseph F. Smith as Counselors. The vacancies thereby occurring in the Council of the Twelve Apostles were partly filled by the calling of Francis M. Lyman and John Henry Smith to the Apostleship. These two brethren were ordained Apostles Oct. 7th.

October 23, 1880 (Saturday)

The first number of the Bear Lake Democrat was issued at Paris, Bear Lake Co, Idaho.

October 23, 1880 (Saturday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 258 Saints, in charge of John Nicholson. The company arrived at New York Nov. 2nd, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 11th.

October 29, 1880 (Friday)

At a special meeting held at Milford, Beaver Co., Utah, the Saints residing at that place were organized as Milford Ward, of the Beaver Stake of Zion; Wm. McMillan, Bishop.

October 30, 1880 (Saturday)

Gordon S. Bills and another Elder were mobbed in Lawrence County, Ky.

October 31, 1880 (Sunday)

The Willow Springs branch, Malad Valley, Idaho, was organized as the Cherry Creek Ward; John D. Jones, Bishop.

November 2, 1880 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah for delegate to Congress, Geo. Q. Cannon, the Peoples' Party candidate, received 18,568 votes; and Allen G. Campbell, the Liberal party candidate, 1,357 votes.

November 6, 1880 (Saturday)

Apostle Albert Carrington succeeded Wm. Budge as president of the European mission.

November 7, 1880 (Sunday)

The mining town of Bingham, Bingham Canyon, Salt Lake Co., was partly destroyed by fire.

November 8, 1880 (Monday)

Hon. Jonathan C. Wright died at Brigham City.

December 6, 1880 (Monday)

Geo. H. Luke and Hans C. Madsen, of Manti, Sanpete Co., were accidentally killed, while working on the Denver and Rio Grande Railway, in Colorado.

December 11, 1880 (Saturday)

The Utah Eastern Railway was completed from Coalville to Park City.

December 14, 1880 (Tuesday)

Elder Geo. Lamb died in St. George.
