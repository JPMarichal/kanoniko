Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year Judge James B. McKean made himself obnoxious to the Saints in Utah by his absurd rulings and his judicial persecutions of the "Mormons". The settlements of the Saints on the Muddy, in Nevada, were vacated because of the excessive taxation. The people in Utah again subscribed liberally towards emigrating the poor Saints from Europe. The first Utah edition of the Book of Mormon was printed. Several hundred stands of the Italian honey-bee were imported into the Territory. The Utah Southern Railway was built to Draper, Salt Lake Co. Latter-day Saint Sunday Schools were organized in all the large branches of the Church in the Scandinavian mission.

January 17, 1871 (Tuesday)

The Utah Southern Railway Company was organized, with Wm. Jennings as president.

January 19, 1871 (Thursday)

Mary Phillips, one of the old Herefordshire (England) Saints, died at Kaysville, Davis Co.

February 1871

Judge McKean made some absurd rulings in the naturalization of foreigners, making their belief in polygamy a test question.

February 1871

The settlements of St. Joseph, St. Thomas and Overton, on the Muddy, were broken up, because of their being set off into Nevada, where taxation was oppressive.

February 2, 1871 (Thursday)

The nomination of Geo. L. Woods, of Oregon, for governor of Utah, and Geo. A. Black, of Illinois, for secretary, was confirmed by the U.S. Senate.

February 6, 1871 (Monday)

The meeting house, tithing office and post office in Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., was burned.

February 10, 1871 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young and Geo. A. Smith arrived in Salt Lake City from their winter visit to St. George.

February 19, 1871 (Sunday)

The new governor, Geo. L. Woods, arrived in Salt Lake City.

March 1871

Geo. R. Maxwell's infamous memorial, praying for a seat in Congress, as a contestant against Wm. H. Hooper, was presented to Congress.

March 9, 1871 (Thursday)

The Deseret Philharmonic Society was organized in Salt Lake City, with David O. Calder as president.

March 13, 1871 (Monday)

Bishop Alfred Cordon died at Willard City, Box Elder Co.

March 18, 1871 (Saturday)

Commercial Street, Salt Lake City, was opened.

March 27, 1871 (Monday)

The Salt Lake fire department was reorganized.

March 31, 1871 (Friday)

The Emma mine, in Little Cottonwood Canyon, was sold for $1,500,000.

April 1871

Numerous grasshoppers appeared in the northern part of Cache County. During the summer these insects again damaged the crops considerably in various parts of the Territory.

April 3, 1871 (Monday)

Mary Champlin, a survivor of the Haun's Mill massacre, died in Salt Lake City.

April 3, 1871 (Monday)

Gov. Alvin Saunders, of Nebraska, visited Salt Lake City.

April 6, 1871 (Thursday)

The 41st annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 9th.

April 15, 1871 (Saturday)

The first number of the Salt Lake Daily Tribune was issued instead of the Mormon Tribune, suspended.

April 18, 1871 (Tuesday)

Ralph Waldo Emerson, the eminent litterateur, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

April 24, 1871 (Monday)

Bishop Peter Maughan, one of the founders of the Cache Valley settlements, died.

May 1871

The Corinne Daily Journal, an anti-Mormon paper, was first published at Corinne, Box Elder Co., Utah.

May 1, 1871 (Monday)

Ground was first broken for the Utah Southern Railway.

May 3, 1871 (Wednesday)

Major J.W. Powell, the Colorado explorer, and party arrived in Salt Lake City.

May 10, 1871 (Wednesday)

Elder Joseph Parry with ten Saints, sailed from Liverpool on the steamship Wyoming, bound for Utah.

June 1871

Apostle Albert Carrington succeeded Horace S. Eldredge as president of the European mission.

June 11, 1871 (Sunday)

The first camp-meeting ever held in Utah, took place in Salt Lake City, under the auspices of the Methodists.

June 14, 1871 (Wednesday)

While shoveling snow in American Fork Canyon, Clark Thompson was accidentally killed and a companion wounded.

June 21, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 248 Saints, under the direction of Robert F. Neslen and Geo. Lake. The company arrived at New York July 3rd, and at Salt Lake City July 12th.

June 26, 1871 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young, Geo. A. Smith and others left Salt Lake City, on a trip to the northern settlements, returning in the latter part of July, after visiting Soda Springs, Bear Lake Valley, etc.

June 28, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 397 Saints, in charge of Wm. W. Cluff. The company landed at New York July 13th, and arrived at Ogden July 21st.

June 30, 1871 (Friday)

Geo. A. Black, acting-governor of Utah, issued a proclamation, forbidding the assembling of any of the militia of the Territory, to participate in the celebration of the 95th anniversary of American Independence, in Salt Lake City.

July 4, 1871 (Tuesday)

Notwithstanding Act.-Gov. Black's proclamation against the assembling of the Territorial militia, the day was celebrated in good style in Salt Lake City.

July 10, 1871 (Monday)

Hon. S.S. Cox, of New York, visited Salt Lake City.

July 12, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Colorado sailed from Liverpool, England, with 146 Saints, under the direction of Hamilton G. Park. The company arrived in New York July 25th, and at Salt Lake City Aug. 4th.

July 20, 1871 (Thursday)

The Pioneer Mill, Ophir Mining District (the first stamp mill in Utah), commenced running; Walker Bros., proprietors.

July 21, 1871 (Friday)

The Lady of the Lake, a little steamer bought by John W. Young and intended for an excursion boat on the Salt Lake, arrived in Salt Lake City. It was launched in the Jordan on Aug. 2nd.

July 23, 1871 (Sunday)

A meeting and dwelling house, erected by the Saints in Christiania, Norway, was dedicated.

July 26, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 93 Saints, under the direction of Lot Smith. The company arrived at New York Aug. 7th, and in Salt Lake City Aug. 16th.

August 1, 1871 (Tuesday)

M.T. Patrick, U.S. marshal, took possession of the Utah Penitentiary, under protest of Albert P. Rockwood.

August 4, 1871 (Friday)

Briant Stringham, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

August 9, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 60 Saints, under the direction of Wm. Douglass. The company arrived at New York Aug. 21st, and at Ogden Aug. 30th.

August 11, 1871 (Friday)

Prof. J.D. Runkle, president of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, visited Salt Lake City. He was engaged in extensive explorations in Utah and Nevada.

August 23, 1871 (Wednesday)

A company for building the Utah and Northern Railway was organized, with John W. Young as president and general superintendent.

August 26, 1871 (Saturday)

Ground was broken for the Utah and Northern Railway, at Brigham City.

September 1871

At this time the U.S. officials in Utah acted more like bigoted missionaries than administrators of the law. Absurd rulings, illegal processes and packed juries characterized their proceedings.

September 1, 1871 (Friday)

The National Bank of Deseret commenced business on the corner of East Temple and First South Street, Salt Lake City.

September 2, 1871 (Saturday)

U.S. Marshal Patrick made a demand of Warden Albert P. Rockwood to deliver up the prisoner Kilfoyle to the marshal's custody, which was refused on legal grounds.

September 2, 1871 (Saturday)

The Deseret Telegraph Company extended a branch line to Coalville, Summit Co.

September 6, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 263 Saints, under the direction of John I. Hart. The company arrived at New York Sept. 18th, and at Ogden Sep. 27th.

September 8, 1871 (Friday)

After several days' preliminary examination before Associate Justice C. M. Hawley, Marshal McAllister and Warden Rockwood (Salt Lake City) were held to bail in $1,000 each to await the action of the grand jury.

September 11, 1871 (Monday)

A detachment of U.S. cavalry surrounded the houses of Messrs. John J. Baum and H.L. Davis, near Provo, Utah Co., and fired several shots at the former. A packed grand jury had indicted Baum and Davis for murder.

September 19, 1871 (Tuesday)

Caleb Parry, missionary from Utah, died at Birmingham, England. He was buried in the same grave as James H. Flanigan, who died Jan. 29, 1851.

September 22, 1871 (Friday)

James Hendry was shot and fatally wounded at Hooperville, by the father and son of a girl, whom he had seduced.

September 23, 1871 (Saturday)

The Utah Southern Railway was completed to Sandy.

September 24, 1871 (Sunday)

The corner stones of the new Catholic Church, in Salt Lake City, were laid, the ceremonies being conducted Rev. Patrick Walsh.

October 2, 1871 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young was arrested by U.S. Marshal Patrick, on an indictment charging him with lascivious cohabitation with his polygamous wives. The President was guarded in his own house for some time afterwards.

October 3, 1871 (Tuesday)

Daniel H. Wells was arrested by U.S. Marshal Patrick, on a charge of "lascivious and unlawful cohabitation," and placed under $5,000 bonds.

October 7, 1871 (Saturday)

Geo. Q. Cannon and Henry W. Lawrence were arrested on charges of lascivious cohabitation; Cannon was placed under $5,000 bonds.

October 9, 1871 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young went into court. After several days' trial, Judge McKean (on the 12th) rendered a decision, admitting the defendant to bail in $5,000, and the case was postponed until the prosecution was better prepared for action. In delivering his opinion the judge said that while the case was called "The people versus Brigham Young, its other and real title is Federal Authority versus Polygamic Theocracy."

October 10, 1871 (Tuesday)

Hon. O.P. Morton, senator from Indiana, accompanied by several distinguished ladies and gentlemen, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit. They used their influence against the Federal crusade, then being carried on in Utah.

October 11, 1871 (Wednesday)

A mass meeting convened in answer to the mayor of Salt Lake City, to adopt measures for the relief of the sufferers by the Chicago fire.

October 12, 1871 (Thursday)

A terrific wind storm visited Salt Lake City and vicinity.

October 14, 1871 (Saturday)

Mayor Daniel H. Wells remitted $12,000 for the relief of the sufferers by the Chicago fire. He subsequently sent another amount.

October 18, 1871 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 300 Saints, in charge of Geo. H. Peterson. The company arrived at New York, Nov. 1st, and at Salt Lake City, Nov. 11th.

October 23, 1871 (Monday)

The Deseret Telegraph line was completed to Pioche, Nevada.

October 24, 1871 (Tuesday)

Pres. Brigham Young left Salt Lake City for St. George, with the intention of spending the winter there. It was soon afterwards extensively published that he had fled from justice.

October 28, 1871 (Saturday)

Mayor Daniel H. Wells, Hosea Stout and W.H. Kimball were arrested on a trumped up charge of murder, the notorious outlay, "Bill" Hickman, being their accuser, and committed to the military prison at Camp Douglas.

October 28, 1871 (Saturday)

Thomas Hawkins was sentenced by Judge McKean to three years' imprisonment and $500 fine, for adultery with his own wives. He appealed his case to the Territorial Supreme Court, but not being able to get $20,000 bonds, he was imprisoned.

October 30, 1871 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Mayor Daniel H. Wells was admitted to $50,000 bail, for his appearance, when wanted, on the charge of murder.

November 2, 1871 (Thursday)

Captain Jacob Hamblin met in council with the principal chiefs of the Navajo Indians, at Ft. Defiance, and concluded a treaty of peace with them in behalf of the people of Utah.

November 6, 1871 (Monday)

James P. Brown, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Rockville, Kane Co.

November 9, 1871 (Thursday)

The site for the St. George Temple was dedicated.

November 9, 1871 (Thursday)

The Deseret Telegraph Company opened an office at Paris, Bear Lake Co., Idaho.

November 20, 1871 (Monday)

Elder Caleb W. Haws, missionary from Utah, died at Barugh Bridge, near Barnsley, Yorkshire, England.

November 20, 1871 (Monday)

The corner stones of the Methodist Episcopal Church, in Salt Lake City, were laid, Rev. Geo. M. Pierce officiating.

November 22, 1871 (Wednesday)

Ellen Sanders Kimball, one of the three Pioneer women of 1847, died near Salt Lake City, Utah.

November 22, 1871 (Wednesday)

Salt Lake City was entered under the "Town site law".

November 26, 1871 (Sunday)

The Roman Catholic Church in Salt Lake City was dedicated.

November 27, 1871 (Monday)

Through intense malice, Judge McKean called up the case of Pres. Brigham Young and thus compelled him to travel all the way from St. George to Salt Lake City in the dead of winter. The judge fixed the trial for Dec. 4th.

November 27, 1871 (Monday)

The Summit County Railway Company was organized.

December 1871

The Salt Lake City authorities arrested a number of prostitutes, who subsequently were released by the Federal officials.

December 13, 1871 (Wednesday)

Alexander Burt, John L. Blythe, James Toms and John Brazier were arrested in Salt Lake City, accused of the murder of Dr. J. King Robinson in 1866. On the 19th Policeman Brigham Y. Hampton was arrested on a similar charge.

December 18, 1871 (Monday)

An examination of the Robinson murder case was commenced before Justice McKean, in chambers; it was continued for several days. On the 22nd Alexander Burt, one of the accused, was discharged from custody.

December 22, 1871 (Friday)

Harriet Page Wheeler Young, one of the three Utah Pioneer women of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

December 23, 1871 (Saturday)

Patriarch John Murdock died in Beaver, Utah.

December 26, 1871 (Tuesday)

Pres. Brigham Young arrived in Salt Lake City from St. George.
