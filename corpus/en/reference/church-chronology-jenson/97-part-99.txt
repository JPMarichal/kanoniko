Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year a number of new Wards were organized in Salt Lake City and elsewhere; Pres. Francis M. Lyman visited Palestine and other countries; a new mission house was built and dedicated in Copenhagen, Denmark; a Church Bureau of Information was established on the Temple Block, in Salt Lake City, etc.

January 1902

Asahel H. Woodruff succeeded Walter C. Lyman in the presidency of the Northern States mission.

January 6, 1902 (Monday)

Reuben W. Dotson succeeded George Eyre as Bishop of Minerville, Beaver Co.

January 6, 1902 (Monday)

An important Priesthood meeting, attended by Pres. Francis M. Lyman and 134 other Elders and one sister from Zion (including three musical students), was held at Berlin, Germany.

January 7, 1902 (Tuesday)

Jeduthan Averett, a pioneer and a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Springville. He was born June 12, 1816, in Alabama.

January 13, 1902 (Monday)

Wm. B. Thornley, a prominent Church veteran, died in Layton, Davis Co.

January 19, 1902 (Sunday)

The Sixth Ward, Salt Lake City, was divided and the west part was organized as a separate Ward, called the Twenty-fifth Ward, with Carl A. Ek as Bishop.

January 22, 1902 (Wednesday)

Patriarch Henry Lunt died at Pacheco, Mexico. He was born July 20, 1824, in Cheshire, England.

January 26, 1902 (Sunday)

The east part of the Twenty-first Ward, Salt Lake City, was organized as the Twenty-seventh Ward with James Maxwell as Bishop.

January 26, 1902 (Sunday)

Wm. W. Williams succeeded Wm. H. Mendenhall as Bishop of Mound Valley Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho.

February 1, 1902 (Saturday)

Myron Shadrach Roundy, a pioneer of 1847, died at Kanarra, Iron Co.

February 2, 1902 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Cumberland, a coal-mining town in Wyoming, were organized as a Ward with George C. Wood as Bishop.

February 2, 1902 (Sunday)

The Kemmerer branch, in Uinta Co., Wyo., was organized as a Ward, with David McMillan as Bishop.

February 3, 1902 (Monday)

Preston Ward, Oneida Co., Idaho, was divided into four Wards with Henry T. Rogers as Bishop of the First Ward, Hugh S. Geddes as Bishop of the Second Ward, George H. Carver as Bishop of the Third Ward and Allen Riley Cutler as Bishop of the Fourth Ward.

February 3, 1902 (Monday)

The Saints residing in Diamondville, Wyo., were organized into a Ward with Andrew Easton as Bishop. A branch organization had existed there since May 16, 1897.

February 4, 1902 (Tuesday)

Pres. Francis M. Lyman left Liverpool, England, on a tour to Palestine. On the 6th he arrived at Paris, France, where he was joined by Pres. Sylvester Q. Cannon of the Netherlands mission.

February 8, 1902 (Saturday)

Patriarch Archibald Gardner, formerly Bishop of West Jordan and a prominent pioneer, died in Salt Lake City.

February 9, 1902 (Sunday)

At a meeting held in the Twenty-first Ward meeting house, Salt Lake City, that Ward was divided and the detached portions organized into two new Wards named respectively the Twenty-eighth Ward (William McMillan, Bishop) and the Twenty-ninth Ward (Theodore McKean, Bishop).

February 10, 1902 (Monday)

Henry Eyring, first counselor to Pres. Anthony W. Ivins of the Juarez Stake died at Juarez, Mexico. He was born March 9, 1835, in Germany.

February 16, 1902 (Sunday)

A part of the Fourth Ward, Salt Lake City, was organized as the Thirtieth Ward with Heber S. Cutler as Bishop.

February 16, 1902 (Sunday)

The Saints residing between 9th and 10th South streets and eat of 6th East street, Salt Lake City, were separated from the First Ward and organized as the Thirty-first Ward with Edwin S. Sheets as Bishop.

February 26, 1902 (Wednesday)

Pres. Francis M. Lyman and Elder Sylvester Q. Cannon, after visiting Marseilles, Alexandria, Cairo, Port Said and Beyrout, arrived in Jaffa, Palestine. From Jaffa the journey was continued by rail to Jerusalem.

February 28, 1902 (Friday)

Patriarch Albert Brown, a member of Zion's Camp and of the Mormon Battalion, died at Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

March 1902

Nephi Pratt succeeded Franklin S. Bramwell as president of the Northwestern States mission.

March 2, 1902 (Sunday)

A part of the Lyman Ward, Fremont Co., Idaho, was organized as a separate Ward called Archer with Geo. Briggs as Bishop.

March 4, 1902 (Tuesday)

Pres. Francis M. Lyman offered up a dedicatory prayer on the Mount of Olives, Palestine. Prior to this he and his companion (Sylvester Q. Cannon) had visited Jericho, the Dead Sea, the river Jordan, Bethlehem, etc. From Jerusalem the brethren returned to Jaffa, thence they traveled by way of Caesarea to Haifa, thence inland to Nazareth, the Sea of Galilee, etc.

March 8, 1902 (Saturday)

The Seventies residing in Weston and Dayton, Oneida Co., Idaho, were organized as the 142nd quorum of Seventy, with David J. Sandberg as senior president.

March 8, 1902 (Saturday)

Henry C. Rogers, counselor in the presidency of the Maricopa Stake, died at Mesa, Arizona.

March 8, 1902 (Saturday)

The first native convert to "Mormonism" in Japan, Hajime Nakazawa (formerly a Shinto priest), was baptized and confirmed at Tokyo.

March 9, 1902 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Union, Union Co., Oregon, were organized as a Ward with James England as Bishop.

March 11, 1902 (Tuesday)

Elder David Candland died in Mount Pleasant, Sanpete Co.

March 16, 1902 (Sunday)

Christian Christensen succeeded William R. Stevens as Bishop of Aurora, Sevier Co.

March 19, 1902 (Wednesday)

Pres. Francis M. Lyman and Elder Sylvester Q. Cannon left Haifa, Palestine by steamer for Beyrout; thence they traveled by rail to Damascus. Having returned to Beyrout, they sailed for Alexandretta, where they arrived on the 27th; thence they went inland to Aintab. On their return to England they visited Constantinople, Athens, Naples, Rome and other places of interest. Pres. Lyman returned to Liverpool April 1.

March 23, 1902 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held in Malad valley, and attended by Apostles John W. Taylor and Rudger Clawson, Oliver C. Hoskins was honorably released as president of the Malad Stake on account of failing health, and a new Stake presidency chosen and sustained as follows: Milton H. Welling, president; Wm. H. Gibbs, first, and Moroni Ward, second counselor.

March 25, 1902 (Tuesday)

James S. Brown, a Church veteran, a member of the Mormon Battalion and one of the discoverers of gold in California, died in Salt Lake City. He was born July 4, 1829, in North Carolina.

March 30, 1902 (Sunday)

The Bishopric of Redmond Ward, Sevier Co., was reorganized with Martin Jensen as Bishop.

April 1902

The 72nd annual conference of the Church was opened in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 6th. Orson F. Whitney, A. Milton Musser and Brigham H. Roberts were sustained as assistant Church historians. The attendance at this conference was greater than ever before in the history of the Church.

April 13, 1902 (Sunday)

A portion of Burton Ward, Fremont Co., Idaho, was organized as a separate Ward called Independence with Andrew P. Anderson as Bishop.

April 14, 1902 (Monday)

Sister Matilda M. Barratt died in Salt Lake City. She was born Jan. 17, 1837, in England.

April 17, 1902 (Thursday)

John B. Forster, one of the first fifteen persons baptized by Apostle Erastus Snow in Copenhagen, Denmark, in 1850, died in Salt Lake City.

April 20, 1902 (Sunday)

Daniel G. Miller was installed as Bishop of Parker Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho.

April 24, 1902 (Thursday)

Elder Peter Reid, a Church veteran and well known citizen of Salt Lake City died.

April 26, 1902 (Saturday)

Cacey P. Brown was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over Lyman Ward Fremont Co., Idaho.

April 27, 1902 (Sunday)

Philemon C. Merrill succeeded David H. Weech as Bishop of Pima Ward, Graham Co., Arizona.

April 28, 1902 (Monday)

The Saints residing between Blackfoot and Basalt, Bingham Co., Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church with John L. Wheeler as president.

May 3, 1902 (Saturday)

The Bishopric of the St. David Ward, Cochise Co., Arizona, was reorganized. John S. Merrill succeeded Peter A. Lofgren as Bishop.

May 18, 1902 (Sunday)

A part of Brighton Ward, formerly known as East Brighton, was organized as the Thirty-second Ward, Salt Lake City, with Robert Sherwood as Bishop.

May 18, 1902 (Sunday)

Judson B. Tomlinson, who labored as a missionary in Samoa, died at Fagalii, Samoa.

May 25, 1902 (Sunday)

Willard G. Homer succeeded Edwin S. Tuttle as Bishop of Haden Ward, Teton Stake, Idaho.

June 1902

Elder Levi E. Young succeeded David L. McDonald as president of the Swiss mission.

June 8, 1902 (Sunday)

Frank J. Stone succeeded Ebenezer Beesley, jun., as Bishop of Chapin Ward, Teton Stake, Idaho.

June 22, 1902 (Sunday)

The Darby branch, Teton Stake, Idaho, was organized as a Ward with Harold D. Winge as Bishop.

June 29, 1902 (Sunday)

Heman H. Hunter succeeded Oliver L. Robinson as Bishop of Egin (formerly Brighton) Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho.

June 30, 1902 (Monday)

The Southern States mission was divided, the States of North and South Carolina, Florida, Georgia, Mississippi and Alabama remaining in the Southern States mission with headquarters at Atlanta, Georgia, and Ephraim H. Nye as president. The northern portion of the Southern States mission was continued under the presidency of Ben E. Rich with headquarters in Cincinnati, Ohio, and called the Middle States mission.

July 1902

Elder William R. Cutler, who labored as a missionary in the Eastern States, died in his field of labor.

July 4, 1902 (Friday)

The new mission house erected in Copenhagen, Denmark, was dedicated by Pres. Francis M. Lyman. Among the 120 other Elders from Zion present on that occasion were Christian D. Fjeldsted, Andrew Jenson, Anthon L. Skanchy, Hugh J. Cannon and Levi E. Young.

July 20, 1902 (Sunday)

The Bishopric of Scofield Ward, Carbon Co., was reorganized by Apostle Hyrum M. Smith with George Ruff as Bishop.

July 21, 1902 (Monday)

John D. Chase, a member of the Mormon Battalion and a Utah pioneer of 1847, died in Huntington, Emery Co.

July 21, 1902 (Monday)

Elder Leslie C. Atwood, who labored as a missionary in New Zealand, died at Auckland, New Zealand.

July 26, 1902 (Saturday)

Mrs. Lucinda Morgan Howd, a Nauvoo veteran and a pioneer of 1847, died at Beaver City, Beaver Co.

July 27, 1902 (Sunday)

Nelson J. Hogan was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over Hatch Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, to succeed Peter J. Williams.

July 28, 1902 (Monday)

Oliver G. Workman, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Jan. 7, 1827, in Tennessee.

July 29, 1902 (Tuesday)

James W. Nixon succeeded Peter Johnson as Bishop of Huntington, Emery Co.

August 1, 1902 (Friday)

William S. Godbe, the leading figure in the so-called Godbeite movement in 1869, died at Brighton, Salt Lake Co.

August 4, 1902 (Monday)

A Bureau of Information was opened on the Temple Block, Salt Lake City, under the direction of Elders Benjamin Goddard, Arnold H. Schulthess and Thomas Hull. The object was to distribute Church literature and impart all necessary information to tourists and other strangers visiting Salt Lake City. A small building for the entertainment of strangers and for the distribution of literature had been erected on the Temple Block at a cost of $500. About seventy-five active members of the Church, of both sexes, were called to spend part of their time in entertaining visitors and escorting them around the block. During the first year of its operation the registry books indicated that 150,000 people were entertained.

August 7, 1902 (Thursday)

Elder James L. McMurrin, a prominent missionary in the Church, died in Salt Lake City.

August 17, 1902 (Sunday)

Stone Ward, Box Elder Stake, Idaho, was organized by Pres. Charles Kelly and others with Thomas W. Roe as Bishop.

August 23, 1902 (Saturday)

William Williams, a pioneer, died at Logan, Cache Co.

August 28, 1902 (Thursday)

Thomas Wall, a pioneer and a Black Hawk veteran, died at Diamond, Juab Co.

September 7, 1902 (Sunday)

A new Stake presidency was chosen at Cardston, Alberta, Canada. Pres. Charles O. Card was honorable released as president of Alberta Stake and Heber S. Allen chosen as his successor with Theodore Brandley as his first and Edward J. Wood as his second counselor.

September 7, 1902 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in a scattered condition west of Weston, Oneida Co., Idaho, were organized as a Ward called Cedarville, with William Georgeson as Bishop.

September 7, 1902 (Sunday)

The Indian Valley branch, Idaho, was organized as a Ward, with George W. Rose as Bishop.

September 13, 1902 (Saturday)

The Emmett branch in Canyon Co., Idaho, was organized as a Ward with Wilford E. Anderson as Bishop.

September 13, 1902 (Saturday)

The Saints residing at Bramwell, Canyon Co., Idaho, were organized as a Ward with Hans J. Jeppson as Bishop.

September 21, 1902 (Sunday)

Willard T. Cannon succeeded Sylvester Q. Cannon in the presidency of the Netherlands mission.

October 1902

William A. Moody was installed as Bishop of Thatcher Ward, Graham Co., Arizona, succeeding Isaac E. D. Zundel.

October 1902

Old Tabby, a noted Indian chief, died at Price, Carbon Co. at the age of 104.

October 4, 1902 (Saturday)

The 73rd semi-annual conference commenced in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 6th.

October 12, 1902 (Sunday)

Elder James C. Wood, counselor to the Bishop of the 10th Ward, in Salt Lake City, died there.

October 12, 1902 (Sunday)

James L. McLaren, the pioneer type founder in Utah, died in Salt Lake City. He cast type for the "Deseret News" for many years.

October 13, 1902 (Monday)

Joseph Foreman, a pioneer school teacher of Utah, died in Salt Lake City.

October 14, 1902 (Tuesday)

Patriarch Canute Peterson, president of the Sanpete Stake of Zion, died in Ephraim, Sanpete Co. He was born May 13, 1824, in Hardanger, Norway.

October 19, 1902 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Cove, Union Co., Ore., were organized as a branch of the Church with Josiah Richardson as presiding Elder.

October 28, 1902 (Tuesday)

Mary Ellen Kimball, widow of the late Pres. Heber C. Kimball, died in Salt Lake City.

November 4, 1902 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, the Republican ticket won; Hon. Joseph Howell was elected to Congress.

November 10, 1902 (Monday)

Bishop Lewis M. Grant died at his home in West Bountiful, Davis Co.

November 14, 1902 (Friday)

Orville L. Thompson succeeded Thomas Yates as Bishop of Scipio, Utah.

November 16, 1902 (Sunday)

At a quarterly conference held at Manti, Sanpete Co., Lewis Anderson was chosen president of the South Sanpete Stake with Joseph Y. Jenson as first and Gustav A. Iverson as second counselor.

November 18, 1902 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Vilate Young Decker, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Lewisville, Bingham Co., Idaho.

November 22, 1902 (Saturday)

The decision of the Bishops' court of the 12th Ward, Salt Lake City and the decision of the High Council of the Salt Lake Stake, excommunicating from the Church Otto Rydman, editor of the Swedish paper, the "Utah Korrespondenten," for apostasy, was sustained by the First Presidency.

November 24, 1902 (Monday)

The Salt Lake Ministerial Alliance adopted resolutions protesting against the candidacy of Reed Smoot for the U.S. Senate on the ground that he believed in polygamy, was an Apostle in the "Mormon" Church, etc.

November 26, 1902 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Ruth A. Reese Kimball, widow of the late Pres. Heber C. Kimball, died in Salt Lake City.

December 2, 1902 (Saturday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith stated to the Associated Press that the Church did not sanction, authorize or perform marriages contrary to law.

December 8, 1902 (Monday)

Elder John J. Fife, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died in Atlanta, Georgia.

December 13, 1902 (Saturday)

The Brigham Young College Society of American Archaeology was organized in Logan, with Prof. James H. Linford as president. The object of the society was the systematic and scientific study of the Book of Mormon.

December 13, 1902 (Saturday)

Bishop Niels Hansen died at AEtna, Canada. Later, Nathan W. Tanner succeeded him as Bishop.

December 14, 1902 (Sunday)

St. George, Washington Co., was divided into two Wards, namely, St. George East Ward with Isaac C. Macfarlane as Bishop, and St. George West Ward with James McArthur as Bishop.

December 24, 1902 (Wednesday)

Elder George W. Thatcher died in Logan.

December 24, 1902 (Wednesday)

Shadrach Holdaway, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Provo. He was born Oct. 15, 1822, in Tennessee.

December 27, 1902 (Saturday)

Wm. M. Shaw, a pioneer of 1847, died at Ogden.

December 28, 1902 (Sunday)

The Thirty-third Ward, Salt Lake City, was organized out of the east part of the Tenth Ward, with Robert A. Brighton as Bishop.
