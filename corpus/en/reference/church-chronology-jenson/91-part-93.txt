Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Utah was admitted into the Union as a State. A number of new settlements inhabited by Saints in Idaho were organized as Wards.

January 1, 1896 (Wednesday)

Elder Joseph Lawson died at Ogden, Weber Co.

January 1, 1896 (Wednesday)

Charles Bauer was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 3, 1896 (Friday)

Elder Hanmer Magleby died at Monroe, Sevier Co.

January 4, 1896 (Saturday)

Pres. Grover Cleveland signed the proclamation which admitted Utah into the sisterhood of States. The occasion was honored by grand celebrations in Salt Lake City and other places in the new State. Geo. M. Cannon was chosen president of the State Senate and Presley Denny speaker of the lower house.

January 6, 1896 (Monday)

The State officers were installed. Great crowds gathered in Salt Lake City to witness the procession and attend the ceremonies. The State legislature convened.

January 17, 1896 (Friday)

Elder Elijah Mayhew died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

January 19, 1896 (Sunday)

Elder John Telford died at Richmond, Cache Co.

January 21, 1896 (Tuesday)

Frank J. Cannon and Arthur Brown were elected United States senators from Utah, by the State legislature.

January 22, 1896 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake City were entertained at the Theatre.

January 24, 1896 (Friday)

James Stevenson was released from the Penitentiary, having been pardoned by Pres. Cleveland.

January 27, 1896 (Monday)

Senators Cannon and Brown were sworn in as Utah's first senators in the U.S. Congress.

January 31, 1896 (Friday)

Sarah Thompson Phelps, relict of Patriarch Morris Phelps, died at Mesa, Ariz.

February 9, 1896 (Friday)

The Stake Tabernacle at Brigham City, Box Elder Co., was destroyed by fire.

February 12, 1896 (Wednesday)

Elder Geo. Wm. Ingram died at Mesa, Ariz.

February 14, 1896 (Friday)

Bishop Henry Tingey died at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

February 19, 1896 (Wednesday)

Bishop Edwin Lucius Whiting died at Mapleton, Utah Co.

February 27, 1896 (Thursday)

In Judge Higgins' court, at Beaver, the long pending indictment against John M. Higbee, for alleged participation in the Mountain Meadow massacre, was dismissed.

March 4, 1896 (Wednesday)

Almera Smith Barton, a widow of the Prophet Joseph Smith, died at Parowan, Iron Co.

March 16, 1896 (Monday)

Benjamin Ralphs was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 22, 1896 (Sunday)

Crescent branch (formerly called Dry Creek), of the Draper Ward, Salt Lake Co., was organized as Crescent Ward; James P. Jensen, Bishop.

March 23, 1896 (Monday)

The Saints who had settled on Little Wood river, Blain Co., Idaho, were organized as a Ward by Apostle Francis M. Lyman; Geo. S. Harris, Bishop.

March 23, 1896 (Monday)

Moreland Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized by Apostle John H. Smith and the Bannock Stake presidency, with Hans Peter Christiansen as Bishop. On the same occasion the Saints residing at Blackfoot, Bingham Co., Idaho, were organized as Blackfoot Ward; Edwin Watson, Bishop.

March 24, 1896 (Tuesday)

Patriarch Jacob Weiler, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

March 27, 1896 (Friday)

Jane Wells Cooper Hanks, relict of Ebenezer Hanks, and an attache of the Mormon Battalion, died at Parowan, Iron Co.

April 3, 1896 (Friday)

A reunion of Australasian missionaries and other Saints was held in Salt Lake City by Zion's Maori Association.

April 4, 1896 (Saturday)

The sixty-sixth annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City; it was continued for three days. In voting for the general Church authorities, on the 6th, Charles W. Penrose was sustained as an assistant Church historian; Moses Thatcher, was not upheld as one of the Twelve, because of his refusal to sign a manifesto issued by the general authorities of the Church to the Saints, in which the leading men of the Church were requested to seek counsel before accepting political offices which would interfere with their ecclesiastical duties.

April 6, 1896 (Monday)

Edward Dalton, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Parowan, Iron Co.

April 13, 1896 (Monday)

The Tabernacle choir left Salt Lake City on a pleasure trip to California, having been invited to sing in San Francisco, and other cities of the Golden Gate.

April 13, 1896 (Monday)

Patriarch Wm. Derby Johnson died at Colonia Diaz, Mexico.

April 14, 1896 (Tuesday)

The Tabernacle choir gave an evening concert at Oakland, Cal., and the next night the programme was repeated in San Francisco.

April 19, 1896 (Sunday)

The Tabernacle choir gave a sacred concert in San Francisco, Cal.

April 20, 1896 (Monday)

The Tabernacle choir gave a concert at San Jose, Cal., and on Tuesday evening they gave their last concert in California, in Sacramento.

April 23, 1896 (Thursday)

The Tabernacle choir returned to Salt Lake City from their visit to California.

April 26, 1896 (Sunday)

Bishop Elmer Taylor died at Juab, Juab Co.

April 28, 1896 (Tuesday)

Susan A. Stringam died in Salt Lake City.

May 20, 1896 (Wednesday)

The dead body of a woman was found in the basement of the Scandinavian Methodist church, Salt Lake City, which subsequently led to the discovery of at least two murders, alleged to have been committed by Rev. Francis Herman, the pastor of the Church, who, however, had disappeared.

May 20, 1896 (Wednesday)

The Rio Grande Western Ry. was completed to Richfield, Sevier Co.

May 26, 1896 (Tuesday)

Geo. Ramsden, a gentleman long and favorably known in connection with Latter-day Saint emigration from Europe, died at Liverpool, England.

May 29, 1896 (Friday)

The Saints residing in the so-called Basin, Davis Co., were organized as Clinton Ward; Orlando Hadlock, Bishop.

May 30, 1896 (Saturday)

Israel Evans, who had served in the Mormon Battalion, died at Lehi, Utah Co.

May 31, 1896 (Sunday)

The Cannon Ward, Salt Lake Co., was organized by Geo. Q. Cannon, Apostle Abraham H. Cannon, and the Stake presidency; Lewis M. Cannon, Bishop.

June 1896

Fish and Game Commissioner A. Milton Musser was succeeded in office by Warden John Sharp. during the long period Com. Musser served in that capacity he introduced and planted in the public waters of Utah, practically without cost to the Territory, over eleven millions of choice fishes, which were brought from the Potomac, Delaware, Missouri, Mississippi and Illinois rivers, and from Lakes Michigan and Erie. They consisted of white fish, black bass, sunfish, shad, eel, perch, brook trout, crappie, rainbow trout, lake trout, scale, mirror and leather carp, catfish, and gold and silver fish.

June 1, 1896 (Monday)

The first number of De Ster (The Star), a monthly periodical, was published in Rotterdam, Holland, as the missionary organ of the Church, in the Dutch language.

June 4, 1896 (Thursday)

A branch of the Church was organized in Chicago, Ill., with Lars F. Soderlund as president. This was the first branch of the Church ever organized in that city.

June 5, 1896 (Friday)

A grand reunion, of Pacific Islands or Polynesian missionaries was held at Calder's Park, near Salt Lake City.

June 6, 1896 (Saturday)

The Saints who had settled a few miles west of Cardston, Alberta, Canada, were organized as the Leavitt branch.

June 7, 1896 (Sunday)

Warren Ward, Weber Co., was organized by the Weber Stake presidency; Wm. L. Stewart, Bishop.

June 8, 1896 (Tuesday)

Patriarch Ephraim K. Hanks, a Church veteran, and a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at his home, on Pleasant Creek, Wayne Co.

June 22, 1896 (Monday)

The board of county commissioners divided Salt Lake City into 63 voting precincts, and the county outside of the city into 35 voting precincts—87 in all.

June 22, 1896 (Monday)

Wm. Carter, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at St. George, Washington Co.

June 22, 1896 (Monday)

Frank Allen, a prominent young man of Taylor, Ariz., was murdered by white men, on the Navajo Indian reservation, Colo. Parts of his body were recovered by friends, Aug. 2nd.

July 1896

Heavy floods did great damage in Utah this month.

July 2, 1896 (Thursday)

Immense crowds gathered in Salt Lake City to witness a midsummer carnival, which was kept up three days.

July 6, 1896 (Monday)

Elder Andrew Jenson, on his special mission around the world, arrived at Jerusalem, Palestine.

July 16, 1896 (Thursday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County enjoyed a pleasant excursion to Ogden, where they were royally treated by the citizens.

July 19, 1896 (Sunday)

Apostle Abraham Hoagland Cannon died in Salt Lake City.

July 23, 1896 (Thursday)

Elder Rulon S. Wells, with Joseph W. McMurrin and Edwin F. Parry as counselors, succeeded Apostle Anthon H. Lund in the presidency of the European mission.

July 24, 1896 (Friday)

Elder George Mayer, a Church veteran, died at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

July 24, 1896 (Friday)

Dr. J.M. Benedict died in Salt Lake City.

July 26, 1896 (Sunday)

Morgan Henry Merrill, counselor in the St. Joseph Stake presidency, died at Thatcher, Graham Co., Ariz.

August 5, 1896 (Wednesday)

Sister Margaret M. Foutz, a survivor of the Haun's Mill massacre, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

August 8, 1896 (Saturday)

Elder Wilford E. Cragun died at North Ogden, Weber Co.

August 9, 1896 (Sunday)

Gray Ward, Bingham County, Idaho, was organized by Robert S. Bybee, on the Bingham Stake presidency. Geo. H. Muir was ordained Bishop by Apostle John H. Smith, Sept. 11, 1897.

August 11, 1896 (Tuesday)

The Arcadia branch of the Church, in the Bannock Stake of Zion, Idaho, was organized; M. Joseph Kerr, president.

August 13, 1896 (Thursday)

Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Geo. Q. Cannon, with their wives, left Salt Lake City on a visit to Oregon and California. They returned Sept. 16th.

August 16, 1896 (Sunday)

Twin Groves branch, Fremont County, Idaho, was organized as a Ward; Wm. D. Williams, Bishop.

August 19, 1896 (Wednesday)

Elder Andrew Bjorkmand, of Salt Lake City, Utah, died at Solfvarbo, Kopparberg's, Laen., Sweden, where he labored as a missionary.

August 23, 1896 (Sunday)

The Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co., was divided, and a new Ward called Forest Dale was organized out of its southwestern part; James Jensen, Bishop.

August 25, 1896 (Tuesday)

Sister Ellen Spencer Clawson, wife of Bishop Hiram B. Clawson, died in Salt Lake City.

August 28, 1896 (Friday)

Patriarch Hiram Mace, a Church veteran, died at Fillmore, Millard Co.

August 31, 1896 (Monday)

Members of the Tabernacle choir, Knights of Pythias band and friends (about four hundred persons altogether) left Salt Lake City on a special train for Denver, Colo., to participate in the Great Western Eisteddfod; they arrived in Denver the next day.

September 1896

Elder John J. Tanner, a member of Zion's Camp, died at South Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

September 1896

Elder Ezra F. Richards succeeded Elder Wm. Gardner as president of the Australasian Mission:

September 1896

This mission, which included Australia, New Zealand, Tasmania and some smaller islands, was opened in 1851, and John Murdock was the first president. He was succeeded in the presidency by the following Elders: Charles W. Wandell, June 2, 1852; Augustus Farnham, April, 1853; Absalom P. Dowdle, May, 1856; Andrew J. Stewart, June 7, 1857; Thomas Ford, May 30, 1858; Wm. Broadbent, 1863; Robert Beauchamp, 1867; Wm. Geddes, 1874; Job Welling, October, 1875; Isaac Groo, July 29, 1876; Fred J. May and Thos. A. Shreeve, August, 1878; Elijah F. Pearce, Dec. 25, 1878; George Batt, 1880; Wm. M. Bromley, Jan. 20, 1881; Wm. T. Stewart, July 17, 1883; Wm. Paxman, May 25, 1886; Angus F. Wright, Aug. 12, 1889; John S. Bingham, Oct. 6, 1890; Wm. T. Stewart (second term), Sept. 13, 1891, and William Gardner, Dec. 30, 1893.

September 1, 1896 (Tuesday)

Fred. H. Auerbach, a prominent merchant of Salt Lake City, died suddenly in New York.

September 1, 1896 (Tuesday)

The Great Western Eisteddfod commenced at Denver, Colo. The Tabernacle choir, under the direction of Prof. Evan Stephens, and the Knights of Pythias band, under the leadership of Prof. Anton Pedersen, all of Salt Lake City, Utah, competed very successfully for the prizes awarded the best performers. The Eisteddfod continued its session four days.

September 6, 1896 (Sunday)

The Tabernacle choir and their friends returned to Salt Lake City from their visit to Denver.

September 9, 1896 (Wednesday)

Elder Oscar O. Stoddard, a Church veteran, died at West Porterville, Morgan Co.

September 12, 1896 (Saturday)

Elder Thos. Rowberry died at Rexburg, Idaho.

September 14, 1896 (Monday)

Eleanor Snow, wife of Apostle Lorenzo Snow, died at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

September 14, 1896 (Monday)

Elder Daniel F. Miller succeeded Elder Frank Cutler as president of the Society Islands mission.

September 14, 1896 (Monday)

This mission, which includes the Society, the Tuamotu and the Austral Islands, and is the oldest Latter-day Saint mission in Polynesia, was opened in 1844. Noah Rogers was the first president. He was succeeded by Addison Pratt in July, 1845, and he in turn by Benjamin F. Grouard, in March, 1847. Addison Pratt, on his return from America, in 1850, presided a second time. After the banishment of the American Elders by the French, in 1852, native Elders kept up more or less missionary work for forty years. The mission was reopened by two Elders from Zion (Joseph W. Damron, and Wm. A. Seegmiller), in January, 1892, and Joseph W. Damron, presided. He was succeeded by James L. Brown, in June, 1892, who was succeeded by Elder Damron, in July 1893. Frank Cutler succeeded Elder Damron as president, May 11, 1895.

September 21, 1896 (Monday)

Elder Warren S. Snow died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

September 23, 1896 (Wednesday)

Miguel Ahumada, governor, and Joaquin Cortezar, secretary, of the State of Chihuahua, Mexico, arrived at Colonia Diaz, accompanied by other leading officials of the State, and attended the opening of the Third Annual Exposition of Colonia Diaz.

October 2, 1896 (Friday)

Elder Samuel W. Richards and other Utah Elders visited the hill Cumorah, State of New York.

October 4, 1896 (Sunday)

The general semi-annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City, continuing till Oct. 6th.

October 8, 1896 (Thursday)

The Sixteenth Infantry, U.S. army, which had been stationed at Fort Douglas for some time, left for Fort Sherman.

October 12, 1896 (Monday)

Bishop Robert Dansie died at Herriman, Salt Lake Co.

October 15, 1896 (Thursday)

The first company of the Twenty-fourth U.S. Infantry (colored troops) arrived at Fort Douglas. They were ordered to take the place of the Sixteenth, which had left. The bulk of the troops arrived on the 22nd and 23d.

October 18, 1896 (Sunday)

Patriarch John Stock died at Fish Haven, Bear Lake Co., Idaho.

November 3, 1896 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, the Democrats elected most of their candidates. In the national election Wm. McKinley, Republican, was elected President of the United States.

November 4, 1896 (Wednesday)

Patriarch John Brown, one of the Pioneers of 1847, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

November 14, 1896 (Saturday)

Lodi branch of the Church, Bannock Stake of Zion, Idaho, was organized with Hyrum Cunningham as presiding Elder.

November 15, 1896 (Sunday)

Sister Helen Mar Whitney, relict of Pioneer Horace K. Whitney, died in Salt Lake City.

November 18, 1896 (Wednesday)

Eli H. Murray, ex-governor of Utah, died at Bowling Green, Kentucky.

November 19, 1896 (Thursday)

At a council of the Apostles, held in Salt Lake City, Moses Thatcher was dropped from the council of the Twelve Apostles.

November 23, 1896 (Monday)

The Leavitt branch, in Alberta, Canada, was organized as a Ward; Frank Leavitt, Bishop.

November 24, 1896 (Tuesday)

Elder Henry Howell died at Fish Haven, Bear Lake Co., Idaho.

November 27, 1896 (Friday)

Elder Wm. S. Muir died at West Bountiful, Davis Co.

December 1, 1896 (Tuesday)

Elder Edward Phillips died at Kaysville, Davis Co.

December 2, 1896 (Wednesday)

Geo. Pierce Billings, a Pioneer of 1847, died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

December 11, 1896 (Friday)

Gov. Drake, of Iowa, arrived in Salt Lake City on a visit.

December 14, 1896 (Monday)

Tilden Ward was organized in Bingham County, Idaho, by Apostle John H. Smith and the Bingham Stake presidency; Geo. Y. Pugmire, Bishop.

December 25, 1896 (Friday)

Edward J. Wood succeeded Orlando Barrus, as president of the Samoan mission. This mission was founded in 1888 by Elder Joseph H. Dean, who was its first president. He was succeeded by the following Elders: Wm. O. Lee, August 16, 1890; Geo. E. Browning, Feb. 4, 1892; Ransom M. Stevens, Nov. 8, 1893; Thomas H. Hilton, May 17, 1894; John W. Beck, march 27, 1895; Orlando Barrus, April 22, 1896.

December 28, 1896 (Monday)

The mission house at Fagalii, near Apia, Samoa, was attacked by rebel natives, who destroyed considerable missionary property.
