Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Settlements of the Saints were located in Castle Valley, Utah; San Luis Valley, Colo.; and on Salt river, Ariz. Two Stakes of Zion were organized in Arizona. The Book of Mormon was translated into the Swedish language by August W. Carlson, and published at Copenhagen, Denmark, by Nils C. Flygare.

January 1878

The first Latter-day Saint settlers at Mesa, Maricopa Co., Ariz., located.

January 2, 1878 (Wednesday)

The first number of the Salt Lake Independent was issued in Salt Lake City. It only continued its career about two months.

January 14, 1878 (Monday)

The 23rd session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City, and organized by appointing Lorenzo Snow president of the Council, and Orson Pratt speaker of the House.

January 20, 1878 (Sunday)

Elder Llewellyn Harris arrived at a village of the Zuni Indians, in New Mexico. About four hundred of these Indians, who were suffering with small pox, were said to have been healed under his administration.

January 25, 1878 (Friday)

Ebenezer Brown, member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Draper, Salt Lake Co.

January 27, 1878 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the Little Colorado river, Apache Co., Arizona, were organized as a Stake of Zion, with Lot Smith as president, and Jacob Hamblin and Lorenzo H. Hatch as counselors. Geo. Lake was ordained Bishop of Brigham City (formerly Ballinger): Levi M. Savage, Bishop of Sunset. John Kartchner was appointed presiding Elder of Taylor, and John Bushman, acting Bishop of St. Joseph (formerly Allen). This was the first Stake of Zion organized in Arizona.

February 9, 1878 (Saturday)

Capt. John Robinson died at Birch Creek, Weber Co.

March 15, 1878 (Friday)

Philip T. Van Zile took the oath of office as district attorney for Utah.

March 16, 1878 (Saturday)

Major Howard Egan and Burr Frost, both Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

March 17, 1878 (Sunday)

Col. Stephen Markham died at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

March 20, 1878 (Wednesday)

Elder James Z. Stewart, of Draper, left Salt Lake City on a special mission to explore for a location, upon which the Saints, who emigrated from the Southern States, could settle. This mission led to the purchase of Mexican claims in Conejos County, Colorado, where settlements subsequently were made.

March 24, 1878 (Sunday)

Adamsville, Beaver Co., was organized as a Ward of the Beaver Stake of Zion; Joseph Henry Joseph, Bishop.

April 3, 1878 (Wednesday)

The Utah Northern Railway was sold at auction in Salt Lake City, the Union Pacific Railroad Company being the purchaser. The name of the road was changed to the Utah and Northern.

April 6, 1878 (Saturday)

The 48th annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City. It was continued till the 8th.

April 12, 1878 (Friday)

Elder E.W. Street, a young missionary from Utah, died at Breachwood, Green Heath, Herts, England.

May 17, 1878 (Friday)

Bishop David Brinton died suddenly at Big Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

May 17, 1878 (Friday)

Hon. E.B. Washburn and party arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 19, 1878 (Sunday)

Daniel R. Sellers and Mary A. Kirtland, with their respective families, arrived at a place near Los Cerritos, Conejos Co., Colo., as the first Saints from the Southern States to settle in San Luis Valley, which had been selected by the authorities of the Church as a gathering place for the Saints from the Southern States. Other families soon followed.

May 25, 1878 (Saturday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 354 Saints, under the direction of Thos. Judd. The company arrived at New York June 5th, and at Salt Lake City June 13th.

June 1878

A small four-page paper called the St. George Union was first published at that place by J.W. Carpenter.

June 1878

Grasshoppers did considerable damage in Utah.

June 1, 1878 (Saturday)

Berne, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, was organized as a branch of the Church, with John Kunz, sen., as president. The branch was organized into a Ward in 1890.

June 3, 1878 (Monday)

Isaiah Huntsman, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Annabella, Sevier Co.

June 6, 1878 (Thursday)

Richmond, Ray Co., Mo., was partly destroyed by a cyclone, in which a number of the old anti-Mormon mobocrats were injured and others killed.

June 8, 1878 (Saturday)

Lydia Partridge, relict of the late presiding Bishop Edward Partridge, died at Oak City, Millard Co.

June 9, 1878 (Sunday)

O. Porter Rockwell died in Salt Lake City.

June 11, 1878 (Tuesday)

About five hundred and fifty persons participated in the Salt Lake County Old Folks' excursion to Ogden, where the aged people had a splendid time.

June 15, 1878 (Saturday)

The steamship Montana sailed from Liverpool, England, with 221 Saints, in charge of Theodore Brandley. The company arrived at New York June 25th, and at Salt Lake City July 3rd.

June 19, 1878 (Wednesday)

Wm. V. Morris, a painter of ability, died in Salt Lake City.

June 22, 1878 (Saturday)

A party of eleven persons were accidentally drowned in Funk's Lake, near Manti, Sanpete Co., while boat-riding.

June 27, 1878 (Thursday)

Elder Daniel S. Thomas died at Lehi, Utah Co.

June 29, 1878 (Saturday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 569 Saints, in charge of John Cook. The company arrived at New York July 10th, and at Salt Lake City July 18th.

July 2, 1878 (Tuesday)

In a horse race, at St. Charles, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, Thomas G. Rich, son of Apostle Charles C. Rich, was accidentally killed.

July 5, 1878 (Friday)

Elder Joseph E. Hyde, who was returning from a mission to England, died on board the steamship Nevada.

July 6, 1878 (Saturday)

William Budge, of Paris, Idaho, arrived in Liverpool, England, as successor to Apostle Joseph F. Smith in the presidency of the European mission.

July 11, 1878 (Thursday)

John Whitmer, one of the Eight Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, died at Far West, Caldwell Co., Mo.

August 1, 1878 (Thursday)

A fire broke out in Alta, Little Cottonwood Canyon, destroying nearly the whole camp, except a few cabins. Loss: $100,000.

August 16, 1878 (Friday)

The tower of the St. George Temple was struck by lightning and slightly damaged.

September 3, 1878 (Tuesday)

Apostles Orson Pratt and Joseph F. Smith, accompanied by other Elders, left Salt Lake City on a special mission to the States.

September 6, 1878 (Friday)

Two small boys, sons of Joshua Terry, of Draper, Salt Lake Co., were buried in a sandbank and killed.

September 9, 1878 (Monday)

Apostles Orson Pratt and Joseph F. Smith visited Far West, Mo., after previously visiting David Whitmer at Richmond. They afterwards visited Kirtland, O., and the hill Cumorah, N.Y.

September 14, 1878 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 609 Saints, in charge of Henry W. Naisbitt. The company arrived at New York, Sept. 25th, and at Salt Lake City, Oct. 3rd.

September 21, 1878 (Saturday)

A small company of Saints sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Nevada, in charge of J.C. Christensen.

September 24, 1878 (Tuesday)

The Saints who were settling on Silver Creek, Apache Co., Ariz., were organized by Apostle Erastus Snow as a Ward; John Hunt, Bishop. The next day (Sept. 25th) Apostle Snow located the townsite, which was named Snowflake, in honor of Erastus Snow and Wm. J. Flake.

September 25, 1878 (Wednesday)

Joseph Farnsworth was accidentally killed, while working in a coal mine at Coalville, Summit Co.

September 27, 1878 (Friday)

Sam Kaealoi, a native of the Marquesas Islands, was accidentally killed on the Temple Block, Salt Lake City.

September 28, 1878 (Saturday)

The Saints who had settled near the top of the Mogollon Mountains, Arizona were organized by Apostle Erastus Snow as the Forest Dale Ward; Oscar Mann, Bishop.

October 6, 1878 (Sunday)

Bishop Hans Jensen and other brethren from Manti, Utah, arrived at Los Cerritos, Conejos Co., Colo., on a special mission to help locate the Saints from the Southern States in the San Luis Valley.

October 8, 1878 (Tuesday)

The trial of Sylvanus Collett for the murder of the Aiken party in 1857, commenced at Provo. On the 16th, after a long trial, the jury returned a verdict of not guilty.

October 12, 1878 (Saturday)

The Saints who were settling in the San Luis Valley, Colo., were organized as a branch of the Church with Bishop Hans Jensen as president, and John Allen and Soren E. Berthelsen as counselors. This was the commencement of settlements which afterwards became the San Luis Stake of Zion.

October 18, 1878 (Friday)

A destructive fire at the Ontario mine, near Park City, caused a loss of $100,000, and heavy consequential damage.

October 19, 1878 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 145 Saints, in charge of Aurelius Miner. The company arrived at New York Oct. 29th, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 6th.

October 25, 1878 (Friday)

John Miles was arrested for bigamy or polygamy, Caroline Owen, claiming to be his first wife, being the principal witness. The defendant was admitted to bail in $1,500.

October 31, 1878 (Thursday)

After several days' preliminary examination before Commissioner Sprague, John Miles, accused of polygamy, was again placed under $1,500 bonds, to await the action of the grand jury.

November 14, 1878 (Thursday)

On this and the following day the case of Geo. Reynolds was argued before the Supreme Court of the United States.

November 16, 1878 (Saturday)

A woman's mass meeting was held in the Salt Lake Theater, numerously attended and addressed by prominent ladies. Resolutions were adopted with unanimity, in which the "Mormon" women claimed ability and the right to represent themselves.

November 20, 1878 (Wednesday)

James Fielding, a Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

November 27, 1878 (Wednesday)

A letter was sent by Pres. John Taylor, directing the division of the settlements of the Saints in Arizona into two Stakes, making Berardoes (now Holbrook) the dividing point between the Little Colorado Stake, on the West, and the Eastern Arizona Stake, on the East.

November 28, 1878 (Thursday)

Apostle Orson Hyde died at Spring City, Sanpete Co.

November 30, 1878 (Saturday)

Washington Phipps was murdered by John H. Boynton, near Escalante, Iron Co.

December 9, 1878 (Monday)

Annie White and Mercy Robinson were burned to death at the Insane Asylum, near Salt Lake City.

December 21, 1878 (Saturday)

Apostle Orson Pratt, accompanied by Elder Brigham S. Young, again arrived in Liverpool, England.

December 24, 1878 (Tuesday)

Isaac Sampson, one of the early members of the Church, died at Glenwood, Sevier Co., Utah.
