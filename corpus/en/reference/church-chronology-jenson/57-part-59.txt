Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Utah was divided, and the western part organized into the Territory of Nevada. A large number of teams were sent to the Missouri river for the poor Saints. The U.S. soldiers stationed at Camp Floyd were withdrawn from Utah. the overland telegraph line was completed from the States via G.S.L. City to California. In the fall of the year a large number of people were called from the middle and northern counties of Utah Territory to settle in southern Utah, on the Rio Virgen and Santa Clara. The city of St. George and the towns on the upper Rio Virgen were located and the resources of the country rapidly developed. A missionary field was opened in Holland.

January 1, 1861 (Tuesday)

The 13th Ward assembly rooms in G.S.L. City were dedicated.

January 3, 1861 (Thursday)

Capt. David R. Evans died at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

January 19, 1861 (Saturday)

The Utah legislature adjourned.

January 29, 1861 (Tuesday)

Wm. S. Champlin, a survivor of the Haun's Mill massacre, died at Lehi, Utah Co.

February 1861

The 62nd quorum of Seventy was organized at G.S.L. City, with James F. Cleary, Wm. L. Brundage, Richard Golightly, Francis Platt, Henry W. Naisbitt, J.D. Ross and Claude Clive presidents.

February 2, 1861 (Saturday)

A band of thieving Indians (Goshutes) were taken prisoners by a posse of men, near Grantsville, Tooele Co., but a few days later they escaped, after shooting one of the guard.

February 6, 1861 (Wednesday)

By order of the commander the military post of Camp Floyd changed name to Fort Crittenden. Secretary of War John B. Floyd, after whom the camp originally was named, had allied himself with the South against the Union.

March 1, 1861 (Friday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Deseret, Millard Co., Utah, with Jacob Croft as president.

March 2, 1861 (Saturday)

A bill, providing for the organization of Nevada Territory out of the western portion of Utah, was approved by President James Buchanan.

March 4, 1861 (Monday)

A branch of the Church was organized in Round Valley (now Scipio), Millard Co., Utah, with B.H. Johnson as president.

April 6, 1861 (Saturday)

On this and the following day the 31st annual conference of the Church was held in G.S.L. City.

April 14, 1861 (Sunday)

Logan, Cache Co., was divided into four wards, with Benjamin M. Lewis, Henry Ballard, John B. Thatcher and Thos. X. Smith as Bishops, respectively.

April 16, 1861 (Tuesday)

The packet ship Manchester sailed from Liverpool, England, with 380 Saints, under the direction of Claudius V. Spencer. They arrived at New York May 18th.

April 23, 1861 (Tuesday)

The clipper ship Underwriter sailed from Liverpool, with 624 Saints, under the presidency of Milo Andrus, Homer Duncan and Charles William Penrose. The company arrived at New York May 22nd, and at Florence June 2nd.

April 23, 1861 (Tuesday)

From the 23rd to the 31st of this month upwards of two hundred Church wagons, with four yoke of cattle each, carrying 150,000 pounds of flour, left G.S.L. Valley for the Missouri river to bring in the poor. They traveled in four companies under Capts. Joseph W. Young, Ira Eldredge, Joseph Horne and John R. Murdock.

April 29, 1861 (Monday)

Elder Reynolds Cahoon died at South Cottonwood, G.S.L. Co., of dropsy.

May 15, 1861 (Wednesday)

Pres. Brigham Young and others left G.S.L. City on a trip to the southern settlements, from which they returned June 8th. A little later the President visited Cache Valley.

May 16, 1861 (Thursday)

The packet ship Monarch of the Sea sailed from Liverpool, with 955 Saints of various nationalities, under the direction of Jabez Woodard, H.O. Hansen and Niels Wilhelmsen. The company arrived in New York June 19th.

May 17, 1861 (Friday)

Gov. Alfred Cumming and wife left G.S.L. City, quietly, for the States.

July 1861

The rest of the army at Camp Floyd, or Fort Crittenden, was ordered to the States. In consequence of this, government property and outfit at Camp Floyd was sold at extraordinarily low prices. It was estimated that $4,000,000 worth of goods was sold for $100,000.

August 5, 1861 (Monday)

Paul A. Schettler and A.W. Van der Woude arrived as missionaries in Rotterdam, Holland. After laboring several months, they succeeded in organizing a branch of the Church of 14 members.

August 16, 1861 (Friday)

The first company of immigrating Saints of the season, which had left Florence May 29th, under Capt. David H. Cannon's charge, arrived in G.S.L. City. The company consisted of 225 persons, with 57 wagons.

September 2, 1861 (Monday)

A company of settlers left G.S.L. City for the Uintah country, intending to locate a settlement, in which, however, they did not succeed.

September 6, 1861 (Friday)

Apostles Orson Pratt and Erastus Snow arrived in G.S.L. City from a mission of gathering the poor Saints in the Eastern States.

September 12, 1861 (Thursday)

Captains Milo Andrus and John R. Murdock arrived in G.S.L. City with their respective companies of immigrants.

September 12, 1861 (Friday)

Captains Joseph Horne and Homer Duncan arrived in G.S.L. City with their companies of immigrants. Horne's company left Florence July 1st.

September 15, 1861 (Sunday)

Capt. Ira Eldredge's train of immigrants arrived in G.S.L. City, having left Florence June 30th.

September 21, 1861 (Saturday)

Wm. Cockcroft, the murderer of Robert Brown, was executed in G.S.L. City.

September 22, 1861 (Sunday)

Capt. Samuel A. Woolley arrived in G.S.L. City, with his company of immigrants, mostly Scandinavians.

September 23, 1861 (Monday)

The last Church train of the season arrived in G.S.L. City, under the direction of Capt. Ansel P. Harmon.

September 27, 1861 (Friday)

Capt. Sextus E. Johnson's company of immigrating Saints arrived in G.S.L. City, with about sixty wagons. This was the last company of immigrants that arrived this season.

October 3, 1861 (Thursday)

John W. Dawson was appointed governor of Utah.

October 6, 1861 (Sunday)

The semi-annual conference of the Church was commenced in G.S.L. City. It was continued three days. A number of brethren were called to settle in southern Utah and turn their special attention to the raising of cotton.

October 8, 1861 (Tuesday)

Parshall Terry died at Draper, G.S.L. Co.

October 18, 1861 (Friday)

The overland telegraph line was completed from the States to G.S.L. City. Pres. Brigham Young sent the first telegram, which passed over the line, to J. H. Wade, president of the company.

October 24, 1861 (Thursday)

The first telegram was sent from G.S.L. City to San Francisco by Pres. Brigham Young.

November 18, 1861 (Monday)

The Toquerville branch, southern Utah, was organized as a Ward, with Joshua T. Willis as Bishop.

November 28, 1861 (Thursday)

A company of Swiss Saints, under the leadership of Daniel Bonnelli, arrived at Santa Clara, southern Utah, having been called to settle there.

November 29, 1861 (Friday)

Apostles Geo A. Smith and Erastus Snow, Elder Horace S. Eldredge and others left G.S.L. City for southern Utah, with a view to locating settlements in the valleys of the Rio Virgen and Santa Clara for the purpose of raising cotton.

December 4, 1861 (Wednesday)

At a meeting of southern Utah settlers who had arrived from the north, it was decided, on motion of Apostle Erastus Snow, to build a city to be called St. George.

December 7, 1861 (Saturday)

John W. Dawson, Utah's third governor, arrived in G.S.L. City, accompanied by James Duane Doty, superintendent of Indian affairs.

December 9, 1861 (Monday)

Luke S. Johnson, once a member of the Twelve Apostles, died at Orson Hyde's residence, G.S.L. City.

December 9, 1861 (Monday)

The 11th annual session of the Utah legislature convened in G.S.L. City, and organized by electing Daniel H. Wells president of the Council and John Taylor speaker of the House.

December 13, 1861 (Friday)

The Saints who were settling Grafton, southern Utah, were organized into a Ward, by Apostles Orson Pratt and Erastus Snow, with Franklin W. Young as Bishop.

December 23, 1861 (Monday)

Peteetneet, the famous Ute Indian chief, died near Fort Crittenden, Utah Co.

December 31, 1861 (Tuesday)

Gov. John W. Dawson, left G.S.L. City for the States, under peculiar circumstances. Secretary Frank Fuller succeeded him as acting governor.
