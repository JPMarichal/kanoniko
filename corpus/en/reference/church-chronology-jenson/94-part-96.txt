Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

During this year a number of good and faithful men and women belonging to the Church died, including Apostle Franklin D. Richards. The missionaries laboring abroad met with considerable success in their administrations.

January 7, 1899 (Saturday)

Salt Lake City ministers, including Dr. Iliff, of the M.E. Church, W.M. Paden, of the First Presbyterian, and Clarence T. Brown, of the First Congregational Church, forwarded to Washington, D.C., a formal protest against the seating of Brigham H. Roberts in Congress.

January 9, 1899 (Monday)

The third Utah State legislature convened in Salt Lake City. Aquila Nebeker was chosen president of the Senate and Wm. M. Roylance speaker of the House.

January 12, 1899 (Thursday)

Elder Geo. Goddard, assistant superintendent of Latter-day Saints Sunday schools, died in Salt Lake City.

January 17, 1899 (Tuesday)

The Utah legislature began balloting for United States senator. Hon. Wm. H. King, Alfred W. McCune, Judge Orlando W. Powers, and Hon. Frank J. Cannon were candidates for the office.

January 24, 1899 (Tuesday)

Elder Edward J. Wood, who had presided over the Samoan mission since December, 1896, left Samoa, homeward bound, leaving the mission in the temporary care of Elder Wm. L. Worsencroft.

February 1, 1899 (Wednesday)

Arnold H. Schulthess succeeded Peter Loutensock as president of the German mission.

February 2, 1899 (Thursday)

The 116th quorum of Seventy was organized at Preston, Idaho, with Willard S. Fjeldsted as senior president.

February 5, 1899 (Sunday)

The 117th quorum of Seventy was organized at Lewiston, Cache Co., with Harvey M. Rawlins, jun., as senior president.

February 6, 1899 (Monday)

The 118th quorum of Seventy was organized at Paradise, Cache Co., with Samuel J. Holbrook as senior president.

February 7, 1899 (Tuesday)

Elders Christian D. Fjeldsted and J. Golden Kimball organized the 119th quorum of Seventy at Logan, Cache Co., with Hans J. Christiansen as senior president.

February 12, 1899 (Sunday)

Niels Hansen was installed as Bishop of AEtna Ward, Alberta, Canada, having succeeded Richard Pilling as acting Bishop Dec. 18, 1898.

February 17, 1899 (Friday)

The Utah State senate passed the bill for the removal of the University of Utah to the hill side east of Salt Lake City.

February 20, 1899 (Monday)

Elder Lewis Albert Warren, of Price, Carbon Co., Utah, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died with pneumonia.

March 2, 1899 (Thursday)

The mining town of Alta, situated at the head of Little Cottonwood Canyon, was destroyed by a snowslide. No lives were lost; the miners who were in the town took refuge in the mines.

March 5, 1899 (Sunday)

James Chadwick, formerly Bishop of Whitney Ward, Idaho, died at Preston, Idaho.

March 9, 1899 (Thursday)

The joint assembly of the Utah legislature adjourned sine die without electing a United States senator.

March 11, 1899 (Saturday)

Wm. H. Smart succeeded Alonzo P. Kesler as president of the Eastern States mission.

March 16, 1899 (Thursday)

The Utah legislature adjourned sine die.

March 20, 1899 (Monday)

The directors of the Deseret Agricultural and Manufacturing Society met and organized. John R. Winder was elected president and Septimus W. Sears secretary. Steps were taken toward the holding of a State fair this year and committees were appointed.

April 6, 1899 (Thursday)

The sixty-ninth annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City; it was continued until the 9th.

April 16, 1899 (Sunday)

The Saints constituting the south end of the Kanesville Ward, Weber Co., Utah, were organized into a separate Ward called Roy, with Thomas Hollands as Bishop.

April 23, 1899 (Sunday)

Grace Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, was organized, with Alma E. Hubbard as Bishop.

April 30, 1899 (Sunday)

The Saints who had recently settled in Preston, White River valley, Nevada, were organized as a branch of the Church, with Zebediah R. Bradley as presiding Elder.

April 30, 1899 (Sunday)

Capt. Wm. A. Brown, chief of police of Ogden, Utah, was shot and killed by two bandits near the Hot Springs, north of Ogden.

May 3, 1899 (Wednesday)

Elder Wm. G. Sears, who had been appointed to preside over the Samoan mission, arrived in Apia, Samoa.

May 10, 1899 (Wednesday)

The 120th quorum of Seventy was organized at Lake Town, Rich Co., with George H. Robinson as senior president.

May 14, 1899 (Sunday)

The beautiful new Tabernacle at Coalville, Summit Co., Utah, was dedicated.

May 20, 1899 (Saturday)

Bishop Peter C. Jensen, of Mantua, Box Elder Co., Utah, died, aged 76 years, after being Bishop twenty years.

May 28, 1899 (Sunday)

Lund Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, was organized, with Frederick H. Reddish as Bishop.

May 28, 1899 (Sunday)

The third general conference of the Y.M. AND Y.L.M.I. Associations convened in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

May 28, 1899 (Sunday)

Admiral Winfield S. Schley, the hero of Santiago bay, arrived in Salt Lake City, and visited the various points of interest.

May 31, 1899 (Wednesday)

Rev. H.B. Steelman, pastor of the First Baptist Church in Salt Lake City, made a characteristic "Christian" minister's speech, filled with misrepresentations of the Latter-day Saints, at the sixty-seventh anniversary of the American Baptist Home Mission Society, in San Francisco, Cal.

June 5, 1899 (Monday)

The sharpest frost, known for many years in the month of June, visited Utah and Idaho.

June 12, 1899 (Monday)

Frederick Kesler, Bishop of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City since 1856, died in Salt Lake City.

June 12, 1899 (Monday)

The 121st quorum of Seventy was organized at Cardston, Alberta, Canada, with Charles McCarty as senior president.

June 15, 1899 (Thursday)

Francis Armstrong, chairman of the Board of Commissioners of Salt Lake County and ex-mayor of Salt Lake City, died in Salt Lake City.

June 19, 1899 (Monday)

The Bishopric of Centerville Ward, Davis Co., Utah, was reorganized; Melvin H. Randall, Bishop.

June 20, 1899 (Tuesday)

Elder Hiram R. Hamson, of Oxford, Idaho, (a Mormon Elder) was accidentally drowned while bathing in Kenka lake, New York.

June 25, 1899 (Sunday)

Heber C. Iverson was installed as Bishop of the Second Ward, Salt Lake City, succeeding Leonard G. Hardy.

June 26, 1899 (Monday)

Geo. R. Emery was installed as Bishop of the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City, succeeding the late Bishop Frederick Kesler.

July 5, 1899 (Wednesday)

Bishop John P. Newman, who discussed polygamy with the late Orson Pratt in Salt Lake City in August, 1870, died in Saratoga, N.Y.

July 20, 1899 (Thursday)

Prof. Theodore B. Lewis, of Ogden, Utah, died in Boston, Mass., from the effects of an operation.

July 23, 1899 (Sunday)

Daniel Seegmiller, counselor to Pres. Edwin D. Woolley of Kanab Stake, was shot and killed by Wm. H. Roundy in Kane County, Utah.

July 30, 1899 (Sunday)

The Saints who had recently settled north of the Gray Bull river, in the Big Horn Basin, Wyo., were organized into a Ward with William H. Packard as Bishop.

August 4, 1899 (Friday)

Elder Arthur Stayner died in Salt Lake City.

August 5, 1899 (Saturday)

Elder Joseph Hogan, junior, of South Bountiful, Davis County, Utah, was accidentally drowned in the Rhine river, near Arnhem, Holland.

August 13, 1899 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled in Lucerne valley, Uintah County, Utah, were organized as a branch of the Church with Willis Twitchell as presiding Elder.

August 15, 1899 (Thursday)

The State of Ohio was transferred from the Northern States to the Southern States mission.

August 19, 1899 (Saturday)

The Utah volunteers, who had served in the Philippine Islands, arrived in Salt Lake City and were given an enthusiastic reception by the citizens. By proclamation issued by Governor Heber M. Wells the day was observed as a legal holiday.

September 18, 1899 (Monday)

The Saints residing in Caldwell, Alberta, Canada, were organized as a Ward with David H. Caldwell as Bishop.

September 21, 1899 (Thursday)

Elder Samuel Thomas Clark, of Newton, Cache Co., Utah, who labored as a missionary in Great Britain, died in Wales.

October 1, 1899 (Sunday)

The Bingham branch (Salt Lake Co., Utah) was organized as a Ward with William Thompson as Bishop.

October 3, 1899 (Tuesday)

The first State fair in Utah opened in Salt Lake City.

October 6, 1899 (Friday)

The seventieth annual semi-annual conference commenced in Salt Lake City; it was continued until the 8th.

October 7, 1899 (Saturday)

Charles Mostyn Owen filed a complaint against Pres. Lorenzo Snow, charging him with unlawful cohabitation, but on the 13th the county attorney of Salt Lake County refused to prosecute on the ground of insufficient evidence to convict.

October 8, 1899 (Sunday)

Bishop Edwin Stratford died in Ogden.

October 14, 1899 (Saturday)

Charles M. Owen filed a complaint against Congressman-elect Brigham H. Roberts, charging him with adultery.

October 22, 1899 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church, called the Academy branch of the Beaver Ward Beaver Co., Utah, was organized at what was formerly Fort Cameron, with Ernest D. Partridge as presiding Elder.

October 29, 1899 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized in Indian valley, Idaho, with Geo. W. Rose as president.

October 30, 1899 (Monday)

Apostle Marriner W. Merrill was chosen president of the Cache Stake of Zion; he selected Joseph Morrell and Isaac Smith as his counselors.

November 1899

The headquarters of the Northwestern States mission were moved from Anaconda, Mont., to Baker City, Ore.

November 7, 1899 (Tuesday)

At the municipal election, Ezra Thompson (Republican) was elected mayor of Salt Lake City.

November 20, 1899 (Monday)

Small pox was reported to have broken out in Sanpete County. From there it spread to different parts of the State of Utah.

November 21, 1899 (Tuesday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Imbler, Union Co., Ore., with Hans Westenkow as presiding Elder.

December 9, 1899 (Saturday)

Franklin D. Richards, president of the Twelve Apostles and Church Historian, died in Ogden. He was buried there on the 12th.

December 9, 1899 (Saturday)

Mrs. Emily Dow Partridge Young, wife of Pres. Brigham Young and daughter of Bishop Edward Partridge, died in Salt Lake City.

December 14, 1899 (Thursday)

At a meeting of the First Presidency and Apostles, held in Salt Lake City, Francis M. Lyman, Rudger Clawson and Andrew Jenson were appointed a Committee on Church Records.

December 26, 1899 (Tuesday)

Elder Charles W. Stayner died at his home in East Bountiful, Davis Co., Utah.

December 30, 1899 (Saturday)

Elder Marriner W. Merrill, jun., son of Apostle Merrill, died in Salt Lake City.
