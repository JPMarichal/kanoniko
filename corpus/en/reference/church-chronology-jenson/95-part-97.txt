Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The closing year of the Nineteenth Century marked an important epoch in the history of the Church and the State of Utah. Salt Lake County was divided into three and Sanpete County into two Stakes of Zion; a number of new Wards were organized in different parts of the country, and the Church membership increased both at home and abroad, notwithstanding the strong anti-Mormon sentiment that prevailed in the United States and the opposition encountered by the Elders in several foreign countries.

January 1, 1900 (Monday)

Louis S. Cardon succeeded Henry Bowman as president of the Swiss Mission.

January 4, 1900 (Thursday)

Six cases of small pox were reported in Salt Lake City, and the Board of Health advised the closing of the public schools.

January 5, 1900 (Friday)

Congressman Brigham H. Roberts argued his case before the Taylor committee, speaking for nearly five hours. He finished his speech the following day, after speaking seven hours.

January 8, 1900 (Monday)

Pres. Lorenzo Snow issued a proclamation, declaring that the Church had positively abandoned polygamy, and that if any member of the Church disobeyed the law either as to polygamy or unlawful cohabitation, he must bear his own burden and be answerable to the tribunals of the land for his own action pertaining thereto.

January 15, 1900 (Monday)

The Board of Health in Salt Lake City decided that unvaccinated children would not be admitted to the city schools.

January 17, 1900 (Wednesday)

The Taylor committee reached a conclusion in the Roberts case, two members rendering a report favoring admission and then expulsion, while six (the majority report) recommended that he be excluded without admission.

January 19, 1900 (Friday)

Horton D. Haight, president of the Cassia Stake, died at Oakley, Cassia Co., Idaho. Wm. T. Jack succeeded him as president of the Stake May 9, 1900.

January 21, 1900 (Sunday)

The Jordan Stake of Zion, with about 7,000 members, was completely organized. Orrin P. Miller was chosen president with Hyrum Goff and James Jensen as counselors. The following Wards, which hitherto had belonged to the Salt Lake Stake, constituted the new Stake at its organization: Bingham, Bluffdale, Crescent, Draper, East Jordan, Granite, Herriman, Riverton, Sandy, South Jordan, Union and West Jordan.

January 21, 1900 (Sunday)

Lars F. Johnson was installed as Bishop of Bear River City Ward, Box Elder Co., Utah, succeeding the late Bishop Carl Jensen.

January 25, 1900 (Thursday)

Elder Adolph C. L. Keilholtz, who labored as a missionary in the Netherlands, died at Arnhem, Holland.

January 27, 1900 (Saturday)

The Granite Stake of Zion was completely organized. Frank Y. Taylor was chosen president with James R. Miller as his first and Edwin Bennion as his second counselor. The following Wards were detached from the Salt Lake Stake to constitute the new Stake: Big Cottonwood, East Mill Creek, Farmers, Forest Dale, Granger, Hunter, Mill Creek, Mountain Dell, South Cottonwood, Sugar House and Taylorsville (North Jordan).

January 27, 1900 (Saturday)

The Salt Lake City schools were closed pending an appeal to the Supreme Court of a decision of Judge Cherry, ordering a writ of mandamus compelling the Board of Health to admit unvaccinated children.

January 27, 1900 (Saturday)

Governor Heber M. Wells issued a proclamation calling for a special election to be held Monday, April 2nd., to elect a successor to Brigham H. Roberts.

January 30, 1900 (Tuesday)

Elder George B. Wallace, formerly president of the Salt Lake Stake, died at Granger, Salt Lake Co.

February 1900

Frederick E. Barker, who had presided over the Australian mission since May, 1898 (when he took charge after Andrew Smith, jun.), was succeeded in the presidency of said mission by George H. Islaub.

February 1, 1900 (Thursday)

The Board of Health decided to open the Salt Lake City schools, as the smallpox epidemic had passed.

February 1, 1900 (Thursday)

Mrs. Catherine Salisbury, sister of the Prophet Joseph Smith, died at her home in Fountain Green, Illinois. She was born in Lebanon, New Hampshire, July 8, 1812.

February 5, 1900 (Monday)

The bodies of Dr. Harry A. Young and John G. Young, who were killed in battle at Manila Feb. 6, 1899, arrived in Salt Lake City.

February 11, 1900 (Sunday)

Impressive services in memory of the dead Utah heroes of Manila were held in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

February 11, 1900 (Sunday)

The Bishopric of Union Ward, Salt Lake Co., Utah, was reorganized with Willard C. Burgon as Bishop.

February 11, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints who had quite recently located on the land known as the Batapito ranch, in Sonora, Mexico, were organized as a branch of the Church (belonging to the Oaxaca Ward), with Alonzo S. Huish as presiding Elder. The place became known as Colonia Morelos.

February 12, 1900 (Monday)

The sectarian ministers of Salt Lake City issued a statement supporting a proposed amendment to the constitution prohibiting polygamy.

February 13, 1900 (Tuesday)

The bodies of five dead Utah batterymen were buried in the Salt Lake City cemeteries.

February 18, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints residing between Willow Creek and Shelton, in Bingham County, Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church with Parley J. Davis as presiding Elder. The branch was organized as the Milo Ward Sept. 23, 1900.

February 20, 1900 (Tuesday)

The Deseret Telegraph company's lines were purchased by the Western Union.

February 21, 1900 (Wednesday)

Elder Henry Ward Berg, of Provo, Utah, died in Christiania, Norway, where he labored as a missionary.

February 27, 1900 (Tuesday)

Elder Bryan Ward Peck, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died with measles in Putnam County, Tennessee.

March 14, 1900 (Wednesday)

Elder Jacob Johannes Martinus Bohn, a Church veteran, died at Murray, Salt Lake Co., Utah. He was an early convert to the Church in Denmark.

March 14, 1900 (Wednesday)

Patriarch John Keynaston died at East Bountiful Davis County, Utah.

March 23, 1900 (Friday)

Bishop Samuel Amos Woolley, of the 9th Ward, Salt Lake City, died. He was born in Pennsylvania Sept. 11, 1825.

March 26, 1900 (Monday)

Judge Charles Foote, of Nephi, Juab Co., died.

April 2, 1900 (Monday)

At the special election held in Utah, Hon. Wm. H. King was elected to Congress (to succeed Hon. Brigham H. Roberts), by the Democratic vote. Hon. James T. Hammond was the Republican candidate.

April 6, 1900 (Friday)

The 70th annual conference of the Church opened under favorable auspices in the Salt Lake City Tabernacle. It was continued until the 8th, on which date Reed Smoot was sustained as one of the Twelve Apostles to fill the vacancy caused by the demise of Franklin D. Richards.

April 15, 1900 (Sunday)

Elder Richard Fry, president of the Morgan Stake, died in Morgan, Morgan Co., Utah. He was born April 15, 1831.

April 15, 1900 (Sunday)

Jabez W. West was installed as Bishop of the 9th Ward, Salt Lake City, succeeding the late Samuel A. Wooley.

April 16, 1900 (Monday)

Bishop Thomas Maycock, of the 3rd Ward, Salt Lake City, died. He was born in 1832.

April 18, 1900 (Wednesday)

Pres. Geo. Q. Cannon was elected fourth vice-president of the Trans-Mississippi Congress.

April 22, 1900 (Sunday)

The Bishopric of Smithfield, Cache Co., Utah, was reorganized with Newton Woodruff as Bishop.

April 22, 1900 (Sunday)

A new Bishopric was installed at Richmond, Cache Co., Utah, with Thomas H. Merrill, Bishop.

April 22, 1900 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in East Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co., Utah, were organized as the 122nd quorum of Seventy, with Charles Stillman as senior president.

April 22, 1900 (Sunday)

Owing to prosecutions on the part of the civil authorities in Hamburg, the headquarters of the German mission were moved from Hamburg to Berlin. On Aug. 1, 1900, a comfortable mission office was opened at Frankfurter Allee, 196, Berlin.

April 24, 1900 (Tuesday)

Charles A. Harper, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Big Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

April 25, 1900 (Wednesday)

The Bishopric of Wellsville, Cache Co., Utah, was reorganized with Evan R. Owen, jun., as Bishop.

April 26, 1900 (Thursday)

The Supreme Court of Utah sustained the order of the Board of Education of Salt Lake City, to exclude unvaccinated children.

April 26, 1900 (Thursday)

The Bishopric of Mendon Ward, Cache Co., was reorganized with John H. Anderson as Bishop.

April 26, 1900 (Thursday)

The Saints residing in Kemmerer, a coal-mining town in Uinta Co., Wyo., were organized as a branch of the Church with Daniel Clark as presiding Elder.

April 27, 1900 (Friday)

Anton Anderson was installed as Bishop of the Second Ward of Logan, Cache Co.

April 28, 1900 (Saturday)

The Seventies residing in the 1st and 2nd Wards of Provo, Utah Co., Utah, were organized as the 123rd quorum of Seventy with Joseph T. McEwan as senior president.

May 1, 1900 (Tuesday)

An explosion in Mine No. 4 at Winter Quarters, near Scofield, Utah, killed about two hundred miners, including many members of the Church.

May 5, 1900 (Saturday)

Two funeral trains carried victims from Scofield to various parts of the State, where the killed miners had resided. Governor Heber M. Wells appealed to the public for aid in behalf of the sufferers.

May 6, 1900 (Sunday)

Apostles Heber J. Grant, George Teasdale and Reed Smoot attended memorial services in the meeting house at Scofield.

May 10, 1900 (Thursday)

The Republicans elected Arthur Brown, George Sutherland, Heber M. Wells, Thomas Kearns, C.E. Loose and George M. Hanson as delegates to the National Presidential Nomination Convention at Philadelphia.

May 11, 1900 (Friday)

The Church appropriated $2,500 to the Schofield relief fund.

May 11, 1900 (Friday)

Samuel L. Sprague, a conspicuous figure in the history of Utah, died in Salt Lake City.

May 13, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at Alice, Union Co., Ore., were organized as a branch of the Church with Leonard J. Jordan as president.

May 14, 1900 (Monday)

The Seventies residing in the 18th Ward, Salt Lake City, Utah, were organized as the 12th quorum of Seventy with Bernard H. Schettler as senior president.

May 15, 1900 (Tuesday)

Elder John Kienke died at Nephi, Juab Co.

May 16, 1900 (Wednesday)

Cyrus Sandford, a pioneer of Springville, Utah Co., died.

May 17, 1900 (Thursday)

Ex-Bishop George Halliday died in Santaquin, Utah Co., Utah.

May 19, 1900 (Saturday)

At a special meeting held at Moab, Grand Co., Utah, attended by Apostle Brigham Young and Pres. Seymour B. Young, the Seventies residing at Moab and other places were organized as the 125th quorum of Seventy with Joseph H. Dean, of Mancos, Colo., as senior president.

May 20, 1900 (Sunday)

The Brooklin Branch of the Elsinor Ward (Sevier Co., Utah) was organized as a Ward with Bryant Jolley as Bishop.

May 22, 1900 (Tuesday)

Stephen Kelsey, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died in Paris, Idaho.

May 23, 1900 (Wednesday)

Mrs. Emily Smith Patten, wife of John Patten, died at Manti, Utah.

May 27, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints who were locating on the Shoshone river, Wyo., were organized as a branch of the Church with Fred. Kohler as presiding Elder. The settlement soon became known as Byron.

May 28, 1900 (Monday)

Elder Wm. J. Beecher, a missionary laboring in California, was found dead in his bed from asphyxiation by gas.

May 31, 1900 (Thursday)

Pres. Lorenzo Snow, in behalf of the Church, donated to the Latter-day Saints' College a land grant north of the old "Deseret News" corner.

May 31, 1900 (Thursday)

A Ward called Taylor was organized in Alberta, Canada, with Geo. A. Nelson as Bishop.

May 31, 1900 (Thursday)

Haskel S. Jolley resigned his position as Bishop of Mt. Carmel Ward, Kane Co., Utah, and Hans C. Sorenson was appointed presiding Elder of that settlement.

June 1, 1900 (Friday)

John Jaques, assistant Church Historian, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Jan. 7, 1827, at Bosworth, Leicestershire, England.

June 2, 1900 (Saturday)

Moses D. Harris was honorably released as Bishop of Glendale Ward, Kane Co., Utah, and James W. Watson appointed presiding Elder of the settlement.

June 2, 1900 (Saturday)

Governor Heber M. Wells issued a proclamation appealing for means to help the starving millions in India, where about 10,000 persons at that time died daily.

June 5, 1900 (Tuesday)

The headquarters of the Northwestern States mission were moved from Baker City to La Grande, Ore.

June 6, 1900 (Wednesday)

Elder George H. Islaub, who had presided over the Australian mission a few months, sailed from Sydney, homeward bound, leaving the mission in temporary charge of John. B. Matthias.

June 14, 1900 (Thursday)

The Scandinavian Jubilee, celebrating the fiftieth anniversary of the introduction of the Gospel into the Scandinavian countries, opened with a meeting in the Assembly Hall, Salt Lake City, presided over by Apostle Anthon H. Lund. The festivities were continued until June 17th. Among the surviving pioneer missionaries from Scandinavia present on that occasion were Christian D. Fjeldsted, Canute Peterson, Ole N. Liljenquist, Carl C.A. Christensen, Christian J. Larsen, etc. During the festivities speeches were delivered by Apostles Anthon H. Lund and Reed Smoot, John Smith, Christian D. Fjeldsted, Andrew Jenson, Janne M. Sjodahl, Ole H. Berg and many others.

June 17, 1900 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in the Pocatello Stake, Idaho, were organized as the 126th quorum of Seventy with Benjamin W. Henderson as senior president.

June 17, 1900 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at La Grande, Union Co., Ore., with James England as presiding Elder.

June 23, 1900 (Saturday)

The Grass Creek branch (in Summit Co., Utah) was organized as a Ward with John F. Salmon as Bishop.

June 25, 1900 (Monday)

Pres. George Q. Cannon laid the corner stone of the Stake Academy at Rexburg, Idaho.

June 28, 1900 (Thursday)

The Baker City branch of the Church (in Oregon) was transferred from the Oneida Stake to the Northwestern States mission.

June 29, 1900 (Friday)

Andrew C. Brixen, manager of the Brigham Young Trust company, died in Salt Lake City.

July 15, 1900 (Sunday)

Thos. S. Browning was chosen and ordained Bishop of the Third Ward, Salt Lake City, succeeding the late Thos. Maycock.

July 17, 1900 (Tuesday)

The Saints living in Sunnyside (a coal mining camp in Carbon Co., Utah) were organized as a Ward with John Potter as Bishop.

July 20, 1900 (Friday)

Bishop John Lewis died in Provo, Utah. He was born Sept. 10, 1831, in Wales.

July 24, 1900 (Tuesday)

The exercises in the final unveiling of the Brigham Young monument in Salt Lake City were attended by over three thousand persons. Addresses were made by Pres. George Q. Cannon, Secretary of State James T. Hammond and Sculptor C.E. Dallin. Miss Margaret Young, great granddaughter of the late Pres. Brigham Young, unveiled the monument.

July 26, 1900 (Thursday)

Apostle Anthon H. Lund was chosen as General Church Historian, to succeed the late Apostle Franklin D. Richards.

July 28, 1900 (Saturday)

Prof. James S. Linford was elected president of the B.Y. College at Logan, Utah.

July 29, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints residing on the west side of the Teton river, opposite Driggs, in Teton Basin, Bingham Co., Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church with William I. Norton as presiding Elder.

August 5, 1900 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church called Springville (afterwards changed to Mt. Glen) was organized five miles northeast of La Grande, Ore., with Edward D. Whiting as president.

August 7, 1900 (Tuesday)

Alfred Nilson, a musician and actor, died in Bonneville, Davis Co., Utah. He was born in Lund, Sweden, in 1852.

August 9, 1900 (Thursday)

The Black Hawk war veterans held a reunion at Lake Shore, Utah Co., Utah.

August 10, 1900 (Friday)

Hon. William H. Creer, a citizen of Spanish Fork, Utah Co., Utah, died there.

August 10, 1900 (Friday)

Alma Harris, one of the oldest residents of Logan, Utah, died.

August 16, 1900 (Thursday)

Patriarch Thomas Waddoups died at Centreville, Davis Co., Utah.

August 17, 1900 (Friday)

Patriarch Joseph Bates Noble, of Bountiful, Davis Co., Utah, died at Wardsboro, Idaho.

August 20, 1900 (Monday)

Culbert L. King was installed as Bishop of Marion Ward, Garfield Co., Utah.

August 23, 1900 (Thursday)

The Bishopric of Cannonville, Garfield Co., Utah, was reorganized with James N. Henderson as Bishop.

August 23, 1900 (Thursday)

William H. Smart was released from presiding over the Eastern States mission and was succeeded by Elder Howard H. Snow, of St. George.

August 23, 1900 (Thursday)

Elder Hannibal L. Christensen, a missionary on his way to Denmark, died in Liverpool, England.

August 24, 1900 (Friday)

Joseph W. Jolley was appointed Bishop of Tropic Ward, Garfield Co., Utah.

August 26, 1900 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference of the Panguitch Stake, David Cameron was installed as president of said Stake (succeeding Jesse W. Crosby, jun., who had moved away) with Moroni M. Steele as first and James Houston as second counselor. James B. Heywood was chosen as Bishop of Panguitch Ward, succeeding Allen Miller, honorably released.

August 27, 1900 (Monday)

Elder Robert A. Paxton, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died at Blacksburg, Cherokee Co., South Carolina.

August 27, 1900 (Monday)

Rasmus Lynn succeeded Aaron Asay as Bishop of Hatch Ward, Garfield Co. Utah.

August 28, 1900 (Tuesday)

Royal James Cutler, jun., was installed as Bishop of Glendale Ward, Kane Co., Utah, succeeding presiding Elder James W. Watson.

August 30, 1900 (Thursday)

Pres. Alfred A. Farrell sailed from Rotterdam, Holland, homeward bound. Sylvester Q. Cannon succeeded him in the presidency of the Netherlands mission.

August 30, 1900 (Thursday)

Hans C. Sorensen was installed as Bishop of Mt. Carmel Ward, Kane Co., Utah.

August 31, 1900 (Friday)

Bishop Thomas J. Stevens died in Ogden, Utah. He was born in Bristol, England, Jan. 24, 1848.

September 9, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at Goshen, Bingham Co., Idaho, were separated from Basalt, and organized as a Ward with Lars Sorensen as Bishop.

September 9, 1900 (Sunday)

The 127th quorum of Seventy was organized at Lehi, Utah Co., Utah, with Jesse Smith as senior president.

September 9, 1900 (Sunday)

Daniel Heiner was sustained as president of the Morgan Stake, succeeding the late Pres. Richard Fry. Soon afterwards William H. Rich was chosen as his first and William W. Francis as his second counselor.

September 12, 1900 (Wednesday)

Elder John Dye Frankland, of the 12th Ward, Salt Lake City, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died at Ausley, Alabama.

September 16, 1900 (Sunday)

The 128th quorum of Seventy was organized at American Fork, Utah Co., Utah, with Henry Moyle as senior president.

September 17, 1900 (Monday)

Elder Carl W. J. Hecker, an early missionary in Scandinavia, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., Utah.

September 23, 1900 (Sunday)

The 130th quorum of Seventy was organized in Bingham Co., Idaho, with David Kingham, of Lewisville, as senior president.

September 23, 1900 (Sunday)

Elder Christian Christensen (born in Denmark Oct. 7, 1842) died in Manti, Sanpete Co., Utah. He was the first man ordained to the office of an Elder in the Scandinavian mission.

September 26, 1900 (Wednesday)

Simon P. Eggertsen, a Church veteran, died at Provo, Utah.

September 30, 1900 (Sunday)

Mill Creek Ward, Salt Lake Co., Utah, was divided and its eastern part organized into a new Ward called Wilford with James D. Cummings as Bishop.

September 30, 1900 (Sunday)

Dr. John R. Park died in Salt Lake City.

October 5, 1900 (Friday)

The seventy-first annual conference of the Church opened in the Salt Lake City Tabernacle, Pres. Lorenzo Snow presiding. It was continued until the 7th.

October 6, 1900 (Saturday)

Elder Henry Donerald Bronson, of Oneida Co., Idaho, was drowned in the Oconee river, Georgia, in trying to ford it.

October 13, 1900 (Saturday)

Bishop Joseph S. Rawlins died at his home in South Cottonwood, Utah. He was born April 9, 1823.

October 14, 1900 (Sunday)

The Saints who were settling on Sage Creek (in Big Horn Co., Wyo.) were organized as a branch of the Church called Shoshone with William C. Partridge as presiding Elder. This branch subsequently became Cowley Ward.

October 15, 1900 (Monday)

Samuel Stephen White, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., Utah.

October 18, 1900 (Thursday)

Prof. Joseph W. Whiteley died suddenly in Salt Lake City, aged fifty-nine years.

Elder Solomon Eliphet Hale, who labored as a missionary in the Eastern States, died with typhoid fever, at Great Falls, Maryland.

October 28, 1900 (Sunday)

The South Cottonwood Ward was divided into three Wards, one part of which was organized as the Murray Ward with Uriah G. Miller as Bishop.

October 28, 1900 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in Salem, Lake Shore and Leland, Utah Co., Utah were organized as the 129th quorum of Seventy with Zachariah S. Taylor, of Salem, as senior president.

October 30, 1900 (Tuesday)

On this date Utah's population was 276,565, showing an increase of 68,660 in ten years.

November 4, 1900 (Sunday)

John Watson was installed as Bishop of the Fifth Ward, Ogden, succeeding the late Bishop Thomas J. Stevens.

November 6, 1900 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, the Republican ticket won by a large majority.

November 8, 1900 (Thursday)

The Saints who had located in Spring Valley, Uinta Co., Wyo., were organized as a branch of the Church with Edward Burton as president. Most of the members of the new branch had recently moved in from Almy, Wyo.

A monument, erected over the grave of Pres. Wilford Woodruff, was dedicated at the Salt Lake City cemetery.

November 10, 1900 (Saturday)

The Chuichupa branch, in Chihuahua, Mexico, was organized as a Ward with Geo. M. Haws as Bishop.

November 11, 1900 (Sunday)

November 17, 1900 (Saturday)

Edward Partridge, president of the Utah Stake of Zion, died in Provo, Utah. He was born in Independence, Jackson Co., Missouri, June 25, 1833.

November 19, 1900 (Monday)

The Seventies residing in Hyde Park and Benson, Cache Co., Utah, were organized as the 132nd quorum of Seventy with Joseph Waite, of Hyde Park, as senior president.

November 24, 1900 (Saturday)

Elder Henry W. Bigler died in St. George, Utah. He was born Nov. 24, 1815, in Harrison Co., West Virginia.

November 25, 1900 (Sunday)

The 58th quorum of Seventy, located in Brigham City, Box Elder Co., Utah, was divided and a new quorum called the 133rd organized with Oluff Peterson as senior president.

Pres. Francis A. Hammond, of San Juan Stake, was killed in a runaway in Bloomfield, New Mexico. He was born in Long Island, N.Y., Dec. 31, 1827.

Andrew Fjeld took the presidency of the Australian mission, succeeding Elder John B. Matthias, president pro tem.

November 29, 1900 (Thursday)

Elder Simpson M. Molen, formerly counselor in the Cache Stake presidency, died in Hyrum, Utah.

November 29, 1900 (Thursday)

Pres. Geo. Q. Cannon, Elder Wm. W. Cluff and seven others left Salt Lake City, Utah, on a visit to Hawaii. They arrived in Honolulu Dec. 10th.

December 1900

The headquarters of the Southwestern States mission were moved from St. John, Kansas, to Kansas City, Missouri.

December 2, 1900 (Sunday)

Sarah A. Snow, wife of Pres. Lorenzo Snow, died in Brigham City, aged 74 years.

December 5, 1900 (Wednesday)

The Inter-Denominational Council of Women in New York passed a resolution, urging Congress to pass a constitutional amendment prohibiting polygamy and disfranchising all polygamists.

December 8, 1900 (Saturday)

Thomas Taylor, formerly a Bishop of Salt Lake City, died in Los Angeles, Cal.

December 9, 1900 (Sunday)

The Sanpete Stake of Zion was divided into two Stakes, to wit, the North and the South Sanpete Stakes. Canute Peterson was continued as president of the South Sanpete Stake and Christian N. Lund was chosen as president of the North Sanpete Stake. The latter Stake, when organized, consisted of the following Wards which formerly belonged to the Sanpete Stake: Chester, Fairview, Fountain Green, Freedom, Indianola, Milburn, Moroni, Mount Pleasant, Spring City and Wales.

December 9, 1900 (Sunday)

The Congregationalists of Washington passed resolutions urging a constitutional amendment against polygamy.

December 11, 1900 (Tuesday)

Elder Laban Morrill died at Junction, Piute Co., Utah.

December 12, 1900 (Wednesday)

The fiftieth anniversary of the introduction of the Gospel to the Sandwich Islands was celebrated at Honolulu, Hawaii. Pres. George Q. Cannon was the honored guest of the occasion. Over twelve hundred people were present, many having come from other islands.

December 14, 1900 (Thursday)

Ex-Bishop Andrew Cahoon died in Murray, Salt Lake Co., Utah.

December 16, 1900 (Sunday)

The Fairview branch of Bear River Ward, Box Elder Co., Utah, was organized as a Ward named Manilla with Peter M. Hansen as Bishop. In November, 1901, the name was changed to Elwood.

December 16, 1900 (Sunday)

A part of South Cottonwood Ward, Salt Lake Co. was organized as a separate Ward called Grant, with Peter J. Sanders as Bishop.

December 21, 1900 (Friday)

Ira Allen (aged 86 years), founder of Hyrum, Cache Co., Utah, died.

December 22, 1900 (Saturday)

Duncan Chapman, a veteran of the Church and a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Caineville, Wayne Co., Utah.

December 26, 1900 (Wednesday)

Jane Ballantyne Taylor, wife of Pres. John Taylor, died in Salt Lake City.

December 31, 1900 (Monday)

According to the official reports of this date, the Church consisted of 206,344 baptized members, including 3 members of the First Presidency, 12 Apostles, 197 Patriarchs, 6,194 High Priests, 8,425 Seventies, 17,994 Elders, 3,924 Priests, 4,434 Teachers, 15,420 Deacons and 149,741 lay members. Adding 77,452 children under eight years old (not baptized), the numerical strength of the Church was 283,796.

December 31, 1900 (Monday)

At the close of the century the Church in its gathered condition was organized into forty-three Stakes of Zion, composed of nearly five hundred and fifty Bishops' Wards. This included one Stake in Canada and one in Mexico. Thirty Stakes had headquarters in Utah, seven in Idaho, four in Arizona, one in Wyoming and one in Colorado. There were also settlements of Latter-day Saints in New Mexico, Nevada and Oregon, but their headquarters were in adjoining States.
