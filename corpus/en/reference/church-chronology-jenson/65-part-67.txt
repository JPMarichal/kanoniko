Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year the Saints residing in Millard and Beaver Counties and in Bear Lake Valley were organized into Stakes of Zion. The great Pacific railroad was completed through the Territory and a branch road built from Ogden to Salt Lake City. Missionary labor was considerably revived in the United States.

January 1869

The first general directory of Salt Lake City was compiled by Edward L. Sloan.

January 1, 1869 (Friday)

The first number of Der Stern, a monthly 16-page octavo periodical, published in the interest of the Church in Switzerland, in the German language, was issued in Zurich; Karl G. Maeser, editor and publisher.

January 11, 1869 (Monday)

The 18th annual session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City and organized with Geo. A. Smith president of the Council and Orson Pratt speaker of the House.

January 15, 1869 (Friday)

Ira Ames, a true and faithful Elder, died at Wellsville, Cache Co.

January 15, 1869 (Friday)

The end of the Union Pacific Railroad track reached Echo, Summit.

January 19, 1869 (Tuesday)

Elder John Mace, missionary from Utah, died in Leeds, England.

January 21, 1869 (Thursday)

An observatory was erected on the south-east corner of the Temple Block, Salt Lake City.

January 23, 1869 (Saturday)

Miss Augusta St. Clair, a talented lecturess, died in Salt Lake City.

January 29, 1869 (Friday)

Chauncey W. Millard, a murderer, was executed at Provo.

February 1869

Patriarch Asahel Perry died at Springville, Utah Co.

February 3, 1869 (Wednesday)

Simeon Carter, formerly a member of Zion's Camp and prominent in the Church, died at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

February 15, 1869 (Monday)

Rio Virgen County, Utah, was created by act of the Utah legislature.

February 19, 1869 (Friday)

The Utah legislature closed its 18th session.

February 25, 1869 (Thursday)

The Navajo Indians invaded southern Utah and stole stock at Harrisburgh. A number of armed men from St. George and other settlements started in pursuit.

February 25, 1869 (Thursday)

Delegate Wm. H. Hooper, by an able speech in the House of Representatives, frustrated a plan to divide the Territory of Utah.

March 1, 1869 (Monday)

Z.C.M.I. commenced business in the Eagle Emporium, Salt Lake City.

March 8, 1869 (Monday)

The Utah Central Railway company was organized, with Brigham Young as president.

March 8, 1869 (Monday)

A United States land office was opened in Salt Lake City.

March 8, 1869 (Monday)

The University of Deseret was opened in the Council House, Salt Lake City.

March 8, 1869 (Monday)

The Union Pacific Railroad was completed to Ogden, and a celebration held there in honor of the event.

March 9, 1869 (Tuesday)

At a special conference held in the State House, at Fillmore, Millard Co., Utah, a Stake of Zion was organized in Millard County, by Pres. Geo. A. Smith and Apostles Erastus Snow and Joseph F. Smith, with Thos. Callister as president. Daniel Thompson was sustained as Bishop of Scipio (Round Valley), and Culbert King as Bishop of Kanosh.

March 12, 1869 (Friday)

The Saints residing in Beaver County, Utah, were organized into the Beaver Stake of Zion, by Apostles Geo. A. Smith and Erastus Snow, with John R. Murdock as president. The town of Beaver was divided into two Wards, with Marquis L. Shepherd as Bishop of the First and John Ashworth as Bishop of the Second Ward. The villages of Greenville and Adamsville were organized into a third Ward, with David B. Adams, of Adamsville, as Bishop. James McKnight was sustained as Bishop of Minersville.

March 12, 1869 (Friday)

Elder Carl Widerborg died suddenly at Ogden.

March 16, 1869 (Tuesday)

Senator Pomeroy introduced a bill in the U.S. Senate, to establish woman suffrage in Utah.

March 21, 1869 (Sunday)

Franklin B. Woolley, son of Bishop Edwin D. Woolley, of Salt Lake City, was killed by Indians, on the Mohave river, near San Bernardino, Cal.

March 25, 1869 (Thursday)

Parowan, Iron Co., was divided into two Wards, with Herman D. Bayles as Bishop of the First and Samuel H. Rogers as Bishop of the Second Ward.

March 25, 1869 (Thursday)

Corinne, Box Elder Co., on the Central Pacific Railroad, was located by non-Mormons.

March 27, 1869 (Saturday)

The 75th quorum of Seventy was organized at Ogden, with Archibald Macfarlane, David G. Nelson, Henry J. Newman, Joseph A. West, Sanford Bingham, jr., Wm. Stoker, Richard White and Ivar Isaacson as presidents.

March 27, 1869 (Saturday)

Indians made a raid on the stock near Scipio, Millard Co., and took about one hundred head of cattle and horses.

March 28, 1869 (Sunday)

The 76th quorum of Seventy was organized in Weber County, with Wm. F. Critchlow, David H. Peery, Jeppe G. Folkman, Wm. Halls, James Barker, Enoch Farr and Edward Edwards as presidents.

April 1, 1869 (Thursday)

Major J.W. Powell finished his explorations on the Colorado river.

April 3, 1869 (Saturday)

By action of the county court, St. Joseph, on the Muddy, was made the county seat of Rio Virgen County.

April 5, 1869 (Monday)

Three men were killed by a snowslide in Mill Creek Canyon, Salt Lake Co.

April 6, 1869 (Tuesday)

On this and the two following days the 39th annual conference of the Church was held in Salt Lake City; forty-six missionaries were called.

April 14, 1869 (Wednesday)

The dead body of John V. Long was found in a ditch, in Salt Lake City.

April 20, 1869 (Tuesday)

Apostle Orson Pratt left Salt Lake City for New York, to publish the Book of Mormon in the Deseret alphabet.

April 24, 1869 (Saturday)

The Salt Lake Daily Telegraph was moved from Salt Lake City to Ogden.

May 10, 1869 (Monday)

The great Pacific Railroad was completed by the junction of the Union Pacific and Central Pacific Railroads, at Promontory, northwest of Ogden, Utah, where the last rail was laid and the last spike (gold) driven, in the presence of the chief officers of both roads, and a large concourse of people.

May 17, 1869 (Monday)

Ground was broken by Pres. Brigham Young at Ogden for the Utah Central Railway, a branch road soon afterwards built from Ogden to Salt Lake City.

June 1, 1869 (Tuesday)

The Provo Co-operative Woolen Manufacturing Company was organized; Brigham Young, president; Abraham O. Smoot, vice president. A site for the factory was also selected, and Nathan Davis appointed architect.

June 2, 1869 (Wednesday)

Elder Barnabas L. Adams, a Pioneer of 1847, died suddenly in City Creek Canyon, near Salt Lake City.

June 2, 1869 (Wednesday)

The Guion & Co's. steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 338 Saints, under the direction of Elias Morris. It arrived at New York June 14th.

June 11, 1869 (Friday)

Elder Heman Hyde died in Salt Lake City.

June 12, 1869 (Saturday)

James Davidson and wife died from want of water on the desert, between St. George and the settlements on the Muddy.

June 15, 1869 (Tuesday)

Hon. B. Q. Wade, late president of the U.S. Senate, and Major-General Philip H. Sheridan and staff visited Salt Lake City.

June 20, 1869 (Sunday)

The Saints residing in Bear Lake Valley were organized by Pres. Brigham Young into a Stake of Zion, with David P. Kimball as president.

June 25, 1869 (Friday)

The first company of Latter-day Saint immigrants who came all the way from the Missouri river by rail arrived in Ogden by the U.P.R.R., in charge of Elias Morris.

July 9, 1869 (Friday)

Senator L. Trumbull and the Chicago Commercial party arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

July 10, 1869 (Saturday)

The Chicago Commercial delegation, headed by Col. J.H. Rowen, called upon Pres. Brigham Young, in Salt Lake City.

July 15, 1869 (Thursday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 598 Saints, mostly from Scandinavia, under the direction of O.C. Olsen. The company arrived at New York July 28th, and at Taylor's Switch, near Ogden, Aug. 6th.

July 25, 1869 (Sunday)

The first shipment of Utah ore to California took place. It consisted of ten tons from the Monitor and Magnet mine, Little Cottonwood, shipped by Woodhull Bros. to T.H. Selby, San Francisco.

July 26, 1869 (Monday)

Thomas L. Frazier, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Wanship, Summit Co., from the effects of stabbing inflicted a few days before by a Mr. Kilfoyle.

July 28, 1869 (Wednesday)

The fine steamship Colorado sailed from Liverpool, England, with 365 Saints, in charge of John E. Pace. The company arrived at New York about Aug. 10th, and at Ogden Aug 20th.

July 31, 1869 (Saturday)

Woodhull Bros. made the first shipment of copper ore, ten tons, from the Kingston mine, Bingham Canyon.

August 1869

The grasshoppers destroyed a large portion of the growing crops in Cache, Washington, Kane and Iron Counties; other parts of the Territory escaped the visitation and gathered abundant crops.

August 21, 1869 (Saturday)

The Joint Congressional Committee on retrenchment, including several distinguished statesmen, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

August 25, 1869 (Wednesday)

Frederick Woesner was killed by unknown parties, at Montpelier, Rich Co., Utah (now in Idaho).

August 25, 1869 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 443 Saints, in charge of Marius Ensign. The company arrived at New York Sept. 6th, and at Ogden Sept. 16.

August 30, 1869 (Monday)

Geo. Francis Train delivered an interesting lecture in the Theatre, Salt Lake City. The following evening he lectured on the subjects, "Doctor, Lawyer and Clergyman."

September 3, 1869 (Friday)

Apostle Ezra T. Benson died at Ogden, Utah.

September 10, 1869 (Friday)

John Goddard, son of Geo. Goddard, was accidentally drowned in the Jordan river, near Salt Lake City.

September 18, 1869 (Saturday)

Bishop Wm. W. Wall died at Provo.

September 20, 1869 (Monday)

The Indians made a raid on Fairview, Sanpete Co., and stole eighteen head of horses.

September 22, 1869 (Wednesday)

Tracklaying was commenced on the Utah Central Railway at Ogden.

September 22, 1869 (Wednesday)

The steamship Manhattan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 239 Saints, in charge of Joseph Lawson. The company arrived at New York Oct. 7th, and at Ogden Oct. 16th.

September 26, 1869 (Sunday)

Apostle Orson Pratt arrived in Salt Lake City from his mission to the East.

October 3, 1869 (Sunday)

Vice-President Schuyler Colfax and party arrived in Salt Lake City, en route from California to the East.

October 5, 1869 (Tuesday)

Vice-President Colfax delivered a speech from the portico of the Townsend House, Salt Lake City, in which he praised the industries of the Mormon people, but denounced polygamy. This led to an important open correspondence between Colfax and Apostle John Taylor.

October 5, 1869 (Tuesday)

A company of 40 Saints from Georgia and other States arrived at Ogden, in charge of Jesse W. Crosby, jun.

October 6, 1869 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 294 Saints, in charge of James Needham. The company, which was the sixth ship-load of the season sailing from Liverpool, arrived at New York Oct. 17th, and at Ogden Oct. 28th. By a collision with an express train, at Evanston, Oct. 27th, two of the immigrants were killed and others wounded.

October 7, 1869 (Thursday)

A mass meeting was held in Salt Lake City, with a view of again appealing to Congress for the admission of Utah into the Union as a State.

October 8, 1869 (Friday)

About one hundred and ninety missionaries were called at the general conference, held at Salt Lake City, to go on missions.

October 9, 1869 (Saturday)

The surviving members of Zion's Camp had a party in Salt Lake City, arranged by Bishop Edward Hunter and Counselors.

October 18, 1869 (Monday)

John Walker, a survivor of the Haun's Mills massacre, died at Farmington, Davis Co., Utah.

October 20, 1869 (Wednesday)

The ground was broken for the Coalville and Echo Railway, in Summit County.

October 25, 1869 (Monday)

Elias L.T. Harrison, Wm. S. Godbe and Eli B. Kelsey were excommunicated from the Church, by the High Council, in Salt Lake City, for apostasy.

October 31, 1869 (Sunday)

Indians made a raid on the town of Kanarra, Iron Co., Utah, and drove off horses.

November 7, 1869 (Sunday)

At a conference held in St. George, Utah, the settlements of the Saints in southern Utah were organized into a stake of Zion with Joseph W. Young as president, and Robert Gardner and Jas. G. Bleak as counselors; St. George was divided into four wards with David Milne, Henry Eyring, Walter Granger and Nathaniel Ashby as their respective bishops. Hebron and Clover Valley were organized into a Ward, with Geo. H. Crosby as Bishop.

November 8, 1869 (Monday)

Carpenters began work on the gallery in the New Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

November 14, 1869 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on Cherry Creek, Malad Valley, Idaho, were organized into the Willow Springs branch of the Church, with Richard J. Davis as president.

November 18, 1869 (Thursday)

Miss Annie Lockhart, a favorite actress, died in Salt Lake City.

November 22, 1869 (Monday)

Ogden was decided upon as the junction of the Union Pacific and Central Pacific Railways.

November 24, 1869 (Wednesday)

Street lamps were first used in Salt Lake City.

November 29, 1869 (Monday)

Emer Harris, brother of Martin Harris, died at Logan, Utah.

December 6, 1869 (Monday)

The Utah Central Railway was permanently opened for traffic from Ogden to Farmington.

December 6, 1869 (Monday)

Senator Aaron H. Cragin, of New Hampshire, introduced an anti-polygamy bill in the U.S. Senate.

December 18, 1869 (Saturday)

The Deseret Telegraph Line was extended to Franklin, Idaho.

December 19, 1869 (Sunday)

The "Godbeite Movement" began to take definite shape.

December 30, 1869 (Thursday)

Samuel Gould, formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Parowan, Iron Co.

1870.

The women of Utah were enfranchised. The Liberal Party was organized in Salt lake City, and commenced its warfare against the "Mormons." The annual muster of the Utah militia was forbidden by Gov. Schaffer. Judge James B. McKean commenced his inglorious career in the Territory. Dr. Taggart, assessor of internal revenue, made a despicable attempt to compel the Church to pay an enormous tax on tithing, but failed in his scheme.

January 1, 1870. (Saturday)

The first number of the Ogden Junction, a semi-weekly newspaper, was issued at Ogden, by the Ogden Junction Publishing Company; Franklin D. Richards, editor. Later it was edited by Charles W. Penrose. The paper was continued under that name until Feb. 14, 1881.

January 1, 1870. (Saturday)

The first number of the Mormon Tribune, a weekly paper, was published by the Godbeites, in Salt Lake City.

January 9, 1870. (Sunday)

Bishop Chauncey W. West, of Ogden, died at San Francisco, Cal.

January 10, 1870. (Monday)

The last rail of the Utah Central Railway was laid and the last spike driven, at Salt Lake City, by Pres. Brigham Young, in the presence of 15,000 people.

January 11, 1870. (Tuesday)

The nineteenth annual session of the Utah legislature assembled in Salt Lake City, and organized by electing Geo. A. Smith president of the Council, and Orson Pratt speaker of the House. It was an important session.

January 12, 1870. (Wednesday)

Woodhull Bros. shipped the first car-load of ore over the Utah Central Railway.

January 13, 1870. (Thursday)

A great mass meeting was held by the ladies of Salt Lake City, to protest against the passage of the Cullom anti-polygamy bill, which had been introduced in Congress. Similar meetings were subsequently held by the ladies in most of the settlements in the Territory.

January 13, 1870. (Thursday)

The first coal shipped by rail, direct to Salt Lake City, arrived there, consisting of two carloads from the Wasatch Coal Company's mines, consigned to Frederick A.H.F. Mitchell.

February 1870.

The "Liberal Party" of Utah was formed by a union of the Gentiles and Godbeites of Salt Lake City.

February 10, 1870. (Thursday)

A political mass meeting, appointed by the "Liberal Party" of Salt Lake City and held in Walker's old store, was carried by the "People's Party."

February 12, 1870. (Friday)

An act passed by the legislature, conferring the elective franchise upon the women of Utah, was approved by Acting-Governor S. A. Mann.

February 17, 1870. (Thursday)

Some soldiers from Camp Douglas beat an Indian boy and fired on the police, who interfered with and arrested them.

February 24, 1870. (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young, accompanied by a number of leading men, left Salt Lake City on a trip to the southern settlements. They arrived at the Colorado river, at the mouth of the Rio Virgen, Arizona, March 16th.

March 2, 1870. (Wednesday)

Elder Jabez Woodard died at Milton, Morgan Co.

March 2, 1870. (Wednesday)

The first number of the Keepapitchinin, a small semi-weekly periodical, devoted to fun and amusement, was issued in Salt Lake City, by Geo. J. Taylor and Joseph C. Rich.

March 7, 1870. (Monday)

Ole Bull, the great Norwegian violinist, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit. He gave two concerts in the theatre and left on the 10th.

March 20, 1870. (Sunday)

Hon. J. Wilson Schaffer, seventh governor of Utah, arrived in Salt Lake City. He proved to be one of the most bitter officials that the Territory ever had.

March 23, 1870. (Wednesday)

Although Delegate Wm. H. Hooper made a very able speech in defence of religious liberty in Utah, the Cullom Bill was passed by the House of Representatives.

March 29, 1870. (Tuesday)

A company of 30 Elders returned to Salt Lake City from missions to the States.

March 31, 1870. (Thursday)

The citizens of Salt Lake City held an immense mass meeting to protest against the Cullom Bill, which had not yet passed the Senate. Afterwards mass meetings were held in the settlements for the same purpose, and a petition drafted and forwarded to the Senate.

April 1870.

Sidney Alvarus Hanks, one of the Pioneers of 1847, froze to death in Parley's Park, Summit Co., Utah.

April 1870.

The gallery in the large Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, was finished.

April 1870.

Camp Rawlins, a military post, was established near Provo, Utah.

April 1870.

An abandoned child was left at the door of Mrs. Prescinda L. Kimball—the first occurrence of the kind known in Salt Lake City.

April 1, 1870. (Friday)

The first number of the Utah Pomologist and Gardener, devoted to the orchard, vineyard, farm and garden, was issued by Joseph E. Johnson, at St. George, Utah.

April 7, 1870. (Thursday)

Elder Edward Stevenson preached in the Kirtland Temple, O.

April 12, 1870. (Tuesday)

The resolutions adopted by the Salt Lake City mass meeting, on March 31st, were presented to the U.S. Senate and referred to the committee on Territories.

April 13, 1870. (Wednesday)

Elder Moroni Bigelow was killed on the steamboat Mary McDonald and thrown into the Missouri river, between Camden and Wellington, Mo. He was returning from a mission to the States.

April 16, 1870. (Saturday)

Pres. Brigham Young and party returned to Salt Lake City, from a preaching trip to the southern settlements.

April 21, 1870. (Thursday)

The dead body of Sidney Alvarus Hanks was found near Silver Creek, Summit Co.

April 27, 1870. (Wednesday)

Patriarch John Young, Pres. Brigham Young's eldest brother, died in Salt Lake City.

May 5, 1870. (Thursday)

The 40th annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 8th.

May 8, 1870. (Sunday)

General Philip Henry Sheridan and staff arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 8, 1870. (Sunday)

Rev. Geo. M. Pierce entered his field of labor as the first Methodist missionary in Salt Lake City.

May 10, 1870. (Tuesday)

A land-slide in Bingham Canyon resulted in the death of Charles A. Freeman and James Leicester.

May 12, 1870. (Thursday)

Amasa M. Lyman, once a member of the Twelve Apostles, was excommunicated from the Church for apostasy.

May 13, 1870. (Friday)

Geo. Knighton and Henry Langford were drowned in the Jordan river, northwest of Salt Lake City.

May 13, 1870. (Friday)

Col. M.T. Patrick, U.S. Marshal for Utah, arrived in Salt Lake City.

May 14, 1870. (Saturday)

Nathaniel H. Felt and Thos. Jackson arrived at Salt Lake City, with a small company of Saints from New York State.

May 20, 1870. (Friday)

Elder Wm. I. Appleby died in Salt Lake City.

May 27, 1870. (Friday)

James Taylor, Apostle John Taylor's father, died in Salt Lake City, 87 years of age.

May 28, 1870. (Saturday)

The corner stones of the Provo Co-operative Woolen Factory were laid.

June 1870.

The grasshoppers did much damage in the Territory.

June 1870.

Horace S. Eldredge succeeded Albert Carrington as president of the European Mission.

June 5, 1870. (Sunday)

The first number of the Salt Lake Daily Herald was issued; Wm. C. Dunbar and Edward L. Sloan, publishers; Edward L. Sloan, editor.

June 13, 1870. (Monday)

Johan C. Christensen was killed by lightning, while in the field irrigating, near Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

June 17, 1870. (Friday)

In the Probate Court at Manti, John Steward, of Fairview, Sanpete Co., was sentenced to be shot, for the killing of Sally Woodward, an Indian girl, some time previous.

June 23, 1870. (Thursday)

Fifteen wagons, loaded with machinery for a woolen factory at Beaver, left Salt Lake City.

June 28, 1870. (Tuesday)

A company of 20 Saints sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Colorado, for the United States.

July 1870.

Pres. U.S. Grant appointed James B. McKean chief justice and Vernon H. Vaughan secretary of Utah. They succeeded Judge Charles C. Wilson and Secretary S.A. Mann.

July 3, 1870. (Sunday)

Albert Carrington was ordained one of the Twelve Apostles, in Salt Lake City.

July 8, 1870. (Friday)

James Hendricks, who was crippled at the Crooked River battle, Oct. 25, 1838, died at Richmond, Cache Co.

July 12, 1870. (Tuesday)

Lady Franklin, widow of Sir John Franklin, visited Ogden, on her return trip from searching for her lost husband. She afterwards visited Salt Lake City.

July 13, 1870. (Wednesday)

The steamship Manhattan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 269 British, German and Swiss Saints, in charge of Karl G. Maeser. The company arrived at New York July 26th, and at Salt Lake City Aug. 5th.

July 20, 1870. (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 357 Saints, mostly Scandinavians, in charge of Jesse N. Smith. The company arrived at New York Aug. 1st, and at Salt Lake City Aug. 10th.

July 23, 1870. (Saturday)

Geo. Francis Train lectured in the Salt Lake Theatre, in defense of Brigham Young.

August 1, 1870. (Monday)

At the general election in Utah, Wm. H. Hooper received over twenty thousand votes for delegate to Congress, and Geo. R. Maxwell, the Liberal candidate, only a few hundred.

August 12, 1870. (Friday)

A discussion commenced in the large Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, between Apostle Orson Pratt and Dr. John P. Newman, chaplain of the U.S. Senate, on the question: "Does the Bible sanction Polygamy?" It was continued three days.

August 13, 1870. (Saturday)

S.D. Woodhull, of the firm of Woodhull Bros., the earliest active mining operations in Utah, was shot in Little Cottonwood Canyon, in a difficulty over a claim. He died on the 14th.

August 27, 1870. (Saturday)

The establishment of Paul Engelbrecht was broken up, and his stock of liquors destroyed under authority of Salt Lake City, because he sold liquor without a license.

August 27, 1870. (Saturday)

Pres. Brigham Young and party left Salt Lake City for southern Utah, from which he returned Sept. 24th.

August 29, 1870. (Monday)

Alderman Jeter Clinton and several police officers were arrested by the U.S. marshal for participation in the abatement of the Engelbrecht liquor establishment.

August 30, 1870. (Tuesday)

Martin Harris, one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, arrived in Salt Lake City. He was 88 years old. In the ensuing conference he bore a faithful testimony to the truth of the Book of Mormon.

August 30, 1870. (Tuesday)

Judge James B. McKean arrived in Salt Lake City.

September 2, 1870. (Friday)

The first number of the semi-weekly edition of the Salt Lake Herald was issued.

September 5, 1870. (Monday)

Chief Justice James B. McKean was assigned to the Third Judicial District, and forthwith commenced his infamous official career in Utah.

September 7, 1870. (Wednesday)

The steamship Idaho sailed from Liverpool, England, with 186 Saints, in charge of Frank H. Hyde. The company arrived at New York Sept. 21st, and at Ogden Oct. 1st.

September 9, 1870. (Friday)

Messers. Jones & Robius began the erection of smelting works on the State Road, south of Salt Lake City.

September 10, 1870. (Saturday)

A town site was located by Pres. Brigham Young at Kanab, Kane Co., and the following day a Ward organization was effected, with Levi Stewart as Bishop.

September 14, 1870. (Wednesday)

A company of Scandinavian Saints (19 souls), in charge of B.N. Walter, sailed from Liverpool, England, on board the steamship Nevada, bound for Utah.

September 15, 1870. (Thursday)

Gov. J. Wilson Schaffer issued a proclamation appointing Patrick E. Connor major-general of the Utah militia (Nauvoo Legion), and Wm. M. Jones assistant adjutant-general. On the same day he issued a proclamation prohibiting all drills, musters and militia gatherings, except upon his orders, or those of the U.S. marshal. He also ordered the delivery of all arms belonging to the Territory of Utah, or the United States (except those in possession of U.S. soldiers), to Col. Wm. M. Johns.

September 20, 1870. (Tuesday)

The first run of crude bullion was made at the first smelting works built in Utah, erected six miles south of Salt Lake City by Woodhull Brothers.

September 22, 1870. (Thursday)

On the night of this day a party of U.S. troops, stationed near Provo, made a raid on some of the citizens in that town, some of whom they abused shamefully.

October 10, 1870. (Monday)

The surviving members of Zion's Camp and the Mormon Battalion had an enjoyable party at the Social Hall, Salt Lake City. Of the members of Zion's Camp 32 were present, and 63 of the Battalion boys participated.

October 12, 1870. (Wednesday)

The old arsenal building in Salt Lake City was burned to the ground.

October 14, 1870. (Friday)

A scientific exploring party from Yale College, under direction of Prof. Marsh, arrived in Salt Lake City.

October 23, 1870. (Sunday)

The stage from Pioche was robbed near Nephi, Juab Co., by three men, who were afterwards caught and punished.

October 31, 1870. (Monday)

Gov. J. Wilson Schaffer died at his residence in Salt Lake City. Secretary Vernon H. Vaughan succeeded him as acting governor.

November 4, 1870. (Friday)

Prof. Ferdinand V. Hayden, United States geologist, arrived in Salt Lake City.

November 4, 1870. (Friday)

In the Third District Court the jury returned a verdict against Salt Lake City, allowing Engelbrecht & Co., $59,063.25 damages. The case was appealed.

November 8, 1870. (Tuesday)

Gen. Chas. A. Washburn, U. S. minister to Paraguay, and Hon. Alvin Flanders, governor of Washington Territory, visited Salt Lake City.

November 16, 1870. (Wednesday)

A company of 59 Saints, in charge of Ralph Thompson, sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Manhattan, which arrived at New York Dec. 2nd. The company reached Salt Lake City, Dec. 11th.

November 21, 1870. (Monday)

The so-called "wooden gun rebellion" in the 20th Ward, Salt Lake City, occurred. Messrs. Charles R. Savage, Geo. M. Ottinger, John C. Graham, Charles and Archibald Livingstone, Wm. G. Phillips and Jas. Fennimore were arrested on a charge of treason and confined at Camp Douglas.

November 23, 1870. (Wednesday)

Charles R. Savage and the other prisoners were admitted to bail and liberated.

November 25, 1870. (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young, Geo. A. Smith and Brigham Young, jun., left Salt Lake City for southern Utah, where they spent part of the winter.

December 2, 1870. (Friday)

Richard Soper and Anton Valardie (?), guilty of committing rape, were killed between Levan and Nephi, Juab Co., while trying to escape from the officers.

December 14, 1870. (Wednesday)

Six members of Levi Stewart's family were burned to death in Kanab, Kane Co., Utah.

December 24, 1870. (Saturday)

No. 1 of the Footlights, a programme of the entertainments at the Theatre, in Salt Lake City, was issued.

December 28, 1870. (Wednesday)

Richard Brown was shot and killed at Provo by John J. Baum, whose niece Brown had seduced. Baum was subsequently arrested, tried and acquitted, on the ground of justifiable homicide.
