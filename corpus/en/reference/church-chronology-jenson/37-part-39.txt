Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

During this year Nauvoo, Ill., began its career as an incorporated city; the Nauvoo Legion was organized, and the cornerstones of the Nauvoo Temple were laid. The Twelve Apostles returned from their missions to England, and baptism for the dead was commenced in the Church.

January 1841

The first number of the Gospel Reflector, a semi-monthly periodical published in the interest of the Church, was issued in Philadelphia, Pa.; Benjamin Winchester, editor.

January 1841

The first British edition of the Book of Mormon was published in Manchester, England.

January 19, 1841 (Tuesday)

The Saints were commanded by revelation to build a Temple at Nauvoo, Ill., and also a "boarding house" for the accommodation of strangers, which subsequently became known as the Nauvoo House. The general authorities of the Church and other officers were named in the revelation, which also contains important explanations on the order of the Priesthood. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 124.)

January 24, 1841 (Sunday)

Hyrum Smith succeeded his father, Joseph Smith, sen., as Patriarch to the Church, and Wm. Law was appointed a Counselor in the First Presidency, succeeding Hyrum Smith, in that capacity, according to revelation.

January 30, 1841 (Saturday)

At a meeting held at Nauvoo, Ill., Joseph Smith was elected sole Trustee for the Church, to hold the office during life, his "successors to be the First Presidency" of the Church.

February 1, 1841 (Monday)

The first election took place for members of the city council of Nauvoo. John C. Bennett was elected mayor; Wm. Marks, Samuel H. Smith, Daniel H. Wells and Newel K. Whitney, aldermen; Joseph Smith, Hyrum Smith, Sidney Rigdon, Charles C. Rich, John F. Barnett, Wilson Law, Don Carlos Smith, John P. Greene and Vinson Knight, councilors.

February 3, 1841 (Wednesday)

The city council of Nauvoo elected Henry G. Sherwood, marshall; James Sloan, recorder; Robert B. Thompsen, treasurer; James Robinson, assessor; Austin Cowles, supervisor of streets.

February 4, 1841 (Thursday)

The Nauvoo Legion, originally consisting of six companies, was organized with Joseph Smith as lieutenant-general.

February 7, 1841 (Sunday)

The ship Sheffield sailed from Liverpool, England with 235 Saints, under the leadership of Hiram Clark.

February 13, 1841 (Saturday)

Apostle Orson Hyde sailed from New York for Liverpool, on his mission to Jerusalem.

February 14, 1841 (Sunday)

The London (England) conference was organized with Lorenzo Snow as president.

February 16, 1841 (Tuesday)

The ship Echo sailed from Liverpool, England, with 109 Saints, under the direction of Daniel Browitt.

March 1841

The Saints were commanded by revelation to build a city in Iowa Territory, opposite Nauvoo, to be called Zarahemla. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 125.)

March 1, 1841 (Monday)

The city council divided the city of Nauvoo into four wards. An ordinance was passed, giving free toleration and equal privileges in the city to all religious sects and denominations.

March 10, 1841 (Wednesday)

Governor Thos. Carlin, of Illinois, commissioned Joseph Smith lieutenant-general of the Nauvoo Legion.

March 17, 1841 (Wednesday)

The ship Uleste sailed from Liverpool, England, with 54 Saints, under the direction of Thomas Smith and Wm. Moss, bound for America.

March 29, 1841 (Monday)

Charles C. Rich and Austin Cowles were chosen counselors to Wm. Marks, president of the Nauvoo Stake of Zion.

April 6, 1841 (Tuesday)

A general conference of the Church was commenced at Nauvoo, and the corner stones of the Nauvoo Temple were laid. The conference was continued till the 11th.

April 8, 1841 (Thursday)

Lyman Wight was chosen one of the Twelve Apostles, in place of David W. Patten, martyred in Missouri.

April 21, 1841 (Wednesday)

Apostles Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, Orson Pratt, Wilford Woodruff, John Taylor, Geo. A. Smith and Willard Richards sailed from Liverpool, England, on the ship Rochester, accompanied by 130 Saints. They arrived at New York May 20th.

May 22, 1841 (Saturday)

At a conference held at Kirtland, O., Almon W. Babbitt was chosen president of the Kirtland Stake, with Lester Brooks and Zebedee Coltrin as counselors.

May 24, 1841 (Monday)

The First Presidency at Nauvoo called upon all scattered Saints to gather to Hancock County, Ill., and Lee County, Ia. All neighboring Stakes outside of these two counties were discontinued.

June 5, 1841 (Saturday)

Joseph Smith was arrested on a requisition from the State of Missouri. He was tried on the 9th and liberated on the 10th on a writ of habeas corpus, at Monmouth, Warren Co., Ill.

June 22, 1841 (Tuesday)

Theodore Curtis, who had been under arrest in Gloucester, England, five days for preaching the gospel, was acquitted.

July 1, 1841 (Thursday)

Apostles Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball and John Taylor arrived at Nauvoo from their missions to England.

July 9, 1841 (Friday)

By revelation, through Joseph the Prophet, Apostle Brigham Young was commanded to send the "word" abroad, and to take special care of his family. (Doc. and Cov., Sec. 126.)

July 13, 1841 (Tuesday)

Apostle Geo. A. Smith returned to Nauvoo from his mission to England.

July 25, 1841 (Sunday)

Wm. Yokum lost his leg by amputation, as the result of a wound received in the massacre at Haun's Mill.

August 7, 1841 (Saturday)

Don Carlos Smith, the youngest brother of the Prophet, died at Nauvoo.

August 7, 1841 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith preached to about one hundred Sac and Fox Indians (among whom were the chiefs Keokuk, Kiskuhosh and Appenoose), who had come to visit him at Nauvoo.

August 16, 1841 (Monday)

Apostle Willard Richards arrived at Nauvoo from his mission to England.

August 25, 1841 (Wednesday)

Oliver Granger died at Kirtland, O.

August 27, 1841 (Friday)

Robert B. Thompson, Joseph Smith's scribe, died at Nauvoo.

September 21, 1841 (Tuesday)

The ship Tyrean sailed from Liverpool for New Orleans with 204 Saints, under the direction of Joseph Fielding, bound for Nauvoo.

September 22, 1841 (Wednesday)

A company of brethren left Nauvoo for the Pineries, Wisconsin, about five hundred miles north, to procure lumber for the Nauvoo Temple.

October 2, 1841 (Saturday)

An important general conference was commenced in the Grove at Nauvoo. It was continued till the 4th. Joseph Smith declared, as the will of the Lord, that the Church should not hold another general conference until the Saints could meet in the Temple. James Sloan was elected Church clerk, instead of Robert B. Thompson deceased.

October 6, 1841 (Wednesday)

Apostle Wilford Woodruff arrived at Nauvoo from his mission to England.

October 7, 1841 (Thursday)

In a council of the Twelve, a number of brethren were called on missions, among whom were Joseph Ball to South America and Henry Harrison Sagers to Jamaica, West Indies.

October 24, 1841 (Sunday)

Apostle Orson Hyde, who had arrived at Jerusalem, ascended the Mount of Olives and dedicated the land of Palestine by prayer for the gathering of the Jews.

November 8, 1841 (Monday)

The temporary baptismal font in the Nauvoo Temple was dedicated.

November 8, 1841 (Monday)

The ship Chaos sailed from Liverpool with 170 Saints, under the direction of Peter Melling, bound for Nauvoo.

November 21, 1841 (Sunday)

Baptisms for the dead were commenced in the font in the basement of the Nauvoo Temple.

November 24, 1841 (Wednesday)

The Tyrean company of British Saints arrived at Warsaw, intending to settle Warren, a new town site, one mile south of Warsaw, which had been selected for a settlement of the Saints, but they soon afterwards removed to Nauvoo, because of oppression on the part of anti-Mormons.

December 4, 1841 (Saturday)

The Stake organization at Ramus, Hancock County, Ill., was discontinued.

December 13, 1841 (Monday)

Apostle Willard Richards was appointed Joseph Smith's private secretary and general clerk for the Church.

December 22, 1841 (Wednesday)

John Snider was called by revelation on a special mission to Europe, bearing a message from the Twelve.
