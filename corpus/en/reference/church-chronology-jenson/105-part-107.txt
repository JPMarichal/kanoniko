Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year two new Stakes of Zion (Carbon and Duchesne) were organized in Utah. Pres. John R. Winder died; John Henry Smith was chosen as a counselor in the First Presidency, and Joseph F. Smith, jun., was ordained an Apostle. Pres. Joseph F. Smith made another visit to Europe.

January 1910

The first number of the "Utah Genealogical and Historical Magazine" was published in Salt Lake City; Anthon H. Lund, editor.

January 1, 1910 (Saturday)

The Vernal Ward, Uintah Co., Utah, was divided into two Wards called respectively the Vernal First Ward, with John N. Davis as Bishop, and the Vernal Second Ward, with David Bennion as Bishop. Both Bishops were ordained May 22, 1910.

January 1, 1910 (Saturday)

A flood in the Santa Clara river, southern Utah, caused great loss of property.

January 1, 1910 (Saturday)

Elbert D. Thomas succeeded Alma O. Taylor as president of the Japan mission.

January 3, 1910 (Monday)

John Cook, a Utah pioneer of 1849, died in Salt Lake City.

January 5, 1910 (Wednesday)

Claudius Victor Spencer, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died in Salt Lake City, and George Reese, a Utah handcart veteran of 1856, died at Bloomington, Idaho.

January 6, 1910 (Thursday)

A new Relief Society hall was dedicated at Parowan, Utah; the building was a brick and stone structure erected at a cost of $3,300.

January 9, 1910 (Sunday)

A new chapel was opened in the Ogden Sixth Ward; it had been erected at a cost of $20,000.

January 9, 1910 (Sunday)

Some of the Saints residing in Fremont county, Idaho, were organized into the Herbert branch of the Rexburg First Ward, with Henry C. Blunck as president.

January 10, 1910 (Monday)

Margaret F.C. Morrison, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died at Mt. Pleasant, Utah.

January 11, 1910 (Tuesday)

Two shocks of earthquake were felt at Elsinore, Sevier Co., Utah; it caused the destruction of several chimneys.

January 11, 1910 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Ann Everington Woolley, mother of Elder Brigham H. Roberts, died at Bountiful, Davis Co., Utah.

January 12, 1910 (Wednesday)

John Tracy, a Utah pioneer, died at American Fork, Utah.

January 14, 1910 (Friday)

The Opera House at Kaysville, Utah, was destroyed by fire.

January 16, 1910 (Sunday)

Mrs. Sarah Gamble, an early pioneer of Utah, died in Salt Lake City; Mrs. Maria Loomis Davery, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Spanish Fork, Utah, and Peter Madsen, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Ephraim, Utah.

Daniel S. German, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Grace, Idaho.

John H. Hougaard, a Utah pioneer of 1862 and a prominent business man, died at Manti, Utah, and Samuel Green, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah.

January 20, 1910 (Thursday)

Henry Brown, a Utah pioneer of 1847, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah.

January 22, 1910 (Saturday)

John D.T. McAllister, a Utah pioneer of 1851, and formerly president of the St. George and Manti Temples died at St. George, Utah.

During the following days they attended the great International Peace Congress in Stockholm, of which Pres. Smith, Bishop Nibley, and Presidents Peter Sundwall and Andrew Jenson were members.

The Seventies residing in West Layton, Layton, and South Weber, Davis Co., Utah, were organized by J. Golden Kimball as the 158th quorum of Seventy with Richard Scofield as senior president.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

Joseph Eckersley succeeded Gearson S. Bastian as president of the Wayne Stake, Utah.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

George E. Hellewell was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Hayburn Ward, and David Hubbard was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Elba Ward, Cassia Co., Idaho.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

Lewis C. Olsen was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Sterling Ward (South Sanpete Stake), Utah.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

Jacob S. Bastian was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Loa Ward, Wayne Co., Utah.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

Edwin Caldwell was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Molen Ward, Emery Co., Utah.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

The new Ward chapel in the Provo Sixth Ward was dedicated by John Henry Smith.

November 6, 1910 (Sunday)

John Conover, a Utah pioneer of 1850, died in Salt Lake City.

November 7, 1910 (Monday)

Thomas Cannell, a Utah handcart pioneer of 1852, died at Nephi, Utah, and Mrs. Sarah Kinsman Young, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Provo, Utah.

November 12, 1910 (Saturday)

The San Juan Stake presidency was reorganized, Lemuel H. Redd, jun., being chosen president, with David Halls and Albert R. Lyman as his counselors. Kumen Jones was chosen as Bishop of the Bluff Ward San Juan Co., Utah; he was ordained the next day.

November 13, 1910 (Sunday)

William N. Patten was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Moore Ward (Blackfoot Stake), Blaine Co., Idaho, and James P. Fugal was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Woodville Ward (Blackfoot Stake), Bonneville Co., Idaho.

November 13, 1910 (Sunday)

Mrs. Elizabeth D. Williams, a Utah pioneer of 1855, died at Ogden, Utah.

November 14, 1910 (Monday)

Joseph H. Taylor of the Fourteenth Ward, Salt Lake City, died at Stuttgart, Wurtemburg, Germany, while laboring as a missionary in the Swiss and German Mission.

November 16, 1910 (Wednesday)

James A. Park, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Wellsville, Cache Co., Utah.

November 19, 1910 (Saturday)

Frank M. Snow was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Turner Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho.

November 19, 1910 (Saturday)

Anna Nielsen Brown, a Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Mt. Pleasant, Sanpete Co., Utah.

November 20, 1910 (Sunday)

Charles L. Milne was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Union Ward (Jordan Stake), Salt Lake Co., Utah.

November 20, 1910 (Sunday)

Caroline B. Obray, a Utah handcart pioneer of 1856, died at Paradise, Cache Co., Utah.

November 21, 1910 (Monday)

Sanford Bingham, a Utah pioneer of 1847 and a prominent Church member, died at Ogden, Utah, and Baltzar Peterson, a Utah pioneer of 1863, died at Richville, Morgan Co., Utah.

November 22, 1910 (Tuesday)

Christen Nielsen, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died in Richmond, Utah.

November 23, 1910 (Wednesday)

The new Commercial Club building was opened in Salt Lake City.

November 23, 1910 (Wednesday)

William R. Cole, a Utah pioneer of 1852, died at Roy, Weber Co., Utah.

November 24, 1910 (Thursday)

Mrs. Susannah Pierce Booth, a Utah pioneer of 1851, died at Brigham City, Utah.

November 24, 1910 (Thursday)

The recently erected Academy Building at Snowflake, Ariz., was destroyed by fire.

November 25, 1910 (Friday)

William H. Lee, a Utah pioneer of 1848 and formerly a Bishop in the Church, died at Woodruff, Rich Co., Utah, and Mrs. Ellen M. Williams, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Ogden, Utah.

November 27, 1910 (Sunday)

Niels C. Christensen was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Salem Ward (Nebo Stake), Utah Co., Utah.

November 27, 1910 (Sunday)

The first meeting was held in the recently erected chapel in the Tenth Ward, Salt Lake City.

November 27, 1910 (Sunday)

Thomas Butler Allred, a Utah pioneer of 1849, and Mrs. Betsey Whittaker, a Utah pioneer of 1855, died in Salt Lake City.

November 28, 1910 (Monday)

George M. Porter was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Lovell Ward, and John H. Hinckley was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Cowley Ward (Big Horn Stake), Wyoming.

November 28, 1910 (Monday)

The Bishopric of the Orderville Ward (Kanab Stake), Kane Co., Utah, was reorganized, Henry Chamberlain being chosen as Bishop. He was ordained on the 30th.

November 28, 1910 (Monday)

Jane Jenkins Bowen, a Utah pioneer of 1864, died at Salem, Utah; Patriarch Mads Andersen, a Utah pioneer of 1862 and a faithful Church worker, died at Mt. Pleasant, Sanpete Co., Utah, and Mrs. Anna Lang, the oldest member of the Church in Switzerland, died at Geneva, Switzerland.

December 1910

Charles P. Smith, a Utah pioneer of 1851, died at St. David, Ariz.

December 2, 1910 (Friday)

The Duchesne Stake of Zion was organized with William H. Smart as president and Ephraim Lambert and Joseph H. Harding as his counselors. At the time of its organization the new Stake consisted of only two Wards, namely, Roosevelt and Theodore. Geo. V. Billings was chosen as Bishop of the Theodore Ward.

December 2, 1910 (Friday)

Wheatley Gibson, a Utah handcart pioneer of 1860, died at West Weber, Weber Co., Utah.

December 3, 1910 (Saturday)

Mrs. Harriet Daly, a Utah pioneer of 1862, died at Springville, Utah.

December 4, 1910 (Sunday)

The Kanab Stake presidency was reorganized, William W. Seegmiller being chosen as president with Heber J. Meeks and Israel Heaton as counselors.

December 4, 1910 (Sunday)

George Heber Robinson was chosen as Bishop of the Kanab Ward and the Saints at Moccasin were organized as a branch with Charles C. Heaton as president. Bishop Robinson was ordained on the 5th.

December 4, 1910 (Sunday)

Some of the Saints who had formerly belonged to the Theodore and Roosevelt Wards were organized as a separate Ward named the Boneta Ward, with Oscar Wilkins as Bishop. The Roosevelt Ward was further divided and a new Ward called the Hayden Ward organized with Roger Horrocks as Bishop.

December 4, 1910 (Sunday)

James Daniel Taylor was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Banida Ward, David S. Evans was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Riverdale Ward, and Lorenzo Johnson was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Preston Second Ward, Oneida Co., Idaho.

December 5, 1910 (Monday)

The Seventies residing in the Teton and Canyon Creek Wards, Idaho, were organized by Rulon S. Wells as the 159th quorum of Seventy with C. Walter Riggs as senior president.

December 7, 1910 (Wednesday)

Ottilie M. Schoenfeld, a Utah pioneer and a prominent Church worker, died in Salt Lake City.

December 10, 1910 (Saturday)

Thomas Passey and Franklin D. Rich, both pioneers of Bear Lake Valley, Idaho, died at Paris, Idaho.

December 11, 1910 (Sunday)

Soren Rasmussen succeeded W.B. Enniss as Bishop of Draper (Jordan Stake), Salt Lake Co., Utah; he was ordained Jan. 1, 1911.

December 11, 1910 (Sunday)

Charles W. Pope was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over the Garden City Ward, Rich Co., Utah.

December 11, 1910 (Sunday)

Amelia Folsom Young, wife of the late Pres. Brigham Young, and Captain Joseph N. Betenson, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died in Salt Lake City; Jens P. Anderson, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died at Ephraim, Sanpete Co., Utah, and Mary A. Billings Gardiner, a Utah pioneer of 1848, died at Thurber, Wayne Co., Utah.

December 12, 1910 (Monday)

The Seventies residing in the Ogden Fifth, Ogden Ninth and Uintah Wards were organized by B.H. Roberts as the 160th quorum of Seventy, with James A. Douglas as senior president.

December 12, 1910 (Monday)

J. Reuben Clark, a young "Mormon" attorney, was appointed solicitor for the State department of the United States.

December 13, 1910 (Tuesday)

Levi Savage, a member of the Mormon Battalion and a Church veteran, died at Toquerville, Washington Co., Utah.

December 13, 1910 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Johanna Thompson Eggertson, a Utah handcart pioneer of 1847, died at Provo, Utah.

December 14, 1910 (Wednesday)

John Martin Stoddard, Utah pioneer of 1850, died at Clinton, Utah Co., Utah.

December 16, 1910 (Friday)

A new chapel was dedicated by Pres. Joseph F. Smith at North Ogden, Weber Co., Utah.

December 17, 1910 (Saturday)

Nathan Tanner, the last surviving member of Zion's Camp, died at Granger, Salt Lake Co., Utah, and Mrs. Mary Ann Johnson, an aged member of the Church, died at Mesa, Ariz.

December 18, 1910 (Sunday)

The presidency of the Fremont Stake, Idaho, was reorganized, Mark Austin being chosen as president with Albert Heath and James Blake as his counselors.

December 18, 1910 (Sunday)

Mrs. Mary Cunningham, a Utah pioneer of 1854, died at Woodville, Idaho.

December 26, 1910 (Monday)

Esther Davis Frost, a Utah pioneer of 1851, died at Marion, Cassia Co., Idaho.

December 27, 1910 (Tuesday)

Patriarch John McDonald, a Utah handcart pioneer, died at Heber, Wasatch Co., Utah, and Mrs. Mary Bennett Hill, a Utah pioneer of 1850, died at Kaysville, Utah.

December 29, 1910 (Thursday)

Hannah Crook, a faithful Church worker, died at East Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co., Utah.

December 31, 1910 (Saturday)

Eliza S. Kellar, a Utah pioneer of 1853, died at Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co., Utah, and Mrs. Hilda Amelia Otterstrom, a handcart pioneer of 1857, died at Cleveland, Emery Co., Utah.
