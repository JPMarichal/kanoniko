Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

A number of prominent Elders in the Church died. A number of prominent Elders in the Church died. Several new settlements were founded by the Saints, and there was general prosperity throughout the Church.

January 8, 1881 (Saturday)

Notwithstanding the fact that Geo. Q. Cannon was elected delegate to Congress with 17,211 majority, Gov. Eli H. Murray issued a certificate of election to the minority candidate, Allen G. Campbell.

January 9, 1881 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Beaver Bottom, Millard Co., with Thos. Naylor as presiding Elder.

January 12, 1881 (Wednesday)

Between this date and the 17th fifteen lives were lost through snowslides in Little Cottonwood and American Fork canyons; $60,000 worth of property was also destroyed.

January 15, 1881 (Saturday)

The Wasatch Flour Mill, on the State Road, Salt Lake Co., was destroyed by fire.

January 15, 1881 (Saturday)

The dead body of Charles Jensen, of Rush Valley, Tooele Co., was found. He had been murdered.

January 20, 1881 (Thursday)

Geo. Reynolds was released from the Penitentiary, his term of imprisonment having expired.

January 23, 1881 (Sunday)

Freeborn Demill, one of the early members of the Church, died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

February 4, 1881 (Friday)

Z.C.M.I. store at Ogden was dedicated.

February 15, 1881 (Tuesday)

The Saints who had settled in Grand Valley, Utah, were organized as the Moab Ward, by Apostles Francis M. Lyman and Heber J. Grant; Randolph H. Stewart, Bishop.

February 16, 1881 (Wednesday)

The trial of Fred. Hopt (Welcome), for the murder of John F. Turner, was commenced in the third District Court, Salt Lake City. On the 19th the jury returned a verdict of guilty.

February 27, 1881 (Sunday)

Aurora Ward, Sevier Co., was organized; Jabez Durfee, Bishop.

March 3, 1881 (Thursday)

A number of men were killed by an explosion in a coal mine, at Almy, near Evanston, Wyoming.

March 12, 1881 (Saturday)

Elder Samuel L. Evans died in Salt Lake City.

April 3, 1881 (Sunday)

On this and the following three days the 51st annual conference of the Church was held in Salt Lake City. A number of missionaries were called to go abroad and others to settle in Arizona.

April 4, 1881 (Monday)

Fred. Hopt, alias Welcome, the murderer, was sentenced to be shot on May 20th. The case was appealed.

April 4, 1881 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court reversed the decision of the Utah courts in the John Miles polygamy case, and a new trial was ordered.

April 17, 1881 (Sunday)

James Drysdale was shot and killed by Peter Moore, at Hooperville, Weber Co.

April 17, 1881 (Sunday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 186 Saints, in charge of David C. Dunbar. The company arrived at New York April 26th, and at Salt Lake City May 5th.

May 2, 1881 (Monday)

The first issue of the Ogden Herald was published in Ogden, Utah; John Nicholson, editor; Edward H. Anderson, business manager.

May 21, 1881 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 278 Saints, in charge of Joseph R. Matthews. The company arrived at New York June 1st, and at Salt Lake City June 10th.

May 23, 1881 (Monday)

A company of 27 Saints from New Zealand arrived in Salt Lake City, in charge of George Batt.

May 25, 1881 (Wednesday)

The Old Mill, or Locust Farm, containing 110 acres, was purchased by Salt Lake City, for a public park.

May 26, 1881 (Thursday)

The Union Pacific and Central Pacific transfer depot and twenty cars of merchandise, at Ogden, were destroyed by fire.

June 1881

Three railroads, namely, the Utah Central, Utah Southern and Utah Southern Extension, were consolidated as one corporation under the name of the Utah Central Railway, with a capital of $4,325,000. The new corporation commenced business July 1st.

June 4, 1881 (Saturday)

Elder Henry Emery died in the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City.

June 11, 1881 (Saturday)

Patriarch John Stoker died at Bountiful, Davis Co.

June 12, 1881 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled in the Tonto Basin and vicinity, Ariz., were organized as a Ward; Riel Allen, Bishop.

June 22, 1881 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County had a pleasant excursion to Ogden.

June 25, 1881 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 775 Saints, in charge of Samuel Roskelley. The company arrived at New York July 7th, and at Ogden July 15th.

June 27, 1881 (Monday)

The Saints residing at Frisco, Beaver Co., were organized as a branch of the Church; Benjamin Bennett, presiding Elder.

July 16, 1881 (Saturday)

Joseph Young, sen., brother of the late Pres. Brigham Young, and senior president of all the Seventies, died in Salt Lake City.

July 16, 1881 (Saturday)

Twenty-two Saints from Iceland, in charge of John Eyvindson, sailed from Liverpool, England, bound for Utah.

July 18, 1881 (Monday)

Two little girls, daughters of John C. Harper, were killed by lightning at Payson, Utah Co.

July 24, 1881 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the Provo river, northeast of Heber City, Wasatch Co., were organized as the Woodland Ward; Henry Moon, Bishop.

July 27, 1881 (Wednesday)

Senator John Sherman, of Ohio, General Benjamin Harrison, of Indiana, Judge Strong and Albert Bierstadt, the landscape painter, visited Salt Lake City.

July 30, 1881 (Saturday)

Architect Obed Taylor died at Salt Lake City.

August 1, 1881 (Monday)

Elder Niels Wilhelmsen, president of the Scandinavian mission, died at Copenhagen, Denmark. He was the first Elder from America who died in Scandinavia.

August 3, 1881 (Wednesday)

Elder Wm. C. Staines died in Salt Lake City.

August 4, 1881 (Thursday)

The corner stone of the Walker Opera House, Salt Lake City, was laid.

August 16, 1881 (Tuesday)

A Ward was organized at Clinton, Utah Co.; John Spencer, Bishop.

September 3, 1881 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 644 Saints, in charge of James Finlayson. The company arrived at New York, Sept. 13th, and at Salt Lake City, Sept. 21st.

September 11, 1881 (Sunday)

The Saints in Ashley Valley, Uintah Co., were organized into two Wards, namely, Ashley and Mountain Dell, with Jeremiah Hatch and Thos. Bingham as Bishops, respectively.

September 18, 1881 (Sunday)

Apostle Orson Pratt delivered his last public discourse, in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

September 20, 1881 (Tuesday)

Elder Solomon Angell died at Leeds, Washington Co.

September 27, 1881 (Tuesday)

Feramorz L. Young died of typhoid fever and was buried at sea, about one hundred miles from Havana, while returning from a mission to Mexico.

September 28, 1881 (Wednesday)

Hon. John M. Bernhisel died at his residence in Salt Lake City.

October 3, 1881 (Monday)

Apostle Orson Pratt died in Salt Lake City.

October 10, 1881 (Monday)

Geo. J. Belliston was killed by lightning at Nephi, Juab Co.

October 14, 1881 (Friday)

Bishop Edwin D. Woolley died in Salt Lake City.

October 22, 1881 (Saturday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 396 Saints, in charge of Lyman R. Martineau. The company landed in New York Nov. 2nd, and arrived at Ogden and Salt Lake City Nov. 11th.

October 24, 1881 (Monday)

George D. Watt, the first man baptized in the British mission, died at Kaysville, Davis Co.

October 25, 1881 (Tuesday)

After a lengthy trial in the Third District Court, Jack Emerson was adjudged guilty of the murder of John F. Turner, as an accomplice of Fred. Hopt.

October 25, 1881 (Thursday)

Joel Hinckley, railroad agent at Franklin, Oneida Co., Idaho, was murdered by two masked men, who subsequently were arrested.

October 28, 1881 (Friday)

Stephen Hales, an old member of the Church, died in the 16th Ward, Salt Lake City.

November 10, 1881 (Thursday)

Wm. Falconbridge, a centenarian, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Oct. 24, 1780.

November 11, 1881 (Friday)

David D. Morgan, of Salt Lake City, was accidentally killed, while working in a coal mine, in Pleasant Valley.

November 23, 1881 (Wednesday)

Robert Pixton, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Taylorsville, Salt Lake Co.

November 25, 1881 (Friday)

The Saints who had settled on Poole's Island, Snake River Valley, Idaho, were organized as a branch of the Church, by Marriner W. Merrill, of the Cache Stake presidency, with John R. Poole as presiding Elder.

November 26, 1881 (Saturday)

Jacob M. Truman, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Hamblin, Washington, Co.

November 28, 1881 (Monday)

Geo. Beebe died at Provo.

December 4, 1881 (Sunday)

Elder Daniel A. Miller, of Farmington, Davis Co., died at Providence, Cache Co.

December 8, 1881 (Thursday)

Albert R. Carrington, conductor on the Utah Central Railway, was fatally hurt at the depot, in Salt Lake City. He died the following day.

December 20, 1881 (Tuesday)

Evan Morgan, one of the first who joined the Church at Swansea, Wales, died in the 21st Ward, Salt Lake City.

December 22, 1881 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled in Pleasant Valley, Emery Co., were organized as a branch of the Church; David Williams, president.
