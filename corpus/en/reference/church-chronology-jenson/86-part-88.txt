Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The People's Party in Utah was dissolved and most of its members united with the two great national parties—Democrats and Republicans. Under the Liberal rule Salt Lake City became a regular rendezvous for foot pads, burglars and thieves. Immorality, wickedness and lawlessness had full sway; taxation was made oppressive and unjust.

January 1891

The Deseret Museum was removed from its old quarters, opposite the south gate of the Temple Block, Salt Lake City, to Zion's Saving Bank building (Templeton building).

January 5, 1891 (Monday)

Emma Abbott, the celebrated American opera singer, died suddenly in Salt Lake City.

January 6, 1891 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, John Halgren, of Richmond, was sentenced by Judge Miner to 45 days' imprisonment; Fred. Yeates, of Millville, to 45 days; Ira Allen, of Hyrum, to 10 days; Wm. Popleton, of Wellsville, to 45 days, and Archibald McFarland, of Ogden, to three months; all for u.c.

January 14, 1891 (Wednesday)

Geo. Hancock's case of alleged murder came up on appeal from the First District Court, before the Supreme Court of Utah, which finally granted him a new trial.

January 16, 1891 (Friday)

Ira Allen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 17, 1891 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Utah, Thos. Stirland, of Providence, was sentenced by Judge Miner to 2 1/2 years' imprisonment and $75 fine, for breaking the Edmunds law.

January 18, 1891 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on or near the Provo river, northeast of Provo, Utah Co., were organized as the Pleasant View Ward; Alexander Gillispie, Bishop.

January 19, 1891 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court rendered a decision, that polygamous children born within one year after the passage of the Edmunds law were legitimate and entitled to inherit after their fathers, thus reversing the findings of the Supreme Court of Utah.

January 20, 1891 (Tuesday)

The First Ward meeting house at Ogden was destroyed by fire.

January 24, 1891 (Saturday)

Lucy Decker Young, widow of Pres. Brigham Young, and one of the first three Pioneer women of Utah, died in Salt Lake City.

January 24, 1891 (Saturday)

Luke Sherwood was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 26, 1891 (Monday)

Elder Wm. Greenwood died at American Fork, Utah Co.

January 27, 1891 (Tuesday)

Mayor Geo. M. Scott's report for 1890, submitted to the city council of Salt Lake City, exhibited an enormous squandering of public funds in the city since the Liberals came into power.

February 2, 1891 (Monday)

The Deseret Museum was reopened in the Zion's Savings Bank building; James E. Talmage, curator.

February 4, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Supreme Court of Utah granted George W. Hancock (accused and convicted of killing Henry Jones, at Salem, Utah Co., April 24, 1858,) a new trial.

February 5, 1891 (Thursday)

The old Deseret Museum building, in Salt Lake City, was removed to make room for a new street (Richards Street).

February 9, 1891 (Monday)

The municipal election at Ogden, Utah, resulted in victory to the Citizens' Party, composed of "Mormons" and the better class of Liberals.

February 10, 1891 (Tuesday)

James Butcane, of Logan, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 11, 1891 (Wednesday)

Pres. Harrison commuted to four months the sentence of two years' imprisonment imposed on James H. Jenkins, of Goshen, Utah Co., who was dying at the Penitentiary. He was released the following day.

February 11, 1891 (Wednesday)

Wilson D. Pace, Fred. W. Ellis and Lars P. Johnson were discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 11, 1891 (Wednesday)

Lewis C. Bidamon (husband of Emma Smith, widow of the Prophet Joseph) died at Nauvoo, Ill.

February 12, 1891 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled in the Corralles Basin, Chihuahua, Mexico, were organized as the Pacheco Ward; Jesse N. Smith, jun., Bishop.

February 12, 1891 (Thursday)

Stephen S. Harding, ex-governor of Utah, died at Milan, Ind.

February 13, 1891 (Friday)

The former residence of Geo. Q. Cannon on South Temple Street, and other valuable property in Salt Lake City, were seized by the U.S. marshal, under the pretence that it was escheated Church property.

February 14, 1891 (Saturday)

A new place of amusement, called Wonderland, was opened in Salt Lake City.

February 17, 1891 (Tuesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Benjamin F. Knowlton was sentenced by Judge Judd to pay a fine of $150; Charles J. Lusty, of Coalville, to $100 fine; Thos. Beard, of Coalville, to $75 fine, and Thos. Copley and John W. Simester, both of Coalville, to $100 fine each; all for u.c. They promised to obey the law. Thos. Beard was imprisoned, not being able to pay his fine.

February 17, 1891 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Thos. Woolley, of Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to one month's imprisonment and $200 fine, for u.c.

February 17, 1891 (Tuesday)

Niels H. Borresen, Mahonri M. Bishop and James M. Stewart were discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 17, 1891 (Tuesday)

Geo. Dunford died in Salt Lake City.

February 18, 1891 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Jonas Ostler, James Shanks, Wm. McDonald, Wm. Foreman, Robert S. Duke and Wm. N. Casper were sentenced by Judge Blackburn to pay a fine of $100 each, for u.c.

February 18, 1891 (Wednesday)

Isaac Whicker was discharged from the Penitentiary.

In the First District Court, at Ogden, August J. Hansen, of Logan, was sentenced by Judge Miner to six months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

February 20, 1891 (Friday)

John Halgren, Fred. Yeates and Wm. Popleton were discharged from the Penitentiary.

Reuben McBride, a member of Zion's Camp, died at Fillmore, Millard Co.

John D. Parker, another member of Zion's Camp, died at Kanarra, Iron Co.

Jens P. Jensen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

Geo. H. Crawford was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 15, 1891 (Sunday)

James Butler was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 16, 1891 (Monday)

Elder Isaac Bullock died at Provo, Utah Co.

March 16, 1891 (Monday)

Thomas Woolley was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 18, 1891 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Richard Jenkins, of Nephi, was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to three months' imprisonment, and Thomas Featherston, of American Fork, to one month's imprisonment, both for u.c.

March 19, 1891 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Samuel Linton, of Nephi, was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

Pres. Daniel H. Wells died in Salt Lake City.

At a meeting of the Salt County Court it was decided to erect a joint city and county building on Washington Square, Salt Lake City.

March 27, 1891 (Friday)

John A. Powell was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 28, 1891 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, John B. Wasden was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to one year, and Orlando F. Herron, of Pleasant Grove, to four months' imprisonment; both for u.c. Herron had previously served two terms for infractions of the Edmunds law.

March 29, 1891 (Sunday)

John Archibald was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 2, 1891 (Thursday)

Nicholas H. Groesbeck was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 4, 1891 (Saturday)

The 61st annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City; continuing till the 6th.

April 4, 1891 (Saturday)

Lauritz B. Miller was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 5, 1891 (Sunday)

Aaron Hardy was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 10, 1891 (Friday)

Benjamin W. Driggs, of Pleasant Grove, Utah Co., was taken to the Penitentiary, having been sentenced by Judge Blackburn, at Provo, to six months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

April 13, 1891 (Monday)

Wm. B. Pace was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 17, 1891 (Friday)

Patriarch Thos. E. Jeremy died in Salt Lake City.

April 18, 1891 (Saturday)

Thos. Featherston was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 19, 1891 (Sunday)

Chauncey M. Depew, Cornelius Vanderbilt and other railroad magnates arrived in Salt Lake City on a visit.

April 20, 1891 (Monday)

Warren B. Smith was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 21, 1891 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, John M. Murdock was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to one month's imprisonment, for u.c.

April 26, 1891 (Sunday)

Elder Wm. C. Rawson died at Farr West, Weber Co.

April 30, 1891 (Thursday)

Hans Jensen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 2, 1891 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Niels Peter Christensen, of Randolph, Rich Co., was sentenced by Judge Miner to one year's imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

May 5, 1891 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Charles L. White was sentenced by Judge Miner to four months' imprisonment, for u.c. This was his third term of imprisonment for the same offence. He was taken to the Penitentiary the following day.

May 9, 1891 (Saturday)

Benjamin Harrison, President of the United States, and escort, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit, and was received with great enthusiasm by the populace, all classes joining in the demonstration.

May 10, 1891 (Sunday)

Henry Teeples was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 17, 1891 (Sunday)

Henry Harriman, one of the First Seven Presidents of the Seventies, died at Huntington, Emery Co.

May 17, 1891 (Sunday)

Susan Bayless Richards, relict of the late Willard Richards, died at Farmington, Davis Co.

May 18, 1891 (Monday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, John Thomas, of Logan, was sentenced by Judge Miner to sixty days' imprisonment; and Niels C. Andersen, of Hyrum, to pay a fine of $50; both for u.c. Both were taken to the Penitentiary, as Andersen, who had been imprisoned once before for the same offence, was unable to pay his fine.

May 19, 1891 (Tuesday)

John A. Mower was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 20, 1891 (Wednesday)

At a mass meeting held in the Salt Lake Theatre, it was decided to organize the Republican Party of Utah.

May 21, 1891 (Thursday)

John M. Murdock was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 25, 1891 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court rendered a decision, that the escheated Church property should still remain in the hands of the Receiver, and the Utah Supreme Court should take further action in the case.

May 25, 1891 (Monday)

A Republican political club was organized in Salt Lake City.

May 27, 1891 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Charles S. Hall, of West Portage, was sentenced by Judge Miner to 30 days' imprisonment, and Jens Frandsen, of Huntsville, was fined $100; both for u.c. They had previously served one term in the Penitentiary for the same offence.

May 29, 1891 (Friday)

At a meeting of the chief officers of the People's Party, held in Salt Lake City, it was decided to disorganize the People's party and advise its members to join the national parties.

May 29, 1891 (Friday)

At a Liberal rally held in Salt Lake City, bitter opposition was manifested to the division on national party lines, and the "Mormons" were accused of insincerity.

May 30, 1891 (Saturday)

Michael Stanley was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 1, 1891 (Monday)

The first number of the Lehi Banner was published at Lehi, Utah Co.

June 3, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Knutsford hotel on State Street, Salt Lake City, was opened to the public.

June 3, 1891 (Wednesday)

Richard Jenkins, of Nephi, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 4, 1891 (Thursday)

Samuel Linton was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 6, 1891 (Saturday)

Miss Emma Thursby sang at the musical festival given in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

June 10, 1891 (Wednesday)

At a meeting of the Territorial Central Committee of the People's Party, resolutions were adopted dissolving the People's Party of Utah, leaving its members free to unite with the great national parties, according to individual preferences.

June 15, 1891 (Monday)

Plymouth, Box Elder Co., was divided into two Wards, and the northern portion organized as Pierson Ward; Thos. Archibald, Bishop. The residents of the new Ward, however, soon changed its name to North Plymouth.

June 16, 1891 (Tuesday)

Catharine Campbell Steele, one of the Pioneer women of Utah, and mother of the first white child born in Great Salt Lake Valley, died at Toquerville, Washington Co.

June 17, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Saints who had settled at Freedom, in Lower Salt River Valley, on the boundary line between Idaho and Wyoming, were organized as Freedom Ward; Arthur B. Clark, Bishop.

June 18, 1891 (Thursday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County were treated to a free excursion to Springville, Utah Co.

June 18, 1891 (Thursday)

Niels C. Andersen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 18, 1891 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled in Lower Salt River Valley, Wyo., on the east side of the river, were organized as Glencoe Ward; John W. Titensor, Bishop.

June 20, 1891 (Saturday)

At a political meeting held in Salt Lake City, the Territorial Democratic Central Committee passed resolutions favoring division on national party lines.

June 20, 1891 (Saturday)

Bishop Alexander McRae died in Salt Lake City.

June 20, 1891 (Saturday)

The track on the Sevier branch of the Rio Grande Western Ry. was finished to Salina, Sevier Co.

June 26, 1891 (Friday)

Elizabeth Gilbert, relict of the late Algernon Sidney Gilbert, (who died of cholera in Missouri in 1834), died at Minersville, Beaver Co.

June 27, 1891 (Saturday)

Jens Frandsen and Henry Sudweeks were discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 28, 1891 (Sunday)

Farmers branch, Cache Co., was organized as a Ward; Charles O. Dunn, Bishop.

June 29, 1891 (Monday)

Katie Eliza Hale Merrill, wife of Elder Joseph H. Merrill, of Smithfield, Utah, died in childbed at the mission house at Fagalii, Samoa. Her baby boy died the same day.

July 1, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Utah Supreme Court appointed Judge Charles F. Loofbourow to take testimony in the Church suits as a master of chancery.

July 5, 1891 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled south of Paradise, Cache Co., were organized as the Avon Ward; Henry W. Jackson, Bishop

July 6, 1891 (Monday)

A Democratic convention, held in Salt Lake City, placed the first Democratic ticket for Salt Lake County officers in the field, after the division of the People's Party on party lines.

July 8, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Republicans of Salt Lake County held a convention in Salt Lake City and placed the first Republican ticket for county officers in Salt Lake County in the field, after the division of the People's Party on party lines.

July 8, 1891 (Wednesday)

Orlando F. Herron was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 10, 1891 (Friday)

At a large mass meeting held in Salt Lake City, resolutions were passed favoring division on national party lines, and denouncing the actions of the Liberals who were determined to continue the local warfare.

July 14, 1891 (Tuesday)

The Salt Lake County Liberals held a convention in Salt Lake City and placed candidates for county officers in the field.

July 15, 1891 (Wednesday)

Elders Brigham Smoot and Alva J. Butler arrived at Nukualofa, Tongatabu, Tonga, or the Friendly Islands, as the first Latter-day Saint Elders sent to that group.

July 16, 1891 (Thursday)

A Democratic convention was held in Salt Lake City, which nominated candidates for the Utah legislature.

July 17, 1891 (Friday)

Alfred G. Jackson and John Thomas were discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 18, 1891 (Saturday)

The San Francisco branch, near Casas Grandes, Chihuahua, Mexico, was organized as Dublan Ward; Winslow Farr, Bishop.

July 19, 1891 (Sunday)

August J. Hansen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 20, 1891 (Monday)

A Territorial Democratic convention met in Salt Lake City, passed resolutions and effected a complete Territorial organization.

July 21, 1891 (Tuesday)

Albert G. Slater was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 22, 1891 (Wednesday)

Republican conventions were held in the various legislative districts of Utah, at which candidates were nominated for the Utah legislature, to be voted for in August following.

July 24, 1891 (Friday)

Peter E. Olsen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 26, 1891 (Sunday)

The Saints residing on the uplands, northeast of Logan, Cache Co., were organized as Greenville Ward; Nicholas W. Crookston, Bishop.

July 27, 1891 (Monday)

John B. Wasden, Hans Jespersen and Charles A. Terry were discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 3, 1891 (Monday)

The election in Utah for members of the legislature resulted in the election of eight Democratic members for the Council and sixteen for the House, while the Liberals elected four for the Council and eight for the House. The Republican vote was small. In Salt Lake County the Liberals elected nearly the entire county ticket.

August 5, 1891 (Wednesday)

Dr. T. De Witt Talmage lectured in the Salt Lake Theatre.

August 6, 1891 (Thursday)

Samuel B. Axtell, formerly governor of Utah, died at Morristown, N.J.

August 6, 1891 (Thursday)

Rasmus Justesen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 9, 1891 (Sunday)

Hyrum S. Crane was discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 15, 1891 (Saturday)

Philander Colton, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Vernal, Uintah Co.

August 15, 1891 (Saturday)

Charles L. White was discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 20, 1891 (Thursday)

The first number of the Springville Independent was issued at Springville, Utah Co.

August 21, 1891 (Friday)

Elder John Pulsipher died at Hebron, Washington Co.

August 22, 1891 (Saturday)

Jay Gould, the railroad magnate, and party, visited Salt Lake City.

August 23, 1891 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled in Castilla County, Colo., were organized as a branch of the Church, with Simeon A. Dunn as president.

August 24, 1891 (Monday)

Mary Ann Pratt, widow of Apostle Parley Pratt, died at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

August 30, 1891 (Sunday)

Return Jackson Redden, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Hoytsville, Summit Co.

September 1891

The Utah Commission formulated a misleading report about the "Mormons" and the situation in Utah, to the Secretary of the Interior (John W. Noble). Gen. John A. McClernand prepared a minority report, dissenting from the other Commissioners.

September 2, 1891 (Wednesday)

A Territorial Republican convention met in Salt Lake City, adopted a platform, appointed delegates, etc.

September 6, 1891 (Sunday)

At the Stake conference, held at St. Johns, Ariz., the 104th quorum of Seventy was organized by John Morgan, with Charles G.D. Jarvis, Samuel D. Moore, W.C. Davis, Frithoff G. Nielsen, J.W. Brown, A.E. Cheeney and W.D. Rencher as presidents.

September 15, 1891 (Tuesday)

A great Irrigation Congress was opened and organized at the Exposition building, Salt Lake City, with 450 delegates present. Sixteen States and Territories were represented. The congress elected C.C. Wright, of California, chairman, and continued its sessions for three days.

September 16, 1891 (Wednesday)

The Irrigation Congress was addressed by Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Geo Q. Cannon.

September 18, 1891 (Friday)

Members of the Irrigation Congress, accompanied by Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Geo. Q. Cannon, visited Lehi, Provo and other places.

September 20, 1891 (Sunday)

Samuel H. Rogers, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Snowflake, Ariz.

September 23, 1891 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, John L. Butler, of Elsinore, and Josiah Bennett were each sentenced by Judge Blackburn to ten days' imprisonment, for u.c.

September 25, 1891 (Friday)

Judge U.G. Wenner died at his lonely retreat on Fremont Island, in the Great Salt Lake.

September 27, 1891 (Sunday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith, who had not appeared in public for several years, addressed the congregation at the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

September 30, 1891 (Wednesday)

Elder Joseph A. Allred died at Spring City, Sanpete Co.

October 5, 1891 (Monday)

The annual Territorial fair was opened in Salt Lake City.

A tin box, containing a number of papers and periodicals, was deposited in the base of one of the pillars of the Eagle Gate, which was in course of re-construction, in Salt Lake City.

October 6, 1891 (Tuesday)

The general conference of the Church in Salt Lake City passed a number of resolutions, condemnatory of the false reports which the Utah Commission had forwarded to the Secretary of the Interior.

October 8, 1891 (Thursday)

The Latter-day Saints' College building in the Seventeenth Ward, Salt Lake City, was dedicated and opened.

A Democratic Territorial convention, in Salt Lake City, condemned the report of the Utah Commission.

The new school building of the Central Seminary of the Salt Lake Stake of Zion, in Mill Creek, was dedicated.

The Lehi sugar factory commenced operations.

In the First District Court, at Provo, Chris. Anderson, of Monroe, was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to six months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

Arthur H. Campbell was discharged from the Penitentiary, his sentence having been commuted to one year.

In the Third District Court, at Provo, James M. Allred, of Fairview, Sanpete Co., was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to two months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

The first carload of granulated sugar from the Utah sugar factory, near Lehi, Utah Co., reached Salt Lake City, assigned to Cunnington & Co.

Judge Charles F. Loofbourow, as a master in chancery, commenced taking testimony in the Church cases, with a view to deciding what charitable uses the escheated Church property should be applied to. Presidents Wilford Woodruff and George Q. Cannon testified as witnesses.

October 20, 1891 (Tuesday)

Joseph H. Felt was arrested in Salt Lake City, on a charge of violating the Edmunds' law, examined and discharged for lack of evidence.

October 24, 1891 (Saturday)

In the Third District Court, at Salt Lake City, Judge Charles S. Zane decided that the Whitney property of the Tithing Office block, the Cannon House on South Temple street, the Council House corner, Salt Lake City, were exempt from confiscation, as well as the Tabernacle square and other property, at Ogden.

November 1, 1891 (Sunday)

Nathan B. Baldwin, a member of Zion's Camp, died at Fillmore, Millard Co.

At a meeting held in the Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co., the Seventies residing in that locality were organized as the 105th quorum of Seventy, with Martin Garn, John M. Whitaker and Thomas Alston as presidents.
