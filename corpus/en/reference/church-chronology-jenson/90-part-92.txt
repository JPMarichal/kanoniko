Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

A State constitution was made and adopted by the constitutional convention, held in Salt Lake City, and every preparation made for Utah's admission into the Union as a State.

January 4, 1895 (Friday)

A delegation of settlers waited upon Governor West and presented affidavits to the effect that the Ute Indians were committing depredations in San Juan County.

January 5, 1895 (Saturday)

Gov. West ordered Captains John Q. Cannon and Geo. W. Gibbs to proceed to the San Juan country and investigate the Indian troubles, caused by the presence of Colorado Utes in the county.

January 10, 1895 (Thursday)

The Board of Education inaugurated compulsory education in Salt Lake City.

January 10, 1895 (Thursday)

David Bigelow was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 20, 1895 (Sunday)

Elder John W. Turner, ex-sheriff of Utah County, died at Provo, Utah Co.

January 25, 1895 (Friday)

The first direct shipment of Utah flour to the Orient, was forwarded from Salt Lake City.

January 26, 1895 (Saturday)

Niels-Peter Thomson, who had been imprisoned since Feb. 27, 1894, serving a sentence for polygamy, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 26, 1895 (Saturday)

Major John H. Gilbert, who, as an employee in the printing office of Egbert Grandon, set the first edition of the Book of Mormon in type, in 1829-1830, died at Palmyra, N.Y.

January 26, 1895 (Saturday)

Captains Cannon and Gibbs returned to Salt Lake City from the San Juan country and reported on the Ute situation.

January 31, 1895 (Thursday)

The Utah militia organized and elected Henry Page colonel.

February 7, 1895 (Thursday)

Zeb. Barkdull was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 8, 1895 (Friday)

Elder John A. Clark, of Farmington, Utah, died at Haifa, Palestine, where he labored as a missionary.

February 10, 1895 (Sunday)

The Shelley branch, Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized as the Shelley Ward; John F. Shelley, Bishop.

February 13, 1895 (Wednesday)

The snow-fall in Sanpete Co. beat the record for twenty years.

February 17, 1895 (Sunday)

Magnus Erickson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 22, 1895 (Friday)

West Layton Ward, Davis Co., was organized with David E. Layton as Bishop.

February 24, 1895 (Sunday)

The Saints, who had settled on an island near Rexburg, Fremont Co., Idaho, were organized as the Island branch of the Church; Geo. Hibbard, presiding Elder.

February 25, 1895 (Monday)

In the First District Court, Ogden, Lorenzo Huish was sentenced by Judge Wm. H. King to 30 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

February 27, 1895 (Wednesday)

Richard W. Young was appointed brigadier-general of the Utah militia.

February 28, 1895 (Thursday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Edward Martin was sentenced by Judge Geo. W. Bartch to five months' imprisonment, for "adultery".

March 4, 1895 (Monday)

Utah's seventh constitutional convention convened in Salt Lake City.

March 6, 1895 (Wednesday)

Abraham O. Smoot, president of the Utah Stake, died at Provo, Utah Co.

March 7, 1895 (Thursday)

The laying of natural gas pipes was completed to Main Street, Salt Lake City. The next day (8th) the natural gas was turned on and lighted.

March 8, 1895 (Friday)

Prince Namah Imad Namey, of India, visited Salt Lake City.

March 13, 1895 (Wednesday)

Hyrum G. White was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 15, 1895 (Friday)

Elder David Mustard died at Treasureton, Bannock Co., Idaho.

March 18, 1895 (Monday)

Woman suffragists appeared in the constitutional convention in Salt Lake City and presented memorials.

March 20, 1895 (Wednesday)

General Philip St. George Cook, who had charge of the Mormon Battalion during part of its march to California, in 1846-47, died at Detroit, Mich.

March 27, 1895 (Wednesday)

Lorenzo Huish was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 3, 1895 (Wednesday)

The constitutional convention adopted the preamble and declaration of rights.

April 5, 1895 (Friday)

The sixty-fifth annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

April 5, 1895 (Friday)

The constitutional convention, after a heated debate of several days, adopted the woman suffrage clause.

April 6, 1895 (Saturday)

The Natural History Society of Utah was organized. Its purpose is to institute original researches on the subjects of geology, mineralogy, archaeology and biology in all their branches.

April 9, 1895 (Tuesday)

Bishop Richard S. Gibby died at Provo, Utah Co.

April 9, 1895 (Tuesday)

The first carload of Utah guano was placed on the market.

April 10, 1895 (Wednesday)

Taxpayers held a mass-meeting in Salt Lake City and recommended the issuance of $300,000 5-per cent ten-year bonds.

April 13, 1895 (Saturday)

Several county schools in Utah were closed for lack of funds.

April 14, 1895 (Sunday)

The Saints, who had settled between Hyrum and Wellsville, Cache Co., were organized as the Mt. Sterling Ward; Wm. John Hill, Bishop.

April 24, 1895 (Wednesday)

The constitutional convention voted down the prohibition clause after an exciting debate.

April 26, 1895 (Friday)

Dr. Wm. H. Groves died in Salt Lake City. He left all his real estate, valued at $75,000 or more, for the founding of the Dr. W.H. Groves Latter-day Saints' hospital.

April 28, 1895 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in the northern part of the Oneida Stake of Zion, Idaho, were organized by Seymour B. Young. Christian D. Fjeldsted and J. Golden Kimball, as the 108th quorum of Seventy. Wm. Thos. Higginson, Jonathan H. Hale and George Michael Smith were set apart as presidents.

May 4, 1895 (Saturday)

John McKellar was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 6, 1895 (Monday)

The constitutional convention adopted the constitution as a whole. The delegates were banqueted in the evening.

May 8, 1895 (Wednesday)

Delegates to the constitutional convention signed the constitution, and the convention adjourned sine die. The convention had been in session 66 days.

May 11, 1895 (Saturday)

Elder Andrew Jenson left Salt Lake City, on a special mission to the different Latter-day Saint missionary fields throughout the world, in the interest of Church history.

May 13, 1895 (Monday)

Prince Francis Joseph, of Battenberg, visited Salt Lake City.

May 13, 1895 (Monday)

The Intermountain Woman Suffrage convention convened in Salt Lake City; Susan B. Anthony presided.

May 14, 1895 (Tuesday)

Marysville Ward, in the Bannock Stake of Zion, Idaho, was organized by Apostle Franklin D. Richards and the Bannock Stake Presidency, with James H. Wilson as Bishop.

May 15, 1895 (Wednesday)

The great Western silver conference met in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City; Hon. Thos. G. Merrill, of Montana, chairman.

May 17, 1895 (Friday)

The silver convention effected a permanent organization under the name of the Bimetalic Union.

May 24, 1895 (Friday)

In the Fourth District Court, Ogden, Ole Sonne was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for "fornication."

May 24, 1895 (Friday)

Sister Ella Adelia Moody, who, with her husband, labored as a missionary in Samoa, died at Fagalii, Upolu, Samoa.

June 7, 1895 (Friday)

Elder Philo Dibble, an aged Church veteran, died at Springville, Utah Co.

June 9, 1895 (Sunday)

At a conference, held at Iona, Bingham Co., Idaho, the Bannock Stake of Zion was divided and its western part organized as the Bingham Stake; James E. Steeple, president, Robert L. Bybee and Joseph S. Mulliner, counselors.

June 9, 1895 (Sunday)

The Saints comprising the three Bishops' Wards (Cardston, AEtna and Mountain View) in Alberta Canada, were organized as the Alberta Stake of Zion; Charles O. Card, president; John A. Woolf and Sterling Williams, counselors.

June 9, 1895 (Sunday)

The Islands branch, Fremont Co., Ida., was organized as a Ward; Geo. Hibbard, Bishop.

June 11, 1895 (Tuesday)

Elder J. Hoglund baptized J. M. Lindelof and wife (a Swedish family), in the river Neva, St. Petersburg, Russia. This is believed to have been the first baptism performed by divine authority in that country.

June 20, 1895 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled at Bedford, Uinta Co., Wyo., were organized as a branch of the Church; John B. Thatcher, presiding Elder.

June 25, 1895 (Tuesday)

Commander in chief General T.G. Lawler arrived in Salt Lake City and was tendered a reception by the local G.A.R. members.

July 3, 1895 (Wednesday)

Edward Martin was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 11, 1895 (Thursday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County, on their annual excursion, spent a pleasant day at Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

July 24, 1895 (Wednesday)

Pioneer day was celebrated in grand style at Saltair, Salt Lake Co.

July 31, 1895 (Wednesday)

Elder Eli Bell died at Logan, Cache Co.

August 3, 1895 (Saturday)

Elder Charles Scott Hall, of West Portage, Box Elder Co., died near Mineota, Wood Co., Texas, where he labored as a missionary. His remains were brought home.

August 4, 1895 (Sunday)

Elder Miles Hudson Jones, a Church veteran, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

August 9, 1895 (Friday)

Elder Peter O. Hansen, the last survivor of the first four Latter-day Saint Elders who introduced the gospel in Scandinavia in 1850, died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

August 9, 1895 (Friday)

Ole Sonne was discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 10, 1895 (Saturday)

In the Fourth District Court at Ogden, Judge Harvey W. Smith decided that women had a right to vote on the State constitution.

August 10, 1895 (Saturday)

The school building for the deaf and dumb in Salt Lake City sustained a severe loss by fire.

August 11, 1895 (Sunday)

Elder Swen M. Lovendahl died at South Cottonwood, Salt Lake Co.

August 19, 1895 (Monday)

Forty-five buildings were burned at Bingham, Salt Lake Co, involving a loss of $200,000. Many people were rendered homeless.

August 21, 1895 (Wednesday)

Veterans from the Black Hawk Indian war had a re-union at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

August 22, 1895 (Thursday)

Bishop John Carson died at Fairfield, Utah Co.

August 25, 1895 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled north of Salem, Fremont Co., Idaho, were organized as the North Salem branch; Heber C. Roylance, presiding Elder.

August 27, 1895 (Tuesday)

By order of the war department, and in accordance with the enactment of Congress that a star should be added to the national flag for each State admitted into the Union, a new star was added for Utah. This increased the number of the stars in the national emblem to 45.

August 27, 1895 (Tuesday)

A fire in Cedar City, Iron Co., destroyed several buildings; damage, $4,000.

August 28, 1895 (Wednesday)

A Republican State convention was held in Salt Lake City.

August 31, 1895 (Saturday)

The Utah Supreme Court decided that women were not entitled to vote at the November election.

September 5, 1895 (Thursday)

The Democratic State convention was held at Ogden.

September 6, 1895 (Friday)

Congressman Wm. J. Bryan, of Nebraska, lectured in Salt Lake City, on the silver question.

September 8, 1895 (Sunday)

Darby Ward was organized in Fremont County, Idaho (Bannock Stake of Zion), with Emanuel Bagley as Bishop. Leigh Ward, in the same Stake, was organized, with Edwin S. Little as Bishop.

September 14, 1895 (Saturday)

A Populist State convention was held in Salt Lake City.

September 14, 1895 (Saturday)

In the Fourth District Court, Ogden, Samuel K. Obray was sentenced to two-years' imprisonment, and Mira Griffith Obray to one month's, both for "adultery". Charles Bauer was sentenced to four months' imprisonment, for "fornication."

September 17, 1895 (Tuesday)

Republican women held a monster mass meeting at the Grand opera house, Salt Lake City.

September 18, 1895 (Wednesday)

Patriarch Robert Wilson died at Oakley, Cassia Co., Idaho.

September 18, 1895 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, James Stevenson was sentenced to seven months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

September 30, 1895 (Monday)

Patriarch Geo. W. Brimhall died at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

October 2, 1895 (Wednesday)

The Denver choir, one of the best musical organizations in the West, gave a concert in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City.

October 3, 1895 (Thursday)

The great Eisteddfod was opened in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

October 4, 1895 (Friday)

The general semi-annual conference of the Church commenced in Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

October 5, 1895 (Saturday)

The Republicans in Salt Lake City had the greatest political demonstration ever seen in Utah.

October 8, 1895 (Tuesday)

Zion's Maori Association, consisting of returned missionaries from New Zealand, Australia and Tasmania was organized in Salt Lake City; Wm. Paxman, president; Benjamin Goddard, vice-president; Clarence W. Taylor, secretary.

October 10, 1895 (Thursday)

The court house at Ogden, Weber Co., was destroyed by fire.

October 14, 1895 (Monday)

Mira Griffith Obray was discharged from the Penitentiary.

October 15, 1895 (Tuesday)

Sir Michael Meyendorff, who had been in exile in Siberia, for taking part in the Polish attempt to obtain freedom from Russia, lectured in Salt Lake City, on his personal experience as an exile.

October 27, 1895 (Sunday)

Palisade Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized by James E. Steele and Robert S. Bybee, of the Bingham Stake presidency. Robert Oakden was ordained Bishop by Apostle Heber J. Grant, Dec. 14, 1895.

November 1895

Elder Daniel Jones Stewart, of Adamsville, Beaver Co., died in the Southern States, where he labored as a missionary.

November 1895

Elder Samuel E. Woolley succeed Elder Matthew Noall as president of the Hawaiian mission. This interesting mission was founded in December, 1850, and its first president was Hiram Clark, who was succeeded by the following Elders: Philip B. Lewis, Aug. 9, 1851; Silas Smith, July, 1855; Henry W. Bigler, pro tem, 1850; native Elders, 1858; Walter M. Gibson (without proper appointment), 1861; Ezra T. Benson and Lorenzo Snow (in temporary charge of reorganization purposes), April, 1864; Alma L. Smith, Oct., 1864; Geo. Nebeker, July, 1865; Fred. A.H.F. Mitchell, 1873; Alma L. Smith, (second term), Feb. 2, 1875; Ward E. Pack, June 20, 1876; Simpson M. Molen, March 20, 1878; Harvey H. Cluff, July 8, 1879; Edward Partridge, July 31, 1882; Enoch Farr, March 14, 1885; Wm. King, May 11, 1887; Ward E. Pack (second term), May 9, 1890; and Matthew Noall, Jan. 5, 1892.

November 2, 1895 (Saturday)

Elder Lauritz Larsen died at Spring City, Sanpete Co.

November 4, 1895 (Monday)

Elder Geo. B. Bailey died at Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

November 5, 1895 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, the Republicans elected Congressman Clarence E. Allen, a majority of the legislature, and the entire State ticket. Republicans also elected James Glendinning mayor of Salt Lake City.

November 5, 1895 (Tuesday)

At the instance and prearrangement of Fish and Game Commissioner A. Milton Musser, the generous fishermen of Utah and Sevier river lakes seined about six thousand pounds of the common fishes (including carp) from those waters; and the railroads forwarded them to Salt Lake City, free of charge, where they were distributed among the poor, through the Bishops, ministers and others, without distinction of class or color, under the supervision of Presiding Bishop Wm. B. Preston.

November 5, 1895 (Tuesday)

Christopher B. Heaton was shot and killed while attempting to capture thieves, near Colonia Pacheco, Chihuahua, Mexico.

November 13, 1895 (Wednesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, John Beck was fined $300 by Judge Wm. H. King, for u.c.

November 15, 1895 (Friday)

Elder Theodore Petersen who had just returned from a mission to Scandinavia, died at Logan, Cache Co.

November 18, 1895 (Monday)

Peter Ranck, one of Utah's early pioneers, died at East Mill Creek, Salt Lake City.

November 19, 1895 (Tuesday)

Bedford Branch, Uinta Co., Wyoming, was organized as Bedford Ward by Apostle John Henry Smith and others; John B. Thatcher, Bishop.

November 21, 1895 (Thursday)

Patriarch Lorenzo D. Young died in Salt Lake City. He was the last surviving brother of the late President Brigham Young.

November 23, 1895 (Saturday)

Judge Harvey W. Smith, of the Fourth District Court, died at Ogden. Henry H. Rolapp was subsequently appointed his successor in office.

December 1, 1895 (Sunday)

A part of South Hooper Ward, Davis County, was organized as Syracuse Ward; David Cook, Bishop.

December 9, 1895 (Monday)

At a special conference held at Colonia Juarez, Chihuahua, Mexico, the settlements of the Saints in Mexico were organized as the Juarez Stake of Zion by Apostles Francis M. Lyman and George Teasdale; Anthony W. Ivins, president; Henry Eyring and Helaman Pratt, counselors.

December 9, 1895 (Monday)

Hiatt Ward, in the Bannock Stake of Zion, Idaho, was organized; Hyrum J. Lucas, Bishop.

December 11, 1895 (Wednesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Benjamin Ralphs was sentenced by Judge Wm. H. King, to five days' imprisonment, for "adultery."

December 14, 1895 (Saturday)

Sister Mary Loretta P. Teasdale, wife of Apostle George Teasdale, died at Colonia Juarez, Mexico.

December 14, 1895 (Saturday)

Elder Joseph M. Watson died in Salt Lake City.

December 15, 1895 (Sunday)

East Jordan Ward, Salt Lake City., was organized by Apostle Abraham H. Cannon and the Salt Lake Stake presidency, with Hyrum Goff as Bishop. The Saints thus organized had formerly belonged to West Jordan Ward.

December 20, 1895 (Friday)

Elder Henry Talbot, one of the first converts to "Mormonism" in South Africa, died at Layton, Davis Co.

December 21, 1895 (Saturday)

Bishop George Knight died at Croyden, Morgan Co.

December 22, 1895 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Lima, Beaverhead Co., Montana; Daniel Clark, presiding Elder.
