Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Young Men's Mutual Improvement Association work was made universal throughout the settlements of the Saints. Judge McKean and supporters became very aggressive and caused considerable bitter feeling between the Mormon and anti-Mormon elements in Utah; the judge, however, was superceded by David B. Lowe.

January 5, 1875 (Tuesday)

An act of the Idaho legislature, creating Bear Lake County, was approved; Paris was made the county seat.

January 8, 1875 (Friday)

Wm. Fotheringham, of Beaver, was arrested on a charge of "committing adultery with his wife," and placed under $300 bonds.

January 10, 1875 (Sunday)

The Utah Western Railway (later the Utah and Nevada) was opened for traffic to Black Rock, on the shore of Great Salt Lake.

January 11, 1875 (Monday)

The explosion of a quantity of oil at the Utah Central Railway station, at Salt Lake City, did considerable damage to property.

January 11, 1875 (Monday)

A terrible snowslide, resulting in the loss of four lives and much property, occurred in Little Cottonwood Canyon.

January 16, 1875 (Saturday)

Six persons were killed by a snowslide, near Alta, Little Cottonwood Canyon.

January 20, 1875 (Wednesday)

Thomas Broderick, Joseph Ferguson and four other men were killed by a snowslide in Big Cottonwood Canyon. Their bodies were not found until the following spring.

January 24, 1875 (Sunday)

A delegation from Salt Lake City had an interview at Ogden with His Majesty Kalakaua, king of the Hawaiian Islands.

February 2, 1875 (Tuesday)

Samuel B. Axtell, ninth governor of Utah, arrived in Salt Lake City.

February 16, 1875 (Tuesday)

The Utah Southern Railway was completed to York, Juab Co.

February 19, 1875 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young, who had spent the winter in St. George, arrived in Salt Lake City.

February 25, 1875 (Thursday)

In the case of Brigham Young vs. Ann Eliza Young, Judge McKean decided that the defendant, Pres. Brigham Young, should pay $9,500 alimony.

March 3, 1875 (Wednesday)

W.G. Thomas was killed by a snowslide, in Little Cottonwood Canyon.

March 7, 1875 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at West Porterville, Morgan Co.

March 8, 1875 (Monday)

Joseph S. Schofield, of Salt Lake City, died at Bellevue, southern Utah.

March 9, 1875 (Tuesday)

The case of Kate Flint, vs. Jeter Clinton et al., for the abatement of her house of ill fame, by the Salt Lake City police officers, was commenced in the Third District Court. The jury disagreed.

March 11, 1875 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young was sentenced to confinement in the Penitentiary, by Judge James B. McKean, for alleged contempt of court, in the Ann Eliza Young case.

March 12, 1875 (Friday)

After 24 hours' confinement, Pres. Brigham Young was released from the Utah Penitentiary.

March 16, 1875 (Tuesday)

Richard Fryer shot his wife and baby and Thomas Batty, and Fryer was killed by the sheriff, who attempted to arrest the insane murderer, at Toquerville, southern Utah. Mrs. Fryer died of her wounds the same day, Batty on the 17th, and the baby on the 18th.

March 17, 1875 (Wednesday)

Major Seth M. Blair died at Logan.

March 18, 1875 (Thursday)

Chief Justice James B. McKean, was superceded by the appointment of David B. Lowe, of Kansas.

March 20, 1875 (Saturday)

About two hundred Indians from the desert were baptized at St. George.

March 25, 1875 (Thursday)

William Kay, the founder of Kaysville, Davis Co, died at Ogden.

March 31, 1875 (Wednesday)

The trial of George Reynolds, for polygamy, was commenced in the Third District Court, in Salt Lake City. The following day (April 1st) the jury brought in a verdict of guilty.

April 2, 1875 (Friday)

The case of Geo. Q. Cannon, indicted for polygamy, was dismissed in the Third District Court.

April 3, 1875 (Saturday)

Wm. H. Dame, indicted on a charge of having participated in the Mountain Meadows massacre in 1857, and who had been imprisoned since October, 1874, was taken out of the Utah Penitentiary and sent to Beaver.

April 6, 1875 (Tuesday)

The 45th annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City. It was continued till the 10th.

April 10, 1875 (Saturday)

Geo. Reynolds was sentenced to one year's imprisonment and a $300 fine for polygamy. The case was appealed, and the defendant admitted to bail in bonds of $5,000.

April 14, 1875 (Wednesday)

John D. Lee, who had been confined at Fort Cameron, was brought before the Second District Court, at Beaver, but the trial was continued for the term.

May 12, 1875 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 176 Saints, under the direction of Hugh S. Gowans and others. The company arrived at New York May 24th, and at Salt Lake City June 3rd.

May 14, 1875 (Friday)

Elder John B. Fairbanks died at Payson, Utah Co.

May 14, 1875 (Friday)

About two hundred and fifty aged people from Salt Lake County had a pleasant excursion to Dr. Clinton's Hotel, at Lake Point on the Great Salt Lake. This was the beginning of the Old Folks' annual excursions.

June 1875

General James A. Garfield arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

June 5, 1875 (Saturday)

Elder Wm. Gibson died at Salt Lake City.

June 7, 1875 (Monday)

Elder Ralph Harrison died in Salt Lake City, from the effects of an accident a few days previous.

June 8, 1875 (Tuesday)

Geo. W. Emery, of Tennessee, was appointed governor of Utah, in place of Samuel B. Axtell, who was removed because of his friendship to the "Mormons."

June 10, 1875 (Thursday)

The first Young Men's Mutual Improvement Association was organized in the 13th Ward, Salt Lake City, with H.A. Woolley as president, and B. Morris Young and Heber J. Grant as counselors.

June 15, 1875 (Tuesday)

John Burns, a railroad employee, was accidentally killed on the Utah Western (now Utah and Nevada) Railway.

June 16, 1875 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 167 Saints, under the direction of Robert T. Burton. The company arrived at New York June 27th, and at Salt Lake City July 8th.

June 19, 1875 (Saturday)

The Territorial Supreme Court reversed the decision in the case of George Reynolds, owing to the illegality of the grand jury that found the bill of indictment.

June 28, 1875 (Monday)

Bishop Culbert King baptized 85 Indians of Kanosh's band, at Kanosh, Millard Co. More than two thousand Indians had been baptized previous to this time.

June 30, 1875 (Wednesday)

The steamship Idaho sailed from Liverpool, England, with 765 Saints, under the direction of Christen G. Larsen. The company arrived at New York July 14th, and at Ogden July 22nd.

July 3, 1875 (Saturday)

Geo. W. Emery, of Tennessee, successor to Samuel B. Axtell as governor of Utah, arrived in Salt Lake City.

July 10, 1875 (Saturday)

Martin Harris, one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, died in Clarkston, Cache Co., 92 years of age.

July 16, 1875 (Friday)

Philip Klingensmith, an important witness for the prosecution in the John D. Lee case, arrived at Beaver, from California.

July 17, 1875 (Saturday)

Pres. Brigham Young, his Counselors and others renewed their covenants by baptism at Ephraim, Sanpete Co. This example was subsequently followed by the Saints generally.

July 17, 1875 (Saturday)

Emeline Free Young, wife of Pres. Brigham Young, died in Salt Lake City.

July 22, 1875 (Thursday)

Governor Samuel B. Axtell left Salt Lake City for New Mexico, where he had been appointed chief justice.

July 22, 1875 (Thursday)

The trial of John D. Lee, indicted for murder, was commenced at Beaver.

August 1, 1875 (Sunday)

Geo. W. Hill baptized over three hundred Indians in Box Elder County, Utah, and many of them, who were sick, were miraculously healed under his administration.

August 5, 1875 (Thursday)

Elder Joseph A. Young died at Manti, Sanpete Co., and Amos Fielding died in Salt Lake City.

August 7, 1875 (Saturday)

Bishop Wm. Miller, alias "Bogus Brigham," died at Provo, Utah Co., and Elder Alphonso Green died at his residence, between Lehi and American Fork, Utah Co.

August 7, 1875 (Saturday)

After a long trial in the case of John D. Lee, at Beaver, the jury disagreed.

August 12, 1875 (Thursday)

A band of peaceable Indians were driven from their grain fields and lodges on Bear river, by U.S. authority. This was evidently the result of a conspiracy on the part of the citizens of Corinne.

August 19, 1875 (Thursday)

Gen. Philip H. Sheridan and wife arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

August 25, 1875 (Wednesday)

Robert E. Biard, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Lynne, Weber Co., Utah.

September 1875

Apostle Albert Carrington succeeded Apostle Joseph F. Smith as president of the European mission.

September 1, 1875 (Wednesday)

Geo. A. Smith, first Counselor to Pres. Brigham Young, died at his home the Historian's Office—Salt Lake City.

September 15, 1875 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wyoming sailed from Liverpool, England, with 300 Saints, in charge of Richard V. Morris. The company landed in New York, Sept. 27th, and arrived at Salt Lake City, Oct. 5.

September 25, 1875 (Saturday)

Elizabeth Henriod suicided at Nephi, Juab Co.

September 27, 1875 (Monday)

Elder Haden W. Church, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States, died at Shady Grove, Hickman Co., Tenn. He was formerly a member of the Mormon Battalion.

October 3, 1875 (Sunday)

U.S. Grant, President of the United States, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit. He was met by Pres. Brigham Young and other prominent men at Ogden.

October 4, 1875 (Monday)

Pres. U.S. Grant and party left Salt Lake City for Denver, Colo.

October 9, 1875 (Saturday)

At the general conference the large Tabernacle, in Salt Lake City, was dedicated. A large number of missionaries were called during the conference.

October 14, 1875 (Thursday)

The steamship Dakota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 120 Saints, in charge of Bedson Eardley. The company arrived at New York Oct. 24th, and at Salt Lake City Nov. 3rd.

October 28, 1875 (Thursday)

Nine buildings in Salt Lake City were destroyed by fire.

October 29, 1875 (Friday)

Pres. Brigham Young was arrested by U.S. Marshal Geo. R. Maxwell, by order of Judge Boreman, on a charge of contempt of court. He had not complied with the order to pay $9,500 alimony to Ann Eliza Young.

October 30, 1875 (Saturday)

President Brigham Young deeded some valuable real estate for the B.Y. Academy, at Provo, to the trustees of that institution.

October 31, 1875 (Sunday)

Baron Lionel de Rothschild and party arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

November 1, 1875 (Monday)

The grand jury having found another indictment against George Reynolds for polygamy, he was again arrested and placed under $2,500 bonds.

November 3, 1875 (Wednesday)

Prince Frederick, of Wittgenstein, Count Turenne and Baron Rothschild paid a visit to Pres. Brigham Young, in Salt Lake City.

November 8, 1875 (Monday)

The Saints who had settled near the Sevier river, between Richfield and Glenwood, Sevier Co., Utah, were organized into the Prattville Ward, with Joseph K. Rogers as Bishop.

November 16, 1875 (Tuesday)

The First National Bank building in Salt Lake City was destroyed by fire; loss about $200,000.

November 18, 1875 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young was discharged from the custody of the U.S. marshal, by order of Chief Justice J. Alexander White.

December 1875

The ladies of Utah sent a petition having 23,626 signatures to Congress, praying for the admission of Utah into the Union as a State, and the repeal of the anti-polygamy laws.

December 9, 1875 (Thursday)

A second trial of George Reynolds for polygamy was commenced in the Third District Court, Salt Lake City.

December 14, 1875 (Tuesday)

A bill was presented to the U.S. House of Representatives, to enable the people of Utah to form a constitution and State government, and for the admission into the Union as a State.

December 19, 1875 (Sunday)

Elder John Snider, one of the first missionaries to England, died in Salt Lake City.

December 19, 1875 (Sunday)

James McKnight was excommunicated from the Church for apostasy.

December 21, 1875 (Tuesday)

Geo. Reynolds was sentenced, in the Third District Court, to two years' imprisonment and to pay a $500 fine. Pending an appeal to the Supreme Court, at Washington, D.C., the defendant was admitted to bail in $10,000.

December 25, 1875 (Saturday)

Another fatal snowslide occurred in Little Cottonwood Canyon.
