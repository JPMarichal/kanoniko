Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Joseph the Prophet became a candidate for the Presidency of the United States. Mobs gathered around Nauvoo, and during the ensuing troubles Joseph and his brother Hyrum were martyred in Carthage jail. The Twelve Apostles returned from their missions to the Eastern States and were accepted by the Saints as the presiding Council of the Church. A great number of Seventies were ordained.

January 2, 1844 (Tuesday)

Jonathan Pugmire, sen., and Thos. Cartwright, who had been imprisoned in Chester, England, about six weeks, for the accidental drowning of Mrs. Cartwright during an attempt to baptize her, Nov. 23, 1843, were acquitted.

January 3, 1844 (Wednesday)

A special session of the city council was held at Nauvoo because of Wm. Law's intimation that his life was in danger.

January 5, 1844 (Friday)

Wm. Marks, president of the Nauvoo Stake of Zion, being alarmed on account of a fire being kindled near his house, made statements before the city council; his fears were unfounded.

January 9, 1844 (Tuesday)

Elder Horace S. Eldredge, a county constable, was prevented by mob force from performing an official duty at Carthage.

January 10, 1844 (Wednesday)

John Smith, uncle to Joseph Smith, the Prophet, was ordained a Patriarch.

January 16, 1844 (Tuesday)

Francis M. Higbee was tried before the municipal court of Nauvoo for slandering Joseph Smith.

January 23, 1844 (Tuesday)

The ship Fanny sailed from Liverpool, England, with 210 Saints under the direction of Wm. Kay, bound for Nauvoo. It arrived at New Orleans, March 7th.

January 29, 1844 (Monday)

At a political meeting, held at Nauvoo, Joseph Smith was nominated a candidate for the Presidency of the United States. Soon afterwards a large number of Elders were sent to the various States of the Union to electioneer for him.

February 6, 1844 (Tuesday)

The ship Isaac Allerton sailed from Liverpool with 60 Saints, bound for Nauvoo.

February 7, 1844 (Wednesday)

Joseph Smith completed his address to the people of the United States, entitled: "Views of the Powers and Policy of the Government of the United States."

February 11, 1844 (Sunday)

The ship Swanton sailed from Liverpool with 81 Saints, bound for Nauvoo, where they arrived April 18th.

February 17, 1844 (Saturday)

The anti-Mormons held a convention at Carthage, Ill., the object being to devise ways and means for expelling the Saints from the State.

February 20, 1844 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith instructed the Twelve Apostles to send a delegation to California and Oregon, to search for a good location, to which the Saints could remove after the completion of the Temple.

February 21, 1844 (Wednesday)

A meeting of the Apostles was held at Nauvoo for the purpose of selecting "a company to explore Oregon and California and select a site for a new city for the Saints." Jonathan Dunham, Phinehas H. Young, David D. Yearsley and David Fullmer volunteered to go; and Alphonso Young, James Emmett, Geo. D. Watt and Daniel Spencer were requested to go.

February 23, 1844 (Friday)

Another meeting was held at Nauvoo, in favor of the California and Oregon expedition. Several of the brethren volunteered to go; among whom were Samuel Bent, John A. Kelting, Samuel Rolfe, Daniel Avery and Samuel W. Richards.

February 25, 1844 (Sunday)

Joseph Smith prophesied that in five years the Saints would be out of the power of their old enemies, whether apostates or of the world.

February 29, 1844 (Thursday)

Moses Smith and Rufus Beach volunteered to join the Oregon exploring expedition.

March 4, 1844 (Monday)

It was decided in council at Nauvoo to cease work on the Nauvoo House until the Temple was completed.

March 5, 1844 (Tuesday)

The ship Glasgow sailed from Liverpool with 150 Saints, led by Hiram Clark, bound for Nauvoo, where they arrived April 26th.

March 11, 1844 (Monday)

Joseph Smith and the leading authorities of the Church held another council at Nauvoo about the Saints moving to the mountains.

March 24, 1844 (Sunday)

Joseph Smith spoke in public meeting against Chauncey L. Higbee, Robert D. Foster, Wm. and Wilson Law and others, as conspirators against his life.

March 26, 1844 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith petitioned Congress to protect the citizens of the United States, emigrating west; this he did in view of the Saints going to the mountains in the near future.

April 5, 1844 (Friday)

The Masonic Temple, which had been erected at Nauvoo, was dedicated. About five hundred and fifty members of the Masonic fraternity from various parts of the world were present.

April 6, 1844 (Saturday)

A conference, which lasted five days, commenced at Nauvoo. The Prophet spoke to 20,000 Saints on the 7th, and on the 8th declared the whole of North and South America to be the land of Zion.

April 13, 1844 (Saturday)

Under the leadership of Wm. Kay, 210 British Saints arrived at Nauvoo.

April 18, 1844 (Thursday)

Wm. and Wilson Law, Robert D. Foster and other apostates, formerly prominent in the Church, were excommunicated.

April 26, 1844 (Friday)

Augustine Spencer, Robert D. Foster, Charles Foster and Chauncey L. Higbee were arrested and fined, in Nauvoo, for assault and resisting the officers.

May 1, 1844 (Wednesday)

Elders Addison Pratt, Noah Rogers and Benjamin F. Grouard landed on the island of Tubuai (one of the Austral group), as the first missionaries of the Church to the islands of the Pacific.

May 6, 1844 (Monday)

Joseph Smith was arrested at Nauvoo on complaint of Francis M. Higbee, but took out a writ of habeas corpus, and was tried on the 8th before the municipal court of Nauvoo, which resulted in Joseph's acquittal, and Higbee was sentenced to pay the cost of the suit.

May 14, 1844 (Tuesday)

Elders Noah Rogers and Benjamin F. Grouard landed at Papeete, Tahiti, Society Islands, as the first Latter-day Saint missionaries to that group.

May 15, 1844 (Wednesday)

Anthon H. Lund was born at Aalborg, Denmark.

May 17, 1844 (Friday)

A State convention was held at Nauvoo, Ill., in which Joseph Smith was nominated as a candidate for the Presidency, and Sidney Rigdon for the Vice Presidency, of the United States.

May 18, 1844 (Saturday)

The first number of The Prophet, a weekly paper devoted to the interests of the Church, was issued in New York City, by a society of Saints.

May 21, 1844 (Tuesday)

Apostles Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball and Lyman Wight, and about a hundred other Elders, left Nauvoo, Ill., on political missions to the East. Apostles Wilford Woodruff and Geo. A. Smith and others had left on the 9th.

May 23, 1844 (Thursday)

Joseph Smith had a talk with a number of Sac and Fox Indians at Nauvoo.

May 25, 1844 (Saturday)

Joseph Smith learned that the grand jury at Carthage had found two indictments against him, one of them for polygamy.

May 27, 1844 (Monday)

Joseph Smith, accompanied by a number of friends, went to Carthage to have the indictments against him investigated by the circuit court, but, the prosecution not being ready, the case was continued until next term.

June 7, 1844 (Friday)

The first and only number of the Nauvoo Expositor was published, edited by Sylvester Emmons.

June 10, 1844 (Monday)

The paper and printing material of the Nauvoo Expositor were destroyed, according to the proclamation of the city council, declaring it a nuisance.

June 12, 1844 (Wednesday)

Joseph Smith was arrested on a charge of destroying the Expositor, tried before the municipal court of Nauvoo and acquitted. The following day the other members of the city council were tried before the same court, on a similar charge, and honorably acquitted.

June 14, 1844 (Friday)

Joseph Smith communicated the facts connected with the removal of the Expositor's printing materials, by letter, to Gov. Thos. Ford.

June 16, 1844 (Sunday)

In a public meeting, held at Nauvoo, a number of delegates were called to visit the different precincts in Hancock County, Ill., to lay a truthful statement of the troubles in Nauvoo before the people. Joseph Smith, as mayor of the city, also stated the facts in a proclamation.

June 16, 1844 (Sunday)

Addison Pratt baptized Ambrose Alexander, a white man, on the island of Tubuai, as the first convert to "Mormonism" on the Pacific Isles.

June 17, 1844 (Monday)

Joseph Smith and a number of others were arrested, on complaint of W. G. Ware, for riot in destroying the Expositor, tried before Justice Daniel H. Wells, and, after a long and close examination, acquitted.

June 17, 1844 (Monday)

Mobs began to gather in the surrounding country, threatening to drive the Saints from Nauvoo.

June 18, 1844 (Tuesday)

The Nauvoo Legion was ordered out and the city declared under martial law, by the proclamation of the mayor, Joseph Smith. The Prophet delivered his last public address. An extra of the Warsaw Signal was read, in which all the "old citizens" were called upon to assist the mob in driving away the Saints.

June 19, 1844 (Wednesday)

Mobs were gathering at different points to attack Nauvoo.

June 20, 1844 (Thursday)

General Joseph Smith, with other officers of the Legion, examined the approaches to Nauvoo as a preparatory measure for defense. The Prophet also sent for the Twelve Apostles, who were on missions, to come home immediately.

June 22, 1844 (Saturday)

Late in the evening Joseph and Hyrum Smith and Willard Richards left Nauvoo and crossed the Mississippi river, with the intention to flee to the West, and thus escape from their enemies.

June 23, 1844 (Sunday)

Through the solicitation of Emma Smith, and several supposed friends, Joseph Smith and his companions returned to Nauvoo.

June 24, 1844 (Monday)

Joseph and Hyrum Smith, accompanied by seventeen friends, started for Carthage, to submit to another trial, under pledge of protection from Gov. Thos. Ford. On the way they received a demand from the governor to surrender the State arms in possession of the Nauvoo Legion; Joseph returned and complied with the request, and then proceeded to Carthage.

June 25, 1844 (Tuesday)

Joseph Smith and his brethren surrendered themselves to a constable at Carthage and submitted to a trial, after which they were, contrary to law, remanded to prison.

June 26, 1844 (Wednesday)

Gov. Thos. Ford had a long interview with the prisoners in Carthage jail. He renewed his promises of protection and said, if he went to Nauvoo, he would take them with him.

June 27, 1844 (Thursday)

Gov. Thos. Ford went to Nauvoo, leaving the prisoners in jail to be guarded by their most bitter enemies, the "Carthage Greys." About 5:20 p.m. an armed mob with blackened faces surrounded and entered the jail, and murdered Joseph and Hyrum Smith in cold blood; Apostle John Taylor was severely wounded, while Apostle Willard Richards only received a slight wound on his ear.

June 28, 1844 (Friday)

Apostle Willard Richards and Samuel H. Smith conveyed the bodies of the martyrs to Nauvoo, where they were met by the officers of the Nauvoo Legion, and a very large number of citizens.

June 29, 1844 (Saturday)

About ten thousand persons visited and viewed the remains of the martyred Prophet and Patriarch at Nauvoo. The funeral took place in the evening.

July 2, 1844 (Tuesday)

Apostle John Taylor was brought home to Nauvoo from Carthage.

July 8, 1844 (Monday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt arrived at Nauvoo; he was the first of the absent Twelve to return.

July 21, 1844 (Sunday)

Addison Pratt baptized four white men and four natives on the island of Tubuai. These natives, whose names were Nabota and his wife Telii, Pauma and Hamoe, were the first of the Polynesian race to embrace the fulness of the gospel.

July 25, 1844 (Thursday)

Erastus Snow and many other Elders arrived at Nauvoo. All seemed weighed down with gloom.

July 28, 1844 (Sunday)

Apostle Geo. A. Smith and a party of brethren arrived at Nauvoo.

July 28, 1844 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church, consisting of eleven members, was organized by Addison Pratt on the island of Tubuai (Society Islands mission). This was the first branch of the Church on the Pacific Islands.

July 30, 1844 (Tuesday)

Samuel H. Smith, brother of the Prophet, died at Nauvoo, as a martyr to persecution.

July 31, 1844 (Wednesday)

Apostle Amasa M. Lyman arrived at Nauvoo.

August 2, 1844 (Friday)

A political meeting of the citizens of Hancock County, Ill., was held near the Temple at Nauvoo. Great excitement prevailed throughout the county. The mob party was determined to elect officers who would screen the murderers of Joseph and Hyrum Smith and exterminate the "Mormons."

August 3, 1844 (Saturday)

Sidney Rigdon arrived at Nauvoo from Pittsburgh, Pa.

August 4, 1844 (Sunday)

Sidney Rigdon preached to the Saints at Nauvoo, declaring that a guardian should be appointed to build up the Church to Joseph, intimating that he was the man who should lead the Saints.

August 6, 1844 (Tuesday)

Apostles Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, Lyman Wight, Orson Hyde, Orson Pratt and Wilford Woodruff arrived at Nauvoo.

August 7, 1844 (Wednesday)

The Twelve met in council with Elder Taylor, at his house at Nauvoo; they found him recovering from his wounds. In the afternoon, the Twelve, the High Council and High Priests held a meeting in the Seventies' Hall, where Sidney Rigdon's claim to lead the Church was considered.

August 8, 1844 (Thursday)

A special meeting of the Church was held at Nauvoo, in which Elder Rigdon harangued the Saints about choosing a guardian, etc. In the afternoon meeting the Twelve Apostles, through their President, Brigham Young, asserted their right to lead the Church, which claim was recognized by the unanimous vote of the people.

August 12, 1844 (Monday)

At a council of the Twelve Apostles, Amasa M. Lyman was admitted into their quorum, having been previously ordained to the Apostleship. Elder Wilford Woodruff was appointed to go to England to preside over the British mission.

August 15, 1844 (Thursday)

The Twelve issued an epistle to the Saints in all the world, giving such instructions and words of counsel to the Church as were necessary after the martyrdom of the Prophet.

August 28, 1844 (Wednesday)

Wilford Woodruff, Dan Jones and Hiram Clark, with their families, left Nauvoo for England.

August 31, 1844 (Saturday)

Brigham Young was elected lieutenant-general of the Nauvoo Legion, and Charles C. Rich, major-general.

September 8, 1844 (Sunday)

At a meeting of the High Council of Nauvoo, Sidney Rigdon was excommunicated from the Church.

September 19, 1844 (Thursday)

The ship Norfolk sailed from Liverpool with 143 Saints, bound for Nauvoo.

September 24, 1844 (Tuesday)

Seventy presidents to preside over the Seventies, and fifty High Priests to preside in different sections of the country, were ordained.

September 27, 1844 (Friday)

Gov. Thos. Ford visited Nauvoo with about five hundred troops and three pieces of artillery, ostensibly for the purpose of bringing the murderers of Joseph and Hyrum Smith to justice.

September 28, 1844 (Saturday)

About this time several persons in Hancock County were indicted for the murder of Joseph and Hyrum Smith, among whom was Jacob C. Davis.

October 7, 1844 (Monday)

At the general conference held in Nauvoo Wm. Marks was rejected as president of the Stake and John Smith appointed in his stead.

October 8, 1844 (Tuesday)

A reorganization of the Seventies took place in the general conference at Nauvoo. At the close eleven quorums were filled and properly organized, and about forty Elders organized as a part of the 12th quorum. The senior presidents of these twelve quorums of Seventy were Joseph Young (1st), Edson Barney (2nd), Elias Hutchins (3rd), Jacob Gates (4th), Henry Jacobs (5th), Israel Barlow (6th), Randolph Alexander (7th), John Pack (8th), Philip Ettleman (9th), Albert P. Rockwood (10th), Jesse P. Harmon (11th), and Hyrum Dayton (12th).

October 8, 1844 (Tuesday)

About the same time the 16th quorum of Seventy was organized, with Dana Jacobs as senior president.

November 23, 1844 (Saturday)

Edward Hunter was ordained a Bishop and set apart to take care of the 5th Ward in Nauvoo.

December 1, 1844 (Sunday)

Apostle Parley P. Pratt was appointed to go to the city of New York to regulate and counsel the emigration from Europe and preside over all the eastern branches of the Church.

December 22, 1844 (Sunday)

The 13th, 14th and 15th quorums of Seventy were organized in Nauvoo, with Charles Bird, Jonathan Dunham and John Lytle as senior presidents.
