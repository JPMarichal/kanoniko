Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year the Temple in Salt Lake City was dedicated and opened for ordinance work; Saltair was built in the Great Salt Lake; About seven thousand people from Utah visited the World's Fair in Chicago, Ill.

January 1, 1893 (Sunday)

A. Johnson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 1, 1893 (Sunday)

Elder Joseph Lee Robinson died at Uintah, Weber Co.

January 3, 1893 (Tuesday)

Lucy S. Grant, wife of Apostle Heber J. Grant, died in Salt Lake City.

January 3, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Hiram S. Wright was sentenced by Judge Zane to six months' imprisonment, for u.c.

January 4, 1893 (Wednesday)

Pres. Benjamin Harrison issued a proclamation of amnesty to polygamists, for past offences, but recommended vigorous prosecution against future infractions of the Edmunds law.

January 6, 1893 (Friday)

Elder Thomas Day died at Circleville, Piute Co.

January 6, 1893 (Friday)

James Anderson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 7, 1893 (Saturday)

Jacob P. Albertson and John G. Jorgensen were discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 8, 1893 (Sunday)

Andrew Bigler suicided at Mendon, Cache Co.

January 11, 1893 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake City were treated to a free entertainment in the Salt Lake Theatre.

January 20, 1893 (Friday)

Benjamin Lewis was killed by an explosion of giant powder at Richmond, Cache Co.

January 20, 1893 (Friday)

Eli A. Day was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 22, 1893 (Sunday)

Anton Nelson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 28, 1893 (Saturday)

The Beehive house, formerly the property of Pres. Brigham Young, was sold at public auction in Salt Lake City.

January 30, 1893 (Monday)

A wind storm did considerable damage at Heber, Wasatch Co.

January 31, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, the cases against the following named individuals, for transgression of the Edmunds law, were dismissed on recommendation of U.S. Attorney Charles S. Varian, who gave as a reason that the evidence in these cases was insufficient to put the defendants on trial; Levi W. Smith, Thos. Magram, W.E. Richardson, Charles O'Connor. Wm. O'Connor, Caroline Phillips, Frank J. Sadler, Frank Sadler, Joseph Porter, Frank E. McWilliams, Albert Murdock, James Taylor, Sarah Nelson, Henry Hughes, W.S. Popperton, Orson Smith, Thos. Obray, John Archibald, Wm. Willey, Andrew J. Kershaw, Moroni Marriott, Wilmer H. Branson, Peter C. Geertsen, Wm. R.R. Stowell, Joseph Parry, Daniel F. Thomas, John Stoddard, Soren L. Petersen, B.C. Critchlow, Mark Lindsey, Christopher O. Folkman, Louis Howells, Chas. O. Card, Arthur Farrell, Thos. Leavitt, Peter Hansen, F.W. Christensen, Samuel Watkins, Christina Nielson, S.H. Putnam, Elisha Campbell, John J. Johnson, Mary Williams, Christian Schneider, Elizabeth Manor, Wm. H. Dopp, Wm. J. Orchard, Mary A. Wheeler, Lars Johnson, Thos. Stirland, Wm. Blair, Peter Christensen, A.F. Randall.

February 5, 1893 (Sunday)

John H. Rumel, jun., died suddenly in Salt Lake City.

February 9, 1893 (Thursday)

The name of South Iona Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho, was changed to Ammon.

February 13, 1893 (Monday)

In the U.S. Congress, Senator Wm. M. Stewart, of Nevada, introduced a bill providing for the refunding, to Geo. Q. Cannon, of $25,000, forfeited on an enormous bond imposed upon him by the Third District Court in 1886, when he was placed under arrest for u.c.

February 14, 1893 (Tuesday)

The gas and electric light companies of Salt Lake and Ogden consolidated by the incorporation of the Salt Lake & Ogden Gas and Electric Light Company.

February 19, 1893 (Sunday)

Gilbert Webb was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 20, 1893 (Monday)

Thos. H. Bullock was discharged from his third term of imprisonment in the Penitentiary for infraction of the Edmunds law. Frank P. Hadlock was also discharged.

February 22, 1893 (Wednesday)

Apostle Brigham Young sailed from Liverpool, England, for Utah, leaving Elder Alfred Solomon in charge of the European mission.

February 25, 1893 (Saturday)

The Seventies residing in Mexico were organized by Brigham H. Roberts as the 99th quorum of Seventy; Helaman Pratt, John C. Harper, Anson B. Call, Geo. W. Hardy, Dennison E. Harris, Wm. W. Galbraith and Sullivan C. Richardson, presidents. This quorum had previously existed in the St. George Stake.

February 27, 1893 (Monday)

The first consignment of Utah exhibits for the World's Fair was shipped from Salt Lake City.

February 28, 1893 (Tuesday)

Elder Jens C.A. Weibye died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

March 6, 1893 (Monday)

In the Fourth District Court at Ogden, Nelson Arave was sentenced by Judge Miner to 60 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

March 7, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Josiah Gough was sentenced to six months' imprisonment for "adultery"; he was incarcerated in the Penitentiary April 10th following.

March 7, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, Wm. Bullam was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, for u.c. He commenced his term in the Penitentiary on the 11th.

March 8, 1893 (Wednesday)

In the Fourth District Court at Ogden, Wm. H. Watson, of Farmington, was sentenced by Judge Miner to four months' imprisonment in the Penitentiary, for u.c.

March 10, 1893 (Friday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, the cases against Sidney Stevens, David Eccles and Wm. Lishman, for u.c., were dismissed.

March 26, 1893 (Sunday)

Elder Moses Martin died at North Jordan, Salt Lake Co.

March 26, 1893 (Sunday)

Nicholas H. Groesbeck was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 27, 1893 (Monday)

The corner stone of a new meeting house was laid at Sandy, Salt Lake Co.

March 30, 1893 (Thursday)

Miss Blanche B. Cox, of London, England, a captain of the Salvation Army, lectured in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, on the "Mission of the Army."

April 1, 1893 (Saturday)

Nils C. Erickson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 1, 1893 (Saturday)

The Deseret Paper Mill, at the mouth of Big Cottonwood Canyon, Salt Lake Co., was destroyed by fire.

April 4, 1893 (Tuesday)

The sixty-third annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

April 6, 1893 (Thursday)

The Salt Lake Temple was dedicated, the prayer being offered by Pres. Wilford Woodruff. The dedicatory services were repeated almost daily till April 24th. Thirty-one meetings were held, which were attended by a total of nearly 75,000 people.

April 7, 1893 (Friday)

John Oberg was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 7, 1893 (Friday)

Caleb W. West was appointed governor of Utah, to succeed Arthur L. Thomas.

April 20, 1893 (Thursday)

Elders Joseph Wood and Reese Morris Harper, Mormon missionaries, laboring in the Indian Territory, were held up by a highwayman and robbed of their watches at Millan Bridge.

April 25, 1893 (Tuesday)

The Trans-Mississippi Congress convened at Ogden, Weber Co. Delegates from twenty States and Territories were present.

May 3, 1893 (Wednesday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, Charles Harris was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

May 3, 1893 (Wednesday)

Geo. Godfrey, having been pardoned by Pres. Cleveland, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 4, 1893 (Thursday)

A public reception was tendered Caleb W. West, the newly appointed governor for Utah, in the Salt Lake Theater, where the oath of office was administered to him by Chief-Justice Charles S. Zane.

May 5, 1893 (Friday)

Bishop Oscar Dunn, of College Ward, Cache Co., was arrested for u.c. and placed under $1,000 bonds.

May 5, 1893 (Friday)

Nelson Arave was discharged from the Penitentiary.

May 6, 1893 (Saturday)

Joseph L. Rawlins resigned his position as Utah's delegate to Congress and left Washington, D.C., for home. Later, however, he resumed his duties at the Capitol.

May 6, 1893 (Saturday)

Charles C. Richards, of Ogden, was appointed Territorial secretary for Utah. He entered upon the duties of his office on the 15th.

May 8, 1893 (Monday)

Castle Gate branch, Emery Co., was organized by Apostle Francis M. Lyman as Castle Gate Ward; Wm. T. Lamph, Bishop.

May 8, 1893 (Monday)

Nat. M. Brigham was appointed U.S. Marshal for Utah, and Harvey W. Smith, associate justice.

May 19, 1893 (Friday)

Elder John S. Lewis died in Salt Lake City.

May 23, 1893 (Tuesday)

The Salt Lake Temple was opened for ordinance work, under the immediate direction of the First Presidency—Wilford Woodruff, George Q. Cannon and Joseph F. Smith. The following were the officers; Lorenzo Show, president; John R. Winder, first assistant; Adolph Madson, second assistant; John Nicolson, chief recorder; Joseph H. Dean, janitor; William H. Salmon, doorkeeper, Zina D.H. Young, president of sisters' department, with Bathsheba W. Smith and Minnie J. Snow, her assistants.

May 24, 1893 (Wednesday)

Wm. H. Jennings, a prominent business man, committed suicide in Salt Lake City.

May 25, 1893 (Thursday)

In the Fourth District Curt, at Ogden, Henry Stander was sentenced by Judge Miner to four months' imprisonment for "adultery".

May 27, 1893 (Saturday)

The Wayne Stake of Zion was organized by Apostles Francis M. Lyman and Marriner W. Merrill, with Willis E. Robison as president. The new Stake that portion of Garfield County lying north and east of Potatoe Valley, known as the Boulder Plateau.

May 27, 1893 (Saturday)

The Bear River canal system, in Box Elder County, and all the lands lying under it, were sold to Geo. L. Walker, an eastern capitalist.

May 31, 1893 (Wednesday)

Articles of incorporation of the Mt. Pleasant (Sanpete Co.) Light Company were filed.

June 1893

Apostle Anthon H. Lund succeeded Apostle Brigham Young as president of the European mission. Elder Alfred Solomon had presided temporarily since Apostle Young's departure for America.

June 1, 1893 (Thursday)

Saltair, the new bathing resort, built in the Great Salt Lake, about eighteen miles west of Salt Lake City, was opened to the public.

June 3, 1893 (Saturday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, Henry Whetstone was sentenced by Judge Miner to ninety days' imprisonment in the Penitentiary, for inducing Geo. Craig (a witness in an adultery and u.c. case against John Hopkin) to leave Utah in February, 1893, and not appear before the grand jury.

June 3, 1893 (Saturday)

Hiram S. Wright was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 11, 1893 (Sunday)

Jesse W. Crosby, a Church veteran, died at Panguitch, Garfield Co.

June 11, 1893 (Sunday)

August Svendsen, of Spanish Fork, having been pardoned by Pres. Cleveland, was released from the Penitentiary.

June 15, 1893 (Thursday)

The Eureka branch, Juab Co. was organized as a Ward; Peter Loutensock, Bishop.

June 18, 1893 (Sunday)

Wm. H. Watson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

June 19, 1893 (Monday)

Patriarch Milo Andrus, a veteran in the Church, died at Oxford, Idaho.

June 25, 1893 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing at Salina, Redmond, Aurora and Vermillion were organized by Christian D. Fjeldsted, Brigham H. Roberts and J. Golden Kimball as the 107th quorum of Seventy; Thos. G. Humphries, Jans Jensen, Christian Meyer, Henry N. Hayes, Niels L. Christensen, Wm. E. Mason and Christian J. Mortensen, presidents.

June 29, 1893 (Thursday)

John Broom, owner of the Broom hotel, at Ogden, Weber Co., died.

July 4, 1893 (Tuesday)

Joseph Hancock, who had been a member of the Church since 1830, died at Payson, Utah Co.

July 8, 1893 (Saturday)

Geo. A. Mears, an old resident of Salt Lake City, and a friend of the "Mormons", suicided by shooting himself in the head.

July 9, 1893 (Sunday)

Eureka, Juab Co., was partly destroyed by fire.

July 15, 1893 (Saturday)

After two trials in the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, the case against Wm. Butler, for infractions of the Edmunds law, was dismissed.

July 17, 1893 (Monday)

The Utah Commission, in accordance with Pres. Harrison's amnesty proclamation, ruled that former polygamists, who, since Nov. 1, 1890, had not broken the Edmunds law, were entitled to vote at elections.

July 18, 1893 (Tuesday)

Susan E. Angell, widow of Truman O. Angell, died in Salt Lake City.

July 18, 1893 (Tuesday)

The Utah Commission adopted a resolution advising that amnestied polygamists be allowed to vote.

July 18, 1893 (Tuesday)

Charles Harris was discharged from the Penitentiary.

July 24, 1893 (Monday)

The Raymond Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized; David R. Sinclair, Bishop.

July 25, 1893 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Ruth Townsend was accidentally shot and killed, at Wasatch, Salt Lake Co.

July 28, 1893 (Friday)

Patriarch Lemuel Mallory died at Logan, Cache Co.

July 30, 1893 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at South Park, near Marysvale, Wyoming, by Apostle Brigham Young.

August 7, 1893 (Monday)

Wm. Bullam and Josiah Gough were discharged from the Penitentiary.

August 10, 1893 (Thursday)

Bolivar H. Roberts, Ex-Territorial treasurer, died in Salt Lake City.

August 28, 1893 (Monday)

Pehr A. Bjorklund, of Provo, Utah, died at Helsingborg, Sweden, where he labored as a missionary.

August 29, 1893 (Tuesday)

The Tabernacle choir and a number of friends (about four-hundred souls altogether) left Salt Lake City on a special train, for Chicago, Ill., whither the choir went to compete in a singing contest at the World's Fair. Presidents Wilford Woodruff, Geo. Q. Cannon and Joseph F. Smith were with the party.

August 30, 1893 (Wednesday)

Elder John M. Chidester, a member of Zion's Camp, died at Washington, Washington Co.

August 31, 1893 (Thursday)

The Supreme Court of Utah handed down a decision in the Church suits, to the effect that the government, under the escheat clause of the Edmunds-Tucker law of 1887, was entitled to confiscate the Gardo House, the coal lands and the Church farm; but that the Historians' office and the Tithing yard were excluded and legally the property of the Church. The case was appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court.

September 1, 1893 (Friday)

Elder Lyman O. Littlefield, a member of Zion's Camp, died at Smithfield, Cache Co.

September 1, 1893 (Friday)

The Tabernacle choir visited the Temple lot at Independence, Jackson Co., Mo; and in the evening gave a concert in the Auditorium, Kansas City.

September 2, 1893 (Saturday)

The Tabernacle choir gave a concert in the Music Hall of the Exposition Buildings, at St. Louis, Mo.

September 4, 1893 (Monday)

The Chamber of Commerce special train left Salt Lake City for the World's Fair.

September 5, 1893 (Tuesday)

Henry Stander was discharged from the Penitentiary.

September 5, 1893 (Tuesday)

Gov. Caleb W. West and company left Salt Lake City, for the World's Fair, to be present on Utah day, (Sept. 9th.)

September 5, 1893 (Tuesday)

The Sanpete Valley railway was completed to Ephraim, Sanpete Co.

September 7, 1893 (Thursday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, Wm. E. Jones, of Paragoonah, Iron Co., was sentenced to five months' imprisonment, for "adultery".

September 8, 1893 (Friday)

The Tabernacle choir won the second prize ($1000) at the singing contest at the World's Fair, Chicago, Ill.

September 8, 1893 (Friday)

This was Utah day at the World's Fair, Chicago. At Festival Hall, Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Geo. Q. Cannon and Governor Caleb W. West, of Utah, delivered short speeches.

September 9, 1893 (Saturday)

The Tabernacle choir gave a concert at the Music Hall in Chicago, and then started for home.

September 11, 1893 (Monday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, Joseph P. Barton and Stephen S. Barton, both of Paragoonah, Iron Co., were each sentenced to five months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery. They were incarcerated in the Penitentiary Sept. 19th.

September 11, 1893 (Monday)

The World's Parliament of Religions, at which the Latter-day Saints were denied representation, commenced at Chicago, Ill.

September 13, 1893 (Wednesday)

The Tabernacle choir returned to Salt Lake City from their visit to Chicago, Ill.

September 13, 1893 (Wednesday)

Elder Newman Bulkley, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Springville, Utah Co.

September 15, 1893 (Friday)

Sister Mercy R. Thompson, a veteran Church member, died in the Sixteenth Ward, Salt Lake City.

September 18, 1893 (Monday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, Fred W. Cook was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, for "fornication," and Robert B. Dalley to 18 months' for "adultery". Both were incarcerated in the Penitentiary the following day.

September 20, 1893 (Wednesday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, Bishop Benjamin M. Lewis, of Logan, was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, for "adultery".

September 22, 1893 (Friday)

Miss Augusta Anderson, a witness in the Oluf Hogan adultery case, was imprisoned in the Penitentiary.

September 25, 1893 (Monday)

On this and the following day, Elder Andrew Jenson, (who was on a special mission in Missouri and other States), copied the old Church record, written by the first Church Historian, John Whitmer. the old record, after passing through several hands, was now found in the possession of Geo. Schweich, at Richmond, Ray Co., Mo.

October 2, 1893 (Monday)

Augusta Anderson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

October 5, 1893 (Thursday)

A bill providing for the restoration of Church property to the rightful owner was passed in the U.S. House of Representatives. Delegate Joseph L. Rawlins championed the bill in an able manner.

October 6, 1893 (Friday)

The general semi-annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City, continuing three days.

October 7, 1893 (Saturday)

Elder John Rowley died at Colonia Pacheco, Chihuahua, Mexico.

October 14, 1893 (Saturday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Hans Christensen was sentenced to two months' imprisonment, for u.c.

October 16, 1893 (Monday)

The Twin Groves branch of the Church, Bannock Stake of Zion, Idaho, was organized; Wm. D. Williams presiding Elder.

October 21, 1893 (Saturday)

The U.S. Senate passed a bill, providing for the restoration of Church property, with certain amendments.

October 23, 1893 (Monday)

The U.S. House of Representatives concurred in the Senate amendments to the bill providing for the restoration of Church property.

November 1893

Discoveries of rich gold bearing ore in the Camp Floyd mining district attracted general attention in Utah.

November 7, 1893 (Tuesday)

The general election in Utah resulted in the election of 5 Democrats, 5 Republicans and 2 Liberals to the legislative Council, and 8 Democrats, 10 Republicans and 6 Liberals to the House. In Salt Lake City the Independent-Citizens candidates were successful for all the municipal offices except treasurer for which a Liberal was elected. The Independent-Citizens elected ten councilmen and the Liberals five. Robert N. Baskin, Independent Citizens' candidate for mayor, was elected.

November 9, 1893 (Thursday)

The Salt Lake Tribune advised the Liberals to disband and divide on national party lines; leading Liberals favored the proposition.

November 13, 1893 (Monday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, John Hopkin was sentenced by Judge Miner to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

November 13, 1893 (Monday)

Wm. Smith, the last surviving brother of the Prophet Joseph, and once a member of the Council of Twelve Apostles, died at Osterdock, Clayton Co., Iowa.

November 26, 1893 (Thursday)

Bishop Wm. L.N. Allen, of the 21st Ward, Salt Lake City, died.

November 22, 1893 (Wednesday)

Sister Elizabeth Richards died at Union, Salt Lake Co.

November 24, 1893 (Friday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, Peter Svendsen was sentenced by Judge Miner to three months' imprisonment in the Penitentiary, for u.c.

November 24, 1893 (Friday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Wm. A. Stewart was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

November 26, 1893 (Sunday)

Richard Slater, a Church veteran, died at Slaterville, Weber Co. The settlement of Slaterville was originally named in his honor.

November 27, 1893 (Monday)

The Pioneer Electric Power Company was organized, with Geo. Q. Cannon as president.

November 29, 1893 (Wednesday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, Wm. Tyril was sentenced by Judge Miner to three months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

November 30, 1893 (Thursday)

Bishop Levi W. Reed died at North Point, Salt Lake Co.

December 5, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Joseph F. Parker was sentenced to four months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 6, 1893 (Wednesday)

Charles A. Allen was arrested at Beaver, for u.c.

December 11, 1893 (Monday)

Martha Seed Thornley, one of the first converts to "Mormonism" in England, died at Layton, Davis Co.

December 11, 1893 (Monday)

In the Fourth District Court, Ogden, Wm. J. Orchard was sentenced to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 12, 1893 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Lucy Pearson, a native of Sweden, died at Richfield, Sevier Co., 99 years old.

December 14, 1893 (Thursday)

In the Second District Court, at Beaver, Charles A. Allen, of Beaver, was sentenced to four months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 14, 1893 (Thursday)

Hans Christensen was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 17, 1893 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the St. Mary's river and tributaries, southeast of Cardston, Alberta, Canada, were organized as the AEtna Ward; Richard Pilling, Bishop.

December 18, 1893 (Monday)

At a convention of the Liberal party, held in the Theatre, Salt Lake City, it was resolved that the party disband.

December 19, 1893 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Jens L. Bruun, of Richfield, was sentenced by Judge Blackburn to 30 days' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 20, 1893 (Wednesday)

M. McMillian, of St. Louis, Mo., and E.B. Bronson, of El Paso, Texas, discovered the remains of five prehistoric towns below Eddy, New Mexico.

December 20, 1893 (Wednesday)

In the Fourth District Court, Ogden, John Lutz was sentenced by Judge Miner to six months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

December 24, 1893 (Sunday)

The Saints, who had settled on Fish Creek, Alberta, Canada, were organized as Mountain View Ward; Vincent I. Stewart, Bishop.

December 25, 1893 (Monday)

Joseph P. Barton, Stephen S. Barton and Wm. E. Jones were discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 26, 1893 (Tuesday)

Co. Jesse Carter Little, a Pioneer of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

December 27, 1893 (Wednesday)

Martin Allred, of Fairview, Sanpete Co., was arrested on the charge of adultery.

December 29, 1893 (Friday)

Edmund Ellsworth, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Showlow, Apache Co., Ariz. He led the first handcart company into Great Salt Lake Valley, in 1856.

December 31, 1893 (Sunday)

The new Assembly Hall of the 22nd Ward, Salt Lake City, was dedicated.
