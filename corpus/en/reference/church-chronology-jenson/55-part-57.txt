Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

The Federal judges in Utah exercised undue authority and caused considerable difficulty by instituting court proceedings against the leaders of the Church and others. A number of settlements were founded in Cache Valley, where a Stake of Zion was organized. Provo Valley, Utah, was settled at Heber, Midway and Charleston.

January 1, 1859 (Saturday)

The Millennial Star announced to the Saints in Europe that emigration to Utah was again open for those who had means to take them through.

January 11, 1859 (Tuesday)

A legislative act, changing the county seat of Washington County from Harmony to the town of Washington, was approved.

January 19, 1859 (Wednesday)

An act passed by the Utah legislature reorganizing Carson and Green River Counties and attaching St. Mary's and Humboldt Counties to Carson County, was approved. Genoa was made the county seat of Carson and Ft. Bridger of Green River County.

February 1859

The Deseret Alphabet was first introduced in Utah.

February 1859

The 58th quorum of Seventy was organized at Brigham City, Box Elder Co., Utah. Some time previously the 56th and 57th quorums had been organized.

February 3, 1859 (Thursday)

The 59th quorum of Seventy was organized by Joseph Young at North Willow Creek (Willard), Box Elder Co., Utah, with George J. Marsh, Thomas W. Brewerton, John M. McCrary, Richard J. Davis, Elisha Mallory, Mathew W. Dalton and Peter Greenhalgh as presidents.

February 11, 1859 (Friday)

The 60th quorum of Seventy was organized at Ogden, Weber Co., Utah, with Luman A. Shurtliff as senior president.

February 25, 1859 (Friday)

The 61st quorum of Seventy was organized at Mill Creek, G.S.L. Co., with John Scott, James Craigan, Wm. Casto, James P. Park, Andrew J. Rynearson, Dudley J. Merrill and Thurston Larson as presidents.

March 1859

Plain City, Weber Co., Utah, was settled by Jeppe G. Folkman, Christopher O. Folkman, Jens Peter Folkman, Joseph Skeen, Daniel Collett, John Spiers, John Carver, Wm. Geddes and others.

March 8, 1859 (Tuesday)

Associate Justice John Cradlebaugh, in his charge to the grand jury, composed of "Mormons," at Provo, called them "fools", "dupes", "instruments of a tyrannical church despotism", etc. Provo was occupied by a detachment of U.S. troops.

March 9, 1859 (Wednesday)

A small company of Saints, under the leadership of Joseph Humphreys, sailed from Port Elizabeth, South Africa, bound for America. They arrived at Boston early in May, 1859.

March 21, 1859 (Monday)

A small company of Saints from Australia arrived at San Francisco, Ca., en route for G.S.L. City.

March 22, 1859 (Tuesday)

Howard O. Spencer, a Mormon youth, was assaulted and brutally beaten on the head by Sergeant Ralph Pike, of the U.S. army, in Rush Valley, Utah.

March 27, 1859 (Sunday)

Gov. Cumming issued a proclamation against the presence of troops in Provo. About this time it was reported that certain U.S. officials had entered into a conspiracy to secure the arrest of Pres. Brigham Young, and that Col. Johnston had promised the assistance of U.S. troops under his command to effect the arrest. As a consequence Gov. Cumming notified General Daniel H. Wells to hold the militia in readiness to prevent the outrage, should it be attempted; 5,000 troops (militia) were placed under arms.

April 4, 1859 (Monday)

The U.S. troops evacuated Provo.

April 6, 1859 (Wednesday)

The 29th annual conference of the Church was commenced at G.S.L. City. Benjamin L. Clapp, one of the presidents of the Seventies, was excommunicated from the Church on the 7th, for apostasy.

April 11, 1859 (Monday)

The ship William Tapscott sailed from Liverpool, England, with 725 Saints, under the direction of Robert F. Neslen. The company arrived at New York May 14th, and at Florence, Neb., May 25th.

May 10, 1859 (Tuesday)

Gen. Albert Sidney Johnston promised protection to all persons who wished to leave the Territory of Utah.

May 11, 1859 (Wednesday)

Isaac Allred was assaulted and killed by Thomas Ivie, at Mount Pleasant, Sanpete Co., Utah.

May 18, 1859 (Wednesday)

Joseph Abbott was killed by lightning, while engaged in planting corn on the "Old Fort Square," G.S.L. City.

May 26, 1859 (Thursday)

James Johnson, a son of Luke S. Johnson, of Shambip County, was shot and mortally wounded by Delos Gibson in G.S.L. City. Death ensued the following day. A number of other murders, principally among bad characters who infested the Territory, took place about the same time.

May 29, 1859 (Sunday)

Leo Hawkins, clerk at the Historian's office, died in G.S.L. City.

June 1859

Logan, Cache Co., was first settled.

July 10, 1859 (Sunday)

Hon. Horace Greeley, editor of the New York Tribune, arrived at G.S.L. City en route for California.

July 10, 1859 (Sunday)

The ship Antarctic sailed from Liverpool, England, with 30 Saints, under the direction of James Chaplow. It arrived at New York Aug. 21st.

July 14, 1859 (Thursday)

Geo. W. Bradley was ordained Bishop of Moroni, Sanpete Co., which place had recently been settled.

August 1, 1859 (Monday)

Wm. H. Hooper was elected Utah's second delegate to Congress, Hon. John M. Bernhisel having served in that capacity since the organization of the Territory.

August 11, 1859 (Thursday)

Sergeant Ralph Pike, a U.S. soldier, was shot in G.S.L. City, in supposed retaliation for having cracked the scull of Howard O. Spencer with a musket, five months previously.

August 15, 1859 (Monday)

U.S. soldiers set fire to a hay stack at Cedar Fort, Cedar Valley, Utah, and fired upon the citizens in the night. The soldiers were excited over the killing of Sergeant Pike.

August 20, 1859 (Saturday)

The ship Emerald Isle sailed from Liverpool, England, with 54 Saints, mostly Swiss, under the direction of Henry Hug.

August 27, 1859 (Saturday)

The first number of the Mountaineer, a weekly newspaper, was published in G.S.L. City; Messrs. Blair, Ferguson & Stout editors and proprietors.

August 29, 1859 (Monday)

Captain James Brown's company of immigrants, which had left Florence June 13th, and consisted of 353 souls with 59 wagons, arrived at G.S.L. City.

September 1, 1859 (Thursday)

Capt. Horton D. Haight's wagon company (called the Church train), bringing merchandise and 134 immigrants, arrived at G.S.L. City.

September 4, 1859 (Sunday)

Capt. George Rowley's handcart company, which had left Florence, June 9th, with 235 souls, 60 handcarts, and 6 wagons, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 15, 1859 (Thursday)

Capt. Robert F. Neslen's company of immigrants, consisting of 372 souls, with 58 wagons, which had left Florence June 26th, arrived in G.S.L. City.

September 16, 1859 (Friday)

Capt. Edward Stevenson's immigrating company, consisting of about three hundred and fifty souls, with 54 wagons, arrived at G.S.L. City. It had started from Florence June 26th.

September 17, 1859 (Saturday)

Alexander Carpenter was shot and mortally wounded by Thomas H. Ferguson in G.S.L. City. Both were non Mormons.

October 10, 1859 (Monday)

Smithfield, Cache Co., was settled by Seth Langton and Robert and John Thornley.

October 28, 1859 (Friday)

Thos. H. Ferguson, the murderer, was executed in G.S.L. City. This was the first execution of a criminal in Utah.

November 14, 1859 (Monday)

A Stake of Zion was partly organized in Cache Valley, Utah. Peter Maughan was appointed presiding Bishop in Cache Valley. Logan Ward was organized, with Wm. B. Preston as Bishop.

December 12, 1859 (Monday)

The ninth annual session of the Utah legislature convened in G.S.L. City and organized by electing Daniel H. Wells president of the Council and John Taylor speaker of the House.

December 12, 1859 (Monday)

This year Spring City, Sanpete Co., Utah, was resettled under the name of Little Denmark.
