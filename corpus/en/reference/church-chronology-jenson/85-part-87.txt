Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Salt Lake City passed from the hands of the People's Party to those of the Liberals, or anti-Mormon element. Nearly all the civil rights left to the Saints were threatened by proposed anti-Mormon legislation. President Woodruff issued his manifesto, suspending plural marriage.

January 1, 1890 (Wednesday)

The Liberals of Salt Lake City gave a grand daylight parade.

January 2, 1890 (Thursday)

David James was arrested in Salt Lake City for u.c.

January 2, 1890 (Thursday)

John Powell, of Fillmore, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

Elder Andrew K. Andersen, of Ephraim, Utah, died in Aalborg, Denmark, where he labored as a missionary.

Joseph Derbidge, of the 19th Ward, Salt Lake City, was arrested for u.c.

January 6, 1890 (Monday)

The U.S. Supreme Court decided that the offices of Territorial Treasurer and Auditor of Public Accounts, held respectively by James Jack and Nephi W. Clayton, should be turned over to Bolivar Roberts and Arthur Pratt, the men appointed by the governor of Utah.

James Bywater, of Brigham City, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

George L. Woods, ex-Governor of Utah, died at Portland, Oregon.

January 9, 1890 (Thursday)

Andrew J. Kershaw, of Ogden, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 9, 1890 (Thursday)

Charles S. Zane's re-appointment as chief justice of Utah was confirmed by the U.S. Senate.

January 10, 1890 (Friday)

The People's Party held their first grand parade in Salt Lake City, notwithstanding the heavy snowstorm. The procession numbered several thousands.

January 10, 1890 (Friday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Albert M. Baker was sentenced by Judge Henderson to one month's imprisonment, for u.c.

January 12, 1890 (Sunday)

Isabella Hay Hunter, one of the first members of the Church in Scotland, died in the Twenty-first Ward, Salt Lake City.

January 13, 1890 (Monday)

The Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City, and organized by electing Franklin S. Richards president of the Council, and James Sharp speaker of the House.

January 13, 1890 (Monday)

Hans Thurgesen, of Koosharem, Piute Co., emerged from the Penitentiary.

January 13, 1890 (Monday)

John W. Stewart, of Vermont, introduced a bill in the House of Representatives to disfranchise all "Mormons."

January 13, 1890 (Monday)

Idaho's admission as a State and the test oath were argued in the U.S. Senate Committee on Territories. The argument was continued the following day, when "Mormons" were heard.

January 14, 1890 (Tuesday)

The Liberals of Salt Lake City held their municipal convention in the Opera House and nominated George M. Scott for mayor. The other men on the ticket were mostly questionable characters and were nominated as a reward for their peculiar work against the "Mormons" during the campaign.

January 16, 1890 (Thursday)

A great Liberal ratification meeting was held at the Walker Opera House, Salt Lake City.

January 16, 1890 (Thursday)

Elias A. Beckstrand, of Meadow Creek,Millard Co., was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 17, 1890 (Friday)

Fredonia, a new settlement, in Arizona, near Kanab, Kane Co., Utah, was organized as the Fredonia branch; Thos. P. Jensen, presiding Elder.

January 18, 1890 (Saturday)

Robert H. Ford fell from a window on the Temple, Salt Lake City, thirty feet to the ground, receiving fatal injuries, from the effects of which he died on the 23rd.

January 20, 1890 (Monday)

Judge Charles S. Zane took the oath of office as chief justice of the Supreme Court of Utah.

January 21, 1890 (Tuesday)

The Supreme Court of Utah decided against the election of aldermen for Salt Lake City.

James Anderson was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 23, 1890 (Thursday)

Mons Anderson, of Lehi, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

January 25, 1890 (Saturday)

The Saints who were employed in the mines at Mammoth, Tintic, Juab Co., were organized as a branch of the Church, with Lewis W. Stout as president.

Orson J. Spencer and George A. Peart were arrested at Randolph, Rich Co., for u.c.

January 27, 1890 (Monday)

The People's Party municipal convention convened in the Theatre, Salt Lake City, and the following day agreed upon a ticket, headed by Spencer Clawson as candidate for mayor.

William McFarland, sen., a Church veteran, 95 years old, died at West Weber, Weber Co.

The Old Constitution Building, Salt Lake City, was torn down to make room for a new five story brick building, erected soon afterwards.

The corner stone of the Sugar Factory at Lehi, Utah Co., was laid.

December 29, 1890 (Monday)

Elder Alfred H. Caine died in Salt Lake City.

December 29, 1890 (Monday)

An indignation meeting was held in the Methodist Church, Salt Lake City, to protest against the vice and wickedness existing in the city.

December 29, 1890 (Monday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, John Archibald, of Clarkston, was sentenced by Judge A. Miner to three months' imprisonment, for u.c.

December 29, 1890 (Monday)

The Sevier branch of the Rio Grande Western Ry. was opened to Manti.

December 29, 1890 (Monday)

The famous Castle Gardens, known to so many immigrating Saints from Europe, as their landing place, was turned over to the New York City authorities.
