Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

A Latter-day Saint hospital was dedicated in Salt Lake City; the railway connecting Salt Lake City with southern California was completed; the Church purchased the old Mack farm in Vermont (where the Prophet Joseph Smith was born) and erected a monument thereon; the first Latter-day Saint missionaries preached in Greece, and the affairs of the Church, at the close of the year, were prosperous.

January 1905

Early in the month, the fourth volume of the History of Utah, by Orson F. Whitney, was placed on the market.

January 2, 1905 (Monday)

The new State officers were inaugurated in Salt Lake City; John C. Cutler, the new governor of Utah, delivered an appropriate inaugural address.

January 3, 1905 (Tuesday)

Patriarch John W. Clark, a pioneer of Tooele County, and a Church veteran, died in Grantsville, Tooele Co.

January 4, 1905 (Wednesday)

The Dr. Groves Latter-day Saints hospital in Salt Lake City was dedicated by Pres. Joseph F. Smith; the hospital was opened for the reception of patients Monday Jan. 9, 1905.

January 9, 1905 (Monday)

The sixth session of the Utah State legislature convened in Salt Lake City. Stephen H. Love was chosen president of the Senate and Thomas Hull speaker of the House.

January 9, 1905 (Monday)

Patriarch Henry Clark Jackson died in Paradise, Cache Co.

January 11, 1905 (Wednesday)

In the examination of the Smoot case before the Senate Committee on Privileges and Elections, the defense began the examination of witnesses. A large number of conservative and influential citizens, mostly non-Mormons of Utah and Idaho, were called to testify. The examination of these witnesses was continued until the 25th, when the case closed and the attorneys began their arguments, each side having five hours to their disposal. Senator Smoot took the stand in his own behalf on the 20th and his testimony made a favorable impression.

January 12, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder John Frantzen died at Spring City, Sanpete Co.

January 13, 1905 (Friday)

Elder Jens Jorgensen, one of the first local missionaries in Scandinavia, died in Mt. Pleasant, Sanpete Co.

January 17, 1905 (Tuesday)

Elder William Edgar Hokanson, of Freedom, Wyo., died in Foxport, Fleming Co., Kentucky, where he labored as a missionary.

January 17, 1905 (Tuesday)

George Sutherland was formally elected as U.S. Senator by both branches of the Utah legislature to succeed Senator Thomas Kearns.

January 22, 1905 (Sunday)

Beaver Ward (Beaver Co.) was divided into two Wards, with Louis W. Harris as Bishop of the East Ward and Samuel O. White, jun., as Bishop of the West Ward. The Frisco branch was also organized as a Ward, with Wm. B. Ashworth as Bishop.

January 22, 1905 (Sunday)

The Sugar House Ward (Salt Lake Co.) was divided and a part thereof organized as the Emerson Ward, with Millen M. Atwood as Bishop. John M. Whitaker was chosen Bishop of Sugar House Ward, succeeding Bishop Atwood.

January 25, 1905 (Wednesday)

Ralph Ramsey, who carved the original eagle that adorned the Eagle Gate, Salt Lake City, died in Snowflake, Ariz. He was born Jan. 22, 1824, in England.

January 27, 1905 (Friday)

Harmon S. Cutler resigned his position as Bishop of Kanab Ward, Kane Co. Heber E. Riggs was appointed presiding Elder at Kanab Feb. 5, 1905, and he was succeeded in that capacity April 30, 1905, by Zadok R. Judd, jun.

January 29, 1905 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at Waterloo, Salt Lake Co., were separated from Farmers Ward and organized as the Waterloo Ward, with Asahel H. Woodruff as Bishop.

January 29, 1905 (Sunday)

Tooele Ward, Tooele Co., was reorganized with Silas C. Orme as Bishop.

January 29, 1905 (Sunday)

Halsey Dean Fullmer succeeded Geo. W. Hendricks as Bishop of Leigh Ward, Teton Stake, Idaho.

January 29, 1905 (Sunday)

Jesse S. Brown, of the Mormon Battalion and a pioneer of Utah, died at Ogden. He was born March 26, 1829, in North Carolina.

January 30, 1905 (Monday)

The last spike on the San Pedro, Los Angeles & Salt Lake Railroad was driven with imposing ceremonies at a point twenty miles north of the California State line, in Nevada. This long wished for road shortens the travel between Utah and southern California points several hundred miles.

February 2, 1905 (Thursday)

Edson Barney, a member of Zion's Camp and a pioneer of southern Utah, died at Provo, Utah. At the time of his death he was the oldest member in the Church. He was born June 30, 1806.

February 3, 1905 (Friday)

Lucy Biglow Young, widow of the late Pres. Brigham Young, died in Salt Lake City.

February 5, 1905 (Sunday)

Ishmael Phillipps, formerly Bishop of Union, Salt Lake Co., died at his home at that place.

February 13, 1905 (Monday)

John S. Smith, a veteran of the Nauvoo Legion, died in Kaysville, Davis Co. He was born March 10, 1809, in Worcestershire, England.

February 23, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder Leonard John Nuttall, a member of the General Sunday School Board, died in Salt Lake City.

February 26, 1905 (Sunday)

Alfred B. Lambson, a Utah pioneer, died in Salt Lake City.

February 28, 1905 (Tuesday)

Senator Thomas Kearns, of Utah, delivered a bitter anti-Mormon speech before the U.S. Senate, at Washington, D.C.

March 6, 1905 (Monday)

Mrs. Ann Bolton Smith, a pioneer of 1847, died at Provo, Utah Co.

March 7, 1905 (Tuesday)

The explosion of an acetyline gas tank in the Granger Ward meeting house, Salt Lake Co., killed Miss Nellie Mackey and injured a number of others, besides wrecking the building.

March 9, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder Serge L. Ballif, jun., succeeded Hugh J. Cannon as President of the Swiss and German mission.

March 12, 1905 (Sunday)

Bishop James Nelson, sen., of Perry Ward, Box Elder Co, was honorably released, and Morris Peters appointed Bishop in his stead.

March 25, 1905 (Saturday)

Henry J. Platt, a pioneer of 1847, died at Thatcher, Graham Co., Ariz.

March 29, 1905 (Wednesday)

Robert H. Bradford succeeded Albert W. Davis as Bishop of Center Ward Salt Lake Co.

March 29, 1905 (Wednesday)

James F.S. Allred, a veteran of the Mormon Battalion, died in Spring City, Sanpete Co.

April 1, 1905 (Saturday)

The Saints' meeting house at Gavin, Miss., was burned to the ground by their enemies. The building had recently been repaired and fitted up with seats.

April 4, 1905 (Tuesday)

The general presidency of the Y.L.M.I.A. was reorganized, Martha H. Tingey being chosen president instead of the late Elmina S. Taylor.

April 6, 1905 (Thursday)

The 75th general conference of the Church commenced in Salt Lake City. General sessions were held on that day and on the 7th and the 10th.

April 6, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder Andrew Jenson returned to Salt Lake City from his seventh mission (five to foreign countries) for the Church.

April 7, 1905 (Friday)

Don Carlos W. Musser and Charles A. Smurthwaite entered suit in the Third District Court against the Trustee-in-Trust to enjoin him from handling the funds of the Church. Their attorneys were Charles S. Zane and Joseph W. Stringfellow.

April 9, 1905 (Sunday)

A branch of the Church was organized at Aberdeen, Scotland.

April 13, 1905 (Thursday)

Anti-polygamy resolutions were passed by the National Council of Women in Washington, D.C.

April 15, 1905 (Saturday)

Willard T. Cannon was succeeded in the presidency of the Netherlands mission by Jacob H. Trayner.

April 22, 1905 (Saturday)

Bishop Ira Hogan, of Thatcher Ward, Idaho, died at Soda Springs.

April 23, 1905 (Sunday)

Charles Kelly, President of the Box Elder Stake, died in Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

April 23, 1905 (Sunday)

David P. Cheney was installed as Bishop of Chapin Ward, Teton Stake, Idaho, succeeding Frank J. Stone.

April 23, 1905 (Sunday)

The general assembly of the Presbyterian church at Winona Lake, Ind., denounced the "Mormon" Church and resolved against the retention of Reed Smoot in the United States senate.

April 24, 1905 (Monday)

Ira Nebeker, Bishop of Laketown, Rich Co., and a pioneer of 1847, died in Los Angeles, Cal.

April 24, 1905 (Monday)

Elder Mads Jorgensen, an early missionary in Scandinavia, died at Lake View, Utah Co.

April 30, 1905 (Sunday)

George H. Robinson was chosen Bishop of Laketown, Rich Co., to succeed the late Bishop Ira Nebeker.

April 30, 1905 (Sunday)

The Iona Ward (Bingham Co., Idaho), was divided and the Lincoln Ward organized out of its western portion, with Heber Austin as Bishop. Most of the members constituting the Ward were connected with the sugar industry.

April 30, 1905 (Sunday)

The San Pedro branch, which formerly constituted a part of St. David Ward, Cochise Co., Ariz.), was organized as a Ward, with Jesse N. Curtis as Bishop.

May 1905

David Kinghorn succeeded Richard F. Jardine as Bishop of Lewisville Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho.

May 1905

Andrew Steedman succeeded Fred Lundberg as Bishop of Mammoth Ward, Juab Co.

May 1, 1905 (Monday)

Elders Frederick Jacob Sorensen and Oluf Jensen were brutally treated by a mob in Tennessee.

May 2, 1905 (Tuesday)

Mrs. Caroline Stoddard, a pioneer of 1847, died in Milford, Beaver Co.

May 17, 1905 (Wednesday)

Albert R. Smith, president of the San Luis Stake, died at his home in Manassa, Conejos Co., Colo. He was born April 1, 1862, in Paragoonah, Iron Co., Utah.

May 18, 1905 (Thursday)

Richard Brimley, formerly Bishop of the Fifth Ward, Salt Lake City, died there. He was born Aug. 26, 1822, in England.

May 21, 1905 (Sunday)

At a conference of the Nebo Stake, held at Payson, the Tintic district of the Juab Stake was annexed to the Nebo Stake, it being more convenient for the Tintic Saints to attend conference in the Nebo Stake. The district of country thus transferred included Eureka, Mammoth, Robinson, Knightsville and Silver City.

May 21, 1905 (Sunday)

John T. Russell succeeded John G. Ahlstrom as Bishop of St. John Ward, Tooele Co.

May 21, 1905 (Sunday)

James Ira May was ordained a Bishop and set apart to preside over Jackson Ward, Teton Stake, Wyo.

May 23, 1905 (Tuesday)

Elder Junius F. Wells in behalf of the Church purchased the Mack farm, in Sharon, Windsor Co., Vermont, where the Prophet Joseph Smith was born.

May 29, 1905 (Monday)

At the conference held in Brigham City, Box Elder Co., Oleen N. Stohl was chosen as president of the Box Elder Stake, (succeeding the late Pres. Charles Kelly), with Lucius A. Snow as first and Wm. Clements Horsley as second counselor.

May 29, 1905 (Monday)

Patriarch Oliver C. Hoskins, formerly president of the Malad Stake, died at West Portage, Oneida Co., Idaho. He was born May 17, 1827, in Illinois.

June 2, 1905 (Friday)

Elder Joseph S.B. Gill, of Weston, Idaho, who labored as a missionary in the Eastern States, died with typhoid fever, in a hospital at Pittsburg, Penn.

June 2, 1905 (Friday)

The first regular passenger train over the San Pedro, Los Angeles & Salt Lake Railroad left Salt Lake City and Los Angeles at the same hour.

June 8, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder William C. Dunbar, a Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

June 22, 1905 (Thursday)

The annual excursion of the old folks of Salt Lake City took place, Brigham City being the destination. The special train that carried the party from Salt Lake City was the largest train in one section that ever pulled out of the Oregon Short Line depot.

July 1905

Elder Jens M. Christensen succeeded Christian D. Fjeldsted in the presidency of the Scandinavian mission.

July 3, 1905 (Monday)

Elder William J. Jolley, a Church veteran, died at Mt. Carmel, Kane Co.

July 9, 1905 (Sunday)

Judge Thomas D. Dee, a prominent Church worker, died suddenly at his home in Ogden, Weber Co.

July 15, 1905 (Saturday)

Elder Joseph H. Ward, editor of the German paper the Salt Lake City Beobachter, died in Salt Lake City.

July 16, 1905 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held at Manassa, Conejos Co., Colo., Levi P. Helm was chosen as president of the San Luis Stake (to fill the vacancy caused by the death of Pres. Albert R. Smith), with Thomas A. Crowther as first and Erastus S. Christensen as second counselor.

July 16, 1905 (Sunday)

Ephraim Coombs was succeeded by John W. Shawcroft as Bishop of Richfield Ward, Conejos Co., Colo.

July 20, 1905 (Thursday)

John Tuttle, a pioneer, died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

July 24, 1905 (Monday)

Elder William H. Hopkins and Chester Liljenquist, who had been arrested the day before on a "trumped-up charge", were imprisoned in Charleston, Tallahatchie, Miss. After a "mock trial" they were liberated on the 26th.

August 1, 1905 (Tuesday)

Dr. Ezra G. Williams, a pioneer and Church veteran and a son of Fred. G. Williams of early Church fame, died in Ogden, Weber Co.

August 1, 1905 (Tuesday)

The work of registering for the drawing for homesteads on the Uintah reservation commenced; it was continued until the 12th. Registration was conducted at Provo, Price and Vernal, Utah, and Grand Junction, Colo.

August 8, 1905 (Tuesday)

Elders Charles C. Pulley and Wm. R. McNeil, traveling in the Mississippi conference, Southern States mission, were subjected to brutal treatment at Shugualak, Miss., by the constable Tom McClure and others, who resorted to violence in their efforts to force the Elders to leave the town.

August 10, 1905 (Thursday)

Elders Heber C. Miller and Joseph H. Walton, jun., who were laboring as missionaries in Tennessee, were brutally assaulted while engaged in tracting and seeking entertainment.

August 13, 1905 (Sunday)

Bishop Arnold Goodliffe, of Snowville, Box Elder Co, was released and the Ward reorganized by Apostle Rudger Clawson, with Jonathan C. Cutler, jun., as Bishop.

August 18, 1905 (Friday)

The Ogden Tabernacle choir, abut two hundred voices, under the direction of Professor Joseph Ballantyne, sang at the opening meeting of the Irrigation Congress at Portland, Ore.

August 19, 1905 (Saturday)

Patriarch Prime Thornton Coleman, a Church veteran, and a pioneer of Utah, died at Thatcher, Graham Co, Ariz.

August 23, 1905 (Wednesday)

Dr. Milton H. Hardy, a leading citizen of Utah and an active Church worker, died in Provo, Utah. He was born Sept. 26, 1844, in Massachusetts.

August 25, 1905 (Friday)

Mary Isabella Horne, a prominent and influential Church worker, died in Salt Lake City.

August 29, 1905 (Tuesday)

Bishop William H. Maughan died at Wellsville, Cache Co.

September 3, 1905 (Sunday)

Wm. W. Seegmiller was installed as Bishop of Kanab Ward, Kane Co., succeeding presiding Elder Zadak K. Judd, jun.

September 4, 1905 (Monday)

While about twelve hundred people were gathered at the race track near Richfield, Sevier Co., a bolt of lightning killed young Atlas Bean, and injured a number of other people.

September 5, 1905 (Tuesday)

Wm. H. Lewis, president of the Benson Stake, died at Lewiston, Cache Co. He was born Oct. 14, 1837, in Illinois.

September 6, 1905 (Wednesday)

Wilford Hudson, a veteran of the Mormon Battalion, died at Grantsville Tooele Co.

September 6, 1905 (Wednesday)

On the generous invitation of Senator Wm. A. Clark, president of the San Pedro, Los Angeles & Salt Lake Railroad a party of Salt Lake City business men left that city on a special train for Los Angeles, Cal.

September 10, 1905 (Sunday)

Huntsville, Weber Co., was divided into two Wards, the east end of the Ward (with 29 families) being organized as a new Ward called Middleton, with Albern Allen Bingham as Bishop. Andrew P. Rehnstrom was chosen as Bishop of Huntsville to succeed Bishop David McKay who was ordained a Patriarch.

September 26, 1905 (Tuesday)

Patriarch Joseph Argyle died at Bountiful, Davis Co. He was born Sept. 18, 1818, in England.

September 26, 1905 (Tuesday)

Catherine Phillips Smith, widow and plural wife of Patriarch Hyrum Smith, died in West Jordan, Salt Lake Co. She was born Aug. 1, 1819.

September 30, 1905 (Saturday)

An interesting conference was held at Nauvoo, Ill., under the presidency of Elder German E. Ellsworth, president of the Northern States mission. About sixty Elders and several Saints from other places were present at the conference. A fast meeting was also held in the Riverside mansion.

October 1905

The Circuit Court grand jury at Greenville, Tennessee, refused to indict the leaders of the mob who brutally assaulted Elders Frederick J. Sorensen and Oluf Jensen in May, 1905, stating, that they recognized no law for "Mormons". The evidence presented to the grand jury was strong and conclusive.

October 1, 1905 (Sunday)

The general conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City with a very large attendance. It was continued daily until the 8th.

October 1, 1905 (Sunday)

Elder J. Wilford Booth, president of the Turkish mission, and his wife, prayed on the historic summit of Mars Hill, at Athens, Greece, and offered a dedicatory prayer to the Lord in behalf of Greece.

October 8, 1905 (Sunday)

A Latter-day Saint Sunday school was organized in Nauvoo, Ill., in the old Nauvoo Mansion.

October 8, 1905 (Sunday)

Pres. J. Wilford booth preached a gospel sermon in a Young Men's Christian society meeting at Athens, Greece, which was the first sermon preached by a Latter-day Saint Elder in that country.

October 10, 1905 (Tuesday)

Reddick N. Allred, a member of the Mormon Battalion and pioneer of 1847, died in Chester, Sanpete Co. He was born Feb. 21, 1822, in Tennessee.

October 17, 1905 (Tuesday)

Elder Hirini Whaanga, a Maori chief, and Elder in the Church, died at the Latter-day Saints hospital in Salt Lake City. He was baptized in New Zealand in 1882 and came to Utah in 1894. In 1898-99 he filled a mission to his native land, he being the first Maori Elder who was sent on a mission from the headquarters of the Church.

October 18, 1905 (Wednesday)

Elder Thomas Jenkins, formerly Bishop of the Fourth Ward, Salt Lake City, died in that city.

October 19, 1905 (Thursday)

The name of Hebron Ward, Washington Co., Utah, was changed to Enterprise; George A. Holt was continued as Bishop.

October 22, 1905 (Sunday)

Pres. J. Wilford Booth baptized five persons (Rigas Pofantis, Andromacha H. Malaveta, Constantine Theodoseon, George Zdralis and John Lazas) in the Saronic Gulf at a point about two miles beyond Old Phaleron, near Athens, Greece. These were the first baptisms administered by Divine authority in Greece in this dispensation.

October 24, 1905 (Tuesday)

A new conference, called Norrkoping, consisting of the branches of Norrkoping, Vingaker, Vestervik and Kalmar was organized in Sweden, with Carl P. Anderson as president.

October 27, 1905 (Friday)

The new Salt Lake City public library, the gift of John Q. Packard, was formally accepted by the city officials.

November 3, 1905 (Friday)

Elder Wilford J. Knudsen of Provo, Utah, who labored as a missionary in Scandinavia, died in St. Joseph's hospital Copenhagen, Denmark, from tuberculosis.

November 7, 1905 (Tuesday)

At the election in Salt Lake City the so-called "American" party elected most of their candidates, including Ezra Thompson for mayor.

November 9, 1905 (Thursday)

Elder Jesse B. Higgs, lately returned from a mission to Great Britain, died in Salt Lake City.

November 10, 1905 (Friday)

Elder Charles B. Robbins, one of the original settlers of Cache county, died in Logan, Utah.

November 11, 1905 (Saturday)

Mrs. Jane M. Carrington Young, a pioneer of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

November 13, 1905 (Monday)

Elder James Armitstead, a pioneer and Church veteran, died in Pleasant Grove, Utah Co.

November 15, 1905 (Wednesday)

Patriarch John L. Bench died in Manti, Sanpete Co., Utah.

November 18, 1905 (Saturday)

Patriarch Benjamin F. Johnson, one of the oldest members of the Church, died at Mesa, Ariz. He was born July 29, 1813, in New York state.

November 29, 1905 (Wednesday)

The Salt Lake City post office opened for business in the new Federal buildings, the office being moved the evening before.

December 1, 1905 (Friday)

A terrible explosion in a coal mine at Diamondville, Wyo., killed 21 men.

December 7, 1905 (Thursday)

The Seventies residing in Shelley, Basalt, Goshen, Taylor, Woodville and Kimball, (Bingham Co., Idaho) were organized by Rulon S. Wells as the 146th quorum of Seventy, with Herbert D. Brown as senior president.

December 12, 1905 (Tuesday)

Wm. D. Park, a pioneer of 1847, died at Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

December 16, 1905 (Saturday)

John Pincock, a Church veteran and an early pioneer of Utah, died at Ogden.

December 18, 1905 (Monday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith and about twenty-five others left Salt Lake City for Vermont; they arrived at South Royalton, Vt., Friday, Dec. 22nd.

December 23, 1905 (Saturday)

The Joseph Smith Memorial Monument was unveiled at Sharon, Windsor Co., Vermont, and dedicated by President Joseph F. Smith. Following are the names of the visitors from Utah who composed Pres. Smith's party and who were present at the dedication: Joseph F. Smith, Anthon H. Lund, Francis M. Lyman, John Henry Smith, Hyrum M. Smith, George A. Smith, Charles W. Penrose, Patriarch John Smith, Seymour B. Young, Rulon S. Wells, Lewis W. Shurtliff, Elias A. Smith, Jesse M. Smith, Frank Y. Taylor, George F. Richards, wife and baby, George Romney, Angus M. Cannon, John McDonald, Brigham F. Grant, Arthur Winter, Lorin Farr, Ashley Snow, Benjamin Goddard, Joseph F. Smith, jun., Edith A. Smith, Ida B. Smith, Julina C. Smith and Susa Y. Gates. Besides Pres. Smith's party the following Latter-day Saints were present at the dedication: John G. McQuarrie, (president of the Eastern States mission), his wife Maggie S. McQuarrie, Hyrum K. Porter, (secretary of the Eastern States mission), Murray K. Jacobs, Fred J. Pack, Sadie G. Pack, Gene G. Pack, Emma Lucy Gates, Cecil Gates, Robert C. Easton, Jennette Y. Easton, Henry Peterson, Frederick J. Jackson, Orestes U. Bean, Hyrum Homer, Joseph J. Larsen, M.S. Gudmansen, Fred J. Clark, Ellen Thomas and Eva Y. Davis.

December 23, 1905 (Saturday)

Christian D. Fjeldsted, one of the first presidents of Seventies, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Feb. 20, 1829, in Copenhagen, Denmark.

December 24, 1905 (Sunday)

Memorial services in honor of the Prophet Joseph Smith were held throughout the Church.

December 24, 1905 (Sunday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith and other members of the Smith family visited Boxford and Topsfield, Mass.

December 26, 1905 (Tuesday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith and party visited Palmyra, N.Y., the old Smith home in Manchester township, the grove where (according to tradition) Joseph Smith had his first vision, and the hill Cumorah.

December 27, 1905 (Wednesday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith and party arrived at Cleveland, Ohio, visited Kirtland and Willoughby, and spent part of the day in the Kirtland Temple.

December 29, 1905 (Friday)

Pres. Joseph F. Smith and party preached in the Latter-day Saints chapel in Chicago, Ill., in the evening, and about midnight started for the west. They returned to Salt Lake City, Jan 1, 1906.

December 31, 1905 (Sunday)

Berrill Covington, an old pioneer, died suddenly in Ogden, Weber Co.
