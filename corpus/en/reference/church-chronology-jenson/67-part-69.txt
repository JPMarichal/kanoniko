Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

This year a secret society, called the "Gentile League of Utah," was organized in Salt Lake City, its alleged object being to break up "Mormon Theocracy." Court proceedings against leading men in the Church were continued. The people of Utah again petitioned Congress for admission into the Union as a State.

January 1872

The Salt Lake City Street Railway Company was organized.

January 1872

Judge James B. McKean refused to have Charles W. Baker arrested for perjury, notwithstanding the proof of his guilt.

January 1, 1872 (Monday)

Zera Pulsipher, formerly one of the seven presidents of the Seventies, died at Hebron, Washington Co., Utah, over 82 years of age.

January 2, 1872 (Tuesday)

Pres. Brigham Young was in the Third District Court, but his case was continued until March. Judge McKean refused $500,000 bail for him, and the President was again guarded in his own house by U.S. Deputy marshals.

January 3, 1872 (Wednesday)

Charles W. Baker, the principal witness against Brigham Y. Hampton and others, declared under oath that his testimony in court against the accused was utterly false.

January 8, 1872 (Monday)

The twentieth session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City and organized by electing Lorenzo Snow president of the Council, and Orson Pratt speaker of the House.

January 15, 1872 (Monday)

Elder James McGaw died at Ogden.

January 20, 1872 (Saturday)

Alexander Burt was again arrested on the old charge of being connected with the Robinson murder case.

January 24, 1872 (Wednesday)

Charles W. Baker was arraigned before Justice Jeter Clinton, in Salt Lake City, for perjury. In default of $3,000 bail, he was sent to prison, awaiting the action of the grand jury.

January 31, 1872 (Wednesday)

James L. High, Deputy U.S. District Attorney, being directed by the U.S. Attorney General at Washington, D.C. and District Attorney Geo. C. Bates, to do so, requested the District Court to admit Brigham Young and other prisoners to bail. The court refused the application.

January 31, 1872 (Wednesday)

A concurrent resolution was passed by the Utah legislature for the election of delegates to a convention, to adopt a State constitution.

February 1872

A "deadlock" existed in the Utah Federal courts for want of funds to defray expenses.

February 1, 1872 (Thursday)

At the first masquerade ball held in Utah (in Faust's Hall, Salt Lake City), a fearful row occurred, in which Police Officer Andrew Smith was considerably hurt.

February 4, 1872 (Sunday)

The Japanese Embassy arrived in Salt Lake City. On the 6th a reception was given it in the City Hall.

February 5, 1872 (Monday)

Edward Samuels and Wm. Hampton were killed by a snowslide in Big Cottonwood Canyon.

February 14, 1872 (Wednesday)

Bishop Abraham Hoagland, of the 14th Ward, Salt Lake City, died.

February 16, 1872 (Friday)

The Utah legislature adjourned.

February 17, 1872 (Saturday)

James G. Blair, of Missouri, delivered a powerful speech in defense of the people of Utah, in the House of Representatives, at Washington D.C.

February 19, 1872 (Monday)

A constitutional convention, for the adoption of proper measures for the admission of Utah into the Union, met in the City Hall, Salt Lake City.

February 19, 1872 (Monday)

John Cradlebaugh, formerly associate justice of Utah, died in poverty, at Eureka, Lander Co., Nev.

February 22, 1872 (Thursday)

The Japanese Embassy left Salt Lake City for the East.

February 28, 1872 (Wednesday)

Patriarch William Cazier, one of the first settlers of Juab County, died at Nephi.

March 2, 1872 (Saturday)

The constitutional convention adopted a constitution and memorial to Congress, asking for the admission of Utah into the Union as a State, and then adjourned sine die.

March 6, 1872 (Wednesday)

Thos. Fitch, Geo. Q. Cannon and Frank Fuller left Salt Lake City for Washington, D.C., as delegates from the late convention, to present to Congress the claims of the proposed State of Deseret.

March 7, 1872 (Thursday)

Wm. W. Phelps died in Salt Lake City.

March 20, 1872 (Wednesday)

A deputation of friends, mostly ladies, paid a visit of condolence to Hosea Stout, Brigham Y. Hampton and fellow-prisoners, at the City Hall, Salt Lake City.

March 22, 1872 (Friday)

Through malice, the prisoners (Hosea Stout, Brigham Y. Hampton, Alexander Burt, Wm. H. Kimball and John L. Blythe) were removed from the City Hall, Salt Lake City, to Camp Douglas, by order of U.S. Marshal Patrick.

March 25, 1872 (Monday)

Tracklaying was commenced on the Utah Northern narrow gauge railway at Brigham City, Box Elder Co.

April 2, 1872 (Tuesday)

The new constitution of the State of Deseret was presented to both houses of Congress, and referred to a special committee, who subsequently reported adversely to Utah's admission as a State.

April 4, 1872 (Thursday)

The members elected to the legislature of the State of Deseret met in Salt Lake City and proceeded to organize. During the session Wm. H. Hooper and Thos. Fitch were elected senators to Congress.

April 6, 1872 (Saturday)

The 42nd annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City. It was continued daily until the 9th, when it wad adjourned to the 14th, then to the 21st and closed on the 28th.

April 15, 1872 (Monday)

A decision was rendered by the Supreme Court of the United States in the Engelbrecht case, overturning the judicial proceedings in Utah for the last eighteen months, and declaring null indictments against about one hundred and twenty persons, some of whom had been imprisoned for some time.

April 25, 1872 (Thursday)

Pres. Brigham Young was released from custody on a writ of habeas corpus from Elias Smith, probate judge of Salt Lake County.

April 30, 1872 (Tuesday)

Hosea Stout, Wm. H. Kimball, Brigham Y. Hampton, John L. Blythe, Alexander Burt and James Toms were released by the Third District Court, on the strength of the Supreme Court decision, at Washington, D.C. John Brazier had previously been released.

May 2, 1872 (Thursday)

Thomas Hawkins, of Lehi, was admitted to $5,000 bail, pending an appeal to the Supreme Court of the Territory, and liberated from prison.

May 8, 1872 (Wednesday)

Ira Reid was killed by lightning, at West Jordan, Salt Lake Co.

May 17, 1872 (Friday)

Columbus Delano, Secretary of the Interior arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

May 20, 1872 (Monday)

Ground was broken for the American Fork (narrow gauge) Railroad, to run up American Fork Canyon.

May 25, 1872 (Saturday)

The Salt Lake City Gas Works Company was organized.

June 1872

The first number of the Woman's Exponent was published in Salt Lake City, Miss Lulu L. Greene editor.

June 8, 1872 (Saturday)

The first passenger train was run on the Utah Northern Railway.

June 12, 1872 (Wednesday)

The First Presidency, in a general circular, called on the people for aid to gather the poor Saints from abroad. The sum of $14,000 was donated during the year.

June 12, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Manhattan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 221 Saints, in charge of David Brinton. The company arrived at New York June 26th, and at Salt Lake City July 4th.

June 16, 1872 (Sunday)

A company of about one hundred journalists from Iowa, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

June 16, 1872 (Sunday)

Niels Heiselt, jun., was killed on Twelve Mile creek, Sanpete Co., by Shiverute Indians, who also drove off considerable stock belonging to the settlers.

June 26, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Nevada sailed from Liverpool, England, with 426 Saints, namely, 396 from Scandinavia, 28 from the British Isles and two from Holland, in charge of Eric Peterson. The company arrived at New York July 8th, and at Salt Lake City July 17th.

July 4, 1872 (Thursday)

Shadrach Roundy, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

July 28, 1872 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on Twin Creek, Bear Lake Co., Idaho, were called to Georgetown branch, with Philemon C. Merrill as presiding Elder.

July 31, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Wisconsin sailed from Liverpool, England, with 179 Saints, in charge of Geo. P. Ward. The company arrived at New York Aug. 12th, and at Salt Lake City Aug. 20th.

August 3, 1872 (Saturday)

The "Gentile League of Utah", and others, armed to the teeth, held a political meeting in front of the Salt Lake Hotel, Salt Lake City.

August 5, 1872 (Monday)

Elder Geo. W. Grant died near Bountiful, Davis Co.

August 5, 1872 (Monday)

At a general election in Utah for delegate to Congress, Geo. Q. Cannon received 20,969 and Geo. R. Maxwell 1,942 votes.

August 8, 1872 (Thursday)

The Utah Southern Railway commenced to run trains to the Point of the Mountain, south of Draper.

August 12, 1872 (Monday)

Gen. James A. Garfield, after a short visit, left Salt Lake City for Montana.

August 17, 1872 (Saturday)

Gen. Henry A. Morrow, with a body of troops, left Camp Douglas for Sanpete Valley, where Indian difficulties of a serious nature existed.

August 22, 1872 (Thursday)

General Morrow made a treaty with Ute Indians, at Springville, Utah Co.

August 24, 1872 (Saturday)

Gen. Geo. B. McClellan and party arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

August 29, 1872 (Thursday)

An attempt was made to assassinate Officers Brigham Y. Hampton and Alexander Burt, in Salt Lake City.

August 29, 1872 (Thursday)

Two houses of ill fame, kept by Kate Flint and Cora Rubodo, were abated in Salt Lake City, under municipal authority, the furniture and other effects being demolished.

August 31, 1872 (Saturday)

Indians make a raid on Spanish Fork, Utah Co., stealing horses.

September 2, 1872 (Monday)

The Walker House, in Salt Lake City, was formally opened.

September 3, 1872 (Tuesday)

Ground was broken for the Salt Lake City water works, up City Creek.

September 4, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 602 Saints, in charge of Geo. W. Wilkins. The company landed in New York, Sept. 17th, and arrived at Salt Lake City, Sept. 26th.

September 7, 1872 (Saturday)

A treaty of peace was concluded by Gen. Morrow with several Indian chiefs, at Mount Pleasant, Sanpete Co.

September 10, 1872 (Tuesday)

The Bingham Canyon and Camp Floyd Railway Company was organized.

September 21, 1872 (Saturday)

Miss Phoebe W. Couzins, of St. Louis, and Miss Georgie Snow, daughter of Judge Zerubbabel Snow, of Salt Lake City, were admitted to the bar in the Third District Court—the first ladies thus admitted in Utah.

September 23, 1872 (Monday)

The Utah Southern Railway was completed to Lehi, Utah Co.

September 26, 1872 (Thursday)

Indians killed Daniel Miller, near Bernard Snow's mill, in Sanpete Valley, and wounded his little son.

October 4, 1872 (Friday)

Wool was carded at the Provo Woolen Factory for the first time.

October 14, 1872 (Monday)

The Wasatch and Jordan Valley Railway Company was organized. Ground was broken for the road, Nov. 4th.

October 15, 1872 (Tuesday)

Pres. George A. Smith left Salt Lake City on his trip to Palestine. He was accompanied by Feramorz Little and daughter, and Willis T. Fuller. Afterwards he was joined by others.

October 16, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Minnesota sailed from Liverpool, England, with 203 Saints, in charge of Thos. Dobson. The company arrived at New York, Oct. 29th, and at Salt Lake City, Nov. 7th.

October 17, 1872 (Thursday)

A delegation of Ute Indians (Wanderodes, Antero, Tabiona and Kanosh), accompanied by Dr. Dodge, Indian agent, and Geo. W. Bean, interpreter, left Salt Lake City for Washington, D.C. There they had an interview with President U.S. Grant.

October 20, 1872 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the bench northwest of Richmond, Cache Co., Utah, were organized into a branch of the Church (now Lewiston), with Wm. H. Lewis as president.

November 6, 1872 (Wednesday)

Twenty-six Saints sailed from Liverpool, England, on the steamship Nevada, which, after several days' rough sailing, was forced to return to Liverpool.

November 19, 1872 (Tuesday)

The Palestine party, consisting of Pres. Geo. A. Smith, Apostle Lorenzo Snow, Elders Feramorz Little, Paul A. Schettler and Geo. Dunford, Sisters Eliza R. Snow and Clara S. Little, arrived in Liverpool, England, from New York.

November 26, 1872 (Tuesday)

General Thos. L. Kane, of Pennsylvania, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

November 26, 1872 (Tuesday)

The Germania Smelting and Refining Works, the first of the kind in Utah, commenced operation on Little Cottonwood creek, below the State road.

November 26, 1872 (Tuesday)

The American Fork Railroad was completed to Deer creek, in American Fork Canyon.

December 3, 1872 (Tuesday)

Bengt Swenson, of Santaquin, died at Nephi, from the effects of bodily injuries, inflicted by M. Daley, of Payson, at the coal bed in Sanpete County, Nov. 30th.

December 4, 1872 (Wednesday)

The steamship Manhattan sailed from Liverpool, England, with 35 Saints, including those who had returned with the Nevada. The company, which was in charge of Daniel Kennedy, arrived at New york Dec. 21st, and at Salt Lake City a few days later.

December 4, 1872 (Wednesday)

Pres. Geo. A. Smith and party, having left London, Nov. 30th, arrived in Amsterdam, Holland, and Dec. 11th they arrived in Paris, France, after having visited Antwerp and Brussels, in Belgium.

December 8, 1872 (Sunday)

Major J.W. Powell, chief of the Colorado Exploring Expedition, arrived in Salt Lake City, and reported that the exploration of the Grand Canyon of the Colorado was completed.

December 17, 1872 (Tuesday)

Pres. Geo. A. Smith and party visited Versailles and were admitted to the hall of the Corps Legislatif. In the evening they had an interview with M. Thiers, President of the French Republic.

December 18, 1872 (Wednesday)

John R. Clawson, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Salt Lake City.

December 19, 1872 (Thursday)

The Utah Northern Railway was opened to Mendon, Cache Co.

December 23, 1872 (Monday)

Pres. Brigham Young and party, including Gen. Thos. L. Kane, wife and two sons, arrived at St. George, to spend the winter, having left Salt Lake City about December 12th.

December 26, 1872 (Thursday)

A snowslide at Alta, Little Cottonwood Canyon, resulted in the loss of several lives.

December 27, 1872 (Friday)

Susannah L. Richards, relict of the late Willard Richards, died near Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

December 28, 1872 (Saturday)

In the Probate Court, Salt Lake City, Charles W. Baker was sentenced to two years' imprisonment for perjury.
