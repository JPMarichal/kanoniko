Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

Reed Smoot was elected U.S. Senator from Utah, and because he was a prominent member of the Church, an anti-Mormon crusade was inaugurated by sectarian ministers, commencing in Salt Lake City. Brigham Young, president of the Twelve Apostles, died, and George A. Smith was chosen to fill the vacancy. The Book of Mormon was published in the Samoan language. New mission houses were built and dedicated in Denver, Colo., and Christiania, Norway; a new Stake was organized in Alberta, Canada.

January 12, 1903 (Monday)

The fifth State legislature of Utah convened in Salt Lake City, and organized by electing Edward M. Allison president of the Senate and Thomas Hull speaker of the House.

January 16, 1903 (Friday)

Elder Niels Mortensen (Petersen), a Church veteran and a pioneer of Richfield, Utah, died in Richfield. He was born Nov. 12, 1819, in Denmark.

January 18, 1903 (Sunday)

Mrs. Ella Hunt, who returned home from a mission to Germany three weeks before, died in Loa, Piute Co. Utah.

January 20, 1903 (Tuesday)

The Utah legislature elected Reed Smoot senator to the U.S. Senate from Utah.

January 26, 1903 (Monday)

"Nineteen Citizens" of Salt Lake City signed and forwarded to the Senate of the United States a protest asking for the expulsion of Senator Reed Smoot from the Senate. The signers were: W.M. Paden, Parley L. Williams, Edward B. Critchlow, E.W. Wilson, Charles C. Goodwin, L.W. Colbath, William A. Nelden, Clarence T. Brown, Ezra Thompson, John J. Corum, J.L. Leilich, W. Mont Ferry, Harry C. Hill, S.H. Lewis, George R. Hancock, George M. Scott, Clarence E. Allen, Abiel Leonard and H.G. McMillan.

January 26, 1903 (Monday)

Joshua K. Whitney, an early pioneer and missionary, died in Salt Lake City.

February 1903

Heber J. Wilson succeeded George T. Coleman as Bishop of Teasdale, Wayne County, Utah.

February 1, 1903 (Sunday)

The Groveland branch (organized April 27, 1902), Bingham Co., Idaho, was organized as a Ward, with Adam Yancey as Bishop.

February 2, 1903 (Monday)

Mrs. Elizabeth Young Ellsworth, a pioneer of 1847, died at Lewisville, Fremont Co., Idaho.

February 5, 1903 (Friday)

Elder James A. Forsyth, who labored as a missionary in the Eastern States mission, died at Allegheny, Pa., from typhoid fever.

February 8, 1903 (Sunday)

At a quarterly conference held in Heber City, Wasatch Co., Utah, Heber City was divided into three Wards with Robert Duke as Bishop of the First Ward, Joseph A. Rasband as Bishop of the Second Ward and Frederick Crook as Bishop of the Third Ward. Midway was divided into two Wards with Joseph W. Francom as Bishop of the First Ward and Joseph Probst as Bishop of the Second Ward.

February 8, 1903 (Sunday)

William J. Facer succeeded George Facer as Bishop of Willard Ward, Box Elder Co., Utah.

February 9, 1903 (Monday)

The protest signed by nineteen citizens of Salt Lake City against Senator Reed Smoot was received by Senator Julius C. Burrows, chairman of the Senate Committee on Privileges and Elections.

February 9, 1903 (Monday)

The Bishopric of Shelley Ward, Bingham, Co., Idaho, was reorganized with Frank Heese as Bishop.

February 13, 1903 (Friday)

Forty leading citizens of Utah, of whom thirty-eight were non-Mormons, signed a petition in favor of Senator Reed Smoot, intending to offset the petition of the "Ministerial Association."

February 15, 1903 (Sunday)

The meeting house in Pleasant Grove, Utah, was destroyed by fire; loss, $6,000.

February 18, 1903 (Wednesday)

Sister Elvira P. Cox, a pioneer of 1847, died at Fairview, Sanpete Co., Utah.

February 21, 1903 (Saturday)

A heavy fog settled down over Salt Lake City, and Walter C. Farrow, a street car motorman, was fatally crushed in a street car collision on East Temple St.

February 26, 1903 (Thursday)

Rev. J.L. Leilich of the Salt Lake Ministerial Association filed an individual protest against the seating of Senator Smoot, declaring upon oath that Mr. Smoot was a polygamist. The protest was dated Feb. 25, 1903.

March 1, 1903 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in Union Stake, Oregon, were organized by Joseph W. McMurrin as the 143rd quorum of Seventy, with Malvin O. Miner as senior president.

March 5, 1903 (Thursday)

Senator Reed Smoot, of Utah, was sworn in as U.S. Senator, without opposition, at a special session of the U.S. Senate at Washington, D.C.

March 10, 1903 (Tuesday)

Levi N. Kendall, one of the Utah pioneers of 1847, died in Springville, Utah.

March 15, 1903 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled between Marysvale and Twin Groves, Fremont Co., Idaho, were organized as a branch called Vernon with Hyrum Larsen as presiding Elder. The branch was organized as a Ward June 28, 1903, with Samuel P. Egbert as Bishop.

March 17, 1903 (Tuesday)

Bishop George H. Maycock died in Springville, Utah.

March 19, 1903 (Thursday)

George Hales was honorably released from his position as Bishop of Mammoth Ward (Juab Co., Utah), and Fred Lundberg installed as his successor.

March 21, 1903 (Saturday)

George W. Boud, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Oct. 30, 1826, in Pennsylvania.

March 21, 1903 (Saturday)

Elder Alexander F. McDonald, an active colonizer and Church veteran, died at his home in Colonia Juarez, Chihuahua, Mexico. He was born Sept. 11, 1825, in Scotland.

March 21, 1903 (Saturday)

The Methodist minsters of Utah asked for the removal of Rev. J.L. Leilich from the Utah mission, accusing him of having treated them harshly. Mr. Leilich was an unscrupulous enemy of the "Mormons."

March 22, 1903 (Sunday)

The Bishopric of Marsh Center Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, was reorganized with Nathan S. Coffin as Bishop; he succeeded Lehi Wright, who had moved to Oregon.

March 25, 1903 (Wednesday)

Thomas Doxey, a pioneer of Utah and a prominent citizen of Weber County, died in Ogden, Utah. He was born March 27, 1829, in England.

March 27, 1903 (Friday)

Mrs. Augusta Cleveland Smith, a pioneer of 1847, died at Hayden, Fremont Co., Idaho.

March 28, 1903 (Saturday)

Bishop Warren, of the Methodist Church, ordered Rev. J.L. Leilich removed from his position as superintendent of the Utah M.E. Church.

April 4, 1903 (Saturday)

The 73rd annual conference of the Church convened in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 6th.

April 7, 1903 (Tuesday)

Chauncey G. Webb, who made the wagon used by Pres. Brigham Young in crossing the plains in 1847, died in Salt Lake City. He was born Oct. 24, 1812, in New York.

April 11, 1903 (Saturday)

Brigham Young, jun., president of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles, died in Sugar House Ward, Salt Lake Co., Utah. He was born Dec. 18, 1836, in Kirtland, Ohio.

April 12, 1903 (Sunday)

Samuel W. Orme succeeded George A. Pincock as Bishop of Wilford Ward, Fremont Co., Idaho.

April 15, 1903 (Wednesday)

James R. Miller, counselor in the presidency of the Granite Stake, died in Mill Creek, Utah.

April 17, 1903 (Friday)

Mrs. Julia Pratt Gardner, a pioneer of 1847, died at Richfield, Sevier Co.

April 19, 1903 (Sunday)

Ralph Poulton succeeded Hugh Clayton as Bishop of the Sixth Ward, Provo, Utah Co.

April 26, 1903 (Sunday)

John Balfour was installed as Bishop of Chesterfield Ward, Bannock Co., Idaho, succeeding Judson A. Tolman.

April 29, 1903 (Wednesday)

John H. Anderson was honorably released as Bishop of Mendon Ward, Cache Co., Utah, and Mormon Delbert Bird installed in his stead.

May 3, 1903 (Sunday)

The Saints residing at Summerville, Union Co., Ore., were organized as a Ward, with Thomas Platt as Bishop.

May 4, 1903 (Monday)

Elizabeth G. Benson, a pioneer, died at Wellsville, Cache Co., Utah.

May 5, 1903 (Tuesday)

John Wesley Adair, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died in Nutrioso, Apache Co., Ariz. He was born Feb. 18, 1820, in Alabama.

May 9, 1903 (Saturday)

The first copies of the Book of Mormon (Ole Tusi A Mamona) in the Samoan language were received from the press of the "Deseret News," and Elder James Ricks started for Samoa with thirty—six copies. The book was translated by Elders William G. Sears, Frank E. Lewis, Edwin Smart and G.C. Pillsbury. The edition consisted of 5,000 copies.

May 10, 1903 (Sunday)

Daniels and Buysville Wards, Wasatch Co., Utah, were amalgamated by Apostles Rudger Clawson and Hyrum M. Smith, and Patrick H. McGuire was chosen and sustained as Bishop of the enlarged Ward, called Daniel.

May 10, 1903 (Sunday)

George B. Garff succeeded F.A. Fraughton as Bishop of Wallsburg, Wasatch Co., Utah.

May 15, 1903 (Friday)

Ephraim H. Nye, president of the Southern States mission, died at Columbia, South Carolina.

May 17, 1903 (Sunday)

William H. Griffin was released as Bishop of Newton Ward, Cache Co., Utah, and Martin C. Rigby appointed Bishop in his stead.

May 23, 1903 (Saturday)

Elder George Oscar Stanger, of Neeleyville, Idaho, who was laboring as a missionary in the Southwestern States mission, died at Kansas City, Mo.

May 24, 1903 (Sunday)

The Cove branch, Union Co., Ore., was organized as a Ward, with John A. Abbott as Bishop.

May 27, 1903 (Wednesday)

Elder Silas Ferry Andrus, who labored as a missionary in the Southern States mission, died at Waycross, Georgia.

May 31, 1903 (Sunday)

Albert Heath succeeded Hyrum J. Lucas as Bishop of Hiatt (now Plano) Ward, Bingham Co., Idaho.

June 1, 1903 (Monday)

The Brigham Young Memorial Building of the Latter-day Saints University, in Salt Lake City, was dedicated.

June 4, 1903 (Wednesday)

Patriarch Thomas H. Giles died in Heber City, Utah.

June 19, 1903 (Friday)

The Saints residing near Black's Springs, north of Marysvale, Fremont Co., Idaho, were organized as a Ward called Vernon, with Parley L. Cutler as Bishop.

July 9, 1903 (Sunday)

Christian A. Madsen was honorably released as Bishop of Gunnison Ward, Sanpete Co., Utah, and Joseph Christensen chosen as Bishop in his stead.

July 24, 1903 (Friday)

The new mission house in Christiania, Norway, was dedicated by Pres. Francis Marion Lyman. Pres. Christian D. Fjeldsted, Anthon L. Skanchy (president of the Scandinavian mission), Levi E. Young (president of the Swiss mission), Willard T. Cannon (president of the Netherlands mission) and about 160 other Elders and visitors from Zion were present.

July 24, 1903 (Friday)

Pioneer Park, Salt Lake City, was thrown open to the public and the day was generally celebrated throughout Utah.

July 25, 1903 (Saturday)

Elders Warren H. Lyon, W.R. Smith, Thomas L. Griffiths and George A. Simpkins arrived at Cape Town, South Africa, to reopen a Latter-day Saints mission in that part of the world.

July 26, 1903 (Sunday)

Yost Ward (Cassia Stake), Cassia Co., Idaho, was organized by the presidency of the Cassia Stake, with Thomas H. Blackburn, jun., as Bishop.

August 2, 1903 (Sunday)

Bishop Hans Jensen was honorably released as Bishop of Manti South Ward, on account of failing health, and Louis C. Kjaer installed as Bishop in his stead.

August 3, 1903 (Monday)

The Elders having been banished from Berlin, Germany, the headquarters of the Swiss and German mission were moved to Zurich, Switzerland.

August 4, 1903 (Tuesday)

Pres. Francis M. Lyman and Elder Joseph J. Cannon visited Abo, Finland, and offered up an earnest prayer for Finland and its people.

August 6, 1903 (Thursday)

(July 24, Russian time). Pres. Francis M. Lyman, accompanied by Elder Joseph H. Cannon, offered up a dedicatory prayer at St. Petersburg, Russia. Thence the brethren proceeded to Moscow, where Pres. Lyman, on August 9th, offered another prayer in Alexander Park, under the walls of the Kremlin. Returning toward England, the brethren passed through Warsaw, Poland, where Pres. Lyman, on August 12th, again prayed, dedicating Poland for the preaching of the Gospel.

August 16, 1903 (Sunday)

Melissa D. Clinton (sister of the late Apostle Erastus Snow), a pioneer school teacher, died in Salt Lake City.

August 17, 1903 (Monday)

Elder Elmer Hooks, of Provo, Utah, who labored as a missionary in Mexico, died of typhoid fever in the city of Mexico.

August 19, 1903 (Wednesday)

Elder Godfred Knutti, of Montpelier, Idaho, who labored as a missionary in Switzerland, died at Zurich, Switzerland.

August 22, 1903 (Saturday)

Edwin M. Peck, a member of the Mormon Battalion and a resident of Provo, died in Goshen, Utah.

August 24, 1903 (Monday)

The ground for the erection of a new mission house for the Colorado mission was broken in Denver, Colo. The building was ready for occupancy Dec. 1, 1903, and was dedicated March 13, 1904. The mission house is a two-story brick structure, 45 x 42 feet.

August 24, 1903 (Monday)

Elder Richard Eli Johnson, a missionary laboring in the Southwestern States mission, died at Hot Springs, Arkansas. He was born May 1, 1877, at Monroe, Utah.

August 30, 1903 (Sunday)

At the regular quarterly conference of the Alberta Stake, held at Magrath, Canada, that Stake was divided, and the eastern portion organized as the Taylor Stake, thus named in honor of Apostle John W. Taylor. Heber S. Allen was sustained as president of the new Stake with Theodore Brandley as his first and J. William Knight as his second counselor.

September 10, 1903 (Thursday)

The Saints who had settled on Smith's Fork, south of Owen, in Uinta Co., Wyo., were organized as a branch called Mountain View with James Graham as presiding Elder.

September 10, 1903 (Thursday)

Patriarch John Ellison died in Kaysville Davis Co., Utah.

September 27, 1903 (Sunday)

Elder Heber J. Grant returned from his mission to Japan, having been honorably released. On his departure from Japan, Sept. 11, 1903, Elder Horace S. Ensign succeeded him in the presidency of the mission.

October 4, 1903 (Sunday)

The 74th semi-annual conference convened in Salt Lake City. It was continued until the 6th, on which day George Albert Smith was sustained as one of the Twelve Apostles to fill the vacancy caused by the death of Brigham Young, jun. Francis M. Lyman was sustained as president of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles.

October 10, 1903 (Saturday)

Bishop Lars P. Madsen, of Mt. Pleasant North Ward, Sanpete Co., Utah, was accidentally killed while coming down Cottonwood Canyon with a load of coal.

October 20, 1903 (Tuesday)

Green Flake (colored), one of the original pioneers of Utah, died at Idaho Falls, Idaho.

November 5, 1903 (Thursday)

The authorities of the Church purchased the old Carthage jail in which the Prophet Joseph Smith and Patriarch Hyrum Smith were assassinated. The cost of the building and the grounds (two acres) was $4,000.

November 8, 1903 (Sunday)

Aaron F. Farr, a member of the original company of Utah Pioneers, died in Logan, Utah. He was born October 31, 1818, in Vermont.

November 15, 1903 (Sunday)

Marinus Larsen was honorably released as Bishop of the Third Ward, Spanish Fork, Utah Co., Utah, and Geo. H. Hales installed as Bishop in his stead.

November 15, 1903 (Sunday)

William E. Anderson was honorably released as Bishop of Emmett Ward, Canyon Co., Idaho, and David Nelson chosen as Bishop in his stead.

November 21, 1903 (Saturday)

Willard Gilbert Smith, a survivor of the Haun's Mill massacre, a member of the Mormon Battalion and formerly president of the Morgan Stake of Zion, died in Logan, Utah. He was a son of Warren (killed in the Haun's Mill massacre) and Amanda Smith, and was born May 9, 1827, in Amherst, Ohio.

November 22, 1903 (Sunday)

At a Stake conference held at Driggs, Teton valley, Idaho, Robert G. Mickle, Bishop of Leigh Ward, Fremont Co., Idaho, was honorably released and Geo. W. Hendrickson installed as Bishop in his stead.

November 23, 1903 (Monday)

At a special meeting held at Deseret, Millard Co., Utah, Frank Hinckley was honorably released as Bishop of that Ward and Hyrum S. Cahoon chosen and sustained as Bishop in his stead.

November 28, 1903 (Saturday)

The magnificent building subsequently known as the Granite Stake tabernacle, located on the corner of State and 14th South streets, was opened to the public. The building cost about $60,000 and the main hall has a seating capacity of 2,500.

December 4, 1903 (Friday)

Patriarch William Eccles, a very active and respected Church worker, died in Ogden, Utah. He was a native of Renfrewshire, Scotland.

December 8, 1903 (Tuesday)

A grand celebration was held in Christiania, Norway, in commemoration of the organization of the branch of the Church in that city fifty years before by Elder Carl. C.A. Christensen.

December 16, 1903 (Wednesday)

John W. Hess, president of the Davis Stake of Zion, died at Farmington, Utah, after a prolonged and painful sickness. He was born Nov. 22, 1816, in England.

December 17, 1903 (Thursday)

George Woodward, one of the original Pioneers of Utah, died at St. George, Utah.

December 20, 1903 (Sunday)

Lehi, Utah Co., Utah (Alpine Stake), was divided into four Wards with Andrew Fjeld as Bishop of the First Ward, James H. Gardner as Bishop of the Second Ward, Henry Lewis as Bishop of the Third Ward and John Stoker as Bishop of the Fourth Ward.

December 23, 1903 (Wednesday)

William D. Owen, a veteran of the Church, died in Salt Lake City, aged 94 years.

December 23, 1903 (Wednesday)

David Rogers, a Church veteran, died at St. George, Utah.

December 23, 1903 (Wednesday)

Elder Benjamin Cluff, jun., president of the Brigham Young University at Provo, resigned his position.

December 27, 1903 (Sunday)

The Granite Stake tabernacle was dedicated, Pres. Joseph F. Smith offering the dedicatory prayer.

December 27, 1903 (Sunday)

The west boundary of the Fourteenth Ward, Salt Lake City was changed from 2nd West street to 4th West street, thus transferring six 10-acre blocks from the Fifteenth to the Fourteenth Ward.

December 27, 1903 (Sunday)

Edwin Bodily succeeded Moroni W. Pratt as Bishop of Fairview Ward, Oneida Co., Idaho.

December 28, 1903 (Monday)

Parley P.P. Prophet, a Church veteran, died suddenly at South Weber, Utah.

December 31, 1903 (Thursday)

John Steel, a member of the Mormon Battalion and a pioneer of Utah, died in southern Utah. He made the first last and pair of shoes ever made in Salt Lake valley and was the father of the first white child ever born in said valley.
