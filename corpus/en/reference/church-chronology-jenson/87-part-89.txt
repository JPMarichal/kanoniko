Andrew Jenson, Church Chronology ( 1914)

A number of prominent Church people died this year. Missionary work was recommenced on the Society and Tuamotu Islands. Home Rule was proposed for Utah instead of Statehood. Respectable Gentiles in Salt Lake City united with "Mormons" to terminate Liberal rule, etc.

January 1892

The Gardo House, Salt Lake City, was rented by the Receiver for a "Keeley Institute."

January 1, 1892 (Friday)

The branch of the Rio Grande Western railway, commencing at Springville and running around the south end of Utah lake to Eureka, Tintic Valley, Juab Co., was opened for traffic.

January 2, 1892 (Saturday)

In the U.S. District Court at Boise City, Idaho, indictments against nearly a hundred "Mormons," accused of violating the election laws by registering and voting in 1888, were dismissed.

January 4, 1892 (Monday)

The new Brigham Young Academy building at Provo, Utah Co., was dedicated.

January 5, 1892 (Tuesday)

The "Home Rule Bill" for Utah was introduced in both houses of Congress. Charles D. Faulkner introduced it in the Senate and Delegate John T. Caine in the House.

January 6, 1892 (Wednesday)

In the Territorial Supreme Court, Salt Lake City, Henry W. Lawrence asked for $300 as a monthly compensation for his services as Receiver of escheated Church property; his attorney asked for $150 per month for his own services.

January 8, 1892 (Friday)

The Tuscarora Club of Utah was organized at a Liberal meeting, held at the Walker House, Salt Lake City, with Orlando W. Powers as grand sachem.

January 10, 1892 (Sunday)

The first Latter-day Saint Deaf Mute Sunday School was organized in the 19th Ward, Salt Lake City, by the Salt Lake Stake Sunday School superintendency. Henry C. Barrell, Supt.; Laron Pratt, (a deaf mute,) asst. supt. and secretary.

January 11, 1892 (Monday)

The 30th session of the Utah legislature convened in Salt Lake City and organized with Wm. H. King as president of the Council and Wm. H. Seegmiller speaker of the House.

January 11, 1892 (Monday)

A large excursion, consisting of members of the International League of Press Clubs, arrived in Salt Lake City on a visit. A musical concert was given in their honor in the Tabernacle and a reception at the Knutsford hotel.

January 12, 1892 (Tuesday)

In the House branch of the Utah legislature, Luther T. Tuttle introduced a bill prohibiting polygamy, etc.

January 13, 1892 (Wednesday)

In the Council branch of the Utah legislature, Peters, of Brigham City, introduced a bill providing for the punishment of polygamy, fornication, etc.

January 15, 1892 (Friday)

Master in Chancery, Charles F. Loofbourow, filed his report in the Church case, in the Territorial Supreme Court, and recommended that the escheated Church property be devoted to the benefit of public schools.

January 15, 1892 (Friday)

Elder Daniel Allen, a Church veteran, died at Escalante, Garfield Co.

January 16, 1892 (Saturday)

The Salt Lake Natural Gas Co. filed its articles of incorporation with the clerk of the Third District Court.

January 18, 1892 (Monday)

In the U.S. Senate, Henry M. Teller, of Colorado, introduced a bill providing for the admission of Utah into the Union as a State.

January 19, 1892 (Tuesday)

Elder Cornelius Green died in Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

January 21, 1892 (Thursday)

Fanny Young Thatcher died in Salt Lake City.

January 23, 1892 (Saturday)

Susan Smith Adams, of Mormon Battalion fame, died at Harrisburgh, Washington Co., Utah.

January 24, 1892 (Sunday)

Elder Geo. C. Riser, the pioneer shoemaker of Utah, and Harriet H. Phelps, wife of the late Judge Wm. W. Phelps, died in Salt Lake City.

January 28, 1892 (Thursday)

Elders Joseph W. Damron and Wm. A. Seegmiller, arrived at Papeete, Tahiti, from Samoa, being sent to reopen the Society Islands mission.

January 31, 1892 (Sunday)

Patriarch Robert Logan died at Orangeville, Emery Co.

February 1, 1892 (Monday)

Prescindia L. Huntington Kimball died in Salt Lake City, and Addison Greene, once a member of Zion's Camp, died at Newbern, Jersey Co., Illinois.

February 2, 1892 (Tuesday)

Elder Geo. W. Price, died in the Fifteenth Ward, Salt Lake City.

February 3, 1892 (Wednesday)

A bill providing for the Fourth Judicial District in Utah was passed by the U.S. Senate.

February 4, 1892 (Thursday)

Bishop Niels Aagaard died of pneumonia, at Levan, Juab Co.

February 5, 1892 (Friday)

Henry Yates was discharged from the Penitentiary.

February 8, 1892 (Monday)

The municipal election in Salt Lake City resulted in victory for the Liberals, except in the Third Precinct, which was carried by the Democrats.

February 10, 1892 (Wednesday)

The "Home Rule Bill" was again argued before the House Committee on Territories, at Washington, D.C.

February 11, 1892 (Thursday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake County were tendered a free entertainment in the Salt Lake Theater, where the Lyceum Dramatic Company played "The Banker's Daughter."

February 11, 1892 (Thursday)

The Congressional enactment, authorizing the creation of the Fourth Judicial District in Utah, became law.

February 12, 1892 (Friday)

Col. O.J. Hollister, a bitter anti-Mormon, died in Salt Lake City.

February 13, 1892 (Saturday)

During the proceedings before the U.S. Senate Committee, in reference to the "Home Rule Bill," an application for amnesty for polygamists signed by the First Presidency and the Apostles and dated Salt Lake City, Dec. 19, 1891, was presented. The application was endorsed by Gov. Arthur L. Thomas and Judge Charles S. Zane.

February 14, 1892 (Sunday)

The Seventies residing in the southern part of the Bannock Stake, Idaho, were organized as the 106th quorum of Seventy; Emil Bochman, Wrol C. Olsen, Lorenzo R. Thomas, George E. Hill, sen., Brigham H. Ellsworth and Joseph Empey, presidents.

February 16, 1892 (Tuesday)

John Fitzgerald, a Pioneer of 1847, died at Draper, Salt Lake Co.

February 17, 1892 (Wednesday)

Elder Wm. King, president of the Hawaiian settlement in Skull Valley, died at the Deseret Hospital, Salt Lake City, and Daniel B. Rawson, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died near Ogden, Weber Co.

February 20, 1892 (Saturday)

At a meeting of the Brigham Young Memorial Association in Salt Lake City, the model offered by C.E. Dallin, of Springville, Utah Co., for a statue of Pres. Brigham Young, was accepted, and the southeast corner of the Temple Block suggested as a site for the monument.

February 20, 1892 (Saturday)

Thos. H. Bullock, of Plain City, was discharged from his third term of imprisonment in the Penitentiary.

February 25, 1892 (Thursday)

Elder Daniel Davis died at Bountiful, Davis Co.

February 28, 1892 (Sunday)

Robert T. Thomas, one of the Utah Pioneers of 1847, died at Provo, Utah Co., and Patriarch John Duggan Gibbs, died at Portage, Box Elder Co.

March 1892

Mormon Elders were mobbed in Denmark, principally on Sjaelland. Elders Niels J. Hendricksen and Ole Sorensen, jun., who labored on the islands of Falster and Moen, were banished from the country.

March 1, 1892 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, Joseph Dilworth was sentenced to one month's imprisonment, for "adultery."

March 2, 1892 (Wednesday)

Niels Peter Christensen, who had been imprisoned since May 2, 1891, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 3, 1892 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Ogden, Judge James A. Miner commuted the sentence passed Nov. 20, 1891, upon Christopher S. Winge for six months' imprisonment, to twenty days' imprisonment.

March 5, 1892 (Saturday)

Christopher S. Winge was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 6, 1892 (Sunday)

Bishop William Andrew Taylor died at Farr West, Weber Co.

March 9, 1892 (Wednesday)

Nicholas H. Groesbeck was arrested at Springville, Utah Co., on a charge of adultery.

March 10, 1892 (Thursday)

A new school law, passed by the Utah legislature, was approved; also a new election law, providing for holding all general and local elections in the Territory, on the Tuesday following the first Monday in November, 1892, and biennially thereafter.

March 12, 1892 (Saturday)

The Utah legislature adjourned. Among the bills passed and approved was one creating Grand County.

March 12, 1892 (Saturday)

Chr. Anderson, who had been imprisoned since Oct. 13, 1891, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 15, 1892 (Tuesday)

The taking of depositions of "Mormons" regarding the ownership of the Temple lot at Independence, Mo., was commenced at the Templeton hotel, Salt Lake City, before John M. Orr, a special commissioner, appointed by the U.S. Circuit Court for the Western Division of the State of Missouri. It was part of a suit "brought by the Reorganized church, commonly called Josephites, against the Church of Christ, commonly called Hedrickites, to obtain possession and title to a piece of land in Independence, Jackson Co., Mo., known as the Temple Lot."

March 16, 1892 (Wednesday)

The Rock Springs branch, Wyo., was organized as a Ward by Apostle John H. Smith; Joseph Soulsby, Bishop.

March 16, 1892 (Wednesday)

Professor Charles William Elliot, president of the Harvard University, visited Salt Lake City. He addressed a large assembly in the Tabernacle in the evening.

March 17, 1892 (Thursday)

The Deseret News published a communication from the First Presidency, declaring that the rumors of their directing members of the Church which political party they should support were false and without foundation in fact. The Presidency had no disposition to direct in such matters, but desired the people to choose for themselves.

March 17, 1892 (Thursday)

The 50th anniversary of the organization of the Relief Society, at Nauvoo, Ill., was celebrated throughout the Church. In Salt Lake City, services were held in the Tabernacle, and the congregation was addressed by Zina D.H. Young, Abraham H. Cannon, Joseph F. Smith, Bathsheba W. Smith, Romania B. Pratt and others.

March 17, 1892 (Thursday)

In the First District Court, at Provo, John A. Mower was sentenced to ten days' imprisonment, for u.c.

March 19, 1892 (Saturday)

An act, passed by the Utah legislature, organizing a fourth judicial district in Utah, comprising Weber, Box Elder, Cache, Rich and Morgan Counties, was approved.

March 23, 1892 (Wednesday)

General Russel A. Alger and family arrived in Salt Lake City on a visit; a banquet was given in his honor at the Templeton hotel.

March 23, 1892 (Wednesday)

The examination in the Independence, Jackson Co., Mo., Temple lot case, at the Templeton hotel, Salt Lake City, was terminated.

March 25, 1892 (Friday)

Ex U.S. Marshal Frank H. Dyer died in Salt Lake City.

March 25, 1892 (Friday)

Elder John R. Young was severely wounded and his grandchild killed by a gun accident, near Demming, New Mexico.

March 26, 1892 (Saturday)

Dr. John Riggs, a Church veteran, died at Provo, Utah Co.

March 26, 1892 (Saturday)

John A. Mower was discharged from the Penitentiary.

March 27, 1892 (Sunday)

Beaver Dams branch, Box Elder Co., was organized as a Ward; Francello Durfee, Bishop.

March 29, 1892 (Tuesday)

Gov. Arthur L. Thomas appointed Nelson A. Empey, R. Macintosh and R.C. Chambers World's Fair Commissioners for Utah.

March 30, 1892 (Wednesday)

The U.S. House Committee recommended the passage of the "Home Rule Bill" for Utah.

March 31, 1892 (Thursday)

Elder Wm. Glover died at Farmington, Davis Co.

March 31, 1892 (Thursday)

Elder Wm. C. Winder and a missionary companion were expelled by a mob from Stanley County, N.C., for preaching the gospel.

April 1, 1892 (Friday)

Joseph Dilworth was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 3, 1892 (Sunday)

The 62nd annual conference of the Church was commenced in Salt Lake City, continuing till the 6th.

April 6, 1892 (Wednesday)

In the general conference of the Church, the Priesthood was arranged in the stands and the auditorium. Pres. Lorenzo Snow explained the order of ceremony at the laying of the capstone of the Temple and trained the congregation in shouting hosannah, after which remarks were made by Pres. Wilford Woodruff. The congregation then proceeded to the Temple in procession, when the capstone of the Temple was laid amid great enthusiasm and rejoicing, Pres. Woodruff pressing the electric button, which caused the stone to be lowered to its place. After the shouting of hosannah, the vast congregation, on motion of Apostle Francis M. Lyman, voted that the Temple should be finished by April 6, 1893. About forty thousand people were present and participated in the ceremonies.

April 7, 1892 (Thursday)

This evening the statue of the angel Moroni, on the main east tower of the Temple, in Salt Lake City, were beautifully illuminated with incandescent lights, for the first time.

April 10, 1892 (Sunday)

Springville, Utah Co., was divided into the First, Second, Third and Fourth Wards, with John Tuckett, Loren Harmer, Geo. R. Hill and Joseph Loynd as their respective Bishops.

April 11, 1892 (Monday)

Edward Hunter, formerly a Bishop and a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Grantsville, Tooele Co.

April 12, 1892 (Tuesday)

Elder Julian Moses died at East Mill Creek, Salt Lake Co.

April 12, 1892 (Tuesday)

Jacob Albertson was arrested at Spanish Fork, Utah Co., for u.c.

April 12, 1892 (Tuesday)

The Salt Lake City council refused to enforce the city ordinance forbidding saloon-keepers to sell liquor on Sunday.

April 14, 1892 (Thursday)

Jacob Gates, one of the First Seven Presidents of Seventies, died at Provo, Utah Co.

April 15, 1892 (Friday)

Elder Robert Bodily, a highly respected Church veteran, died at Kaysville, Davis Co.

April 16, 1892 (Saturday)

Elder Thos. Butler, of Richfield, Sevier Co., died in Salt Lake City, on his way home from a mission to Virginia.

April 18, 1892 (Monday)

Elder Lester J. Herrick, a Church veteran, died at Ogden, Weber Co.

April 18, 1892 (Monday)

The taking of depositions in the suit for the possession of the Temple lot at Independence, Jackson Co., Mo., was commenced at Independence.

April 20, 1892 (Wednesday)

Lars J. Augustson, of Vernal, Uintah Co., Utah, was arrested for u.c., taken before Com. James T. McConnell, at Fort Duchesne, and placed under $600 bonds.

April 22, 1892 (Friday)

Samuel Thompson, a member of the Mormon Battalion, died at Vernal, Uintah Co.

April 25, 1892 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Henry Tuckett was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, for u.c.

April 28, 1892 (Thursday)

Peter Nielsen, of Smithfield, was discharged from the Penitentiary.

April 29, 1892 (Friday)

The first cattlemen's congress held in the United States, convened at Ogden. Fifteen States were represented.

May 1, 1892 (Sunday)

This day was observed by the Latter-day Saints generally as a special day of fasting and prayer.

May 2, 1892 (Monday)

Elder Charles Lambert, a Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

May 7, 1892 (Saturday)

Elder Lars N. Larsen died at Moroni, Sanpete Co.

May 9, 1892 (Monday)

In the Second District Court, Beaver, John Baird was sentenced to four months' imprisonment, for alleged adultery.

May 10, 1892 (Tuesday)

Dr. Jeter Clinton, a Church veteran, died in Salt Lake City.

May 12, 1892 (Thursday)

After trial in the First District Court, at Ogden, the case of Lars C. Christensen, charged with u.c., was dismissed.

May 13, 1892 (Friday)

The Salt Lake City council passed a bill authorizing bonding the city for $600,000 for corporate purposes.

May 14, 1892 (Saturday)

The Utah Democrats, assembled in Territorial convention at Ogden, adopted a party platform.

May 14, 1892 (Saturday)

Elder Wm. D. Kartchner died at Snowflake, Ariz.

May 15, 1892 (Sunday)

About seven hundred Presbyterians, chiefly preachers, arrived in Salt Lake City, en route to a convention in California. They attended services in the Tabernacle.

May 16, 1892 (Monday)

The visiting Presbyterians held a union missionary meeting in the Theatre, Salt Lake City.

May 17, 1892 (Tuesday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Frank Meldrum was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, for "adultery."

May 19, 1892 (Thursday)

The first number of the Spanish Fork Sun, a newspaper, was issued at Spanish Fork, Utah Co.

May 21, 1892 (Saturday)

Elder Seth Langton died at Logan, Cache Co., Utah.

May 23, 1892 (Monday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Geo. E. Cozier was sentenced to one year's imprisonment, for "adultery."

May 26, 1892 (Thursday)

After trial in the First District Court, at Ogden, the jury returned a verdict of "not guilty" in the case of John L. Jones, charged with adultery.

May 26, 1892 (Thursday)

In the Third District Court, Salt Lake City, Nicholas H. Groesbeck was sentenced to one year's imprisonment for alleged adultery.

May 27, 1892 (Friday)

Dr. Foster R. Kenner died at Manti, Sanpete Co.

May 28, 1892 (Saturday)

In the U.S. House of Representatives it was proposed that the so-called Industrial Christian Home of Utah" (which was founded some years previously as a refuge for Mormon wives who abandoned polygamy) be summarily disposed of by Congress, by granting the entire premises to the Territory as a site for a Territorial school for deaf and dumb. The proposition, however, was not sustained.

May 30, 1892 (Monday)

Leo Haefeli, a Utah journalist, died in Salt Lake City.

May 31, 1892 (Tuesday)

Benjamin W. Rolfe, a Utah Pioneer of 1847, died in Salt Lake City.

May 31, 1892 (Tuesday)

Elijah Sells, acting governor of Utah, issued a proclamation, ordering that "the regular terms of court in the Fourth Judicial District" be held at Ogden, and that the first term convene there June 1, 1892.

June 1, 1892 (Wednesday)

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, Wm. H. Dopp and Thos. W. Obray were each sentenced by Judge James A. Miner to four months' imprisonment, and Nils C. Erickson to one year's imprisonment, all for "adultery."

June 1, 1892 (Wednesday)

James S. Brown and two other Elders from Utah arrived at Papeete, Tahiti, as missionaries to the Society Islands. Soon afterwards the mission was successfully reopened.

June 13, 1892 (Monday)

Dr. Karl G. Maeser delivered the first of a series of Sunday School lectures in the Assembly Hall, Salt Lake City.

June 19, 1892 (Sunday)

Elder Noah L. Shurtliff was accidentally killed in a well, at Cardston, Alberta, Canada.

June 21, 1892 (Tuesday)

Elder Lot Smith, famous in the early history of Utah, was killed by Indians, at Tuba, near Moan Coppy, Ariz.

June 22, 1892 (Wednesday)

Hyrum Naegie was terribly mangled by a bear near Colonia Pacheco, Mexico, from the effects of which he soon afterwards died.

June 29, 1892 (Wednesday)

The Old Folks of Salt Lake City and County were treated to a free excursion to Payson, Utah Co.

July 8, 1892 (Friday)

The Utah Supreme Court made an order directing the Receiver, in the suits of the government against the Church, to turn over to the Secretary of the Interior all Church property declared confiscated. This included the Tithing House property, the Church farm, coal lands, the Historian's Office and the Gardo House.

July 8, 1892 (Friday)

The Utah "Home Rule Bill" was passed by the U.S. House of Representatives.

July 10, 1892 (Sunday)

W.M. Allred was arrested at Spring City for u.c. and placed under bonds by Com. Jacob Johnson.

July 11, 1892 (Monday)

Wm. H. Brown died at Provo, Utah Co.

July 16, 1892 (Saturday)

An excursion party, consisting of 85 members of the Kansas Editorial Association, arrived in Salt Lake City, on a visit.

July 24, 1892 (Sunday)

The Granite Ward meeting house, Salt Lake Co., was dedicated.

July 24, 1892 (Sunday)

Wm. C. Winder and missionary companions, who had returned to Stanley County, South Carolina, were mobbed.

July 25, 1892 (Monday)

The corner stone of the joint city and county building was laid in Salt Lake City.

July 26, 1892 (Tuesday)

Sister Melissa N. Allred died at Lehi, Utah Co.

In the Fourth District Court, at Ogden, the cases of the following named persons, charged with polygamy, adultery, and u.c., were dismissed, on motion of U.S. Marshall Eli H. Parsons, who reported that it was impossible to secure evidence sufficient to justify a conviction; Lot Darney, James Kearl, John Burt, Edmond Clark, Joseph Gibbons, Milton D. Hammond, Milvin M. Hammond, Thos. R. Leavitt, John Woolf, Samuel Simmons, Orson Eggleston, Gideon Olsen, Hans Hansen, A.D. Child, Ludvig Erickson, Moroni Brown, Arthur P. Welchman, James Haslem, Wm. Hurd, Moroni Coleman, Wm. Bunderson, John J. Murphy, John I. Hart, and Hans J. Nielsen.

July 30, 1892 (Saturday)

A bill for the admission of Utah into the Union was introduced into the U.S. House of Representatives by Delegate John T. Caine.

July 31, 1892 (Sunday)

The corner stone of St. Mark's hospital, near the Warm Springs, Salt Lake City, was laid.

August 1892

Samuel Tarwater, an old Missouri mobocrat, who was badly wounded by "Mormons" in the Crooked River battle, Oct. 25, 1838, died in Missouri.

August 4, 1892 (Thursday)

John Ball was drowned in the Jordan river, near Salt Lake City.

August 6, 1892 (Saturday)

Lincoln Beach, ten miles northwest of Payson, Utah Co., was opened as a pleasure resort.

August 7, 1892 (Sunday)

August 13, 1892 (Saturday)

Dilworth Grimshaw, of Beaver, was discharged from imprisonment in the Penitentiary, where he had been confined since Oct. 14, 1891, for u.c.

August 14, 1892 (Sunday)

The Star Valley Stake of Zion was organized by Pres. Joseph F. Smith and Apostle Francis M. Lyman; Geo. Osmond, president; Wm. W. Burton and Anson V. Call, counselors; a high council was also organized.

August 14, 1892 (Sunday)

August 15, 1892 (Monday)

August 22, 1892 (Monday)

Elder John L. Dalton, who had been called to open a mission in California, arrived in San Francisco.

August 27, 1892 (Saturday)

Wm. Pugsley, of Salt Lake City, was accidentally drowned in the Weber river, near its source.

August 29, 1892 (Monday)

The Latter-day Saints academy of the Weber Stake was dedicated at Ogden.

The Agricultural College at Logan, Cache Co., was reopened, many changes and improvements having been made for the convenience of students.

November 8, 1892 (Tuesday)

At the general election in Utah, Joseph L. Rawlins (Democrat) was elected delegate to Congress, against Frank J. Cannon (Republican) and C.E. Allen (Liberal). The Liberals carried Salt Lake County.

November 8, 1892 (Tuesday)

Grover Cleveland (Democrat) was re-elected president of the United States, against Benjamin Harrison (Republican).

November 11, 1892 (Friday)

Bishop Henry Giles died at Blue Valley, Wayne Co.

November 12, 1892 (Saturday)

At a session of the Territorial Supreme Court, held in Salt Lake City, Chief Justice Charles S. Zane delivered an opinion in the case of the United States versus the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, confirming a decision of the lower court, and authorizing the use of the personal property of the Church under the direction of a trustee, for the building and repairing of houses of worship and the support of the poor. The court appointed Bishop Leonard G. Hardy trustee, and fixed his bonds at $500,000.

November 13, 1892 (Sunday)

Memorial services were held in the Tabernacle, Salt Lake City, in honor of Elders who had died while laboring as missionaries abroad. The speakers were Presidents Wilford Woodruff and Joseph F. Smith and Apostle Franklin D. Richards.

November 16, 1892 (Wednesday)

Bishop Robert R. Daines died at Hyde Park, Cache Co.

November 19, 1892 (Saturday)

The Utah Sugar Factory at Lehi, Utah Co., completed its second annual run. It had been in operation about forty days, and during that time employed an average of one hundred men and manufactured 1 1/2 million pounds of sugar.

November 19, 1892 (Saturday)

Lars J. Augustson and P.M. Petersen were discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 20, 1892 (Sunday)

Susan Snively Young, a widow of Pres. Brigham Young, died in Salt Lake City; and Sister Almira Green died at West Weber, Weber Co.

November 21, 1892 (Monday)

The Street car line on Centre Street, Salt Lake City, was opened.

November 22, 1892 (Tuesday)

Lewis Smith was discharged from the Penitentiary.

November 24, 1892 (Thursday)

Elizabeth Carter Whitmore died in Salt Lake City.

November 27, 1892 (Sunday)

Elder John L. Dalton organized a branch of the Church in Sacramento, Cal.; Aaron Garlic, president.

December 1, 1892 (Thursday)

A motor line from Five Points, north of Ogden, Weber Co., to the Warm Springs, was opened for traffic.

December 1, 1892 (Thursday)

I.A. Benton succeeded Eli H. Parsons as U.S. marshal for Utah.

December 1, 1892 (Thursday)

James Bywater was released from the Penitentiary, his sentence having been commuted by Pres. Harrison to one actual year of imprisonment from date of sentence, upon payment of costs.

December 4, 1892 (Sunday)

The Saints, who had settled in Ogden valley, north of Eden. Weber Co., were organized as the Liberty Ward; Joshua B. Judkins, Bishop.

December 4, 1892 (Sunday)

Ed. Thomas was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 5, 1892 (Monday)

Thos. Bingham was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 7, 1892 (Wednesday)

Bendt Larsen and Germand Ellsworth were discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 8, 1892 (Thursday)

Charles L. Flake was shot and killed by a desperado, at Snowflake, Ariz.

December 11, 1892 (Sunday)

The Treasureton branch of Clifton Ward, Oneida Co., Idaho, was organized as a Ward; Benjamin Hymas Bishop.

December 11, 1892 (Sunday)

The Saints who had settled on the Chama river, Rio Arriba Co., New Mexico, were organized as a Ward; Asael L. Fuller, Bishop.

December 12, 1892 (Monday)

A fire destroyed the most valuable business corner in Beaver, Beaver Co.

December 18, 1892 (Sunday)

Levi J. Taylor was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 10, 1892 (Monday)

Elder Wm. H. Shearman died in Salt Lake City.

December 20, 1892 (Tuesday)

In the First District Court, Provo, Eli A. Day was sentenced to one month's imprisonment, for "adultery."

December 23, 1892 (Friday)

Gilbert J. Marchant was discharged from the Penitentiary.

December 25, 1892 (Sunday)

The Fifth Ward meeting house, at Logan, Cache Co., was dedicated.

December 25, 1892 (Sunday)

Sister Elizabeth Haven Barlow, an old and faithful member of the Church, died at Bountiful, Davis Co.
