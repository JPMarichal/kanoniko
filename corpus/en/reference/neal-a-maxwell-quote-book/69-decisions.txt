Neal A. Maxwell, Neal A. Maxwell Quote Book ( 1997)

Decisions

There are certain mortal moments and minutes that matter-certain hinge points in the history of each human. Some seconds are so decisive they shrink the soul, while other seconds are spent so as to stretch the soul. ("Taking Up the Cross," p. 255.)

Decisions made in the midst of fatigue are seldom the best decisions, and agreements reached between the exhausted may last only until some of the participants are revived. (The Smallest Part, p. 48.)

It is an act of kindness for prophets to press mankind for a decision, because the absence of a decision to commit is a decision. (That My Family Should Partake, p. 22.)

It is very important that we not assume the perspectives of mortality in making the decisions that bear on eternity! We need the perspectives of the gospel to make decisions in the context of eternity. We need to understand we cannot do the Lord's work in the world's way. ("But for a Small Moment," p. 454.)
