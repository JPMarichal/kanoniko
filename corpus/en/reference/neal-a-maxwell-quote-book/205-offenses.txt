Neal A. Maxwell, Neal A. Maxwell Quote Book ( 1997)

Offenses

Some of us endure life's major afflictions better than we endure small slights. How do we feel, for example, when we are unfairly unmentioned in a public litany of appreciation? Though we may appear outwardly unoffended, do we brood inwardly? Was the oversight deliberate or merely forgetful? For the meek, however, any regret is minimal and concern is reserved for the one who did the overlooking, lest he be embarrassed. (That Ye May Believe, p. 55.)

Our planning usually assumes that our destiny is largely in our own hands. Then come intruding events, first elbowing aside, then evicting, what we had anticipated and even earned. Hence we can be offended by events as well as by people. (Men and Women of Christ, p. 63.)
