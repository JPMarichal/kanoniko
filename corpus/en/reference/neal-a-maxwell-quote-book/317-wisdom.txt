Neal A. Maxwell, Neal A. Maxwell Quote Book ( 1997)

Wisdom

A man cannot be truly "learned" if he deliberately seeks to ignore the wisdom not only of the ages, but of the eternities. If during his mortal journey he drinks only worldly water, he will find that his deepest thirsts are unslaked and inextinguishable. We can obtain "living water" from only one stream. (A Time to Choose, pp. 53-54.)

The wise learn from their past, but the stubborn, by their lack of understanding, err endlessly. (Look Back at Sodom, p. 4.)

Being "learned" (by simply indiscriminately stockpiling a silo of truths) is not necessarily the same thing as being wise, for wisdom is the useable distillation of data-not merely its collection and storage. (The Smallest Part, p. 4.)
