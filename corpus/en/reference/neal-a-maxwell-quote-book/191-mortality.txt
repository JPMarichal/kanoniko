Mortality

Our eventual place in eternity turns on whether our knees bend or shake and what we think and do in daily life. (Lord, Increase Our Faith, p. 100.)

Mortality involves teeth to be brushed, beds to be made, cars to be repaired, diapers to be changed, groceries to be bought-such an endless array of mundane matters. In the midst of these, however, is the real business of living-a friendship to be formed, a marriage to be mended, a child to be encouraged, a truth to be driven home, an apology to be made, a Christian attribute to be further developed. ("Not My Will, But Thine", p. 125.)

The general proving process of mortality is really the stunning sum of its billions of individual subsets, all of which are overseen by an all-knowing and all-loving Father and Son. But, though overseen by Them, we must, through the mists of the moment, keep our eyes on Them; there is actually nowhere else to look. (Even As I Am, pp. 60-61.)

Someday, when we look back on mortality, we will see that so many of the things that seemed to matter so much at the moment will be seen not to have mattered at all. (Even As I Am, p. 104.)

Mortality, this precious micro-dot on the canvas of eternity, is such a brief moment. While in it, we are to prepare ourselves for the time when there will be no time. ("Not My Will, But Thine", p. 11.)
