WRITTEN FOR THE INTERNATIONAL MONTHLY MAGAZINE.

BY H. W. PARKER.

'Tis sad to leave the lovely world, The blazoned banner of the sky, And all the Earth's sublimity, Are, day by day, in light unfurled, In glory float before the eye. The practised ear and eye are clearer, The heart is deeper, Nature dearer, From year to year: 'tis sad to die.

'Tis hard to leave the busy world— To feel our courage mounting high On thoughts that just begin to fly, Then arrow-struck and swiftly hurled Downward to dim obscurity. Our life is always a beginning, A hope of honor worth the winning; We hope to do, and hoping, die.

'Tis hard to leave a stormy world, When every watcher may descry A happy Future drawing nigh, And all the nations, onward whirled, Behold the sunny shores that lie Beyond that ever-heaving ocean— The Present, with its wild commotion; Alas, to see, to sink, to die.

And yet to leave a weary earth For higher life, is well, we know, Our being is a constant flow, And death itself is newer birth; The seed decays that it may grow; A world sublime awaits the dying Who purely lived. Away with sighing; The Past is passed; 'tis well to go.
