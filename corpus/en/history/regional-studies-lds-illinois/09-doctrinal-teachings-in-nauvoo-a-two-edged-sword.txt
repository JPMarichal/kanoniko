Doctrinal Teachings In Nauvoo: A Two-edged Sword

Larry E. Dahl

The focus of this paper is to review how the doctrinal teachings in Nauvoo acted as a two-edged sword, dividing the faithful from the unfaithful, leading some to unwavering commitment to the restored kingdom of God, and others to angry rebellion, even murderous sedition.

125In order to see things in perspective it is helpful to understand the symbolism of a two-edged sword, to address the question of why people respond differently to truth, and to remember that this life is a testing ground to determine the degree to which we will be obedient to God's laws and to his servants.

The "Word" of God is a Two-edged Sword

The Apostle John, the beloved disciple of Jesus, while banished to the isle of Patmos, had a wonderful vision of the Savior. He wrote of that vision:

125I was in the Spirit on the Lord's day, and heard behind me a great voice, as of a trumpet,Â�

125And I turned to see the voice that spake with me. And being turned, I saw seven golden candlesticks;

125And in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle.

125His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire;

125And his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters.

125And he had in his right hand seven stars: and out of his mouth went a sharp two-edged sword: and his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength.

125And when I saw him, I fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not; I am the first and the last:

125I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.

126Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter. ( Rev. 1:10, 12-19 )

126What is the meaning of the two-edged sword proceeding out of the mouth of the Savior? Paul, in writing to the Hebrews, explained:

126For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than a two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.

126Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do. ( Heb. 4:12-13 )

126A careful reading of the scriptures shows that the "word" of God sometimes refers to the truths that come from God (see D&C 11:21-23 ) and which are found in scripture and in the inspired utterances of his servants, while at other times the "word" refers to the Savior himself (see John 1:1-4 ). Either of these meanings fits well. The Savior himself and/or the truths that proceed from him cut like a two-edged sword, discerning the thoughts and intents of the heart, opening, exposing to view, making naked one's true self. Making a point of the fact that the sword has two edges suggests that it cuts both ways-it makes clear both righteousness and wickedness. The prophet Mormon, editorializing upon an interlude of peace and righteousness among the Nephites and the converted Lamanites known as the people of Ammon, explains the two-fold effect of the word of God in this way:

126Yea, we see that whosoever will may lay hold upon the word of God, which is quick and powerful, which shall divide asunder all the cunning and the snares and the wiles of the devil, and lead the man of Christ in a strait and narrow course across that everlasting gulf of misery which is prepared to engulf the wicked.

126And land their souls, yea, their immortal souls, at the right hand of God in the kingdom of heaven, to sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and with Jacob, and with all our holy fathers, to go no more out. ( Hel. 3:29 )

In addition to the Savior himself and his truths cutting in this manner like a two-edged sword, the Lord revealed through the prophet Brigham Young that the "Spirit is sent forth into the world to (1) enlighten the humble and contrite, and to (2) the condemnation of the ungodly" ( D&C 136:33 ). Evidently, the reason the Spirit has such an effect is, in Jacob's words, that "the Spirit speaketh the p.127] truth and lieth not. Wherefore, it speaketh of things as they really are, and of things as they really will be" ( Jacob 4:13 ). Truth, especially true doctrine in God's plan of salvation, is a great divider. But why do people react so differently, and often so passionately, to the truth? Why would anyone become angry because of the truth-angry enough, in Nauvoo, to seek a prophet's life? Or, even more perplexing, angry enough to put to death the Son of God, the very source of truth, as some did two thousand years ago?

128The Doctrine and Covenants adds more witnesses concerning this principle. While Joseph Smith was confined in Liberty Jail, the Lord revealed these words of warning concerning his detractors:

128Cursed are all those that shall lift up the heel against mine anointed, saith the Lord, and cry they have sinned when they have not sinned before me, saith the Lord, but have done that which was meet in mine eyes, and which I commanded them.

And President Harold B. Lee added a caution to all of us in our own day, when he said: "Mark well those who speak evil of the Lord's anointed, for they speak from impure hearts" (Conference Report [Oct 1947] 67).

128Clearly, those who get angry at God's truth or his prophets who deliver it do so either because they are "children of disobedience," or they are being influenced by the spirit of the devil. Neither of those possibilities is a happy one. On the other hand, those who are honestly striving for righteousness welcome the truth, treasure it, and are willing to sacrifice and serve to honor it. Our reaction to the truth, then, is a kind of spiritual litmus test, indicating the degree to which we are in harmony with eternal things and with the Spirit of God, rather than with the spirit of the world and of the devil.

Doctrinal Truths are Used to "Try" and "Prove" the Faithful

The principle of using doctrinal truths to test, try, and prove the Saints is taught in the revelations. For example, during a time p.129] of crisis in Jackson County, Missouri, in August 1833, the Lord revealed to the Prophet Joseph Smith that God would

129give unto the faithful line upon line, precept upon precept; and I will try you and prove you herewith.

129And whoso layeth down his life in my cause, for my name's sake, shall find it again, even life eternal.

129Therefore, be not afraid of your enemies, for I have decreed in my heart, saith the Lord, that I will prove you in all things, whether you will abide in my covenant, even unto death, that you may be found worthy.

129For if ye will not abide in my covenant ye are not worthy of me. ( D&C 98:12-15 )

129In March 1830, Martin Harris was instructed to "preach naught but repentance," and not to divulge to the "world" things he had recently learned until it was wisdom in God to do so. The reason given was that "they cannot bear meat now, but milk they must receive; wherefore, they must not know these things, lest they perish" ( D&C 19:21-22 ).

The milk/meat metaphor, often employed in the scriptures, communicates the principle effectively because of our common experience in feeding children. Children thrive on milk, but would choke on meat. But children also grow up, and their diet changes to suit the growing needs as their bodies mature. And so it is intended in the realm of spiritual things. When we are first introduced to the gospel, we are as children needing doctrinal milk. As we mature in learning and living the gospel, however, it is expected that gradually our spiritual digestive system will be able to handle more meaty doctrines. This process has both individual and institutional implications. Some individuals mature much faster than others. Unfortunately, some never become weaned from the bottle, coughing and choking, as it were, when any solid food is introduced into their diet. This reality presents an interesting challenge to the Lord and his prophets. What is to be taught to the Church as a whole? And what is to be done about those who long for and can digest more solid food than is currently appropriate for the body of the Church? Should they be deprived because some still need only milk? And what is to eventually happen to those who simply do not want and cannot handle much beyond milk? I submit the answer is tied to the two-edged sword analogy-doctrinal truths act as a sharp sword, exposing hearts, dividing loyalties, separating sheep from goats. This process leaves behind, if you will, those who will p.130] not or cannot abide the higher doctrines and covenants of the gospel and, at the same time, proves, strengthens, prepares, and purifies those who hunger for the fulness of the gospel.

130Interestingly, there is real pain involved for those at both ends of the spectrum. Those left behind often become frustrated and bitter. Some of these, with a mean-spirited obsession, lash out against the institution, its leaders, or its members, causing pain and sadness to themselves and to those who remain faithful. Even more painful, however, for the stalwart Saints, is the pain that must be endured in being proven in all things. We earlier quoted from D&C 98 indicating that the Lord will "try" and will "prove" the faithful "in all thingsÂ�even unto death" (vv 12-14). The Prophet Joseph Smith taught that before one could have his calling and election made sure he must be "thoroughly proved"; he must demonstrate that he is "determined to serve [God] at all hazards" (Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith 150; hereafter TPJS). John Taylor said the Prophet also taught the following to the Twelve in Nauvoo:

130You will have all kinds of trials to pass through. And it is quite as necessary for you to be tried as it was for Abraham and other men of GodÂ�God will feel after you, and He will take hold of you and wrench your very heart strings, and if you cannot stand it you will not be fit for an inheritance in the Celestial Kingdom of God. (Journal of Discourses 24:197; hereafter JD)

Examples from the Nauvoo Years

The Nauvoo period in Church history is a showcase that vividly illustrates the principles discussed above. Time will allow mentioning but a few examples. First, we will cite some statements of the Prophet Joseph Smith lamenting the fact that he was not able to teach the Saints all that the Lord had revealed to him. Obviously, he wanted desperately to share what he knew, but the Saints generally were unprepared and unwilling to accept some of those truths. We will then focus upon William Law and his associates as examples of those who were cut asunder by the two-edged sword of doctrine. Finally, as representative of those who were tried in the fire and proved to be pure gold, we will review a tender account of a heart-wrenching test of Heber C. and Vilate Kimball.

Joseph Smith's Lament

The following quotations from Joseph Smith are all from the Nauvoo period. Both the message and the Prophet's depth of feeling are clear.

The reason we do not have the secrets of the Lord revealed unto us, is because we do not keep them but reveal them; we do not keep our own secrets, but reveal our difficulties to the world, even to our enemies, then how would we keep the secrets of the Lord? I can keep a secret till Doomsday. (HC 4:478-79)

131In April 1842 he added:

131The Lord makes manifest to me many things, which it is not wisdom for me to make public, until others can witness the proof of them. (HC 4:608)

131A year later, April 1843, the Prophet encouraged and comforted the Saints at the death of Lorenzo D. Barnes, with these words:

131It is my meditation all the day, and more than my meat and drink, to know how I shall make the Saints of God comprehend the visions that roll like an overflowing surge before my mind.

131Oh! how I would delight to bring before you things which you never thought of! But poverty and the cares of the world prevent. 131The following month, 21 May 1843, Joseph Smith preached a sermon in the Nauvoo Temple. His text was the first chapter of 2 Peter, focusing on making one's calling and election sure. It seems his purpose was to stretch the Saints' understanding and their commitment to move forward in spiritual things. Among other things he said the following:

132I could explain a hundred fold more than I ever have of the glories of the kingdoms manifested to me in the vision, were I permitted, and were the people prepared to receive them.

132The Lord deals with this people as a tender parent with a child, communicating light and intelligence and the knowledge of his ways as they can bear it. The inhabitants of the earth are asleep; they know not the day of their visitation. (HC 5:402)

132In July 1843, the same month that D&C 132 of the Doctrine and Covenants concerning eternal and plural marriage was recorded, the Prophet Joseph spoke about a man's foes being those of his own household. A report of those remarks states:

132The same spirit that crucified Jesus is in the breast of some who profess to be Saints in Nauvoo. 132Six months later, 21 January 1844, the Prophet preached to several thousand people at the southeast corner of the temple. Again he spoke of the difficulty in getting people generally, as well as the Saints, to learn the things of God:

(HC 6:184-85)

132On 7 March 1844, just three months before he died, in a speech in the temple, Joseph said simply, "I know much that I do not tell" (HC 6:244).

But you would call it blasphemy, and there are men upon this stand who would want to take my life" (Quoted in Orson F. Whitney, Life of Heber C. Kimball [Salt Lake City: Bookcraft, 1945], 322).

Wilford Woodruff testified of Joseph Smith: "His mind was opened by the visions of the almighty, and the Lord taught him p.133] many things by vision and revelation that were never taught publicly in his days; for the people could not bear the flood of intelligence which God poured into his mind" (JD 5:83-84).

133Truly, the Prophet Joseph Smith suffered a kind of spiritual loneliness, having so much to give and so few of his followers being willing to receive it. It is no wonder that he delighted in those who were willing to receive the truths of heaven, and who could be trusted-his brother Hyrum, Brigham Young, and Heber C. Kimball among the brethren, and several sisters of equal spiritual sensitivity and commitment.

133As indicated above, the Prophet said there were those upon the stand from which he spoke who would want to take his life. Evidently, one of such a mind was William Law. [^1] William Law, four years younger than Joseph Smith, joined the Church in Canada in 1836, received the Melchizedek Priesthood in 1837, and came to Nauvoo in November 1841. Perhaps it is significant that he missed the Kirtland and Missouri periods of Church history-important schooling and testing times for the Saints. In Nauvoo William and his brother Wilson Law became successful in business. They involved themselves in real estate, a store, and a steammill. William was active in Church matters, and defended Joseph Smith against the accusations of John C. Bennett in 1842. He served two missions and was one of the nine persons who first received the temple endowment rites in this dispensation in May 1842. He was called as a counselor in the First Presidency early in 1841 ( D&C 124:91 ). William rejected the revelation on plural marriage in the summer of 1843. Gradually he became more and more disgruntled. He was released from the First Presidency in January 1844 and excommunicated in April, three months later. He became bitter. He claimed his excommunication violated the established order of the Church. He also claimed the introduction of new doctrines had corrupted the Church. The doctrines cited include plural marriage, plurality of Gods, ecclesiastical control over civil and business affairs, and the unconditional sealing up of persons unto eternal life.

133William Law, like so many others past and present who leave the Church but cannot thereafter leave the Church alone, became the leader of a group of dissenters. Elder Dallin H. Oaks and Marvin S. Hill report in their book, Carthage Conspiracy, that

by mid May [1844] a small but determined group of three hundred seceders was holding regular meetings and demanding repeal of the Nauvoo Charter. p.134] To solicit support for their cause, they visited surrounding towns and represented the prophet as "pretty much a rough character, especially in relation to the `spiritual wife' doctrine." One local historian indicated that they "left no opportunity unimproved to influence the public mind to the highest pitch" against Joseph and Hyrum. The anti-Mormons at Warsaw, recognizing allies, worked closely with the dissenters. (Dallin H. Oaks and Marvin S. Hill, Carthage Conspiracy. [Chicago, IL: Univ of Illinois Press, 1975] 13)

134Such cooperation fuelled the already prevalent anti-Mormon sentiment which found an eager voice in local newspapers, notably the Warsaw Signal. But the denunciations and threats levelled at Joseph Smith, other Church leaders, and the Church itself only served to consolidate the Saints. Joseph Smith was arrested on various charges, but quickly released by Nauvoo civil authorities and protected by the Nauvoo Legion. Such actions infuriated the dissenters. They were convinced that loyalty to Joseph Smith existed only because the Saints were not aware of what the dissenters believed were "corrupting doctrines" and "abominable, immoral" practices of the Prophet and other Church leaders. They determined to expose such things by way of a newspaper, anticipating that the information thus supplied would destroy Joseph Smith's influence, in both religion and politics. On 10 May William and Wilson Law, Francis and Chauncey Higbee, Robert and Charles Foster, and Charles Ivans published a prospectus announcing the paper and its purposes. On Friday, 7 June they published the first and only edition of that paper-the Nauvoo Expositor. Mayor Joseph Smith and the Nauvoo City Council met Saturday and Monday in deliberation of the matter.

134Finally, at about 6:30 p.m. on Monday, June 10, the council resolved that the issues of the Nauvoo Expositor and the printing office from whence it issued were "a public nuisanceÂ�and the Mayor is instructed to cause said printing establishment and papers to be removed without delay, in such manner as he shall direct." The mayor promptly ordered the marshal to "destroy the printing press from whence issues the Nauvoo Expositor, and[pi[??] the type of said printing establishment in the street, and burn all the Expositors and libelous handbills found in said establishment." (Dallin H. Oaks, "The Suppression of the Nauvoo Expositor," in Utah Law Review No. 4 [Win 1965] 9:876)

By eight o'clock that evening the deed was done, and events were set in motion that would result in the martyrdom of the Prophet Joseph and Hyrum Smith. The whole of Hancock County was literally "up in arms." The Warsaw Signal called for an attack on p.135] Nauvoo, and "everywhere the old citizens readied for war" (Oaks and Hill, Ibid., p. 16). Tense moments followed, with formal charges being filed against Joseph Smith and several other Mormon leaders, Joseph Smith's declaring Nauvoo to be under martial law, Governor Ford's involvement, and Joseph's and Hyrum's initial fleeing across the river but returning to give themselves up the next day. Then Carthage, and the death of the Prophet and Hyrum at the hands of an angry mob.

135Truly the Nauvoo Expositor was a match that struck dry tinder. But why was the situation so explosive? What led to all the bitterness and anger? It was truth, doctrinal truth revealed from heaven to a prophet of God! Some, including William Law, would not receive that truth. They fought it, and sought to destroy a prophet's work. And by so doing were cut off from the very powers by which they could have received the fullness of salvation.

Conclusion

Truly, the word of God is like a two-edged sword. It is razor sharp and cuts two ways. Like the Spirit of God, it is "sent forth into the world to enlighten the humble and contrite, and to the condemnation of the ungodly" ( D&C 136:33 ). And, surely, the Nauvoo period in Church history vividly affirms this truth.
