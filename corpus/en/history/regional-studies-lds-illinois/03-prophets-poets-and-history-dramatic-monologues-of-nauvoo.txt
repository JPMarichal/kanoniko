Prophets, Poets, and History: Dramatic Monologues of Nauvoo

Roger K. Petersen [^1]

The historian cannot do his work without the dramatist or poet. Nor can the poet do his without the historian. There comes a point where demonstrated facts are inadequate. They provide a necessary groundwork, but the story cannot be told by them alone. For a more complete understanding and feeling, we must put ourselves into the mind of the subject and seek to unlock its secrets. No one should mistake this for documented history. It is a dimension beyond that. However, a "willing suspension of disbelief" can help us feel what, with some probability, might have happened. Of necessity, the poet must use the historian's data, and the perceptive historian must use the poet's technique in assessing his gathered data. And neither historians nor poets always make correct assessments. Perceived facts may change; impressions and perspectives change.

13 Nephi commanded Jacob that he should touch but "lightly" upon the history ( Jacob 1:2 ). When we think of histories that have endured, it is usually because the poet added his insight. After all, the Book of Mormon is first a prophetic work, but second is its poetry. The history is important to the final outcome, but not the quality that makes it endure. Where would Holinshed be without Shakespeare; or Malory without Tennyson?

13The following poems contain six possibilities. I believe they are close to reality; the feelings they evoke may be similar to those of the actual participants. No one can know for sure. In saying this, I make no apology. What is here presented could have happened just the way it's presented.

The Red Handkerchief (ca. 1839) [^2]

Sarah Anne had three questions:

14Why did my daddy die? Why didn't the priesthood heal him? Why couldn't Mother's prayer heal Daddy?

No Priesthood Leader in Commerce could answer, but Sarah's daddy was dead nevertheless.

Joseph was in jail. Sarah didn't dare think she could talk to the Prophet.

Each day she worked on a red, silk handkerchief To learn her needlework.

But her unanswered questions were nettlesome.

14When she completed the handkerchief it was beautiful. She had embroidered it with skill, Swans delicately outlined in threads of dark red silk.

14"I think you should give this handkerchief to the Bishop! For tithing," Sarah's mother said. "I don't want to!" protested Sarah.

But she did.

14Sarah's mother died. Sarah and her sister Liza were raised In a neighbor's house by kind friends, But her daddy and now her mother were dead.

14Years passed. Sarah married and bore six children of her own. She and her husband moved to Saint George. Still her questions nettled.

14In the red brick tabernacle Sarah went to hear Elder Woodruff. From his pocket he took a red handkerchief. "This handkerchief, given to me by Joseph, the Prophet, Is a symbol of faith. We were healing in Montrose. Joseph was to go back over The river to Commerce. A poor man came to him for a blessing on his sick children. The Prophet could not go. To me he gave this red, silk handkerchief with delicately Embroidered swans. `Go, and wipe the faces of these children, and they will be healed,' he said. `And this Handkerchief will always be a league between you and me.'"

Sarah was content.

Orson Hyde-From Olivet's Top, 1841 [^3]

Orson was a Jew. He did not know this During his lifetime. His fraternal grandmother was Polly Wyman Tilton, an Algonquin Indian. She married Heman Hyde, and so, through Zedekiah's son, Orson had the blood tie.

16In Philadelphia, a stranger gave him A bag of Gold and asked to be remembered When the holy mount was dedicated.

16Solomon saw Orson in vision bending low, Praying on the holy mount, the view of all before him.

16For Orson it was as a dream: Layer upon layer: At first light, hunters went Into the wooded hills-young Nimrods with Bows of adamantine strength; streets of Gold. Blood tinged clouds behind the palace of Shem. The city transfigured.

16 Abraham trod up the Mount, laboring From the Hinnom to make the offering, All-the-while looking for the Holy city.

16Layer upon layer: the ancient Jebusites, without Priesthood, fortified with stone. None dared make afraid Joshua skirted it toward Ai leaving ashes Of Jericho, warning: "Never sleep, city Of Jebus, I am always here!"

16Layer upon layer: Joab stole softly, Wormlike, up the "gutter"; subtle, deadly- Gall blown into Jebus' ear. The City of David. "My Lord; let me build a house for Thy name!" But "Your hands are bloody."

The King Follett Sermon-April 1844

I had heard of Joseph Smith. Tales of his magic appeal had long Shrouded the Western Reserve. Now his band had been justifiably forced To the very outskirts of civilization.

So I stopped in Nauvoo.

19On the brow of the hill I could see A mini Parthenon. Marble, glass, and sad-faced sun replicas Shimmered in the afternoon light.

19In a grove beside this building a large crowd had gathered. Most sat on the April grass with an air of expectation. Soon a wagon was emplaced near the crowd, And the Prophet himself mounted The mobile rostrum.

19A Hush beyond silence fell. I could not believe my good fortune; I had chanced upon an Actual sermon to be given by this Satan of the West.

He began: "I wish to go back to the beginning. To the morn of the Creation."

With his first words I was a captive, willing or not.

19He spoke of the great Elohim, as if familiar. Hebrew translations seemed easily within his ken. "What kind of a being is God!" I marveled at his impertinence. But he seemed to know God.

19Every phrase of his mouth was a challenge- Did I know God? The false teachers he so maligned, could I be one? Was my salvation sure?

19Small wonder they sought his life. I could feel the gore of anger rise in my throat. But I was a Christian gentleman, prided in my tolerance. I was no madcap Knight Templar, or western Campbellite bigot. I was of firm family, an American pillar of the congregation Of God, firm in the Covenant of the Cottons.

Then, he railed on:

20"God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted man, and sits Enthroned in yonder heavens!"

For this blasphemy even I thought to kill him!

20So smooth, so enthralling. This was a demon in a velvet vest. Seeing God emerge from the slime of man, like some legless salamander, Walking upright with stubby stumps until he grew a beard; It was intolerable. Yet, this charlatan seemed to know well the holy writ. Even, "the Devil can cite scripture for his purpose," I thought.

20How could there be two atonements? The Father lay down his life? Jesus is the Father. Didn't our Lord cite it so to Philip? ( John 14:9 ) Thus in flesh appearing. The immaculate miracle. What cunning. Appealing only to such flotsam as there assembled.

These devils Gods!

20Strange it was, but the logic was impelling. I could have believed this man. I could have wanted to be a God. His view, so practical. An eternal quest to traverse the universe In indestructible bodies illumined by the burning flame Of Urim, seeing by Thummim. Kings, priests, and Gods in ruling councils. Man could do something. He was not totally dependent on the whims of Grace?

20Of course, that's what he'd forgotten. Paul would shuck him as a spent ear with blight. Kingdom upon kingdom, as if God were collecting marbles. If God were thus, where is the sure foundation? What would keep God from trading off a galaxy or two In a divine whim of fancy.

Still his Hebrew was right. Berosheit, Roch, the head; The HEAD GOD!

20His appeal was brilliant: "I have got the oldest book in the world In my heart, the Holy Ghost." "When we are ready to come to him He is ready to receive us."

Learned doctor of the scriptures that I am, I could not stop the overflow of his magnetism.

21I believe in salvation. I yearn for salvation. But God has never spoken to me as this Gifted magician claims God has spoken to him.

21He is blasphemous. A blasphemer is a witch and must Be burned. He will burn, but will I? My teachers taught me I had the Holy Ghost. What Spirit has now infested me? My Holy Ghost has taught me no such wonders!

21Gods sitting in council- Ps. 82:1 . God a literal Father of men-So many Fathers, Too many to pin it on one passage. But Co-eternal, co-equal to God, HIMSELF!

21It had gone too far. Too much God, and not enough mystery. Men could not know. They are mere spiders dangling o'er the burning pit, Evil Adams conceived in lust. Capable only of sin, wonderful redeeming sin. Else why a wonderful Redeemer?

Would this man dethrone Jesus?

But the "beginning, end, end, beginning?" I was confused.

21If I teach beginning, am I not teaching end? Do I believe a spirit has end? What then of Hell? Does my Hell no longer stand to punish the wicked? If end, then no punishment. If no punishment, no justice. If no justice, no God!

Am I an atheist!

21God the greatest man, emerging among men To raise them up as himself. I could taste that doctrine. It had the honey sweet of eternity. Save the damned dead.

21Aunt Sade went to hell, But I loved her, no matter she was not baptized. How can I think otherwise? If I were to bend that far, I'd break. But I love Aunt Sade.

He would cool the burning lake, Make brimstone ice water. The vilest sinners could come up. Sade yes! Sinners-fornicators, liars, thieves-NO!

22But am I not a liar? I have lied. Cheated? Would God keep me down? Burning in the lake? I am a Christian gentleman.

22I have wanted bloodshed. I have felt murder in my heart. Could an unbaptized child raise evil in my heart?

Oh, I was weary. It had split my brain.

I'd go and think another day.

22"Repent! Repent! Obey the gospel. Turn to God; for your religion won't save you, And you will be damned."

27Emma, will you come West? Nauvoo is still a lovely spot, But these little men Will grow up outside their father's right.

27You know, John, That Heber, Brigham, and I Could not live in the same city, To say nothing of the same house. Eliza might, But she'll do it with terms. No, Joseph is here. Mother Smith is too feeble. Most of her men are here under these sweet pines. It matters not that God left us living; We died with them. That we still breathe and eat is a kindness to these "little men" and Julia, Not to us. I never knew Joseph, really. I carded the wool that became his stockings. I salted the bacon he ate. I bore him sons. But I could only touch the linen cloth That held the rustling gold leaves. When Zebedee and Oliver lay on his arm, Seeing Father Adam and Mother Eve, I lay on his arm to cry. I was in the inner circle Outside everything. Vilate could see, Mary saw. But I kept the house And dusted the holy things. There's one thing! The manuscript. Would you like to see it?

27I dared not ask. Brigham came once and was rebuffed. I'm just a poor physician.

Brigham should have asked more sweetly.

Look how the leaves have yellowed. Why these pins and strings?

28We had nothing else. John, he could neither spell, nor construct A complete sentence. How do you account for that?

28Rigdon, of course, knew the Bible, But he had no vision. Joseph dissolved the veil. When Peter said the "earth shall melt with fervent heat," Joseph felt the burning east wind and saw the bubbling rock flow out And crystalize into a clear Urim and Thummim. Rigdon could only turn pale. He knew truth, Knew the word, Saw it flow out from Joseph in ever-changing nuances; Could capture it on foolscap, But could never be better than a Baruch to Jeremiah.

28I scribed for Joseph for a while. I was a better writer than all them. I was with him at the last September meeting. I waited in the dark, holding the reins. Heard him crashing through the underbrush. I had to go on faith then. He was all the man I'd ever dreamed of. Anything was worth his love. I think I believed. Now it seems silly, my never having seen The plates-only touching them as if in a dream. I'm sure now, if I'd have asked, I'd have seen. He was spinning an incredibly complex tale. Surely he was a genius, At the least. But, then he'd write me love letters, The grammar so chopped, The spelling so amusing, With no sense for the written word. To see him then spin out perfect Bible phrases, I could only grasp. But he was my man.

When did you first read it?

I read the first copy off the press. Night after night I'd heard the tale For months in bits and pieces.

29

I wanted to see it whole. It appeared to be the strangest mixture of Bible and story. Nephi was so real to me; I felt I knew him. He was almost Joseph's exact likeness.

When did you know it was true?

30When I'd read the last page. I knew it came not alone from him. Yet I knew of no one else. It couldn't have been Oliver or David. I was there the whole time. I heard them talking. And certainly it wasn't Reuben or Martin. We didn't even know Rigdon until after it was in print. There was no other way, except the divine. David and I said all along that it Came in line-O-type through the hat. Oliver thought he knew what had happened, But then none of us really knew. Oliver, perhaps, because, By then he'd seen the Angel.

Do you mind if I read this?

I've loved having you here again. Why don't you stay.

Can I make notes?

Take it and do whatever you wish.

31Joseph, Will you come, or Must I alone make my Troubled way? Surely you have seen The settling flesh and Sinking eye of your Bainbridge Bride; and In spite of all- Your golden books and Cherubic angels, your Worlds of unlimited circumference, Whirling delicately Together, creating From the lonely bits Cosmic orders-you Will not forget Today!

31Heber says You have to shut me up, And like an erring demon I must descend into Isaiah's pit Until I learn what Good a tomb is. Is it so? Have you prepared a Parlor in the Burning sheol where I, like some Darkened Beatrice, Must walk alone?

31I realize you may Have cause: I was there when Emily and Eliza And others, like Trailing sparks, were drawn To the comet's head, Magnetized into an eternal entourage, And I suppose The fiery tail Was meant For you, And for me to grasp you Alone was like the Mother Creator Plucking a piece of the galaxy To ornament her dressing table, But you must have known How they would harm me. Will this hold You away?

32Can you forget that Carthage was mine as well As yours? When your cold form Came to Nauvoo Strapped to the rough hewn Boards, my wound only Freshened; and my Then brittle heart Shattered and became a broken Sea of Glass, whirling Icy darts into Vital portions of My universe. In anguish I retched The hellish emetic, And like Jerubbaal's daughters Lay down weeping At the field of bloody sons, Hate poisoning my soul. Can you forget Such sorrow?

32Perhaps You think The children harmed, That I have left them Lonely, in a sour vortex, Circling forever a Polluted star. Please! David will plead for me. My "Sweet Singer." In the lazy afternoons, In his little chamber, I'm sure he scribbled Some pretty defense For your eyes.

34Oh, Joseph, Come! Or are there dark cords Suspending the weak Forever from your Whirling realms? Will you not see this Black speck as it Wings its way- A scurrilous dervish Caught in the Maelstrom?

"Oh Lord, my God!"
