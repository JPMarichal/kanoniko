Ye Are (or Ought To Be) My Friends: Weighed in the Balance by the Prophet Joseph Smith

David F. Boone

Throughout history God has tested and allowed his children to be tested in countless ways. Two of the best known episodes involve Job, who has become a symbol of integrity and patience, and Abraham, who, through his implicit obedience to divine will, became known as the Father of the Faithful.

77 Job was tested physically. He lost his family, his abundant livestock, his servants and property, and he was stricken with boils. Through it all, however, Job maintained his integrity, refused to murmur, and maintained his belief in his relationship with God. As a result of his faithfulness he was not only healed, but the property, which was initially taken from him, was restored double that which he had lost ( Job 42:10-12 ).

77 Abraham was also severely tested. After Abraham was one-hundred years old, Sarah, his wife, bore him a son. This son, Isaac, was to perpetuate a portion of his father's seed ( Gen. 20:12 ). Following the unusual circumstances of bearing a son in their aged condition, God commanded Abraham to sacrifice this long sought for and beloved son, Isaac, as a burnt offering.

77Following the Lord's instructions implicitly, Abraham was strictly obedient until instructed by an angel of the Lord to do otherwise: "Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou anything unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me" ( Gen. 22:12 ).

77Following this test of his obedience and because of his faithfulness, Abraham was blessed by the Lord:

77I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore; and thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies.

78And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice. ( Gen. 22:17-18 )

78Although tested in different ways, Job and Abraham each suffered severely, and both were later rewarded for their obedience. God knew from these experiences that he could rely on these great men to do exactly as he would command.

78The lessons of obedience were not lost on these faithful ancient participants. To the Saints in our own dispensation, who obediently settled in Missouri, but who had illegally lost their homes, land, and property, the Lord said:

78Verily I say unto you, concerning your brethren who have been afflicted, and persecuted, and cast out from the land of their inheritance-

78I, the Lord, have suffered the affliction to come upon them, wherewith they have been afflicted, in consequence of their transgressions;

78Yet I will own them, and they shall be mine in that day when I shall come to make up my jewels.

78Therefore, they must needs be chastened and tried, even as Abraham, who was commanded to offer up his only son.

78For all those who will not endure chastening, but deny me, cannot be sanctified. ( D&C 101:1-5 )

78There are numerous reasons for these tests. A significant part of the purpose of earth life is to experience trials. Trials increase our sensitivity to spiritual things, help us to acknowledge the existence and relevance of God in our lives, and serve as a measuring stick of our own spiritual progress. Elder Bruce R. McConkie taught that "Chastening is both mental and physical. The Lord and his prophets may rebuke and counsel people for their benefit. The Lord may send calamities upon the people to soften their hearts so they will become more receptive to his will." [^1]

78In addition, Elder George Q. Cannon observed that tests, trials, and chastenings are for the sake of the individual rather than for God, who through his omniscience already knows whether or not we will pass the test:

Now, why did the Lord ask such things of Abraham? God did not do this for his own sake; for he knew by His foreknowledge what Abraham would do; but the purpose was to impress upon Abraham a lesson, and to enable him to attain unto knowledge that he could not obtain in any other way. That is why God tries all of us. It is not for His own knowledge, for he knows all things beforehand. He knows all your lives and everything you will do. But He tries us for our own good, that we may know ourselves, for it is most important that a man should know himself. He required Abraham to submit to this trial because He intended to give him glory, exaltation and honor; He intended to make him a king and a priest, to share with Himself the glory, power and dominion which he exercised. [^2]

79God's knowledge beforehand of what we will do or how we will react in no wise undermines or restricts our agency, accountability, or choice. Notwithstanding God's knowledge of his children and the future events they will face, they will, like Christ, learn obedience by the things which they suffer ( Heb. 5:8 ). Elder Bruce R. McConkie observed, "As with the Master, so with the servant: persecution is always the heritage of the faithful. The Lord's ministers in all dispensations have suffered for his name's sake." [^3] Both Job and Abraham, as well as many others who lived and served well, learned obedience. In addition to the obedience they learned, God called them his friends (see John 15:14 ; D&C 84:63 ; 93:45).

79Throughout the scriptures, the Lord consistently established qualifications for those he would ultimately consider his friends. Abraham was a friend of God ( James 2:23 ). From the qualifications indicated, Adam, Enoch, Noah, Moses, Christ, and, in our dispensation, the Prophet Joseph Smith could likewise be considered as the["Friend[s] of God" ( D&C 93:45 ). The list could also include many others. To be a friend of God requires obedience, righteous living, complete adherence to the commandments, and a desire to be a servant. When we view them in retrospect, we find that those who became the greatest leaders first learned to become the most ardent and obedient followers.

79We, too, can become the friends of God. To do so we must be tried, tested, refined, and found totally obedient. In most cases, trials or purifying experiences may be expected, prepared for and learned from. Obedient children of God know that trials must come into peoples' lives to determine the level of their commitment and their willingness to follow the Lord's counsel.

Additional scriptural examples such as Naaman, David, Saul, Peter, Paul, and the perfect example of Christ could be cited, but valuable examples can also be found in the Church's recent past. Following the Prophet Joseph Smith's innocent declaration that he had seen the Father and the Son in the spring of 1820, he was persecuted by those who, he says, "ought to have been my friends and to have treated me kindly" ( JS-H 1:28 ). From these experiences of ridicule and abuse, the Prophet began to learn a valuable lesson that was enlarged upon throughout the remainder of his life. The Prophet learned that some individuals have unrighteous motives; they would take advantage of him, or the Church. As a part of the young prophet's experiences with Moroni, in an annual stewardship interview, Joseph learned "That God had a work for me to do; and that my name should be had for good and evil among all nations, kindreds, and tongues, or that it should be both good and evil spoken of among all people" ( JS-H 1:33 ).

80From his tutoring and in preparation for the spiritual greatness which was to come, the Prophet learned that few people could be trusted, and still fewer could be trusted with implicit confidence. Even within his own family-the greatest support network the Prophet had, often his only haven from the abuse and ridicule of the world-the Prophet met with opposition: ultimately one of his own brothers turned against him.

80As the Prophet learned from various experiences, he became more wary of those individuals outside of his immediate circle of friends. Despite his carefulness, it was from among those "who ought to have been [his] friends" that turned against him secretly and eventually brought about his death. The Prophet Joseph learned to try his friends, to test their resolve and litmus their loyalty. Those thus tried, refined, and found loyal were usually true friends and ultimately became of great value to the kingdom. Those who failed the test, on the other hand, were exposed-to the great benefit of the Prophet, the Church, and others.

80Today, as then, those who cannot endure the small storms will never be able to weather the fiercer blasts. The tests that the Prophet Joseph subjected his constituents to were nothing more than what he had been exposed to himself, yet they were custom-made for the time, the individual, and the circumstances.

80President Harold B. Lee has taught that "The most important of all the commandments in the gospel to you and to me, is that particular commandment which for the moment requires in each of us the greatest soul-searching to obey." [^4] When we have mastered that commandment, the most important commandment is the next one that we are presently having the most trouble living.

Abraham's test was uniquely suited to him. Likewise, our trials will be uniquely suited to us. Abraham's test was to sacrifice the life of his son. If each of us were to be tried by that same test, some of us would never be tried because some of us don't have children. The test, then, is uniquely fitted to the individual; otherwise, it would lose much of its significance. To some, the spiritual loss or death of a child, spouse, or parent would be the ultimate trial. To others, like pioneer families, the death of a loved one was an anticipated, expected reality and was not as significant and poignant as some other test might have been. Lucy H. W. Flake lost several children in her efforts to help colonize the West, and her loss was very poignant, but she and others learned that, for the women of her day, plural marriage was the severest trial they would ever have to endure:

81Brother Lyman came and Brother Stevenson came also. Brother Stevenson preached in Beaver a powerful sermon on Polygamy and retrenchment in the Sisters' Hall twenty-six years ago. That sermon gave me great comfort. It was a healing balm to my soul. He said polygamy is the greatest trial our Heavenly Father has in store for his[daughter[s]. [^5]

81For many of us today, the loss of a child or a spouse would likely be the severest trial. For others, offering one's own life for a cause, or having one's name or reputation blackened would be harder.

81The Lord, through his prophet, tested Joseph's friends both in groups and as individuals-he tested them doctrinally, emotionally, financially, politically, and socially. He tested them where they worked, where they lived, by their traditions, by their preferences, by their likes or dislikes, and in most every other aspect of their lives. Some remained faithful, becoming stronger through the experience, while others turned, in their exposed weaknesses, against the Prophet as if he was guilty of the weakness that he had exposed in their characters. In many cases those who were closest to the Prophet, and should have been his closest friends and greatest allies, turned out to be his greatest enemies and the most destructive to the work of the Lord. Frequently we emphasize the successes of those who remained faithful, while those who failed are remembered only as they continued to fight against the cause they formerly championed. Even many of those associated with the organization of the Church, who showed the greatest promise and gave initial support, found themselves on the outside looking in. A few brief but poignant examples are outlined below.

Oliver Cowdery

Oliver Cowdery was an important participant in the restoration of the gospel in this dispensation. He participated in the restoration of priesthood authority (D&C 13:18), he stood as a witness of the organization of the Church ( D&C 20 -21), and he was ordained as the Second Elder of that organization ( JS-H 1:72 ). He was present when priesthood keys were restored from heavenly ministrants and translated beings ( D&C 110 ), he was called as the Assistant or Associate President of the Church, second only to the Prophet Joseph ( D&C 124:94-95 ). By revelation he was appointed to choose and assist in choosing the original Quorum of the Twelve Apostles (D&C 18:107), and he entertained angels and participated in many other important events. Yet, unbelievable as it seems, he later distanced himself from all of his earlier accomplishments because of pride and unrighteousness.

82Notwithstanding his early experiences, his endowment of power, his position and prominence in the Church, he commenced to find fault with Church leaders. When he began to encourage "vexatious lawsuits against them and thus distressing the innocent, [and] seeking to destroy the character of President Joseph Smith, he was subsequently (April 11, 1838) excommunicated from the Church." [^6]

82A scenario similar to Oliver Cowdery's could likewise be written for the other two witnesses who accompanied him when they viewed the plates and the angel. The same could be written, with only slight variation, about the majority of the eight Book of Mormon witnesses, about the counselors in the Church's initial First Presidency (activated in 1832-33), about subsequent counselors, about the majority of the original Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, and about members of the Quorum of the Seventy selected in 1835. Furthermore, the same pattern of not remaining faithful following trials and adversity could also be written about many other members of the Church-now as well as then.

The common point among the three witnesses, the eight witnesses, and other early Church leaders is that, despite their prominence, experiences, and even their friendship, most in time fell, and, ultimately, opposed the Prophet and Church. Therefore, despite their previous prominence, the Prophet apparently felt obliged to try "his friends" as a barometer of their faithfulness to the Church. Further, having learned early of the value of opposition in his own life, the Prophet's tests were the catalyst for growth in the lives of others. Some of the leaders retained their balance, some only wavered, still others fell very hard, but the time of their departure from the Church shows some interesting similarities. The spiritual decline of many of the original members of the Quorum of the Twelve and other Church leaders can be associated with the Prophet's financial involvement and the general speculation in Kirtland, Ohio.

83The apostasy of such prominent and promising individuals in this dispensation should not be surprising when we remember that one of our Lord's chosen Twelve, Judas Iscariot, likewise failed to remain faithful. The Prophet Joseph taught of him: "Judas was rebuked and immediately betrayed his Lord into the hands of His enemies, because Satan entered into him." [^7]

83The reasons for the dissent of the apostles during the life of the Prophet Joseph Smith are numerous and varied. They range from a personal dislike of the Prophet, which sprang from the way he acted or conducted his divinely appointed responsibilities, to an individual apostasy from the doctrines he taught.

83In the final analysis, however, the result was the same: those who apostatized could not stand the heat of the refiner's fire and were ultimately left far enough behind that they were unable to learn or be blessed by its warmth. The fact that some of those who apostatized acknowledged the error of their ways, sought forgiveness, repented, and finally returned to membership in the Church, is of credit to them individually.

83The lessons of personal alienation, from the Church generally and the Prophet specifically, and the ultimate apostasy from former doctrines have been often repeated throughout the relatively short era of Mormon history. Today we witness individuals caught in the same cycle. Both the frequency and principles of personal apostasy apply in the same way that the mistakes of childhood, overcome and reconciled, combine to make a man what he is in spite of his youthful mistakes.

83Of greater value to us today are the lessons we learn from those who passed the test, who weathered the worst, and who continued steadfast. Their legacy remains undaunted and their prominence undimmed. We do not remember with any degree of pride or appreciation those who betrayed the Prophet or the cause he championed, but rather those who died loyal to the cause and who remained firm in the faith.

As a result of his early experiences, the Prophet sought to encircle himself with individuals whom he could trust, and who loved the work as he loved it, and who would give themselves for it as he had and would ultimately give himself for it. Numerous accounts are available of the great sacrifices made by individuals for the cause of Christ. One such individual was Dan Jones.

Dan Jones

Dan Jones was a small, sea-loving Welshman who first became acquainted with the Prophet when the Saints were in Nauvoo. He and the Prophet Joseph became co-owners of the Mississippi steamboat, Maid of Iowa. Dan Jones' total commitment and complete loyalty endeared him to the Church leaders generally, and to the Prophet specifically. On one occasion the Prophet determined to test the Welshman's loyalty in an unusual, if not harsh, manner. He went to the wharf where Dan was working on their boat and convincingly portrayed himself in the part of a drunkard. The Prophet never referred to the incident in his own writings, but Dan Jones related the encounter in great detail. Only brief glimpses of the incident are recorded here.

84Upon his arrival at the docks the Prophet found Captain Dan Jones busy at work on the deck of their boat. With appropriate staggering, slurs, repetition of phrases, hiccuping and other signs of obvious inebriation, the Prophet attempted to board the Maid of Iowa.

84Boat ahoy, Hallo Come and help me aboard Captain, for I'm afraid to fall off that plank into the river. Now hold fast, steady there all safe. Now Captain [Jones] you see I'm a leedl boozy tonight, been drinking a leedl wine with a friend; but what of that I'm a Prophet if I am drunk; that I am. Well look here Captain, you hold my note, don't you? Well I have just called to tell you that I don't mean to pay you a cent of it, that I wont. Now ain't I an honest man to tell you so? I tell you I never mean to pay you a cent, there now help yourself.

84You may think I am not a Prophet but I am a Prophet if I am drunk. There I told you what I came for, I wont pay a cent that's all. Now help me ashore again, I know I'm a Prophet, don't push me off the plank, or I'll be a fallen Prophet, if not a drowned Prophet, Ha, Ha, there ashore safe let me go sue, sue away, I tell you I don't care, good night. [^8]

84The Prophet staggered up the bank and away from the wharf toward his home until he met Dr. Willard Richards. After a warm greeting between the Prophet and his personal secretary and friend, the Prophet, very much sober, said,

85"Excuse me, Dr. Richards I have played such a joke just now, I am afraid I'll split my sides laughing; I must tell it you. I have acted the drunken man so natural aboard that Boat that I have made the Captain believe I was really drunk, ha, ha, for he looked as sober as a Judge. Suppose you call on him by and bye, and quiz out of him what he thinks of it; he is an honest man I believe, and if I cant shake him off me, I will make a man of him, let me hear again."

85"Shall I leave him ignorant of the joke?" asked Brother Richards.

85The Prophet responded, "If he stumbles at it [the test] you may, but if not you may let him have the benefit of it too."

85Following the instructions of the Prophet, Elder Richards played the part of a sympathetic friend concerned for the welfare of his inebriated associate:

85"Good night Captain," "Good night Doctor, step aboard." [Richards] [e]nters, puts on a grave face, and draws a long sigh, "Have you seen the Prophet about this evening?"

85"He was here about an hour ago."

85"I hear that he has been drinking again! What a pity that such a good man gives way to drink so-great pity. Wonder they let him go about the streets to expose himself; was he very drunk Captain?"

85"He had his three sheets in the wind or thereabouts."

85"Well what do you think of it?"

85"All I think of it is that if he drinks until doom's day, he can't drown that truth which is in him, nor the little that is in me neither. Tis true that I would rather have a sober Prophet, but then if we can't get a sober one, a drunken Prophet is better than no Prophet at all, so I will hold on to the one we have got, drunken or sober. That's what I think to do Doctor."

85"Ha, Ha you will not be driven to that[Cap[tain]; tis all a joke; the Prophet is as sober as a judge only weighing you."

85"So much the better if any difference, although, every body mind his own business is my motto." [^9]

Marvelous lessons can be learned from individual experiences, but the proof of good men is found in the scriptural injunction, "By their fruits, ye shall know them" ( Matt. 7:20 ; 3 Ne. 14:20 ). Dan Jones, having been "weighed in the balance," was found faithful. Following this episode with the Prophet, Jones accompanied him and others to Carthage, Illinois, and was with the Prophet the last night before his martyrdom. During a disruption, the Prophet got off the bed where he was resting and lay on the floor between John S. Fullmer and Dan Jones and spoke to each of them in turn. He asked Dan if he was afraid to die. Dan answered, "Has that time come think you? Engaged in such a cause [as the gospel] I do not think that death would have many terrors." The Prophet Joseph prophesied, "You will yet see Wales, and fulfill the mission appointed you before you die." [^10] Before the year 1844 was over Dan Jones was enroute with a prominent entourage of other Church officials toward the British Isles, not only fulfilling the prophecy but further proving his own loyalty to the slain Prophet and the cause he died for.

86Later Dan served a second mission and is generally credited with more than 6,000 convert baptisms, the translation of numerous missionary tracts into the Welsh language, and leading the emigration of many of his converts to the Great Basin. After their arrival in the City of the Great Salt Lake, some of those Welsh converts became the nucleus of the world-renowned Mormon Tabernacle Choir. Dan Jones has a long list of accomplishments, most importantly being a strong proponent of the restored gospel. He died firm in that conviction and is buried in the Provo City Cemetery.

Edwin D. Woolley

A contemporary of Dan Jones, and another prominent supporter of the Prophet Joseph, was Edwin D. Woolley. Like Dan Jones, Brother Woolley was a convert to the Church. Brother Woolley was the proprietor of a dry-goods outlet "located on Knight and Partridge streets about two blocks west of the temple site and at the bottom of the hill" in Nauvoo, Illinois. [^11]

86The Prophet, with deep feeling, said to Brother Woolley, "Take your goods, replace them on your shelves and go on with your business." [^12] Woolley had passed his test and, because of his determination to remain steadfast, he accompanied the Saints to the Salt Lake Valley. There he became a bishop and helped to mold the life of future Church president and prophet Heber J. Grant, General Authority B. H. Roberts, and others.

Further, Edwin Woolley was the grandfather of a future prophet, Spencer Woolley Kimball, who exhibited many of the same characteristics of obedience and virtue exhibited by Bishop Edwin Woolley.

Brigham Young

Another contemporary of Bishop Woolley and the Prophet Joseph Smith was Brigham Young. Brigham Young was perhaps as close to the Prophet Joseph Smith during his prophetic ministry as any man except the Prophet's own brother, Hyrum. Brigham Young exclaimed,

87Who can say aught against Joseph Smith? I do not think that a man lives on earth that knew him any better than I did, and I am bold to say that, Jesus Christ excepted, no better man ever lived or does live upon the earth. I feel like shouting Hallelujah all the time, when I think that I ever knew Joseph Smith, the Prophet. [^13]

87Despite their closeness and shared purpose, Brigham was not exempt from being tested of the Lord through the Prophet Joseph. In fact, he may have had more testing than his share because history suggests that the Prophet Joseph knew of Brigham's future prominence. "Brigham Young was once tried to the very utmost by the Prophet, and for a moment his standing in the Church seemed to tremble in the balance." [^14]

87Apparently, as a senior member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, Brigham was asked to prefer charges against a member of his own quorum, William Smith, who was also a brother of the Prophet Joseph. Although reluctant, Brigham was obedient.

87Before the time set for the trial, however, Emma Smith talked to Joseph and said the charge preferred against William was with a view of injuring the Smith family. After the trial had begun, Joseph entered the room and was given a seat. The testimony of witnesses concerning the culprit's sins was then continued. After a short time Joseph arose filled with wrath and said, "Brother Brigham, I will not listen to this abuse of my family a minute longer. I will wade in blood up to my knees before I will do it." [^15]

President Lorenzo Snow, who many years later remembered this experience, observed, "This was a supreme moment. A rupture between the two greatest men on earth seemed imminent." [^16] Elder Young had several choices. He could have defended himself against the charges, he could have excused himself by indicating that he was only doing what he was told, or he could have found fault with or retaliated against Joseph. However, Brigham did none of the above. What he did accentuates the character of a man of God. He arose to his feet and in a solemn voice asked, "Joseph, what do you want me to do?" [^17] Elder Snow further said that Elder Young replied, "`Brother Joseph, I withdraw the charges.' Thus the angry passions were instantly stilled." [^18]

88President Young was a dynamic successor to the Prophet Joseph Smith. He had passed a supreme test, and, having learned a valuable lesson, likewise used the principle of testing and refinement many years later to prove others. Elder Snow, who was also called one day as president of the Church and had been tutored by both the Prophet and President Young, observed, "Everyone of us who has not already had the experience must yet meet it of being tested in every place we are weak, and even our lives must be laid on the altar." [^19] President Young had experiences sufficient to know whereof he spoke, since personally he had to do exactly that.

Heber C. Kimball

Another close associate to the Prophet Joseph and a friend of President Brigham Young was Heber C. Kimball. Heber C. Kimball was another individual who laid his life, and everything else, upon the altar of God. Elsewhere recounted in this volume are his experiences of being asked by the Prophet Joseph to secretly give his wife to the Prophet in marriage (see "Doctrinal Teachings in Nauvoo: A Two-Edged Sword" by Larry E. Dahl). Rehearsed, too, is Elder Kimball's anguish of heart and spirit; he wanted to be obedient, but for a time remained unsettled by so great a sacrifice. Ultimately he did precisely what he was asked to do, irrespective of his own wishes or personal interests.

88Likewise recounted are the rewards for this absolute obedience:

88It was enough-the heavens accepted the sacrifice. The will for the deed was taken, and "accounted unto him for righteousness." Joseph wept at this proof of devotion, and embracing Heber, told him that was all that the Lord required. He had proved him, as a child of Abraham, that he would "do the works of Abraham," holding back nothing but laying all upon the altar for God's glory.

88The prophet joined the hands of the heroic and devoted pair, and then and there, by virtue of the sealing power and authority of the Holy Priesthood, Heber and Vilate Kimball were made husband and wife for all eternity. [^20]

89As a result of his faithfulness, Elder Heber C. Kimball became a trusted apostle, a confidant not only of the Prophet Joseph but likewise a counselor to President Brigham Young. According to one author, "No man of this generation with the exception of the Prophet Joseph Smith possessed in greater degree the gift of prophecy than did Heber C. Kimball." [^21]

Edwin Rushton

Edwin Rushton, a new convert to the Church, remembered landing in Nauvoo in 1843, via the Mississippi River, after emigrating from Great Britain. Being in a hurry to find members of his family already settled there, Rushton recalls being accosted by an unkempt man in rough clothes riding a beautiful black horse. Rushton was asked by the stranger, "Hey, Bub, is that a company of Mormons just landed?" Rushton answered in the affirmative, further admitting that he too was Mormon. Then, to a question from the stranger respecting what he knew about "old Joe Smith," Rushton responded, "I know that Joseph Smith is a Prophet of God."

89The stranger replied, "I suppose you are looking for an old man with a long gray beard. What would you think if I told you I was Joseph Smith?"

89Edwin Rushton responded confidently, "If you are Joseph Smith, I know you are a Prophet of God!"

89"In a gentle voice, the man explained, `I am Joseph Smith. I came to meet these peopleÂ�to see if their faith is strong enough to stand the things they must meet. If not, they should turn back now.'" [^22]

89There are many other experiences in which individuals have faced severe tests of their faith, their resolve, and even physical injury or loss of life, and have been victorious. There are the accounts of Dennison L. Harris and Robert Scott, boys who bravely assisted in uncovering a plot among local apostates to overthrow the Church, undermine the Prophet, and even kill him. It took great courage to be obedient, but they were, and for a time the murderous intent of the apostates was brought to light and thwarted. [^23]

Willard Richards

When Willard Richards was asked by the Prophet if he and Hyrum would go to another cell while incarcerated at Carthage Jail, as encouraged by the jailor, he replied,

90Brother Joseph, you did not ask me to cross the river with you-you did not ask me to come to Carthage with you-you did not ask me to come to jail with you-and do you think I would forsake you now? But I will tell you what I will do; if you are condemned to be hung for treason, I will be hung in your stead, and you shall go free.

90Joseph responded, "You cannot!" The doctor replied, "I will!"

90At that point in time Willard did not know he would not have to lose his life with the Prophet in Carthage, yet he was willing to give everything he had in behalf of the Lord's anointed. [^24]

Ezra T. Benson

President Heber J. Grant remembered a "mean trick" that President Brigham Young played on Ezra T. Benson, a new member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. He had nearly finished a new and very nice home in Salt Lake City. President Grant remembered it as the nicest house in the city except for President Brigham Young's. "It was a beautiful, two-story home with a porch at both levels, a white picket fence, and a yard filled with fruit and ornamental trees and even a small stream." [^25]

90Just as Elder Benson was preparing to move his family from the log cabin home, where they had lived, into this long-anticipated new home, he was called into President Young's office. President Young asked Elder Benson to move his family instead to Cache Valley, where they would once again be subjected to the trials of the pioneer existence. Further, he recommended that Elder Benson sell his home to President Young's second counselor, Daniel H. Wells.

90Little is known of the inner turmoil experienced by Elder Benson in making his decision. Also, little is known of how he explained to his family that their long-awaited dream home would be the home of another. What is known is that Elder Ezra T. Benson sold his home and moved, as he had been instructed, to Cache Valley to assist in and oversee pioneering in that area:

91Mean trick or not, Ezra T's leadership proved most effective. During the nine years under his direction, the settlements in Cache Valley prospered. He organized the building of homes, mills, schools, canals, and churches; fostered the development of cooperative institutions,Â�occupied a seat in the territorial legislature; acted as brigadier general of the Cache Valley militia; and served as spiritual leader to the Saints in that area. [^26]

91What if Elder Benson had failed to follow counsel? Not only would the foregoing accomplishment have gone unrealized or at least severely delayed, but what about the faith of Elder Benson himself? What would have become of his legacy of faith which is so clearly exhibited by his lifetime of service? What about his family and the generations then unborn? What about the thirteenth Church President who carried his name and presided over the Lord's Church? We can all be appreciative of the dedication and obedience and faith of these early Saints because their lives affect our own on a daily basis.

Calls in Preparation For Leadership

Those leaders who succeeded the Prophet Joseph in Church leadership apparently learned well the lesson of trial and testing. During subsequent administrations Presidents Young, Heber C. Kimball, John Taylor and others used the same techniques by which they were proven to test the faithfulness of other individuals with whom they associated and interacted.

91The lesson continues to our day. With what will we be tested? We will be tested in that principle, teaching, or practice that will exact of us appropriate to our need for growth and eternal development. Just as the Lord proved the Saints of Missouri and Nauvoo and those in earlier dispensations, the Lord will prove the Saints of today. Through our trials we must learn obedience and as we do we will be better prepared to serve in whatever capacity we may be called upon. The Prophet Joseph taught, "A religion that does not require the sacrifice of all things never has power sufficient to produce the faith necessary unto life and salvation." [^27]

91When Hugh B. Brown was called as a General Authority, he had an excruciating experience in a struggle between the powers of light and darkness which both got his attention and deeply impressed him for the rest of his life. He was a businessman and according to his own account, had become very successful. He went home, very disturbed spiritually, and went to bed without dinner.

92All night long I wrestled with the evil spirit. I was possessed with the spirit of wishing that I could be rubbed out of existence. I had no thought of suicide, but wished the Lord would provide a way for me to cease to be. The room was full of darkness, and an evil spirit prevailed, so real that I was almost consumed by it. [^28]

92After spending a majority of the night on his knees in prayer, he finally received deliverance from the evil spirit and later on that same day received a telephone call from President David O. McKay.

92Upon going to the phone, I heard a voice which said, "This is David O. McKay calling. The Lord wants you to spend the balance of your life in the service of the Church. The Council of the Twelve just voted that you shouldÂ�become an Assistant to the Twelve." [^29]

92Later, in relating this experience to President McKay, he was informed that each of the apostles, upon being called to the Quorum of the Twelve, had a similar kind of refining experience. This was likewise confirmed by President Harold B. Lee, who said, "I began to realize that all must be tested and tried and that there is a certain refinement that is necessary before one qualifies to the highest station to which the Lord would have him called." [^30]

Our Test Today

All of us will experience trials and opposition; the plan of salvation requires it, and our post-mortal existence depends on it. Opposition and the refining process are integral to our progression. Jesus Christ set the example. President Brigham Young taught:

92You all know that the saints must be made pure, to enter into the Celestial Kingdom. It is recorded that Jesus was made perfect through suffering. If he was made perfect through suffering, why should we imagine for one moment that we can be prepared to enter into the Kingdom of rest with him and the Father, without passing through similar ordeals? [^31]

92Many remain complacent and believe that it could never happen to them or that, if it did, they would remain faithful regardless of their trial. Elder Heber C. Kimball noted, by prophetic voice, what would come in the future:

The time is coming when we will be mixed up in these now peaceful valleys to that extent that it will be difficult to tell the face of a saint from the face of an enemy to the people of God. Look out for the great sieve, for there will come a great sifting time and many will fall. For I say unto you there is a test, there is a test. There is a test coming and who will be able to stand? You imagine that you what would have stood by the Prophet Joseph Smith, for many have said they would have liked to have been associated with him. If you would have stood by him when persecution raged and he was assailed by foes within and without, you would have defended him and been true to him in the[mi[d]st of every trial. You think you would have been delighted to have shown your integrity in the days of mobs and traitors. Let me say to you, that many of you will see the time when you will have all the trouble, trial and persecution that you can stand and plenty of opportunities to show that you are true to God and his way. This Church has before it many close places through which you will have to pass, before the work of God is crowned with victory. [^32]

93What are we to do to prepare for such a time? When it does come, how many Latter-day Saints will respond appropriately to the trials and afflictions? Elder Spencer W. Kimball, quoting from the previous reference, said, "I believe it was from this very pulpit that my grandfather presented a sermon in which he said, `The test, the test, the test is coming.' Well the test is here!" [^33] Elder Angel Abrea explained,

93Tribulation, afflictions, and trials will constantly be with us in our sojourn here in this segment of eternity, just as the Savior said, "In the world ye shall have tribulation" ( John 16:33 ). Therefore, the great challenge in this earthly life is not to determine how to escape the afflictions and problems, but rather to carefully prepare ourselves to meet them. [^34]

93President Harold B. Lee observed that opposition is a sign of discontent on the part of the adversary. He explained,

93We must expect opposition, and sometimes that opposition may come from inside, but remember what the Prophet Joseph Smith, our early leader, said: "The nearer a person approaches the Lord, the greater the power will be manifested by the Adversary to prevent the accomplishment of his purposes." One of the brethren, President John Taylor, said he heard the Prophet say, "You have all kinds of trials to pass through, and it is quite as necessary for you to be tried even as Abraham, and other men of God," and, said he, "God will feel after you, he will take hold of you and wrench your very heartstrings, and if you cannot stand it you will not be fit for an inheritance in the Kingdom of God." [^35]

93Further, the Lord admonished the Prophet Joseph and the Saints of His Church, "Behold, it is my will, that all they who call on my name, and worship me according to mine everlasting gospel, should gather together, and stand in holy places" ( D&C 101:22 ). Elder Heber C. Kimball likewise gave several safeguards of preparation for those trials which the Saints of the last days would face:

94To meet the difficulties that are coming it will be necessary for you to have a knowledge of the truth of the work for yourselves. The difficulties will be of such a character that the man or woman who does not possess this personal knowledge or witness will fall. If you have not got the testimony, live right and call upon the Lord and cease not until you obtain it. If you do not you will not stand. The time will come when no man nor woman will be able to endure on borrowed light. Each will have to be guided by the light within himself. [^36]

94Finally, as the Lord observed to the incarcerated Prophet Joseph Smith, sometimes all we can do is to endure: "Thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment; and then, if thou endure it well, God shall exalt thee on high; thou shalt triumph over all thy foesÂ�. Thou art not yet as Job; thy friends do not contend against thee, neither charge thee with transgression, as they did Job" ( D&C 121:7-8, 10 ). As it was with Job and with the Prophet Joseph Smith, so shall it be for all of us if we are tried as was Abraham and remain faithful.
