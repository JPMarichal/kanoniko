Notes

Speaking of these remarkable scenes, the Prophet writes: "To find ourselves engaged in the very same order of things as observed by the holy apostles of old; to realize the importance and solemnity of such proceedings; and to witness and feel with our own natural senses, the like glorious manifestations of the powers of the Priesthood, the gifts and blessings of the Holy Ghost, and the goodness and condescension of a merciful God unto such as obey the everlasting Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, combined to create within us sensations of rapturous gratitude, and inspire us with fresh zeal and energy in the cause of truth."

Eleven converts were baptized at the close of this conference, by David Whitmer, in Seneca Lake. Among them were Jerusha Smith, wife of Hyrum Smith and his brothers William and Don Carlos and his sister Catherine.

The next day the trial began before three justices. The most able help had been secured to prosecute the case while the defense was again represented by Esquires Reid and Davidson. Again many witnesses were called who bore false testimony which was contradictory. Newel Knight was placed on the witness stand and questioned in ridicule by one of the lawyers, named Seymour, in relation to the casting out of a devil from his person, but the testimony turned to the discomfiture of the prosecution. This account can be found in the Documentary History Vol, 1:89-93. It makes interesting reading.

At the close of the testimony the court deliberated for about thirty minutes, although it was then nearly two o'clock in the morning, and they had been in session since the morning of the previous day. The prisoner was brought before the court where the presiding judge said "Mr. Smith, we have had your case under consideration, examined the testimony and find nothing to condemn you, and therefore you are discharged." The judges then proceeded to reprimand him severly. "Not because anything derogatory to his character in any shape had been proved against him by the host of witnesses that had testified during the trial," said Mr. Reid, "but merely to please those fiends in human shape who were engaged in the unhallowed persecution of an innocent man, sheerly on account of his religious opinions."

Several years later, Mr. Reid visited Nauvoo, and in the course of an address alluded to this trial and said at the time Joseph Knight came to request him to go and defend the Prophet, he was impressed with the thought: "I must go and defend him, for he was the Lord's anointed." Mr. Reid's speech, which is interesting indeed, may be found in a footnote in the Documentary History, Vol. 1:94-96. It was first published in the Times and Seasons, Vol. 5:549-552. It would be very interesting, if time will permit, to have some member of the group read this testimony to the entire group.

This spirit of opposition which took such a decided form, was the result of agitation on the part of professors of religion. The Rev. Shearer, Cyrus McMaster, Dr. Boyington and a Mr. Benton, pillars in the Presbyterian Church, were the leaders in exciting the mob spirit. Benton was the man who signed the first warrant for Joseph Smith's arrest as "a disorderly person" for preaching the Book of Mormon. In this manner Satan stirred up the hearts of the people to extreme bitterness in his endeavor to overthrow the work.

"Thus was our faith much strengthened concerning dreams and visions in the last days, foretold by the ancient Prophet Joel; and although we this time were forced to seek safety from our enemies by flight, yet we did feel confident that eventually we should come off victorious, if we only continued faithful to him who had called us forth from the darkness into the marvelous light of the everlasting Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ."
