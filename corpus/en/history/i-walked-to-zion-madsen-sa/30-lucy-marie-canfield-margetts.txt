Susan Arrington Madsen, I Walked to Zion: True Stories of Young Pioneers on the Mormon Trail ( 1994)

128Lucy Marie Canfield (Margetts)

128Born: November 7, 1846, Ossian, Livingston, New York

128Parents: Isaac Augustus and Pauline Melissa Smith Canfield

1291862: Isaac A. Canfield Company

129Age at time of journey: 15

129My father, cousin Rose Canfield, and myself left Livingston County, New York, in the spring of 1862 for Salt Lake City. We traveled by rail to Iowa, where we stayed a couple of weeks and got a team of oxen and a wagon. We traveled 150 miles across Iowa by team and stopped in Florence, Nebraska, six miles upriver from Council Bluffs, Iowa. Ours was an independent company, and Father was the captain. We had eighteen wagons in all.

129July 2nd. We went down to the boat before breakfast. Al West came and brought us some perfumery.

129July 4th. Brigham Kimball brought us a mule to ride. Sewed some on the wagon cover.

129July 7th. We had a very hard thunderstorm. Joseph Young was badly hurt.

129July 8th. It is a pleasant day. Alvin West and I gathered some wormwood to use for Mr. Young. He is quite bad. Our wagon cover has been repaired.

129July 11th. Al went to Omaha and bought Rose and me each a fan.

129July 21st. It is a very pleasant day. My poor cousin Rose is so very homesick. Hattie and I try to cheer her.

129July 22nd. Brigham Kimball sent Rose a circle comb to cheer her.

129July 23rd. Worked on another wagon cover all day.

129July 25th. Rose got all ready to go back home, but her heart failed her.

129July 27th. Rained last night. Dansie Stickney and I stayed in their tent.

129July 30th. Some boys from Ogden, Utah, camped near us. Al was up twice today. His company is about two miles away.

129August 2nd. Traveled about eight miles. Broke an axle tree and had to stop.

129August 10th. It has not seemed much like Sunday today. We traveled about 15 1/2 miles with no stopping to rest. Camped at Lone Tree, pleasant place.

129August 12th. Had a ride with Mrs. Neil about five miles. Crossed Wood River, and had an awful night with the mosquitoes. Got up about midnight and slept on the ground. Do not like Wood River camping very much.

129August 23rd. Saw some Indians on horse back. Gave them flour and meat.

130Sept. 1st. Made seventeen miles and camped opposite Chimney Rock, Nebraska. 'Tis a wild and romantic country round this rock. There is a stage station near it.

130Sept. 4th. Traveled about nine miles. Roasted a duck for our supper.

130Sept. 9th. Discovered a dead Indian in a tree this morning. Saw two squaws.

130Sept. 19th. Some of the cattle missing. Did not find them until afternoon. Made another buffalo pie.

130Sept. 25th. Traveled ten miles before breakfast. Rose and I slept until we got almost to a camping place. Mr. Merrick shot and the men dressed an antelope. We cooked some over our sagebrush fire.

130Sept. 28th. Traveled ten miles. Camped near a military station. The soldiers invited some of us to take dinner with them.

130Oct. 8th. Went over to the military station. Saw there a tame bear. Joseph West sent us some peaches by Mr. Dickson's brother-in-law.

130Oct. 10th. Camped about noon. One of Mr. Elton's mules died. It seemed so bad, for he thought so much of it. Had some soup for our supper, and it was first rate.

130Oct. 11th. Took dinner at Cache Cave. Quite a cave. Mrs. Blackman's wagon tongue broke and we camped about 5 p.m., opposite some high bluffs. Went on top, and the boys rolled stones down them. Camped in Echo Canyon, Utah.

130Oct. 13th. A beautiful morning. Were ready to start out and found some cattle gone. Our company separated, and we went down the Weber Canyon. We crossed the Weber River five times. Bad roads and bad going. Camped in a valley surrounded by mountains.

130Oct. 14th. A very pleasant day. Got started at half past eight o'clock. Most romantic road we have yet had. Would not have missed coming this way for anything. I never saw such rocks, bigger and prettier in shapes than all we saw in Echo Canyon. Camped at Mr. Merrill's. They gave us some butter.

131Oct. 15th. Started this morning in quite good season. Forded the Weber three times. Stopped at Mrs. Kempton's and took dinner. Had some peaches to eat and a first-rate dinner. Liked Mrs. Kempton well. We crossed the Weber, and the oxen swam across and we camped on the other side.

131Oct. 16th. Started before breakfast. Got to Aunt Hattie's about ten o'clock. Aunt Hattie looks just as natural as can be. Had to empty all the trunks. They were all wet. We dried all and worked fast to get things in shape to pack again.

131Oct. 18th. Did not go to the meeting in the forenoon. I thought I could not stand it to go two whole days. Went in the afternoon. Had a good meeting. Heard Brigham Young speak.

131Oct. 23rd. It won't be long now if all goes well before we will all be in Salt Lake City.

131Twice widowed, Lucy Canfield married John Willard Young, Ara Williams Sabin, and Phillip Henry Margetts. She lived in Ogden and Salt Lake City and later established a boarding house called "The Cottage Hotel" in Preston, Idaho. She was the mother of eight children. Those who knew her described her as lively, strong willed, and a hard worker. She died May 16, 1915, in Preston, Idaho. Lucy Canfield was the great-grandmother of Elder Jack H. Goaslind of the First Quorum of the Seventy of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Lucy's cousin Rose became a prominent teacher in Ogden and taught Jeannette Evans (McKay), the mother of President David O. McKay.

131Sources: Lucy Marie Canfield Margetts. Diary, holograph. LDS Church Archives.
