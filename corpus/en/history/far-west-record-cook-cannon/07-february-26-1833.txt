First. The Bishop laid before the council the effect of the proceedings of the Solemn Assemblies [^2] as held throughout Zion attended by brs Edward Partridge John Corrill Isaac Morley Oliver Cowdery & John Whitmer.

Resolved that a committee be appointed to write an epistle to our brethren in Kirtland: Voted that Oliver Cowdery William W. Phelps & John Corrill be the Committee to write the epistle.

Conference adjourned until six O'clock in the evening, in the meantime the epistle to be written. Prayer by Simeon Carter.

Convened according to adjournment, the epistle accepted by the conference. We then all kneeled before the Lord & asked him to effect a perfect harmony between us & our brethren in Kirtland which was the desire of our hearts.

Conference adjourned until four weeks from to-morrow

Prayer by br. Solomon Hancock.

John Whitmer Clerk.

March 26, 1833

Independence, March 26. 1833. [^3] A Council of Highpriests met according to adjournment 21 [^4] in number resident in Zion at this time.

Opened in Prayer by Br. Thomas B. Marsh.

The order that was taught at the solemn assemblies was accepted after much discussion. [^5]

Resolved that the Churches meet according to the appointments of the several highpriests stationed over their respective branches.

Closed in prayer by A.S. Gilbert.

John Whitmer Clerk.

First: Br. Harvey Whitlock laid before the council the case of Br. Christian Whitmer the President of the Elders who desired the office of the Highpriesthood, he also stated it himself by request of & others. Simeon Carter also said he had a testimony of the Lord that it was his will &c.

Ordained under the hand of Simeon Carter & sit in council with us.

Much consultation concerning the gift of tongues &c. [^7] And a rebuke of the Lord for reasoning in our own wisdom, & c.

Conference adjourned until Wednesday next at 9. O'clock A.M.

Prayer by the Bishop.

John Whitmer, Clerk.

Church leaders from Joseph Smith on were concerned about possible misuse of the gift of tongues. They were wary of Satan's power and desire to counterfeit the works of God. For these reasons they chose to caution the Saints in the exercise of that gift.

Footnotes

Concerning these solemn assemblies held in Zion, David Pettigrew said: "Soon after this [Dec. 1832], Bishop Partridge appointed a Solemn Assembly in all the branches, which was to be held as a day of confession and repentance. He went from branch to branch exhorting, until he had gone through them all, and in a few months we were informed that we had repented and the angels were rejoicing over us, but there yet remained a "Scourge and a judgment to be poured out upon the children of Zion." (See David Pettigrew Journal, Special Collections, Harold B. Lee Library, Brigham Young University, page 15.)

In the early days of the Restoration, the gift of tongues was restored and became manifest in a number of ways. Many of the early Church leaders spoke in tongues and were anxious to make others aware of the use of the gift. Brigham Young, for example, in his history, wrote: "I spoke in tongues. He called upon me to pray; in my prayer I spoke in tongues." (History of the Church 1:297.)

Not only the leaders, but also the Church members experienced the gift of tongues. Describing a baptismal service, Margrette Young wrote: "Elder Babbit then stepped upon a large stone that was near and spoke in tongues . . . . when he had ceased speaking he then gave them the meaning thereof, which was so beautiful, and so filled with the Spirit of God, that every eye was wet with tears" (Autobiography of Margrette W. Pierce Whitesides Young, Church Archives.)

As the use of the gift of tongues became more frequent and some problems and excesses were observed, the leaders of the Church began to issue warnings and cautionary statements. The Prophet Joseph Smith frequently warned the Saints concerning the gift of tongues. Writing to the brethren in Zion in 1833, he said: "We wish you, however, to be careful, lest in this you be deceived . . . . Satan will no doubt trouble you about the gift of tongues, unless you are careful." Later, he noted: "Do not indulge too much in the exercise of the gift of tongues, or the devil will take advantage of the innocent and unwary." (Joseph Fielding Smith, comp., Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith [Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Co., 1938], pages 25 and 229.)

Notas:
[^1] See History of the Church 2:112-13. Note that there are no minutes in the Far West Record for the period September 1833 to June 1834. In the interim the Saints had been expelled from Jackson County and were relocating, primarily in Clay County. The year "1834" was entered by Andrew Jenson.
[^2] Doctrine and Covenants 105. This revelation was received the previous day, June 22, 1834. The meeting apparently convened in George Burkett's field. See History of the Church 2:112.
[^3] Only a chosen few of the Missouri Saints were given the privilege of receiving this endowment. At this meeting Joseph Smith appointed fifteen high priests to travel to Kirtland to receive this blessing. Others were subsequently appointed to receive the endowment (see History of the Church 2:144). The "endowment of power from on high" to be received in Kirtland was to consist of a significant outpouring of spiritual power upon the participants at a solemn assembly (held March 30, 1836). Preparations for the Kirtland endowment included confession of sins, washings, anointings, covenant-making, and sealings. (See History of the Church 2:373-428 and "A Record of the First Quorum of Elders Belonging to the Church of Christ in Kirtland, Geauga Co., Ohio," pp. 2-19, RLDS Library-Archives, Independence, Missouri.)
[^4] Gilbert died June 29, 1834. (See History of the Church 2:118.)
[^5] See History of the Church 2:122. This meeting convened in the yard of Col. Michael Arthur. Arthur, who employed Lyman Wight and others, was sympathetic to the Church.
[^6] The high council, as it was organized in 1834, both in Kirtland and Missouri, consisted of three presidents and twelve councilors, all high priests. The fifteen high priests constituted the high council, not merely the twelve councilors. This arrangement began to change almost immediately, however, and soon only twelve councilors were recognized as the high council. Unlike today, neither the three presidents nor the twelve councilors were to meet alone to transact business for the Church.
[^7] Doctrine and Covenants 102.
[^8] At this meeting nominations were made for a presidency and twelve councilors, but no ordinations took place until July 7, 1834.
[^9] Andrew Jenson changed the date to read "7th inst."
[^10] History of the Church 1:124-26. Zion's Camp was disbanded on this day.
[^11] Doctrine and Covenants 102.
[^12] Doctrine and Covenants 43:3-4.
[^13] That is, the appointment of a successor and a governing body in the Church.
[^14] This action by Joseph Smith established a second high council for the Church. An earlier appointment created the Kirtland high council in February 1834. Both bodies functioned simultaneously.
[^15] Note that the high councilors cast lots to determine the order of speaking as is done in Church court cases today. This procedure was used here for administrative as well as judicial cases.
[^16] With the reorganization of the Church in Missouri, Partridge was no longer the presiding officer of the Church there. Whereas before he met with all the high priests in Missouri to direct the affairs of the Church, now his responsibilities were changed.
