January 6, 1836

A Council convened at Kirtland, Ohio, January 6. 1836, [^1] for the purpose of filling the vacancies of the High Council of Zion.

Presidents D. Whitmer, J. Whitmer & W. W. Phelps and also a goodly number of high priests and Elders to the No. of 15, and one priest.

Address by Pres. W.W. Phelps, announcing the decease of Christian Counsellor Christian Whitmer who died on the 27th of November 1835.

Four Councilors had been chosen as Apostles and special witnesses, viz: Parley P. Pratt, Orson Pratt, Wm. E. Mc.lellin & Thomas B. Marsh. Proceeded and nominated instead of the above named councilors as follows:

Elisha H. Groves instead of

Jesse Hitchcock ,, ,,

George M. Hinkel ,, ,,

Elias Higbee ,, ,,

Peter Whitmer jr. ,, ,,

Parley P. Pratt,

W. E. Mclellin

Orson Pratt,

T.B. Marsh

Christian Whitmer, deceased.

Proceeded and ordained the above named Counsellors and solemnity rested upon the whole congregation, because the Lord was with us, and great blessings were expressed and confirmed upon the heads of the aforementioned Counsellors.

Presidents D. Whitmer, J. Whitmer & W.W. Phelps addressed the congregation in a pathetic manner.

After which, bowed before the Lord with one accord and called upon him to preserve and bless our absent fathers mothers brethren and families until Zion should be redeemed &c. Prayer was made by David Whitmer, sang, Adam on Di Ahman [^2] Closed.

Elijah Fordham, Clerk.

November 15, 1836

November 15, 1836 [^3]

At a meeting of the presidents, in the City at Shoal Creek: being duly organized proceeded to do business. Selected and appointed Jacob Whitmer, Elisha H. Groves, and George M. Hinkle for a building committee to assist the Presidency to build the house of the Lord [^4] in said City. [^5]

Meeting closed as usual.

(signed) John Corrill Clerk.

Notas:
[^1] See History of the Church 2:481. Four days previous to this conference, questions were discussed in a conference relative to Whitmer and Phelps platting a city of Zion without the High Council approval. (See pages 107-9.)
[^2] This plat, which Phelps and J. Whitmer laid out, was one mile square.
[^3] These minutes are out of chronological order, appearing in the middle of notes for 1837.
[^4] "Poor Bleeding Zion" refers to the distressed Saints in Missouri. Marsh and Groves borrowed $1450 from members of the Church in Kentucky and Tennessee at 10 percent interest for the purpose of purchasing federal land in Caldwell County. (See Elders Journal 1(July 1838): 37; see also History of Thomas B. Marsh, Church Archives.)
[^5] On March 11, 1836, in Kirtland, Ohio, Joseph Smith appointed Edward Partridge, Isaac Morley, John Corrill, and William W. Phelps as "wise men" to purchase lands in Missouri where the Saints could settle, and they were given money for this purpose. After returning to Clay County, Missouri, in early May 1836, Partridge and Phelps "took a tour" of the northern country where they found a few good mill sites located about thirty-five miles northeast of Liberty. Shortly thereafter several half-quarter and quarter sections were entered in the names of Joseph Smith, Hyrum Smith, and Oliver Cowdery.
