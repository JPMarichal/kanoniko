Ebenezer ABBOTT (?-?) was present at a Church conference on June 3, 1831, held at Kirtland, Ohio. He is listed as having "denied the faith" by later scribes.

Hazen ALDRICH (Andrew Hazen Aldrich) (?-?) joined the Church in 1832 in New Hampshire. He marched with Zion's Camp and afterward was called to the First Quorum of Seventy. He joined with dissidents in Kirtland and left the Church.

Horace ALEXANDER (1812-81), a native of Virginia, was ordained an elder in Far West, Missouri, in 1838. He marched with the Mormon Battalion and later served a mission to the southern states.

Charles ALLEN (1806-?), a native of Pennsylvania, was tarred and feathered with Edward Partridge during the Jackson County, Missouri, disturbances.

James ALLRED (1784-1876), born in North Carolina, marched with Zion's Camp and lived through the expulsion of the Saints from Far West and Nauvoo. He was kidnapped in Nauvoo and held in Missouri, but was eventually released. He settled Spring City, Utah.

Martin Carroll ALLRED (1806-40), a native of Kentucky, marched with Zion's Camp in 1834 and was among those arrested at the fall of Far West.

John ANDERSON (1796-?) was born in Connecticut and married Lydia Kellog. He is said to have died in Nauvoo, Illinois.

John C. ANNIS (1784-?), born in Vermont, served as a Lieutenant in the Nauvoo Legion.

Major Noble ASHLEY (1798-?) was born in Sheffield, Massachusetts, and was ordained a high priest in 1831. He resided in Jackson County, Missouri, from 1832 to 1833, but left the Church by 1838.

Sampson AVARD (1809-?), a native of the Isle of Guernsey, was baptized in 1835. During the Far West period he was the leader of a retaliatory group that came to be called the Danites. He became a witness against Church leaders, implicating them in the institution of the Danites. He was subsequently excommunicated. He was living in Illinois in 1850.

Daniel AVERY (1793-?), a native of New York, was kidnapped in December of 1843 and carried to Missouri on a charge of horse stealing. He escaped and returned to Montrose, Iowa, where he served as president of the elders quorum.

Almon W. BABBITT (1813-56), born in Massachusetts, joined the Church in 1833. He was a member of Zion's Camp and was ordained a seventy in 1835. He served as president of the Kirtland Stake. He was killed by Indians while serving the State of Deseret in congress.

John BADGER (1800-40) was born in Waterford, Vermont, baptized in June 1832, and ordained an elder the following month. He died in 1840 in Lee County, Iowa Territory.

Caleb BALDWIN (1791-1849), a native of Nobletown, New York, joined the Church in 1830 and soon thereafter moved to Jackson County, Missouri, where he took part in the Battle of the Big Blue. In 1838 he was among those arrested by General Clark, spending the winter in Liberty Jail with Joseph Smith and other Church leaders. He lived in Illinois and settled in the Salt Lake Valley.

Caleb BALDWIN, Jr. (1817-?) was baptized in 1837 and ordained a seventy in 1840.

Nancy Kingsbury BALDWIN (1799-?), a native of Ohio, was married to Caleb Baldwin, Sr.

Wheeler BALDWIN (1793-1887) was ordained an elder in 1831 in Ohio, and a high priest later that same year. He joined with the Reorganized Church in 1863.

John P. BARNARD (1803-74) was born in New Hartford, New York, and was baptized in June of 1835. He died in 1874 in Box Elder County, Utah.

Lorenzo Dow BARNES (1812-42) was born in Toland, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1833. He was a member of Zion's Camp and later served several missions. He died in England, the first missionary to die while preaching the gospel in a foreign land.

William BATSON (1790-?), a native of New Jersey, lost his priesthood license during the Hulet Branch trial. It was apparently restored, however, since he received a mission assignment in 1844.

Calvin BEEBE (also Bebee and BeeBee) (1800-?) was born in Paris, New York, and served on the Far West high council. He was excommunicated by the Zarahemla Stake, Iowa Territory, for keeping a "tippling house."

George BEEBE (1811-81), a native of Oswego, New York, was baptized in October of 1830. He died in Provo, Utah.

Isaac BEEBE (1800-?), a native of Paris, New York, moved with his family to Jackson County, Missouri, in 1833.

BENJAMIN (?-?) was possibly Nahern or Timothy Benjamin, both of whom owned land in Caldwell County, Missouri.

BENNAR (?-?) was possibly Elias Benner, a Mormon who owned land in Caldwell County, Missouri, and was a member of Zion's Camp.

David BENNETT (1801-53) was born in New York. He was wounded in Jackson County, Missouri, during the expulsion of the Saints in 1833. He migrated to Utah and died in Mount Pleasant.

Benjamin BENSON (1779-1846), a native of Maple, New York, was baptized in 1832. He died in Mendon, Illinois, but his wife, Kazia Messenger, migrated to Utah.

Jerome BENSON (1810-73), a native of New York, was the son of Benjamin Benson. He went to Utah in 1852 and was sent to settle San Bernardino, California.

Samuel BENT (1778-1846) was born in Barre, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1833. He was a member of Zion's Camp and later moved to Missouri in 1836. He was a member of the Nauvoo high council and the Council of Fifty. He presided over the settlement at Garden Grove, Iowa, where he died.

Brother BEST (?-?) was possibly Henry Best, one of those filing affidavits concerning the expulsion from Missouri. He had resided there from 1834.

Titus BILLINGS (1793-1866), a native of Greenfield, Massachusetts, was baptized in 1830 and moved to Missouri in 1832. He was ordained a counselor to Bishop Partridge in 1837. He served as president of the branch in Lima, Illinois, until 1845.

Lilburn W. BOGGS (1792-1860) was an enemy of the Saints in Jackson County, Missouri. Later, as governor of the state, he issued the "extermination order," expelling the Saints from Missouri. Orrin Porter Rockwell was accused of an attempt on Boggs's life in 1842. Boggs recovered and later operated a business in California.

William BOLTON (?-?) was an elder residing in Jackson County in 1832.

Ezra BOOTH (1792- ) was a Methodist preacher before joining the Church. He was ordained a high priest in 1831 but was excommunicated later that year. He subsequently wrote nine anti-Mormon letters that appeared in the Ohio Star, thus becoming the first Mormon apostate to publish anti-Mormon literature.

John F. BOYNTON (1811-90) was born in Bradford, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1832. In 1835 he was ordained one of the original Apostles of the Church, but he became disaffected in the financial crisis in Kirtland of 1837. He was dropped from the Twelve and was later excommunicated.

Truman BRACE (1783-1855), a native of Torrington, Connecticut, was also referred to as "Father Brace" because of his age. He was a member of the Colesville Branch, the first to move to Jackson County, Missouri, in 1831. He signed the covenant to assist the poor in the exodus from that state in 1838-39.

Joseph BRACKENBURY (1788-1832) a native of Lincolnshire, England, was converted to the Church in 1831. The same year he left on a mission to New York. While on the mission he died suddenly from the effects of poison administered by enemies.

Benjamin BRAGG (?-?) was an early member of the Church in Warrensville, Ohio. He was silenced by the vote of the conference held in Kirtland on September 12, 1831.

Samuel BROWN (1801-82) was born in Altstead, New Hampshire, and temporarily lost his priesthood license in 1834 in the Hulet Branch trial. He marched in Zion's Camp and served as a doorkeeper in the Kirtland Temple. He settled in Payson, Utah.

Samuel C. BROWN (1820-?), the son of David and Mary Brown, was born in Fredrick County, Virginia.

Seymour BRUNSON (1799-1840) was baptized and made an elder in January 1831. He served missions in 1832 with Daniel Stanton and Luke Johnson, and he was later appointed a temporary member of the high council in Far West.

John BUCKHANNAN (also Buchanan) (1786-1839) was born in Ireland. He was baptized in 1835, but died in Lima, Illinois, in 1839.

Jacob BUMP (1791-?), a native of New York, served with Zion's Camp in 1834 and later was among those who defended the actions of Joseph Smith as leader of that group. However, in 1838, Bump was among those who left the Church and participated in mob action against Church members.

John M. BURKE (1793-1853) was an early resident of Kirtland, later moving to Missouri, where he was among those who petitioned for redress to the U. S. Congress in 1839. He later settled in Ogden, Utah.

George BURKETT (1788-1871) was born in Bedford, Pennsylvania. He joined the Church in 1831 and soon after moved to Jackson County. He was eventually expelled from Missouri with the other Saints. He died in Ogden, Utah.

Sarah Smith BURKETT (1787-?) was wife of George Burkett and joined the Church with her husband in 1831. She died during the Nauvoo period in Augusta, Iowa.

Serenes (also Sirenes) BURNETT (?-?) was among the original settlers of Chagrin Falls, Ohio. At his home in Orange, Ohio, a conference of the Church was held on October 25, 1831.

Stephen BURNETT (1814-?), a native of Ohio, was baptized in 1830 and ordained an elder in 1831. Later that year he was ordained a high priest. He apostatized by 1838.

Charity Lowe BUTLER (1782-1851) was born in North Carolina and joined the Church in 1835. She died in North Pidgeon, Iowa.

John Lowe BUTLER (1808-60), a native of Kentucky, was baptized in 1835 and soon settled with the Saints in Missouri. He was forced from Missouri and Illinois with the rest of the Church. He served as bishop in Spanish Fork, Utah.

Reynolds CAHOON (1790-1861), a native of Cambridge, New York, was baptized in 1830 and ordained a high priest in 1831. He was a counselor to Bishop Whitney in Kirtland and a member of the stake presidency in Adam-Ondi-Ahman and in Iowa. He died in Salt Lake City, Utah.

William Farrington CAHOON (1813-93), the son of Reynolds Cahoon, was born in Harpersfield, Ohio. He marched with Zion's Camp and became a member of the First Quorum of Seventy. He followed the Church to Illinois and eventually to the Great Salt Lake Valley.

Daniel CARN (also Garn) (1802-72), a native of St. Clair, Pennsylvania, was called as bishop of the first German-speaking branch of the Church in Nauvoo. In 1851 he was called as mission president in Germany. He died in Salt Lake City.

Benjamin CARPENTER (?-?) was possibly the Benjamin Carpenter who built the first grist mill on the present site of Chagrin Falls, Ohio. A conference of the Church was held in Carpenter's house in Norton, Ohio, April 21, 1834.

James CARROLL (1795-?) was born in England and was active during the Kirtland period of the Church, working for Oliver Cowdery as a scribe and in the printing office. He moved to Missouri, but he was excommunicated in Nauvoo.

Gideon Haden CARTER (1798-1838) was born in Killingworth, Connecticut, and was baptized in 1831 and served a mission to Vermont in 1832. He was killed in 1838 at the Battle of Crooked River in Ray County, Missouri.

Jared CARTER (1801-ca. 1849), brother of Gideon Carter, was baptized in 1831. He was a member of the high council in Kirtland and Far West. After his disaffection in 1838, he remained only a nominal member of the Church.

Orlando Henry CARTER (1820-60), a native of Vermont, was endowed in the Nauvoo Temple, December 24, 1845.

Simeon Dagget CARTER (1794-1869), a native of Killingworth, Connecticut, was ordained a high priest in 1831 and was a member of the high council in Clay County and in Far West, Missouri. He served a mission to Germany in 1841. He died in Brigham City, Utah.

William CARTER (?-?) lost his elder's license in September 1831.

Daniel CATHCART (1803-?) was born in New Jersey, and served in Zion's Camp during the summer of 1834. He was expelled with the Saints from Missouri in 1838. He had lived at Adam-Ondi-Ahman.

Jacob CHAMBERLAIN (1779-?) was born December 26, 1779, in Dudley, Massachusetts.

Solomon CHAMBERLAIN (1788-1862), a native of Old Canaan, Connecticut, was among the first to settle in Jackson County, Missouri. At the time of the exodus from Nauvoo, he was chosen a member of the original pioneer party that arrived in the Great Salt Lake Valley in 1847.

Darwin CHASE (1816-63) was born in Ellisburg, New York, and was baptized with his father's family in 1831. He was ordained a seventy in 1839. He was imprisoned in Richmond, Missouri, following the fall of Far West. He followed the Church to Utah.

Stephen CHASE (1799-?), a native of New York, held several positions in the Church, including president of the elders quorum in Far West. He served as a member of the high council in Iowa.

John Maddison CHIDESTER (1809-93) was born in Pompey, New York, and joined the Church in 1832. He was a member of Zion's Camp in 1834. At the time of the exodus from Nauvoo, he built and ran one of the ferries that crossed the Mississippi Riber. He helped colonize Washington County, Utah.

Benjamin L. CLAPP (1814-61), a native of Alabama, was baptized a member of the Church in 1835 and settled with the Mormons in Missouri. In 1838, he was called on a mission to Kentucky. He returned to take part in the battle of Crooked River and was forced to flee to Iowa to escape imprisonment at the fall of Far West. While residing in California, he was excommunicated.

John B. CLARK (1802-85), a native of Kentucky, was a brigadier general of militia in the Black Hawk War. He was elected major general in 1836. He was given the assignment in 1838 by Governor Boggs to force the Saints from the state or exterminate them. He served Missouri in the U. S. Congress.

John Wesley CLARK (1808-40) was born in Durham, Connecticut, and was among those who gathered at the temple site in Far West to witness the departure of the Twelve Apostles for England. He died in Illinois in April 1840.

Timothy B. CLARK (1773-1848), a native of New Haven, Connecticut, was generally referred to as "Father Clark" by the Saints in Missouri. He died May 29, 1848.

John CLEMINSON (?-?) served in a number of positions in Far West, including clerk of the circuit court and official recorder, but by the time of the disorders in that place, he was apparently in sympathy with the state, since he was offered free passage with his family out of the city before General Clark entered. He was subsequently excommunicated.

Henry Alanson CLEVELAND (1809-67) was born in Scohaine, New York, and was baptized in 1830. He was wounded during the difficulties at Far West. He went west and settled in Centerville, Utah.

Emily COBURN (?-?) attended the baptism of the Colesville Saints in New York in June 1830. Her minister tried to force her to remain a Presbyterian, but she was baptized. Her sister, Sally Coburn Knight, was Newel Knight's wife.

Joseph COE (1785- ), a native of New Jersey, was ordained a high priest in 1831. He helped lay the foundation stone of the Kirtland Temple in 1833, and he was a member of the Kirtland High Council. He left the Church, however, in 1837.

Barnet COLE (1796-1860) was born in Gruedhall, Vermont, and received a patriarchal blessing in 1846 in Nauvoo. He died in Box Elder County, Utah, in 1860. He is listed in the Far West Record only as "Brother" Cole.

Zebedee (also Zebidee) COLTRIN (also Coultrin) (1804-87) was a member of Zion's Camp and was afterward ordained a member of the First Quorum of Seventy. He also served as a member of the stake presidency in Kirtland. He settled in Spanish Fork, Utah.

Libius Thaddeus COONS (1811-72), a native of Plymouth, New York, was baptized in 1832. In 1834 he marched with Zion's Camp to Missouri. He was an original member of the First Quorum of Seventy. He lived in Richfield, Utah.

John CORRILL (1794-?), a native of Massachusetts, was baptized in 1831 and ordained a high priest and assistant to Bishop Partridge. He was a prominent leader in Jackson County, but he was excommunicated in 1839 in Far West when he joined with other dissenters.

Horace COWAN (?-?) joined the Church in 1833 in Kirtland and traveled to Vermont, where he was ordained an elder. He marched with Zion's Camp and later moved to Missouri. He was incarcerated briefly with Joseph Smith in the Liberty Jail in March 1839.

Marcellus COWDERY (?-?), brother of Oliver Cowdery, was a member of the Church. He testified for Oliver Cowdery in Far West. He was disfellowshipped in April 1838.

Oliver COWDERY (1806-50), a native of Wells, Vermont, with Joseph Smith received the Aaronic and Melchizedek Priesthood in 1829. He was one of the three witnesses to the Book of Mormon and was a charter member of the Church. He was excommunicated in Far West, but was rebaptized in 1848 in Kanesville, Iowa. He died in Richmond, Missouri.

Ralph COX (?-?) was a member of the Church in Far West. He served as acting president of the elders quorum.

Nahum CURTIS (1784- ) was the son of Moses and Molly Curtis and was born in Franklin County, Massachusetts. His wife was Millicent Waite.

Alpheus CUTLER (1784-1864), a native of Plainfield, New Hampshire, was baptized in 1833 and was ordained a high priest in 1836. He was appointed a member of the Nauvoo high council in 1839. He later rejected Brigham Young's leadership and organized the True Church of Jesus Christ.

Moses DALEY (also Daily or Dailey) (1794-1865) was born in Wallbill, New York, and served on a committee to purchase land in Kirtland for the Church. He later moved to Missouri, settling at Adam-Ondi-Ahman. He died in California.

Abigail DANIELS (1800-?), a native of Maine and the daughter of Daniel and Sarah Warren, was married to Sheffield Daniels.

Sheffield DANIELS (1787-1855) was born in Keene, New Hampshire, and was ordained an elder shortly after the exodus of the Saints from Missouri. He died in Iowa in 1855.

William DAVIS (1794 or 1795-1883) was born in Union, Pennsylvania, and was baptized in December 1832. He eventually settled in Brigham City, Utah.

Isaac DECKER (1800-1873), a native of the Netherlands, migrated to Utah with the first pioneer company in July 1847. His family home was in Utah County. Two of his daughters, Lucy and Clarissa, married Brigham Young.

Philo DIBBLE (1806-95), a native of Berkshire, Massachusetts, joined the Church in its infancy, in September 1830. He was wounded in the mob attack in Jackson County, Missouri, but stayed with the Saints through the Missouri and Illinois periods, eventually migrating to Utah.

Alexander W. DONIPHAN (1808-84) represented the Saints as a lawyer on several occasions. He introduced the bill in the Missouri legislature that created Daviess and Caldwell Counties, where the Saints lived for a period of time. He served as a general in the state militia of Missouri, and drew praise from the Saints for refusing to shoot Joseph Smith at the time of the fall of Far West.

David DORT (1790-1841), a native of Gilsum, New Hampshire, was a charter member of the Kirtland Safety Society and a member of the high council in Kirtland, Far West, and Nauvoo. He died in Nauvoo in 1841.

Samuel DROLLINGER (1801-34), a native of Hamilton, Ohio, was baptized into the Church in 1831 with his wife, Rachael. He died in Clay County, Missouri.

James DUNN (1787-?) was born in Pennsylvania and served as assistant steward in Zion's Camp.

Edmund DURFEE (1788-1845), a native of Rhode Island, joined the Church in 1831. In 1837 he moved to Caldwell County, Missouri, only to be expelled with the other Saints the next year. He settled in Illinois, where he was murdered by a mob in 1845.

James DURFEE (1795-1844) was ordained an elder in 1833. He was one of the leaders of the Mormon forces at the battle of Crooked River. He died in 1844 and was buried in the old Nauvoo cemetery.

Peter DUSTIN (1781-?) was born in Grafton, New Hampshire, and served as president of the Blue River Branch in Jackson County, Missouri, where he was ordained a high priest in 1833. He later served on the Missouri high council.

George Parker DYKES (1814-88), a native of Illinois, was ordained a seventy in 1839, laboring afterward as a missionary in Illinois. He enlisted in the Mormon Battalion in 1846. He died in Arizona.

Ellis EAMES (?-?) was ordained an elder in Jackson County, Missouri. He was later president of a conference in Clay County, August 6, 1834. Eames received a mission call while in Nauvoo in the summer of 1844.

Asa C. EARL (1810-91), a native of New York, was baptized in 1833 in Illinois, along with a number of his neighbors, including Charles C. Rich and Hosea Stout. Earl married Rich's sister, Minerva, who died in 1840. Earl later migrated to Utah.

Sylvester Henry EARL (1815-72) was born in Ohio and baptized in 1837. After his baptism, he moved to Far West, Missouri. He served a mission in 1838 and served as guard of the temple in Nauvoo before he was forced to leave with the other Saints. He was a member of the first company of pioneers to arrive in Utah.

Josiah ELLS (1806-85), a native of England, was baptized by Benjamin Winchester in October 1838. Although he was prominent in Nauvoo, he later associated with the apostate organization of William Law and eventually became an apostle in the Reorganized Church.

Ruggles EMES (?-?) was baptized and ordained a priest before October 1831. He resided in Medina, Ohio, at that time.

James EMMETT (1803-52), a native of Kentucky, joined the Church in 1831. He was disfellowshipped temporarily in 1837 and found himself in trouble again when he led a company of one hundred Saints away from Nauvoo against the counsel of Church leaders. He died in the Mormon settlement of San Bernardino, California.

Charles ENGLISH (?-?) was a member of the Church in Clay County, Missouri, where he owned property. He testified in the Hulet Branch trial in 1834.

Horace EVANS (1810-?) was born in Dalton, New Hampshire. He was ordained a seventy in Illinois.

Elisha EVERETT (also Averett) (1810-90), a native of Tennessee, was baptized in 1835. He migrated to Utah and died in Glendale, Washington County.

Pleasant EWELL (1788-1852), a native of Virginia, joined the Church in 1837 and was ordained an elder in 1839 and a high priest in 1844. He migrated with the Saints to Utah, settling in Lehi.

Joshua FAIRCHILD (1797-1891), a Canadian, was the son of Joshua Fairchild and Elizabeth Cooley. He moved to the Great Basin with the Church and settled in Cassia County, Idaho.

John FAWSETT (1804-73) was born in Illinois. He served in later years as bishop in Midway, Utah.

Daniel Babcock FILLMORE (1819-94) was born in Camillers, New York, and was baptized in 1842 and then moved to Nauvoo. He was among those sent to Wisconsin to obtain timber for the Nauvoo buildings. He died in Payson, Utah.

Edmund FISCHER (?-?) served in Zion's Camp and later was ordained to the First Quorum of Seventy.

King FOLLETT (1788-1844), a native of Vermont, joined the Church in 1831. He moved with the Saints to Missouri and was one of those arrested in 1838. He was jailed through the winter of 1838-39, first in Richmond and later in Columbia. After he was released he settled in Nauvoo. His name is still remembered for the funeral oration delivered for him by Joseph Smith in 1844.

Isaac FOLLIS (also Follice and Fallace) (?-?), an elder in Jackson County, Missouri, was among those driven from the county in 1833.

David FRAMPTON (1796-1843), a native of Derry, Pennsylvania, was among those arrested at the time of the fall of Far West, although he was released in a few days. He made his way to Illinois with the body of the Saints, but died in that state in 1843.

Edson FULLER (?-?) was baptized and ordained an elder before June 3, 1831. He was soon after sent on a mission to Missouri, but was troubled by evil spirits and excommunicated later that same year.

Josiah FULLER (?-1838) was possibly the same Josiah Fuller whose name was mentioned in conjunction with work on the Kirtland Temple. He was killed in the mob attack on Haun's Mill in 1838.

David FULLMER (1803-79), a native of Chillisquaque, Pennsylvania, joined the Church in 1836 and resided in Caldwell County, Missouri, by 1837. He was later ordained a high priest in Nauvoo. He served on the Nauvoo high council, the Council of Fifty, and in the Salt Lake stake presidency.

James GALLAHER (?-?) was a Mormon property owner in Caldwell County, Missouri, and was among those who signed a covenant to assist the poor in the removal of the Church from the state.

David M. GAMET (1812-84) was born in Otsego, New York, and was baptized in 1835. Although many of his children settled in Utah, he remained in the midwest and died in Iowa.

Simeon GARDNER (?-?) served as a member of the high council in Far West. Gardner was among those excommunicated by the Twelve Apostles in Far West, April 26, 1839.

Jacob GATES (1811-92), a native of St. Johnsbury, Vermont, moved with his family to Caldwell County in 1836 and was one of those imprisoned at the time of the fall of Far West. In 1844 he was ordained the senior member of the fourth quorum of seventy in Nauvoo. He died in Provo, Utah.

Mary Snow GATES (1813-91), sister to Erastus Snow, was born in St. Johnsbury, Vermont, and married Jacob Gates in 1833. Both were baptized the same year. Mary's name appears in the Far West Record in connection with the trial of Elder John Murdock in 1838.

Thomas GATES (1776-1851) was born in Hennica, New Hampshire, and became a member of the Church in 1833. At a conference in Kirtland in 1835, Gates, as assistant president of the deacons quorum, was called on to testify to the truthfulness of the soon-to-be-published Doctrine and Covenants. He later moved to Utah.

Jesse GAUSE (also Gauss) (?-?) was a Shaker before joining the Church in 1831. He was a member of the presidency of the high priesthood and traveled to Jackson County, Missouri, with Joseph Smith in 1832. However, later that year he fell away and was excommunicated.

Luman GIBBS (?-?), a native of Vermont, was among those arrested in Far West in 1838. He turned state's evidence, however, and was later excommunicated at a special meeting of the Apostles on April 26, 1839.

Alpheus GIFFORD (1793-1841) was born in Adams, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1831. He gathered with the Saints in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1833, relocating with them in Clay County after their expulsion and also in Far West a few years later. He died in the Morley settlement in Illinois in 1841.

Algernon Sidney GILBERT (?-1834) was baptized in Ohio in 1830. He was a partner with Newel K. Whitney in a mercantile business in Kirtland. He was called to operate the Church store in Jackson County, Missouri, and was arrested with other leaders at the time of the expulsion from that county. He died of cholera in 1834 at the time of the visit of Zion's Camp to Clay County.

Cornelius GILLIAM (?-1848) professed to be a minister of the gospel. He was a Missouri state senator from 1838 to 1844. He led one of the militia groups that harassed the Saints in Caldwell and Daviess Counties in 1838.

Thomas C. GORDON (?-?) was chosen to serve on the high council of Adam-Ondi-Ahman in 1838. He was among those who filed a petition to the government for redress from the persecutions in Missouri.

George D. GRANT (1812-76), a native of Windsor, New York, joined the Church with his brother, Jedediah M. Grant, in New York. He moved with the Saints to Utah in 1848 and later served as an officer in the state militia during the Indian wars.

Joshua GRANT (1778-1865), a native of Stonington, Connecticut, was the father of George and Jedediah Grant. Although he was present with the Saints in Missouri, it is not known whether he was baptized.

Alphonzo GREEN (1810-75) was born in Brookfield, New York, and was baptized in 1840. He afterward moved with his family to Nauvoo, Illinois. He migrated to Utah in 1847.

Harvey GREEN (1806-?), a native of Lake Pleasant, New York, was married to Sally who died in Jackson County. He later married Ann Rich, a sister to Charles C. Rich. He migrated to Utah in 1848.

Younger GREEN (?-?) was listed as Doctor Green and was a counselor to William Law in Law's apostate church, founded near the time of Joseph Smith's death. Green was excommunicated in 1844.

John Portineus GREENE (1793-1844) was born in Herkimer, New York, and married Rhoda Young, the sister of Brigham Young. He was baptized in 1832 and moved to Kirtland, Ohio. In 1838 he moved his family to Far West, but was forced to flee back to Illinois soon after. Greene served as marshal and was the officer in charge of the destruction of the Expositor press in 1844. He became ill shortly thereafter and died.

John GREG (?-?) was a property owner in Caldwell County. He was excommunicated April 26, 1839, in Far West.

William GREGORY (?-?) was a member of the Church living in Caldwell County, Missouri, and was among those who signed the covenant to assist the poor in the Church's removal from the state.

Selah J. GRIFFIN (1792-?) was called on a mission with Thomas B. Marsh in 1831. He lived in Jackson County, Missouri, and later in Caldwell County. He was a blacksmith.

Hiram GRIFFITH (?-1845) was an early member in Ohio and was among those who left that state in the fall of 1837 with the "poor camp." He died in Nauvoo, Illinois.

Thomas GROVER (1807-86), a native of Whitehall, New York, joined the Church in 1834. He was a member of the high council in Kirtland, Far West, and Nauvoo. He migrated to Utah in 1847.

Elisha H. GROVES (1797-1867), a native of Kentucky, was baptized in 1832. He was sustained a member of the Far West high council in 1837. He migrated with the Saints to Utah, settling in Washington County.

Thomas GUYMAN (also Guymon) (1781-1855) was born in Stokes County, North Carolina. He migrated to Utah in 1850, settling in Springville.

Isaac HAMBLIN (1776-?), a native of Yarmouth, Massachusetts, was acting president of the deacons quorum in Far West at the conference held there July 6, 1838.

Levi W. HANCOCK (1803-82) was baptized by Parley P. Pratt in November 1830. He was a member of Zion's Camp and was afterward a president of the First Quorum of Seventy. He also marched with the Mormon Battalion. He died in Washington, Utah.

Solomon HANCOCK (1793-1847), a native of Springfield, Massachusetts, was baptized in 1830 and was called on a mission to Missouri in 1831. He moved with his family to Jackson County in 1832. He was later a member of the high council in Clay County.

Emer HARRIS (1781-1869), the brother of Martin Harris, was born in Cambridge, New York. He joined the Church and became an elder in 1831. He moved to Kirtland that same year, and in 1839 moved his family to Nauvoo. He died in Logan, Utah.

George W. HARRIS (1780-1857), a native of Lanesborough, Massachusetts, was baptized in 1834. He was a member of the high council in Far West and Nauvoo. He did not migrate west. He died in Council Bluffs in 1857.

Martin HARRIS (1783-1875) was born in Easttown, New York, and served as a scribe to Joseph Smith during the translation of the Book of Mormon. He was one of the Three Witnesses. He was excommunicated in 1837 but was rebaptized in 1842. He left the Church again and joined with the Strangites, but migrated to Utah in 1870 and was baptized once again.

Peter HAWS (1796-?), a native of Canada, was a member of the Nauvoo Agricultural and Manufacturing Association. He was sent on a mission to collect funds for the Nauvoo House and the temple in 1843. He located in western Iowa in 1846. His children joined the Reorganized Church.

Norval (also Norvil) HEAD (1815-?) was born in Missouri. He was ordained an elder in Far West. He migrated to Nauvoo, where he received his endowment in 1846.

George HEARTLY (?-?) testified before the conference in Randolph County, Indiana, December 6, 1831, about his difficulties with Oliver Walker.

James HENDRICKS (also Hendrix) (1808-70), a native of Kentucky, joined the Church in 1836 and moved first to Clay County and then Caldwell County, Missouri. In 1838 he was wounded at the battle of Crooked River and was crippled for the rest of his life. He journeyed to the Great Basin, arriving in 1847 with the Jedediah M. Grant company.

Alonzo HERRICK (?-?) lived first in Jackson County, then in Clay County, and finally in Far West, Missouri, where he attended the school taught by John Murdock. He was called upon to testify in the case involving his classmates, March 24, 1838.

Elias HIGBEE (1795-1843) was born in Galloway, New Jersey. He was baptized in 1832 and moved to Missouri in 1833. He was a member of the high council in Clay County and Far West. He later traveled with Joseph Smith to Washington to present grievances to congress. He died of cholera in Nauvoo.

Isaac HIGBEE (1797-1874), brother of Elias and a native of New Jersey, joined the Church in 1832 and located in Jackson County, Missouri. In 1835 he was ordained a high priest while residing in Clay County. He was later a bishop in Nauvoo and a justice of the peace. He died in Provo, Utah.

John S. HIGBEE (1804-77), brother of Isaac and Elias Higbee and a native of Tate, Ohio, was baptized in 1832 with the rest of his father's family. He was arrested at the time of the fall of Far West. He settled in Toquerville, Utah.

George M. HINKLE (1801-61), born in Kentucky, joined the Church as early as 1832 and moved to Missouri. In Far West he operated a store. During the fall of Far West he allegedly turned Joseph Smith and other Church leaders over to General Lucas. Most of the Saints felt that he betrayed the Prophet. He was excommunicated in 1839.

Jesse HITCHCOCK (1801-?), a native of North Carolina, was one of the early Mormon settlers in Jackson County. He was ordained a high priest in Clay County in 1834. He was appointed to the Missouri high council in 1836. He was cut off from that body in 1837, but retained membership in the Church.

Chandler HOLBROOK (1807-89), a native of New York, was baptized in 1833. He was among those arrested at the fall of Far West, but he was released within a few days. He emigrated with the Saints to the Great Basin, settling in Fillmore, Utah.

Joseph HOLBROOK (1806-86), born in Florence, New York, and brother of Chandler, was baptized in 1833 and marched with Zion's Camp in 1834. He later returned to Missouri, settling in Caldwell County. He followed the Saints to the Rocky Mountains, eventually settling in Bountiful, Utah.

Charles HULET (1790-1863) was born in Lee, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1831. He moved to the Prairie Branch in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1832. He and his family went through the sufferings of Clay County and Far West and eventually settled in Nauvoo. He came to Utah in 1850, settling in Springville.

Sylvester HULET (1800-?), brother of Charles and a native of Massachusetts, joined the Church in 1831 and removed to Jackson County, Missouri. In Clay County he married Anne Schott Whitmer, widow of Christian Whitmer, shortly after Whitmer's death. He moved to the Salt Lake Valley in 1847.

Solomon HUMPHREY (1775-1834) was converted by Don Carlos Smith and was ordained an elder by 1831. He assisted in laying the foundation stone of the Kirtland Temple in 1833. He was a member of Zion's Camp. He died in Clay County, Missouri, in September 1834.

Jefferson HUNT (1803-79), a native of Kentucky, joined the Church in 1834 and moved to Clay County, Missouri. Later, in Nauvoo, he served as a major in the Nauvoo Legion. He was also a captain in the Mormon Battalion. He settled in Ogden, Utah.

Dimic (also Dimick) B. HUNTINGTON (1808-79) was baptized in 1835. He served in various political positions in Nauvoo, including constable and coroner, and he was also drum major in the Nauvoo Legion military band. He served with the Mormon Battalion as well. He died in Salt Lake City.

Heman T. HYDE (1812-42) was born in Stratford, Vermont, and was baptized in 1834. Although his parents voiced opposition to his conversion, they joined the Church a month later and became the nucleus of the Freedom Branch.

Orson HYDE (1805-78), a native of Oxford, Connecticut, and a former Campbellite, converted to the Church in 1831. He was ordained an Apostle in 1835, but was dropped from the Twelve in 1838. In 1841, after his repentance, he was sent to Jerusalem to dedicate land for the gathering of the Jews. He later published the Frontier Guardian in Kanesville, Iowa. He died in Spring City, Utah.

James R. IVIE (1802-66) was born in Georgia and baptized in 1832. He marched with Zion's Camp in 1834. He journeyed to the Great Basin with the Saints, eventually settling in Springville, Utah. In 1853, Ivie's interference in a quarrel among the local Indians caused the Walker War.

James IVINS (?-?), a convert to the Church from New Jersey, was a prominent property owner in Nauvoo. His brother, Charles, was among those involved in the Nauvoo Expositor incident. He settled in Keokuk, Iowa.

Albert JACKMAN (1812-91), the son of Levi Jackman, was born in Stillwater, New York. He married Lucinda Stone. He died in Salem, Utah, in 1891.

Levi JACKMAN (1797-1876), a native of Vermont, was converted by Harvey Whitlock in 1831. He moved to Jackson County, Missouri, in 1832, and to Clay County in 1833. He was a member of the high council in Clay County. He was a justice of the peace in Far West. He labored on the temple in Nauvoo, leaving for the Great Salt Lake Valley in 1846.

Henry JACKSON (?-?) was ordained an elder in Far West in 1837. His wife, Sarah, was involved in a high council trial in Far West in April 1838.

Sarah JACKSON (?-?), the wife of Henry Jackson, was involved in a high council trial in Far West in April 1838.

Aaron JOHNSON (1806-77) was born in Haddam, Connecticut, and was baptized in 1836. He was among the families that moved from Kirtland to Far West in the spring of 1838. He served on the high council in Nauvoo and was a lieutenant in the Nauvoo Legion. He headed a company of Saints, arriving in Utah in 1850.

Benjamin Franklin JOHNSON (1818-1905), a native of Pomfret, New York, moved with his family to Kirtland in 1833, although he wasn't baptized until 1835. He moved with the Kirtland Camp to Missouri in 1838. He settled in Adam-Ondi-Ahman, but was soon driven out and arrested. Later, in Nauvoo, he was chosen as a member of the Council of Fifty. He died in Gila Bend, Arizona.

Edward JOHNSON (1809-90), was born in Morristown, New Jersey. He married Matilda Archer in 1833.

James JOHNSON (?-?) was listed as a property owner in Caldwell County, Missouri, in 1837.

Joel Hills JOHNSON (1802-82) was born in Grafton, Massachusetts, and was baptized in 1831. His removal to Missouri was apparently late in the Far West period, and he was forced out soon after. He established himself in Ramus, Illinois, where he served as president of the short-lived Ramus Stake. He came to Utah with the Willard Richards company in 1848.

Luke S. JOHNSON (1807-61), a native of Pomfret, Vermont, was baptized in 1831 and ordained a high priest the same year. He was a member of the Kirtland high council and of Zion's Camp. In 1835 he was ordained an Apostle, but he was disfellowshipped in 1837 and excommunicated in 1838. He was rebaptized in Nauvoo in 1846 and migrated to Utah in 1847.

Lyman E. JOHNSON (1811-56), brother of Luke Johnson and a native of Vermont, was baptized in 1831 and was a member of Zion's Camp. He was ordained an Apostle in 1835, but, like his brother, was excommunicated in 1838. He, however, did not return to the Church. He was killed in a drowning accident in the Mississippi River in 1856.

Orson JOHNSON (?-?) was an elder present at the special conference held at the Big Blue settlement in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1832. He served with Zion's Camp and on the high council of Kirtland.

David JUDA (also Judah) (?-?) fought at the battle of Crooked River in 1838. He served as a missionary in the Joseph Smith campaign in Illinois in 1844.

John KILLIAN (1796-1858), a native of North Carolina, was baptized in 1831. He served as a captain of the Caldwell County militia (Mormon) during the "Mormon War" at Far West. He later emigrated to Utah.

Heber C. KIMBALL (1801-68), born in Sheldon, Vermont, was baptized in 1832 and moved to Kirtland 1833. He was ordained an Apostle in 1835. He served missions to Great Britain in 1837 and 1839. He moved to Missouri in 1838 and aided the Saints during the expulsion from Far West. Later, in Utah, he served as a member of the First Presidency.

Samuel KIMBALL (?-?) was one of the witnesses who testified against the Church in Missouri in November 1838.

Newel KNIGHT (1800-1847) was born in Marlborough, Vermont, and was baptized in May 1830. He was leader of the Colesville Branch in New York and led that group to Jackson County, Missouri, as the first Mormon settlers there. He lived through all the Missouri depredations. His wife, Sally Coburn, died in Clay County. He later married Lydia Goldthwait. He died in Nebraska territory.

Vinson KNIGHT (1804-42), a native of Chester, New York, was ordained a high priest and counselor to Bishop Whitney in Kirtland in 1836. He later became bishop at the settlement at Adam-Ondi-Ahman. As a Church land agent, he assisted in purchasing thousands of acres of land from Isaac Galland in Iowa. He also served as a bishop in Nauvoo.

Isaac LANEY (1815-73) was born in Franklin, Kentucky, and was baptized in 1838. He was among the survivors of the raid at Haun's Mill. He migrated to Utah in 1847, settling in Salt Lake City.

Thomas LAPLESH (?-?) was an elder of the Church living in Jackson County, Missouri. He was present at a conference there on September 27, 1832.

Edward LARKEY (1809-?) testified against Lyman Wight in a trial held June 29, 1838, at Far West.

Francis LEE (1811-66), a native of Wilmington, Ohio, married Jane Vail Johnson in 1835 in Clay County, Missouri. After migrating with the Saints to the Great Basin, Lee settled in Panaca, Nevada.

Elizabeth LEGG (?-?) was presumably a younger woman attending the Murdock school in Far West. She was called to testify in the high council trial on March 24, 1838.

William LEGG (1805-35) was a sailor, and his occupation apparently brought him to America from England and eventually to the Church. He died at Liberty, Missouri, July 29, 1835.

William J. LEMANS (?-?) was ordained an elder in Far West on November 10, 1837.

Lyman LEONARD (1793-1877), a native of Springfield, Massachusetts, was baptized in 1832 and moved to Jackson County, Missouri. He accompanied the Saints to Clay County, Far West, Nauvoo, and finally to Utah.

Joshua LEWIS (1795-1835) resided in Jackson County at the time the Mormon settlements were established there. He converted to the Church in the winter of 1830-31. He died in 1835 in Clay County, Missouri.

Adam LIGHTNER (1810-85) was born in Liberty, Missouri, and married Mary Elizabeth Rollins in 1835. His wife was sealed to Joseph Smith in 1842 but never sealed to Lightner, since he was never baptized. Lightner operated a store at Far West, Missouri.

Cornelius P. LOTT (1798-1850), a native of New York City, received his endowment in Nauvoo and left for the Great Basin, arriving in the fall of 1847.

Daniel E. LOVELAND (?-?) was called on a mission to Massachusetts in the spring of 1844.

John LOWRY (also Lowrie) (1799-1867) was born in Nashville, Tennessee. His first wife, Susan Groom, died in Missouri. He came to Salt Lake City with the John Taylor company in 1847.

Samuel D. LUCAS (?-1868) located in Jackson County, Missouri, as early as 1826. He was a general in the Missouri State Militia. He led the forces against the Mormons in Jackson County, and he later led forces against Far West, arriving ahead of General Clark. He took Joseph Smith prisoner and ordered him shot, but Alexander Doniphan refused to carry out the order.

Amasa Mason LYMAN (1813-77) was born in Lyman, New Hampshire, and joined the Church at age eighteen. He was ordained a high priest in 1833. He marched with Zion's Camp and later moved his family to Missouri, only to be expelled in 1838. In 1842 he was ordained an Apostle. He was excommunicated in 1870 for denying the atonement of the Savior.

Aaron LYON (also Lyons) (?-1839) converted to the Church in 1831-32. He worked on the Kirtland Temple and settled Salem, Missouri. He died at Bear Creek, Hancock County, Illinois.

Roxana Palmer LYON (?-1836) died August 23, 1836, in Caldwell County, Missouri.

John LYTLE (1803-92), a native of Turbet, Pennsylvania, was baptized in May 1836 and moved to Missouri. Later, in Nauvoo, he served as a police officer and was among those arrested for the destruction of the Expositor press. He later migrated to Utah.

William MARKS (1792-1872), a native of Rutland, Vermont, became prominent in the Church late in the Kirtland period, serving there on the high council. He was stake president in Nauvoo from 1839 to 1844. He was also an alderman for the city. After Joseph Smith's death, he joined with the reorganization and became a leading figure in that movement.

Thomas B. MARSH (1800-1866) was born in Acton, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in September 1830. He was appointed physician to the Church. He moved to Missouri and became a member of the high council in Clay County. In 1835 he was ordained an Apostle. In 1838 he became one of the "dissidents" who disagreed with Church leadership. He was excommunicated, but returned to be rebaptized in 1857.

Moses MARTIN (?-?) was a member of Zion's Camp and of the First Quorum of Seventy. He also served a mission to New York. He settled in Lee County, Iowa, in 1839.

Silas MAYNARD (1787-?) was among those arrested at Far West, but he was released shortly thereafter.

Dr. James McCORD (?-?) served on the high council at Far West until his excommunication in 1838.

William E. McLELLIN (also Mc.lellin, Mclellin, McClellin, and M'Lellin) (1806-83), a native of Tennessee, was baptized in August 1831. He was a member of the high council in Clay County, Missouri, and became an Apostle in 1835. He left the Church in 1838 and became an enemy of the Prophet.

Daniel S. MILES (1772-1845) was born in Sanbornton, New Hampshire, and was called as a President of the First Quorum of Seventy in 1837. He moved to Missouri in 1838 and was expelled with the rest of the Saints. He died in Illinois in 1845.

Eleazer MILLER (1795-1876), born in Coeymans, New York, was baptized in 1831. He served in Zion's Camp in 1834. During the exodus from Illinois, he served on the committee to expedite the removal of the Saints.

George MILLER (1783-1837) was a priest in the Shalersville, Ohio, Branch. He was silenced at a conference held September 12, 1831, in Kirtland. He is not the same man as the Bishop George Miller of the Nauvoo period.

Henry William MILLER (1807-85), a native of Lexington, New York, became a convert in 1839. He served as stake president in Freedom, Illinois, during the Nauvoo period. He migrated to Utah in 1852.

William MITCHELL (?-?) was baptized and ordained an elder before June 1833. He lived in the Kirtland area.

James MORE (?-?) was a member of the Church in Indiana. His license was taken from him at a conference held in Randolph County, Indiana, December 6, 1831. He was accused of abusing his family.

George MOREY (also Moery) (1803-?), born in Pittstown, New York, served on the high council in Far West and was appointed as a constable in Nauvoo. He was ordained a high priest in 1841. Morey did not leave Nauvoo with the main body of the Church. He later joined the reorganization under Joseph Smith III.

Isaac MORLEY (1786-1865), a native of Montague, Massachusetts, was baptized in November 1830 and was ordained a high priest in 1831. He suffered with other Church leaders through the persecutions in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1833. He was a patriarch in Far West, Missouri, in 1837. He played important roles in Nauvoo and in Utah.

Arthur MORRISON (?-?) was called on twice to testify at high council courts in Far West. One source identifies Morrison as accompanying George Hinkle under the flag of truce to come to terms with General Lucas during the siege of Far West. He died in Keokuk, Iowa.

Justus MORSE (1809-87) was born in Chester, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1833. He died in 1887 in Pleasanton, Iowa.

John MURDOCK (1792-1871) was born in Kortwright, New York, and joined the Church in November of 1830. He was a member of Zion's Camp. He, with George Hinkle, settled DeWitt, Missouri, in 1838, but was soon expelled. He served as a bishop in Nauvoo. He died in Beaver, Utah.

Samuel MURICH (1804-?), a native of Virginia, is listed as living in Jackson County in 1833, in Clay County in 1834, and in Far West in 1838. In Nauvoo, Murich was ordained an elder.

Emanuel MURPHEY (1809-71) was born in Union, South Carolina, and was baptized in 1836. He came to Utah in 1860 and died in Millcreek, Salt Lake County.

Samuel MUSICK (1804-?), a native of Virginia, owned a store in Far West, Missouri. He located in Nauvoo, where he was ordained an elder and later a seventy. He was endowed in the Nauvoo Temple in 1846.

James NEWBERRY (1791-1884) was born in New York and was held at the fall of Far West, but he was released soon afterwards. He helped the other Saints leave the state. He eventually united with the reorganization.

John S. NOAH (1800-?), a native of Pennsylvania, was a farmer and resident of Mentor County, Ohio. He never gathered with the Saints.

Chauncy ODLE (?-?) had his license taken away in Ohio at a conference held in Amherst, September 29, 1831.

John OUTHOUSE (1786-?) and his son, Joseph, were witnesses in the Murdock/Newberry schoolroom trial in Far West, March 24, 1838. He later moved to Nauvoo, where he was ordained a high priest in 1844.

Joseph OUTHOUSE (1820-?), a native of Illinois, attended the school of John Murdock in Far West. This led to his involvement in the trial of Murdock before the high council. He became a seventy in Nauvoo. After the prophet's death, the younger Outhouse joined the reorganization.

Jonathan T. PACKER (1817-89) was born in Perry, Ohio, and was baptized in 1836. He died January 29, 1889, at Safford, Arizona.

Ebenezer PAGE (1807-?), a native of Waterville, New York, ordained his brother, John E. Page, to the office of elder in 1833. He was among the prisoners taken at the fall of Far West.

Hiram PAGE (1800-1852), a native of Vermont, joined the Church in April 1830. He moved to Missouri in 1832, but left the Church in 1838. He was one of the Eight Witnesses.

John Edward PAGE (1799-1867) was born in Trenton, New York, and joined the Church in 1833. After a lengthy and successful mission in Canada, he moved to Missouri. His wife and two of his children died in Far West. He became a member of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles in 1838. He later became disaffected with the Church and was excommunicated in 1846.

Lorin PAGE (1813-?), the brother of John Edward Page, was born April 12, 1813, at Waterville, New York.

Sylvester PARKER (?-?) was a member of the Church in Ohio. He is listed as having left the Church.

Ezra PARRISH (1804-83) was born in Brownsville, New York, and served as an alternate high councilor in Nauvoo. He migrated to the Great Basin in 1850.

Warren PARRISH (?-?) was a member of Zion's Camp and the First Quorum of Seventy in Kirtland. He served as a scribe to Joseph Smith from 1835 to 1837. He renounced his membership in 1837.

Thoret PARSONS (1802-?), a native of New York, was ordained a seventy in Nauvoo in 1844.

Edward PARTRIDGE (1793-1840) was born in Pittsfield, Massachusetts, and was baptized in December 1830. He was the first bishop of the Church, serving as leader of the Saints in Jackson County, Missouri, from the time of the first settlement there. After living through all the trials of the Saints in Missouri, he died in Nauvoo.

Charles PATTEN (1811-?), a native of New York, was ordained a high priest in Nauvoo in 1844. He later served on a committee to aid the destitute of that city.

David W. PATTEN (1799-1838) was born in Theresa, New York, and joined the Church in 1832. He was ordained an Apostle in 1835. He was mortally wounded at the battle of the Crooked River in Ray County, Missouri, just before the fall of Far West.

John PATTEN (1787-1847), a native of New Hampshire, served on the high council in the Iowa Territory during the Nauvoo period.

Hezekiah PECK (1782-1850) was born in Vermont and was baptized a few months after the organization of the Church, in the summer of 1830. As a member of the Colesville settlement, he was one of the first Mormon settlers in Jackson County, Missouri. He served as a bishop in Nauvoo and died near St. Joseph, Missouri.

Joseph PENNOCK (?-?) was a resident of Nauvoo involved in a high council case held June 15, 1844.

William C. PERRY (1813-?) was born in New York and joined the Church in 1835. He left Kirtland for Missouri in the spring of 1838 with the Kirtland Camp. He later moved with the Saints to Nauvoo. There he served as an alternate member of the high council.

Ziba PETERSON (?-1849) was baptized in April 1830. He was ordained an elder soon after and was one of the four missionaries to the Lamanites later that year. He later left the Church, however, and in 1833 he was excommunicated.

David PETTIGREW (1791-1863), a native of Weatherfield, Vermont, joined the Church in 1832 and moved to Jackson County, Missouri. He was driven out of both Clay County and Caldwell County with the body of the Saints. He was jailed at the time of the fall of Far West. He was later a member of the Mormon Battalion, and died in Salt Lake City.

Ethan PETTIT (1810-84), a native of Hempstead, New York, was baptized in 1840 and soon after moved to Nauvoo. He migrated to Utah in 1848.

Albert PETTY (1795-1869) was born in Kentucky and joined the Church in 1835. He served as justice of the peace in Far West. He followed the Saints to Illinois and eventually to the Great Basin.

Burton H. PHELPS (1806-85) was born in Chenango County, New York. He received a "re-baptism" in 1863. He died in Payson, Utah.

Morris PHELPS (1805-76), a native of Northampton, Massachusetts, was baptized in 1831, settling in Missouri that same year. He was one of the Saints imprisoned after the fall of Far West. He reached Illinois and settled in Ramus, where his wife died. He moved to Utah in 1851.

William W. PHELPS (1792-1872) was born in Hanover, New Jersey, and was baptized in June 1831. He was appointed printer for the Church. He was a member of the presidency of the Church in Missouri from 1834-1838. He helped to found the city of Far West. He was excommunicated in 1838, but was received back into the Church in 1841. He died in Salt Lake City.

George PITKIN (1801-73), born in Hartford, Vermont, served as the sheriff of Portage County, Ohio, at the time of his baptism by Joseph Smith in 1831. In Far West, he was elected sheriff of Caldwell County. His first wife, Amanda Eggleston, died in 1847 in Iowa, during the exodus from Illinois. He later settled in Cache County, Utah.

Chauncey Warriner PORTER 1812-68) was born in Holland, New York, and married Emma Summer in 1833 while living in Clay County, Missouri. He migrated with the Church to the west, settling in Centerville, Utah.

Sanford PORTER (1790-1873) was born in Brimfield, Massachusetts, and joined the Church in 1831. By 1833 he had moved to Jackson County, Missouri. He migrated to the west in 1847 with the Charles C. Rich company.

Orson PRATT (1811-81) was born in Hartford, New York, and joined the Church in 1831. He was a member of Zion's Camp and of the high council in Clay County, Missouri, 1834. He was ordained an Apostle in 1835. He was excommunicated in 1842, but was rebaptized in 1843 and returned to his apostleship. He died in Salt Lake City.

Parley P. PRATT (1807-57), brother of Orson and a native of Burlington, New York, was baptized and ordained an elder in September 1830. He was ordained a high priest the next year. He was one of the great early missionaries of the Church, his missions including the Lamanite mission to Missouri in 1830-31. He became an Apostle in 1835. He was assassinated in 1857 while on a mission in Arkansas.

Allen RATHBURN (?-?) was proposed to be ordained a deacon at the conference in Far West on July 6, 1838. He was a property owner in Ray County, Missouri, in 1837. He testified for the state in the trial of Church leaders following the fall of Far West.

Robert RATHBURN (?-?) was among the Saints in Jackson County, where he operated a blacksmith shop. He is listed as one of the survivors of the Haun's Mill massacre.

David Harvey REDFIELD (?-?) was a resident of Kirtland. He traveled with the "poor camp" to Missouri in 1838. He was among those involved in the destruction of the Expositor press in Nauvoo in 1844.

Levi Harlow REDFIELD (1801-66), a native of North Madison, Connecticut, served on the Kirtland high council. He migrated to Utah in 1850.

Calvin REED (1821-95) was born in Ackworth, New Hampshire, and was baptized in 1833. He died at St. David, Arizona, in 1895.

Charles C. RICH (1809-83) was born in Kentucky and was baptized in 1832. He was a member of the Nauvoo high council and stake presidency. He led one of the early companies to the Great Basin. He became an Apostle in 1849. He helped colonize California and Cache County, Utah.

Leonard RICH (?-?) was a prominent member of the Church in Kirtland. He was a president of the First Quorum of Seventy. He became a dissenter, however, and was identified as a member of the mob that persecuted Joseph Smith and members remaining in Nauvoo as late as 1845.

Ezekial RIDER (?-?) was an early convert of the Church in Ohio and was tried before a bishop's court in Kirtland for slandering Bishop Whitney.

Sidney S. RIGDON (1793-1876) was born in St. Clair, Pennsylvania, and joined the Church in November 1830. He served as a member of the First Presidency from 1832 to 1844. He was jailed with Joseph Smith and other leaders in the Liberty Jail in the winter of 1838-39. He attempted to lead the Church after the Prophet's death. He was excommunicated in 1844.

Burr RIGGS (1810-60), a native of New Haven, Connecticut, was baptized and ordained an elder before June 1831. He was ordained a high priest later that year, but in 1833 he was excommunicated. He was baptized again by 1834 and marched with Zion's Camp. He later resided in Far West.

Alanson RIPLEY (?-?) was an early member of the Church in Ohio. He served in Zion's Camp in 1834 and later removed to Missouri. He was held for trial at the time of the fall of Far West. The high council directed him to serve as a bishop in Iowa in 1839, but this was reversed in 1841 when the conference failed to sustain him in that position. He was appointed surveyor for Nauvoo in 1841.

Ebenezer ROBINSON (1816-91) was born in Floyd, New York, and was baptized in 1835 by Joseph Smith. He lived in Ohio until 1837, when he moved to Far West, and later moved to Nauvoo. He became a member of the Reorganized Church after the death of the Prophet.

Lewis ROBINSON (1811-?), a native of Ohio, presented himself to the council meeting on August 6, 1834, in Clay County, Missouri, as being called on a mission. He was ordained a seventy in Nauvoo.

Orrin Porter ROCKWELL (1813-78), a native of Belcher, Massachusetts, was baptized on the day of the organization of the Church when only seventeen. He settled in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1831 and lived through all the Missouri expulsions. In Nauvoo he became a personal bodyguard to Joseph Smith. He was accused of the attempted murder of Lilburn W. Boggs, former Missouri Governor, but he was never actually tried on the charge. He died in Salt Lake City.

Shadrack ROUNDY (1788-1872) was born in Rockingham, Vermont, and was baptized in 1831. He was a prominent leader in Kirtland, Far West, and Nauvoo. He migrated to the Salt Lake Valley, where he served as a bishop.

Isaac RUSSELL (1807-44) was successful in founding a number of branches of the Church in his native England during the establishment of the English mission. However, he became disaffected with the Church during the difficulties in Far West. He was excommunicated in 1839.

Simonds RYDER (1792-1870), a native of Hartford, Vermont, joined the Campbellite movement in 1828 and was among the members of that group who joined the Church in 1831. His name was misspelled in a revelation calling him on a mission. His disillusionment over the misspelling led to his apostasy. He helped tar and feather Joseph Smith in 1832.

Henry Harrison SAGERS (1815-86) was born in Leroy, New York, and joined the Church in 1833. He served on the high council in Adam-Ondi-Ahman. He was charged with teaching "spiritual wifery" in Nauvoo, but apparently came west with the body of the Church.

William SCOBEY (1811-33) joined the Church in his native Fayette, New York, around 1831. He died while visiting relatives in Pontiac, Michigan, in 1833.

John SCOTT (1811-76) was a convert from Ontario, Canada. A member of the Nauvoo Legion, he died at Millcreek, Utah.

Absalom SCRITCHFIELD (?-?) was a member of the Church, first residing in Jackson County, then Clay, and finally in Far West, Missouri. He was charged with riot in the trials following the fall of Far West.

Daniel SHEARER (1791-1874), a native of Saratoga, New York, was among those arrested at the fall of Far West, but he was soon released. He was ordained a high priest in Iowa in 1847. He subsequently joined the Reorganized Church.

Jacob SHERMAN (?-?) was a priest in the Church in Ohio. He was present at the conference held June 3, 1831, in Geauga County. His name appeared in the Kirtland tax records for 1834-37.

Lyman SHERMAN (1804-39) was born in Monkton, Vermont, and was baptized in 1832. He moved to Kirtland in 1833 and was a member of Zion's Camp the next year. He was ordained as a President of the First Quorum of Seventy in 1835 and was in the high council in Kirtland and Far West. He was called to be an Apostle but was never ordained. He died in February 1839 in Far West.

Henry G. SHERWOOD (1785-1862), a native of Kinsbury, New York, was ordained an elder in 1832. He was a member of the high council in Kirtland and Nauvoo. He migrated to Utah in 1848.

Henry SHIBLEY (?-?) was an elder in the Church in Ohio and was a member of Zion's Camp in 1834. He was among those called to the First Quorum of Seventy in 1835.

Luman Andros SHURTLIFF (1807-84) was born in Montgomery, Massachusetts, and was baptized in 1836. During the Nauvoo period, he served in the Nauvoo Legion, and at the time of the exodus he was chosen to be captain of a company.

Roxana SLADE (?-?), the wife of Benjamin Slade, testified against Samuel Brown in the Hulet trial. Her husband was among the disaffected at Far West who testified for the state against the Church.

Albert SLOAN (?-?) was a member of the Church in Caldwell County, Missouri. He was ordained an elder in 1838.

Don Carlos SMITH (1816-41), a native of Norwich, Vermont, was brother to Joseph Smith. He was baptized in June 1830 and was ordained president of the high priests quorum in 1836. He took charge of the Elders' Journal in Kirtland and edited the Times and Seasons in Nauvoo. He was a member of the Nauvoo city council. He died in Nauvoo in 1841.

Emma SMITH (1804-79) was born in Harmony, Pennsylvania, and married Joseph Smith in 1827. She was baptized in June 1830 and was appointed to prepare a hymnal for the Church. She became the first president of the Relief Society in 1842. She married Lewis C. Bidamon in 1847, after the Prophet's death.

Hyrum SMITH (1800-1844), born in Tunbridge, Vermont, was the elder brother of Joseph Smith. He was one of the Eight Witnesses. He became the assistant counselor in the First Presidency in 1837 and then Second Counselor to Joseph Smith later that same year. He was called to the office of Patriarch in 1841. He was killed with the Prophet in 1844.

Jackson Osborne SMITH (1815-80) was born in North Carolina and served in Zion's Camp in 1834. He moved with the Church to Nauvoo and later to the Great Basin.

John SMITH (?-?) was an older man at the time he was baptized by Lyman Wight in 1831. He was ordained a high priest in 1831 but was disfellowshipped in 1833 for exercising his priesthood authority after his license had been demanded from him.

Joseph SMITH, Jr. (1805-44) was born in Sharon, Vermont, translated the Book of Mormon, and was the founder of the Church. He received the priesthood from heavenly messengers. He presided over the Church during the Kirtland, Missouri, and Nauvoo periods. He was killed in Carthage, Illinois, in 1844.

Joseph SMITH, Sr. (1771-1840), born in Topsfield, Massachusetts, was father of Joseph Smith, the Prophet. He was one of the Eight Witnesses. He became Patriarch of the Church in 1833. He was a member of the Kirtland high council and was appointed assistant counselor to the First Presidency in 1837. He died in Nauvoo in 1840.

Joshua SMITH (1788-1845), a native of Nobleborough, Maine, was baptized in 1836. He served as an alternate high councilor in Nauvoo. He died in Carthage, Illinois, where he had been summoned to attend court. Suspicion of poisoning by the militia of Carthage was confirmed by the post mortem examination.

Samuel H. SMITH (1808-44), a native of Tunbridge, Vermont, was a younger brother of Joseph Smith. He was one of the Eight Witnesses. He is traditionally recognized as the first missionary of the Church. He served as a member of the Kirtland high council. He participated in the battle of Crooked River in 1838. He died in Nauvoo in 1844.

Sylvester SMITH (ca. 1805-?) was baptized before June 1831. He was ordained a high priest in 1831, and in 1834 he was a member of Zion's Camp and is known for his heated arguments with the Prophet during the march. He was a member of the Kirtland high council, but became disaffected from the Church and was excommunicated by 1838.

William SMITH (1811-93), a younger brother of Joseph Smith, was born in Royalton, Vermont. He was ordained a high priest in 1833. He became a member of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles in 1835. He edited the Wasp in Nauvoo. He was excommunicated in 1845, following Joseph's death.

Willard SNOW (1811-52), a native of St. Johnsbury, Vermont, joined the Church after his two brothers, William and Erastus, in 1833. He marched with Zion's Camp and afterward was a member of the First Quorum of Seventy. He lived through the difficulties in Far West and Nauvoo, eventually settling in Salt Lake City.

William SNOW (1806-79), a native of St. Johnsbury, Vermont, was baptized in 1832. William served in the first legislature of the Utah territory.

Leonard SOBEY (?-?) served on the Nauvoo high council from 1841 to 1844, when he was disfellowshipped for following Sidney Rigdon.

Daniel STANTON (1795-1872), a native of Manlius, New York, was baptized by Parley P. Pratt in November 1830. He became a high priest in 1831. In 1840 he was ordained president of the Quincy, Illinois, Stake.

David STEPHENS (1819-99), the son of Alexander Stephens, was born in North Carolina. He married Rebecca Kimball.

Urial (also Uzziel) STEVENS (?-?) was brother-in-law to John E. Page. He was ordained an elder in 1835.

Levi STEWART (1812-76) was born in Edwardsville, Illinois, and joined the Church in 1837. Soon after, he moved to Missouri. He followed the Church to Nauvoo, and then to the West.

Calvin W. STODDARD (1801-36), a native of Palmyra, New York, married Sophronia Smith, sister of Joseph Smith. Stoddard was involved in many of the initial events of the organization of the Church. He died in 1836, presumably in Kirtland, Ohio.

Michael STOKER (1805-?), the son of Michael Stoker and Ana Barbara, was born in North Carolina. He married Martha McDavid.

Robert STONE (1777-?) was born in England. He received a patriarchal blessing in 1846. He served as an alternate high councilor in Nauvoo.

Hosea STOUT (1810-89), a native of Kentucky, married Surmantha Peck and gathered with the Saints in Missouri, although he was not baptized until 1838. He had to flee from Far West at the time of the fall of that city. He became clerk of the high council in Nauvoo and entered minutes in the Far West Record. He was also a captain of the police force. He later migrated to Utah.

Stephen STRONG (?-?), a Church member in Orange, Ohio, was ordained a priest in 1831.

Jonathan SUMNER (1774-?) was an elder of the Church in Missouri. His son, Josiah, was the subject of mob attacks during the violence in Jackson County.

Josiah SUMNER (1807-?) was born in Virginia and baptized in December 1830. He was whipped by a mob during the expulsion of the Saints from Jackson County. He was later ordained a seventy, but he left the Church before the end of the Nauvoo period.

John TAYLOR (1808-87), a native of Milnthorpe, England, was baptized in 1836 in Toronto, Canada. He became an Apostle in 1838. He edited the Times and Seasons and the Nauvoo Neighbor. He was seriously wounded by the mob that killed the Prophet at Carthage Jail. He survived and eventually served as President of the Church from 1880 to 1887.

Ezra THAYER (1790-?) was born in Randolph, Vermont, and was a bridge builder. He was baptized in October 1830, and became a high priest the next year. He joined with the Reorganized Church after the Prophet's death.

Robert B. THOMPSON (1811-41), a native of England, joined the Church in 1836 and moved to Kirtland the next year. He moved to Far West in 1838, only to be forced out soon after. He served with the Nauvoo Legion and was associate editor of the Times and Seasons. He died in Nauvoo.

Samuel THOMPSON (1813-92) was born in Pomfret, New York, and was baptized in 1833 by Brigham Young. He marched with Zion's Camp, and later became a seventy. He eventually migrated west with the main body of the Church.

Alva TIPPETS (1809-47) was born in Lewis, New York, and was baptized in 1832. He died in 1847 at Winter Quarters, Nebraska.

TUBB (?-?) was perhaps Amos Tubbs, a member in Jackson County and, later, Caldwell County.

Theodore TURLEY (1801-71), a native of England, was baptized in Ontario, Canada. He moved from there to Missouri and then to Nauvoo, eventually settling in Beaver, Utah.

Thomas TURNER (?-?) was ordained a seventy at Far West in July 1838.

Elizabeth VAN BENTHUSEN (?-?) married Sidney Gilbert. She was baptized with her husband in the winter of 1830-31 in Kirtland, Ohio. In spite of her husband's death in Missouri, she migrated with the Saints to Utah.

Truman WAIT (1810-?), a native of Vermont, was baptized before 1833, when he was called on a mission to the eastern states with Larkins Salisbury. In 1844 he lost his elder's license for running a grog shop.

Oliver WALKER (1782-1843) was born in New York and married Nancy Cressy in 1803. He died in 1843. His widow was subsequently sealed to Brigham Young.

George WALTERS (1796-?) was born in Norfolk, Connecticut, and was baptized in 1834. He was one of the large landowners in Caldwell County, Missouri. During the difficulties at Far West he left the Church and remained in Missouri when the body of Saints was forced out.

Elias F. WELLS (?-?) was a member of Zion's Camp and was approved for an advancement in the priesthood in 1834 in Clay County, Missouri.

Micah B. WELTON (1794-?) was born in Watertown, Connecticut, and was baptized in 1831 in Ohio. He was ordained a priest that same year. He received his endowment in the Nauvoo Temple in January 1846.

Nathan Ayres WEST (1808-?) was born in Hubbard, Ohio. He was involved in the Hulet Branch trial in Clay County in 1834.

Brother WHEELER (1814-?) was possibly Henry A. Wheeler, born August 29, 1814, in Benton, New York.

Harvey WHITLOCK (1809-?), a native of Massachusetts, was ordained a high priest in 1831. He located in Missouri that year. He was disfellowshipped about 1834 and excommunicated in 1838.

Christian WHITMER (1798-1835), a native of Pennsylvania, was baptized in April 1830. He was one of the Eight Witnesses. He moved to Missouri, where he was ordained a high priest. He was a high councilor in Clay County, where he died in full fellowship in 1835.

David WHITMER (1805-88), a native of Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, was one of the first members of the Church and one of the Three Witnesses. In 1834 he was ordained President of the Church in Missouri and successor to Joseph Smith. However, in 1838 he apostatized from the Church and was excommunicated.

Jacob WHITMER (1800-1856), born in Pennsylvania, was baptized in April 1830 and became one of the Eight Witnesses. He was driven from Jackson County, Missouri, in 1833 and followed the Church to Clay and then Caldwell County. He severed his connection with the Church in 1838.

John WHITMER (1802-78), a native of Pennsylvania, was an early member of the Church. He became an elder in June 1830. He was appointed Church historian in 1831 and was a member of the Presidency of the Church in Missouri from 1834 to 1838. He, like his brothers, was excommunicated for apostasy in 1838.

Peter WHITMER (1809-36), son of Peter Whitmer, Sr., and a native of Fayette, New York, was one of the Eight Witnesses. He served with the early Lamanite mission in 1830. He was appointed a member of the Clay County high council in 1836. He died in full fellowship in 1836.

Newel K. WHITNEY (1795-1850) was born in Marlborough, Vermont, and was baptized in November 1830. He was appointed bishop in Kirtland in 1831. He operated the Church store in Kirtland and was an intimate associate of the Prophet from 1831 until Joseph Smith's death. He died in Salt Lake City.

Lyman WIGHT (1796-1858), a native of Fairfield, New York, was baptized in November 1830 and was ordained a high priest in 1831. He became an Apostle in 1841 after having served as a military leader and valiant defender throughout the Missouri difficulties. After the Prophet's death, he led a group that settled in Texas. He was eventually cut off from the Church.

John WILKINS (1800-1890), born in Saratoga, New York, signed the agreement to help the poor leave Missouri in 1839. He eventually settled in Provo, Utah.

Thomas WILLES (1810-36) died in Tomkins, New York, only twenty-six years old.

Alexander WILLIAMS (1803-76), a native of Georgia, moved to Utah in 1848 and settled in Provo where he served as a counselor in a bishopric and as sheriff of the county.

Frederick G. WILLIAMS (1787-1842) was born at Suffield, Connecticut, baptized in November of 1830, and ordained a high priest the following year. In 1833 he became a counselor in the presidency of the high priesthood. He was dropped from the First Presidency in 1837 and was excommunicated in 1839.

Eleazer WILLIS (?-?) was a member in Tompkins, Ohio, and was ordained a priest at a conference at Orange, Ohio, in October 1831.

Calves WILSON (?-?) was baptized before October 1831. He was ordained a priest in 1831. He served a mission to Cincinnati, Ohio, in May 1832.

Lewis Dunbar WILSON (1805-56) was born at Milton, Vermont, and joined the Church in 1836. He became a member of the Nauvoo high council, serving from 1839 to 1846. He migrated to Utah in 1853.

Zecherriah WILSON (?-?) was a member of the Church who lived near Adam-Ondi-Ahman. He served as temporary member of the high council in Far West. He removed with the Saints to Illinois, serving a mission to southern Illinois in 1840.

Stephen WINCHESTER (1795-1873), a native of Vermont, joined the Church in 1833 and marched with Zion's Camp the following year. He removed, with his family, to Caldwell County, Missouri. At his home near Far West, David Patten died after being shot at Crooked River. Following his expulsion from Missouri and Illinois, he migrated to Utah.

Solomon WIXOM (1809-79) was born in Hector, New York, and was baptized in 1832. He was a member of the Pleasant Grove Bank in western Illinois along with Charles C. Rich. He migrated to Utah in 1850.

John WOODARD (1805-?), a native of Vermont, was baptized and ordained an elder in 1831. He was silenced by a conference of the Church held at Orange, Ohio, in September 1831.

Wilford WOODRUFF (1807-98) was born in Farmington, Connecticut, and was baptized in 1833 in New York. He became a member of Zion's Camp in 1834. He was ordained an Apostle in Far West in 1839, and served a mission to England soon after. He was President of the Church from 1889 to 1898.

Brigham YOUNG (1801-77), a native of Whitingham, Vermont, was baptized in 1832 and was a member of Zion's Camp in 1834. He served as an Apostle from 1835 to 1847. He was the central figure in leading the migration of the Saints to the Great Basin. He served as President of the Church from 1847 until his death in 1877.

Phineas Howe YOUNG (1799-1879), a native of Hopkinton, Massachusetts, and brother to Brigham Young, was baptized in 1832 and soon moved to Kirtland, Ohio. He migrated west in the first company, led by his brother. He was a printer by trade.

Henry ZABRISKIE (1788-1857), a native of Pennsylvania, joined the Church in 1832. After the fall of Far West, he was arrested, but was released shortly afterward. He migrated to Utah, eventually settling in Provo.

Lewis ZABRISKIE (1817-72) was born in Hamilton, Ohio, and was baptized in 1836. In 1839 he married Mary Higbee. He was expelled from Missouri and Illinois with the rest of the Saints. He migrated to Utah, settling in Spring City.
