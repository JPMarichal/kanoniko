Lyndon W. Cook, Donald Q. Cannon, Far West Record: Minutes of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, 1830-1844 ( 1983)

Preface

The Far West Record is an early Church minute book containing the proceedings of many general Church meetings and high council minutes from 1830-39. [^1] Essentially, there are only two general Church minute books for the Joseph Smith period, namely, the Far West Record and the Kirtland Council Minute Book. [^2] The Far West Record contains one hundred eighty-one handwritten pages of minutes in the hands of two notable recorders: Ebenezer Robinson and Hosea Stout. [^3] The brown ledger measures 8 1/2 inches by 13 inches. The ink is mostly brown, and two-thirds of the sheets are blank. Located in the office of the First Presidency, in Salt Lake City, the original has not been available for historical research; nevertheless, a typescript as well as a microfilm copy of the original have been made available to serious researchers since about 1960. [^4]

Efforts to publish the entire ledger began in 1974; permission was granted in February 1977. A prime source document for preparing the seven-volume History of the Church, the Far West Record provides information on a wide range of Church activities in five different states, particularly Ohio and Missouri.

The complete, unedited text of the Far West Record, as it appears in the original, is presented in this volume. Every effort has been made to publish a faithful copy of the document. The original spelling, punctuation, and paragraphing have been retained. The occasional use of a bracket denotes the insertion of a letter or word by the editors. Footnotes supply important historical, biographical, doctrinal, organizational, and procedural commentary to the manuscript. Biographical data for most of the people mentioned in the Far West Record will be found in the Biographical Appendix, alphabetically arranged. Other items in the appendixes supplement the text.

Notas:
[^1] 1. Also included in the Far West Record are minutes of two High Council meetings (dated 1 and 15 June 1844) in Nauvoo, Illinois. These minutes constitute three pages of the document.
[^2] 2. While there are several other Church record books for the Joseph Smith period (e.g., Kirtland Revelation Book, Scriptory Book of Joseph Smith, and Nauvoo High Council Minutes), only the two records referred to above served as "general" minute books.
[^3] 3. The Far West Record does not contain the original minutes as taken by the twenty-four clerks whose names appear in the document. The ledger served as a book of final recording for minutes taken earlier. Robinson and Stout were the recorders of the minutes, not the actual clerks.
[^4] 4. The first typescript of the Far West Record was made about 1930.
