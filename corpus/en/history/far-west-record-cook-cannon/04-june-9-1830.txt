Lyndon W. Cook, Donald Q. Cannon, Far West Record: Minutes of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, 1830-1844 ( 1983)

June 9, 1830

Minutes of the first Conference [^1] held in the Township of Fayette, [^2] Seneca County, State of New York, by the Elders of this Church, June 9th 1830. [^3] according to the Church Articles and Covenants

Elders Present: [^4]

Joseph Smith, junior

Oliver Cowdery,

Peter Whitmer,

David Whitmer,

John Whitmer,

Ziba Peterson

Ezekiel 14th read by Joseph Smith jr. and prayer by the same Articles and Covenants [^5] read by Joseph Smith jr. and received by unanimous voice of the whole congregation, which consisted of most of the male members of the Church. Samuel H. Smith was then ordained an Elder under the hand of Oliver Cowdery, & Joseph Smith seignior and Hyrum Smith were ordained Priests.

The following persons were then seated repectively & received their licences, [^6] Viz:

Elders of this church.

David Whitmer

John Whitmer

Peter Whitmer

Ziba Peterson

Samuel H. Smith

Priests of this Church

Martin Harris

Hyrum Smith

Joseph Smith sen

Ziba Peterson

Teachers of this Church

Hiram Page and Christian Whitmer.

Exhortation [^7] by Joseph Smith jr. and Oliver Cowdery, conference adjourned to the 26th September 1830, to be held in the same place. Br. Oliver Cowdery appointed to keep the Church record and Conference minutes until the next conference. [^8] Prayer by all the Brethren present [^9] and dismissed by Br. Oliver Cowdery. The above minutes were taken at the time of this conference by Oliver Cowdery. Clerk.

September 26, 1830

Minutes of the second Conference held by the Elders of this Church according to adjournment, Sept. 26, 1830. [^10]

Elders

Joseph Smith jr.

Oliver Cowdery

David Whitmer

John Whitmer

Peter Whitmer

Samuel H. Smith

Thomas B. Marsh.

Br. Joseph Smith jr. appointed leader of the Conference by vote. [^11]

Brother Joseph Smith jr. was appointed by the voice of the Conference to receive and write Revelations & Commandments for this Church. [^12]

The fifth chapter of Isaiah read by Br. Joseph Smith jr. & prayer by the same. Articles & Covenants read by br. Oliver Cowdery and remarks by Brother Joseph Smith jr.

No. of the several members uniting to this Church since the last Conference, thirty-five, making in whole now belonging to this Church sixty-two. [^13] Br. Newel Knight ordained a priest under the hand of brother Oliver Cowdery and prayer by the same. [^14] Prayer by all present. Exhortation by all the Elders respectively. Singing and prayer in behalf of Br. Oliver Cowdery & Peter Whitmer jr. who were previously appointed to go to the Lamanites. [^15]

Conference adjourned to January 1, 1831. [^16] to be held at this place. Br. David Whitmer appointed to keep the Church records until the next Conference. Prayer by br. Oliver Cowdery. The foregoing minutes were taken at the time of this Conference by Oliver Cowdery.

Notas:
[^1] This is the conference referred to in footnote 7 of the previous conference. Doctrine and Covenants 38, directing the Saints to remove to Ohio, was received during the conference. A meeting of the officers and elders of the Church.
[^2] See Doctrine and Covenants 47. Previous to this time both Oliver Cowdery and David Whitmer had been responsible for the records of the Church. At this time the Lord officially gave this responsibility to John Whitmer. In effect, he became the first Church historian. His history, known as The Book of John Whitmer, has recently been published with scholarly notes. (See F. Mark McKiernan and Roger D. Launius, eds., An Early Latter Day Saint History: The Book of John Whitmer [Independence: Herald Publishing House, 1980].) An association of branches or churches in a given geographical area. This developed after about 1835 and was a forerunner of the mission district. Other American religions such as the Methodists, Mennonites, and Congregationalists also held conferences. (See S. George Ellsworth, "A History of Mormon Missions in the United States and Canada, 1830-1850," Ph.D. dissertation, University of California, 1951, pages 150-51.)
[^3] See History of the Church 1:175-77 and Doctrine and Covenants 44. Although the term general conference is used here, this was not a general conference in the same sense that the Church holds general conference in April and October now. See Jay R. Lowe, "A Study of the General Conferences of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, 1830-1901," Ph.D. dissertation, Brigham Young University, 1972. [^4] When this conference was held, in June 1831, all of these men were members in good standing. However, in 1838, when these minutes were copied from loose pages into the Far West Record, the recorder inserted the notations in parentheses "(denied the faith)" or "(cut off)," thus indicating that these people had left the Church.
[^5] The conference minutes in this period listed those present by priesthood office, as, for example, elders, teachers, priests, followed by the names of the office holders.
[^6] The office of high priest.
[^7] Note that Joseph Smith was ordained by the man whom he had previously ordained (Lyman Wight).
[^8] See Doctrine and Covenants 52, 57. These revelations directed the Saints to hold a conference in Missouri.
[^9] See History of the Church 1:199. This conference was held in the home of Joshua Lewis, a convert of the Lamanite missionaries to Missouri.
[^10] Many of the meetings were opened by congregational singing.
[^11] The members referred to here are the members of the well-known Colesville Branch, who arrived in Independence July 25, 1831. Just prior to this conference Sidney Rigdon consecrated the land of Missouri for the gathering of the Saints, and Joseph Smith dedicated the temple site at Independence.
[^12] See Doctrine and Covenants 58:60.
[^13] It was customary in the early conferences to partake of the sacrament.
[^14] Joseph Smith arrived in Kirtland, Ohio, from Missouri on August 27, 1831. (See History of the Church 1:216.)
[^15] At the June 3, 1831, conference, Joseph, Sidney, and others had been ordained to the high priesthood, and now Oliver, upon his return from the Lamanite mission, was also ordained. Note the use of the minute book to record ordinations.
[^16] As an agent, Whitney and others transacted business for the Church. While the business was essentially temporal, i.e., related to the mercantile establishment, this was an ecclesiastical position. See Doctrine and Covenants 63:45.
