Deseret Gymnasium, October 31, 1910

1910-October 31-Original letter. Church Historian's Library, Salt Lake City, Utah.

The "Greetings from the First Presidency" of December 18, 1909, had mentioned the Deseret Gymnasium as being in course of construction. It said: "It is built for the physical development and training of the youth."

According to Andrew Jenson, assistant Church Historian, (Encyclopedic History . . . p. 181) the Deseret Gymnasium was "erected in 1910, at a cost of $200,000.00, on the corner of College Avenue and Temple St.

This letter to Stake Presidents encourages patronage for the Gymnasium from the youth of the Church.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Salt Lake City, Utah, October 31st, 1910. President Nephi L. Morris and Counselors, CITY.

Dear Brethren:

For several years past representations have been repeatedly made to us to the effect that a great many of our young people, especially young men, were patronizing other places of athletic amusement for the want of a suitable place of our own.

To meet this demand and to protect and safeguard our boys and girls in every possible way, the Deseret Gymnasium has been erected at considerable expense, and is now open. It is, as you know, a fine institution, and we feel that our own people are entitled to its advantages so far as they wish to enjoy them.

We shall appreciate it if you will bring this matter to the attention of your bishops, and urge them to encourage their young people to patronize and sustain the institution.

Your Brethren in the cause of Truth, JOSEPH F. SMITH, ANTHON H. LUND, JOHN HENRY SMITH, First Presidency.
