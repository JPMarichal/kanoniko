Reaction to termination of Reed Smoot case, February 23, 1907

1907-February 23-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives. Provo, Utah.

As indicated in a previous note, the Senate rejected the Dubois resolution, as amended by Senator Hopkins of Illinois, to deny Reed Smoot his seat in the United States Senate on February 20, 1907.

The letter below is the reaction of the President of the Church to the termination of the Smoot case and the seating of Apostle Reed Smoot as a United States Senator.

The letter requests Senator Smoot to convey the thanks of the President of the Church to the members of the minority committee composed of Senators Foraker, Beveridge, Dillingham, Hopkins and Knox for their support of the cause of truth and right. It also instructed Senator Smoot to convey to the President of the United States "our heartfelt appreciation of his absolutely fair and kindly consideration of your case; and please say to him also that all we have to offer in return is the absolute assurance of the righteousness of our cause, and the loyalty of our hearts." The President referred to was, of course, Theodore Roosevelt, with whom Senator Smoot had a warm relationship and who had used his influence in favor of Senator Smoot throughout the Smoot case.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Senator Reed Smoot, Washington, D.C.

Dear Senator:

The termination of your case by the action of the Senate on the 20th inst. was indeed most gratifying to us all. I, in common with your many friends, heartily congratulate you on the outcome, and in this connection I may be excused for expressing the hope that now, since the schemers and plotters who have so persistently sought to ride into political power at the expense of the Mormon Church, have so utterly failed in their purpose, that the doors of Congress will be for all time closed against all such men, and we be left to settle our local differences and troubles among ourselves.

I would thank you to present to Senators Foraker, Beveridge, Dillingham, Hopkins, Knox and other friends of right and justice my compliments, and say to them that I cannot express in words the feelings of my heart because of the high and noble stand taken by them in your case. And I would thank you to assure them, in view of their having accorded to you the rights which you are entitled to, and by so doing risked what men generally desire and seek, namely, the honors and applause of the populace, in view of this, I say, I would like those gentlemen to know, that if my own life as well as your life, and the lives of my ecclesiastical associates and the Mormon people generally, were not what we claim them to be-clean and virtuous before God and man, I would hang my head in shame, and feel that I and my people were unworthy to unloose the latchet of their shoes. Thank God, I can say to them and to all the world, that the "sanctity of the home" is indeed sacred to all Latter-day Saints, and in fact that there is nothing so sacred, not even life itself. No one knows this better than you yourself, and the fact that your own private life is invulnerable in this respect, ought to be, and is, living collateral testimony of the truth of what I say.

I wish from the bottom of my heart that those fairminded gentlemen could know my heart, and the hearts of the Mormon people; in fact, I wish we were known to the whole nation just as we are, that our fellow countrymen might read us and our lives as they read an open book. If this could he done, the current misunderstanding and wide-spread misrepresentation of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints as a body, of its officers individually and collectively, of its doctrines, its teachings and practices, which are most unjust, would forever cease; and the Mormon people would then take their proper place in the nation, and be accorded without stint the rights and privileges belonging to them in common with their fellow citizens.

But notwithstanding the wrongs that are being done us, we shall continue to love our fellow men, the nation and its institutions, and we shall continue to struggle and contend for the rights of freemen; and this we shall do not only because we are entitled to fair and equal treatment, but because we deem it to be our moral and religious duty so to do, for we regard all men as our brethren, the offspring of the same great parentage, and we regard the cause of liberty as the cause of our God, who is the God of our nation, and God of the whole world; and we shall also continue to regard the statesmen who contend for and uphold the right, as the friends and sons of God, as men who can he trusted with the ruling power of His children; and we love and admire all such regardless of creed or faith, and we know that God himself loves them, and that He will sustain them and honor them in this life and the life to come.

Kindly say to the President in my behalf, that God alone knows our heartfelt appreciation of his absolutely fair and kindly consideration of your case; and please say to him also that all we have to offer in return is the absolute assurance of the righteousness of our cause, and the loyalty of our hearts.

And here allow me to remind the President and the Senators who have believed in you and us rather than believe the accusations of disloyalty of our enemies, that no greater proof of loyalty can be found in the history of the whole world than that exhibited by the Mormon people in the year 1846, and that too, under the most adverse and forbidding circumstances. Here was a people on the open prairie a thousand miles from civilization, worn down by hardships from fatigue, exposure and want of clothing and food, having been driven from their homes and possessions, and having spent four long solitary months traveling afoot and in wagons, in the hope of finding a country where they could live and dwell in peace; they had buried the more feeble members of their company because of their physical inability to endure the effects of their accumulated sufferings; and while the camp was halted from dire necessity, unable to proceed on their way for the want of the necessaries of life;—under these circumstances, a call was unexpectedly made upon them by the general government for five hundred of their able bodied men to enlist in the regular army and march to Mexico, and we are proud to say the call was immediately responded to. Here, I repeat, while the Mormon people were in the most deplorable condition imaginable, without adequate shelter and practically without food, and while smarting under the loss of their homes and possessions, from which they had been ruthlessly driven, suffering also from the loss of scores of their own kindred men, women and children, including their Prophet and Patriarch, who had been martyred and become victims of cold-blooded butchery: under these circumstances, circumstances which really cannot be described and but faintly imagined, this extraordinary call made upon them was responded to in a most spontaneous manner, the wives, children and mothers of the enlisted men left to be cared for by their fellow pilgrims as best they could, and the service required of them faithfully and heroically performed.

And here let me add, the feelings of pure and unalloyed loyalty to our government which were deep-seated in the hearts of the Mormon people then, are still a part and parcel of our very being now, and indeed could not be otherwise, for the simple reason that as a community, we are an integral part of the nation itself, and the God whom we worship is the God of this nation, whose destinies from the very beginning He has guided and will continue to guide and overrule until His great and glorious purposes shall have been fully accomplished in and through the nation, and in and through us, His unworthy children, and the right and truth at last prevail. As well might the Jews accuse Jesus himself of intent to take away their place and nation, as our fellow citizens to accuse us of disloyalty to our government.

No, thank God, all such accusations are begotten and made in the spirit of falsehood, and we greatly rejoice because of this. Thank God, we love the government which protects us, and which was intended by the fathers to protect all its citizens in their civil and religious rights. Thank God, we honor and revere the memory of the men who made it possible for us and our religion to live and flourish on this land, for without the liberty of the land neither we nor our religion could endure the persecutions which would be heaped upon us by the wicked at heart, and by bigoted and misguided so-called Christian people. Thank God, we are able to appreciate, and do appreciate, the strong and fair-minded statesmen of the nation who have the courage of their convictions in face of the opposition wrought up by unprincipled politicians, through and by organized women, and the religious element of the nation; and I say from the bottom of my heart, God bless them and theirs now and forever, and grant that His spirit may ever illumine their minds, and strength be given them according to their day; so that they may never be found wanting in the hour of trial and necessity.

And as a United States Senator, I sincerely hope and pray, whenever the rights of your fellow men shall be involved in cases coming under your consideration and jurisdiction, that you will always be found equal to the occasion; no matter who or what may be the reputation or character of the persons affected, or what the circumstances may be, it must be a foregone conclusion that one of your faith entrusted with the rights and liberties of your fellow men must not only be the soul of honor and integrity, but one who can be absolutely relied upon as well to stand shoulder to shoulder with the stalwart men of State and Nation in perpetuating the life and spirit of our free institutions; and when this shall he the current understanding, our own struggles for freedom and equal rights will have been won, and not until then, and may God speed the day.

With love and best wishes, and again congratulating you, I am, yours for truth and right, JOSEPH F. SMITH
