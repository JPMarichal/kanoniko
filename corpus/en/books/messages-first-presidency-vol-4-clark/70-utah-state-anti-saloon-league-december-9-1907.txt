Utah State Anti-saloon League, December 9, 1907

1907-December 9-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives. Provo, Utah

As noted in the "P. S." at the bottom of the letter, John R. Winder, counselor in the First Presidency, who signed this letter of introduction of the Assistant General Superintendent of the Anti-Saloon League of America, was confident that his action in so doing would have the unanimous approval of the First Presidency.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Salt Lake City, Utah, December 9th, 1907. Senator Reed Smoot, Washington, D.C.

Dear Senator:

This will introduce the bearer to you, Dr. C. W. Young, of louisville, Kentucky, Assistant General Superintendent of the Anti-saloon League of America.

We take pleasure in informing you that Dr. Young, with associate gentlemen, is here, and has been for the week past engaged [in] organizing the Utah State Anti-saloon League which, as you will no doubt have seen by the daily papers of our city, is composed of all classes of people.

Dr. Young has been welcomed to our state and with general cooperation, it is believed that the object of the League will have the effect of creating necessary legislation to carry out its purposes.

Anything you can do to assist Dr. Young in his national work we feel assured you will take pleasure in doing.

I am, Yours very respectfully, JOHN R. WINDER.

P. S. Prest. Smith is at Provo today, and Prest. Lund has not come to the office yet, otherwise they would join me in this letter to you.
