Church influence in elections refuted, January 4, 1907

1907-January 4-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives. Provo, Utah.

Two main charges had remained throughout the three years of the Smoot hearing: (1) the L.D.S. Church leaders were still practicing polygamy and (2) the L.D.S. Church leaders dominated and dictated to the members in civil and political affairs. The Smoot case was now on the floor of the United States Senate, and the First Presidency, through their secretary, was supplying information to Senator Smoot to answer the charges being made against him and the Church on the floor of the Senate. This letter is in answer to the second charge.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Salt Lake City, Utah, January 4th, 1907. Senator Reed Smoot, Washington, D.C.

Dear Senator:

This in part is an answer to a telegram received from you, going to show the inaccuracy of the statement of Senator Burrows to the effect that a Mormon ecclesiastic's asking permission to run for public office is tantamount to his winning at the polls.

Apostle Anthon H. Lund ran for the Legislature as a Republican in 1891, and was beaten by his Democratic opponent, Peter Greaves. Both lived in the same town of Ephraim.

Ed. Callister undertook to supply you with two other cases which he thought would cover the whole ground. He is not in town today, will be home tomorrow when I will remind him of this.

Yours, &c., GEO. F. GIBBS

P. S. There are no doubt many other cases, but I have no means of ascertaining them.
