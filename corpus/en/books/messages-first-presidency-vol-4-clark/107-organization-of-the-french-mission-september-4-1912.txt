Organization of the French Mission, September 4, 1912

1912-September 4-Der Stern, 44:341-342 (November 15, 1912).

The letter appears in German in Der Stern. We reproduce a copy in English.

President Rudger Clawson, Liverpool, England

Dear Brother:

"Your communication of the 31st of July was read at our last council meeting, in which you recommend the organization of a new Mission, consisting of the following named Branches of the Swiss part of the Swiss and German Mission: Lausanne, Lyons, Chaux-de-Fonds, Paris, Hauchatel, and Geneva; also the following named Branches of the Netherlands Mission: Liege, Seraing, Verviers, Brussels, Lille; the population of the Branches of the Swiss and German Mission, including children under eight years of age, being 198, and the population of the Branches of the Netherlands Mission being 201, making in all 399, and that it be named the French Mission.

The Council consented to this being done, and you are therefore hereby authorized to affect the organization accordingly, the new mission to include also any other places in the Swiss and German and Netherlands Missions populated by French-speaking people.

Your recommendation that Elder Edgar Brossard, now laboring in Paris, be appointed President of the new Mission, is also approved.

Your Brethren, (Signed) JOSEPH F. SMITH, ANTHON H. LUND, CHARLES W. PENROSE.
