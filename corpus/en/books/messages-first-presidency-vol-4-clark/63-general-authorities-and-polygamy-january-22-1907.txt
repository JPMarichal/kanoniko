James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 4 ( 1970)

General Authorities and polygamy, January 22, 1907

1907-January 22-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives. Provo, Utah.

On December 13, 1906, Senator Dubois had made an exhaustive speech on the floor of the United States Senate in which he had charged, among other things, that the L.D.S. Church was still practicing polygamy. On December 14, 1906, Senator Reed Smoot, (as evidenced in the letter below of January 22, 1907) had sent a telegram to the First Presidency of the Church asking for evidence to refute Senator DuBois' charges. Seemingly the letter sent in response to the December 14th telegram and dated December 15th was lost in transit Senator Smoot again requested the information and the First Presidency instructed their secretary, George F. Gibbs, to send a copy of the December 15th letter containing the desired information.

It is evident from these documents that the natural process of "phasing out" of polygamy was actually taking place among the General Authorities of the Church. It is to be noted, from this official report of the First Presidency, that in 1890 (the year of the Woodruff Manifesto) there were 22 polygamists [the addition of Joseph F. Smith would make 23] and 3 monogamists among the General Authorities of the Church. Ten years later, in 1900, there were 16 polygamists and 10 monogamists. By 1906 the pendulum had swung in the other direction and there were 17 monogamists and only 9 polygamists.

The minority report of the Senate committee issued June 11, 1906, had said:

"The conditions existing in Utah since Reed Smoot became an official of the Mormon church in 1900 have been such that non-Mormons and Mormons alike have acquiesced in polygamous cohabitation on the part of those who married before the Manifesto of 1890, as an evil that could best be gotten rid of by simply tolerating it until in the natural course of events it shall have passed out of existence." Proceedings in the Smoot Case 4:505.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Salt Lake City, Utah, January 22nd, 1907. Dear Senator:

I send you herewith enclosed a copy of a letter dated December 15th last, containing a list of the General Authorities of the Church, asked for in your telegram of the 14th ult., showing the number of the General Authorities living in polygamy in 1890, and the number in monogamy the same year; the number living in polygamy in 1900, and the number in monogamy the same year; the number living in polygamy in 1906, and the number in monogamy the same year.

Can it be possible that the original letter failed to reach you? It has been concluded to confine the balance of the information you have written for along this line to Stake Presidencies, and we are in hopes of mailing it tomorrow. Yours, &c., GEO. F. GIBBS, Secretary.
