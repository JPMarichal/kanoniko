History of the Church and Americana, October 4, 1909

1909-October 4-Original letter. Church Historian's Library, Salt Lake City, Utah.

This letter which went to all Stake Presidents in the Church asks the Stake President to appoint a person in each stake who would act as an agent for The Americana magazine which was currently publishing a "History of the Mormon Church" written by B. H. Roberts. This history so published serially in The Americana from June, 1909, until July, 1915, was later revised by B. H. Roberts, brought up to the date of April 6, 1930, and became the present Comprehensive History of the Church in six volumes.

For additional background see the Preface to volume 1 of the CHC (CHC 1:v-x), which states that the manuscript "was read to a committee of leading elders of the church, eminent for their experience and knowledge of the doctrines and history of the churchÂ�. They were appointed by the First Presidency of the church." (1:x)

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Salt Lake City, Utah, October 4th, 1909. Prest. Nephi L. Morris,

Dear Brother:

We call your attention to "Americana," a monthly magazine published by the National Americana Society, New York, the magazine is devoted chiefly to Historical and Genealogical subjects, and cannot fail to be of very great interest to a community, such as ours, that is vitally concerned in historical and genealogical questions. This magazine is one of the very few high class magazines published in the United States. It appeals chiefly to the scholarly and highly intellectual classes of the community, interested in the subjects with which it deals.

The magazine has opened its pages for the publication of a detailed, exhaustive "History of the Mormon Church," by Elder B. H. Roberts, to be illustrated with beautiful steel engravings and photogravures of the principal characters and places identified with the coming forth of the great latter-day work. Three articles of the series with accompanying illustrations have already appeared, and are in every way satisfactory, and give good earnest of the success of the whole series, which will run through two years of the magazine.

The opportunity afforded us by the publishers of Americana to tell in this adequate way our own story, is something of a new experience; for never before has such an opportunity been given to us to tell our own story of the rise, progress and present status of the Church to the world. This history in Americana will keep Mormonism in some one or other of its developing phases before a large number of people in the United States, for two years; and before a class that could not otherwise, perhaps, be reached. These articles, therefore, will be of greater influence for good to the Church than any book published upon the subject of Mormonism could be.

In view of the great good that will doubtless accrue to the Church by this proclamation of its message to the readers of Americana," it has occurred to us that there would be some Latter-day Saints in all the Stakes of Zion who would be glad to subscribe for this magazine, both that they might have the history, because of its own intrinsic value as Mormon literature (together with the other very valuable reading matter in the magazine), as also to give encouragement to the publishers who have intrepidly afforded us this hearing before the world.

We, therefore, request you, as President of the Stake, to appoint some person who will be capable and reliable to visit such brethren and sisters within your stake as can well afford to subscribe for "Americana," and solicit their subscription, collect the subscription price, giving receipt for same, and send the names with price of subscription to David L. Nelke, 154 East 23rd Street, New York City, less the commission allowed by the publishers, which is twenty five per cent of the subscription price. The price of the magazine is $4.00 per annum.

The very liberal commission paid by the publishers will make it worth while for some energetic young man or woman to give their attention to this business. We are anxious that it should be given into the hands of competent and trustworthy persons, and therefore refer the matter of making the appointment to you, and ask you to give it your prompt attention.

Enclosed you will find blank agent's appointment signed by Mr. Nelke, the President of "Americana Society," which you will fill out with the name of the agent you appoint.

All this is in the interest of the Lord's work, and we pray that it may be blessed to the accomplishment of great good. Very truly your brethren, JOSEPH F. SMITH, JOHN R. WINDER, ANTHON H. LUND, First Presidency.
