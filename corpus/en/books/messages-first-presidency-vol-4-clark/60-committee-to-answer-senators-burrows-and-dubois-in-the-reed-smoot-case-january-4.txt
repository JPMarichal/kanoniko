Committee to answer Senators Burrows and DuBois in the Reed Smoot case, January 4, 1907

1907-January 4-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives. Provo, Utah.

The tense situation in the Reed Smoot case in the first seven weeks of 1907 is thus described by B. H. Roberts, who it will be noted was a member of the eight-man committee appointed by the First Presidency (as given below) to review the speeches being made on the floor of the United States Senate as the Reed Smoot case reached a final "showdown."

"The case was called up in the senate on the 13th of December, 1906, when Senator Dubois, of Idaho, made an exhaustive speech supporting the resolution of the committee, viz: 'that Reed Smoot was not entitled to a seat as a senator of the United States from the state of Utah. And from thence to the time that the vote was taken upon the resolution-20th of February, 190"-many senators made speeches upon the subject, and Utah affairs were fully discussed through all that timeÂ�. "Had the vote been taken in the senate upon this resolution in that form, a majority voting would have determined the question. But an amendment was offered by Senator Hopkins of Illinois, making the resolution read: "Two-thirds of the senators present concurring therein,' and on the resolution so amended the vote stood; yeas 28; nays 42; not voting 20. 'So the resolution was rejected, two-thirds of the senators present not voting therefore' and Mr. Smoot retained his seat." CHC 6:398-399.

Dr. James E. Talmage, head of the Department of Geology and Mineralogy at the University of Utah was also a member of the same eight-man committee under appointment of the First Presidency to handle the final stages of the defense of the Church in the Reed Smoot case. On January 4, 1907, he also wrote to Senator Smoot as follows:

State School of Mines University of Utah

Salt Lake City, Utah, Jan. 4, 1907 Hon. Reed Smoot U. S. Senate

Dear Brother Smoot—

A happy new year and an unquestionable victory be yours.

In connection with certain other brethren I am looking over the speeches in the Senate thus far delivered and hope to scrutinize, if not study, all utterances bearing on the case. As I am not certain or assured as to receiving reports of speeches, etc., in the case I want to ask you to see that at least one copy (preferably a few or several) of each speech or other effort in your case now pending be sent me with all dispatch. To avoid delay in delivery, please address all to me at home-970 First Street, Salt Lake City. I have no copy of speeches by Burrows or DuBois.

Yours heartily, J. E. Talmage

P. S. It may be well to send me one copy or several copies of the "Record." In an interview with the First Presidency today I learn that no copies of this publication are received at the President's Office, neither at the Historian's office.

Yours for success, J. E. T.

The final sentence of the letter below to Senator Smoot, written on the letterhead of the First Presidency and signed by the secretary to the First Presidency, is significant:

"This answer, however, [to be prepared by this committee appointed by the First Presidency] is for the purpose of our own defense as a Church."

This statement, coming at the close of the Smoot hearings, again emphasizes the point that the Smoot hearings were really a trial of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints before the United States Senate and before the nation and the world. Hence the heavy involvement of the First Presidency in all phases of the Smoot case. For additional background see CHC 6:390-411; also the four volumes of Proceedings in the Smoot Case, and the Congressional Record, vol. xli, part 3.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Salt Lake City, Utah January 4th, 1907. Senator Reed Smoot, Washington, D.C.

Dear Brother:

Just a line for your information, to say that a committee consisting of Whitney, Roberts, McKay, Talmage, Morris, LeGrand Young, F. S. Richards and R. W. Young, has been appointed to go through the speeches of Senators Burrows and DuBois for the purpose of preparing for publication an answer to such parts of their speeches as misrepresent and wilfully vilify the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints; and it is thought that there may be points in the answers of the committee which you may be able to use yourself in your proposed speech before the Senate. This answer, however, is for the purpose of our own defense as a Church.

Yours, &c., Geo. F. Gibbs Secretary.
