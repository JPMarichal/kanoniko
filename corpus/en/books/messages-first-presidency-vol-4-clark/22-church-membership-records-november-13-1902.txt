DEAR BROTHER:

It is very desirable, and in fact of the highest importance, that every member of the Church should have a standing in the ward where he resides. Through indifference or neglect, or from some other cause, there are numerous members of the Church "floating" around and living in various wards, but with no standing on the records. This evil is widespread.

We invite your earnest attention to the matter and would suggest that if there is any member of the Church, or family, residing in your ward whose names do not appear on the records, you take up a labor with them, pointing out the importance of their taking immediate steps to secure membership in your ward, that they may thereby receive proper attention and be identified with the organization of the Church, as well that they may become amenable to the authority of the presiding priesthood.

We have learned with some surprise that in a number of instances district school teachers have resided and taught in a ward for months without any standing therein, and it appears that in several cases teachers have failed to pay their tithing for the reason, first, that they were absent from home, and, second, that they had no standing in the ward where they were teaching, and third, that they were not looked after. This is all wrong and should be remedied.

We do not feel that it would be wise to wait for people to bring in their recommends, but our bishops through acting teachers and ward clerks should be on the alert, and search out those who have failed to make application for membership and see that they procure their recommends.

We advise that any member of your ward who signifies his intention of removing permanently be instructed to take a recommend with him and join the ward where he expects to reside. If the party is not in full fellowship but simply has a standing in the Church, recommend him as such. An exception may be made to the foregoing general rule in the case of parties who take up their residence in a ward temporarily, but who wish to retain their standing in the places where they live. In all such cases their bishop should give them a recommend of standing, setting forth that they are members of the Church in such and such a ward and stake. This would entitle them to all the privileges of the Saints, namely, to attend meetings, partake of the sacrament, receive visits from the teachers, and be to all intents and purposes under the jurisdiction of the bishopric during their stay in said ward, although their names do not appear on the ward record.

You will readily see that a strict observance of this rule will bring about good results, for every member of the Church will be carefully looked after, and there will be no time in his life-either long or short-but that he will be subject to the authority of the priesthood; and further, he will be relieved from the embarrassment that arises when he applies for a recommend to the temple or a recommend of removal and cannot secure it.

In the case of school children in your ward, who have come from a distance, and who desire to retain their standing in the wards where their parents reside, we suggest that you take their names and look after them in a general way. They should be encouraged to attend ward meetings and join ward organizations.

The subject of this communication is of very great importance, and we trust will receive careful attention, and this letter should be kept on file for future reference.

Your brethren in the covenant, JOSEPH F. SMITH, JOHN R. WINDER, ANTHON H. LUND.
