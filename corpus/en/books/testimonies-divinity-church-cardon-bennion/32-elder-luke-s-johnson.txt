Various General Authorities, Joseph E. Cardon, Samuel O. Bennion, Testimonies of the Divinity of the Church ( 1930)

Elder Luke S. Johnson

Member of the First Council of Twelve from February 15, 1835, to April 13, 1838.

118Elder Johnson was born November 3, 1807, at Ponfret, Vermont. He was engaged in farming with his father until he received the Gospel. He was baptized by Joseph Smith in 1831, and was soon afterwards ordained a priest, and went on a mission to southern Ohio, where he baptized several and organized a branch in Chippewa. Shortly after this, in company with Sidney Rigdon, he baptized fifty or sixty in New Portage, Ohio, and organized a branch.

118October, 1831, Brother Johnson was ordained a high priest by the Prophet Joseph Smith, and in 1832-33, in company with two others, he traveled as a missionary through Ohio, Virginia, and Kentucky, and baptized over one hundred persons and organized branches of the Church. At the organization of the first High Council of the Church, February 17, 1834, Elder Johnson was chosen one of its members. The following summer, he went as a member of Zion's Camp to Missouri and back. February 14, 1835, he was chosen and the following day ordained one of the Twelve Apostles under the hands of the Three Witnesses. During this summer he traveled through the Eastern States, holding conference, preaching the Gospel and regulating the branches.

118Brother Johnson performed considerable missionary work, baptized many people, and bore his testimony to thousands. He rendered the Prophet Joseph valuable assistance in protecting him from his enemies.

118 After all his faithful labors for the cause of Christ, he partook of the spirit of speculation, which at that time was possessed by many of the Elders and Saints at Kirtland, and neglected his duties as an Apostle and Saint. At a conference held at Kirtland September 3, 1837, he was disfellowshipped. On the following Sunday, however, he confessed his faults, and was received back into fellowship, but was finally cut off for apostasy at Far West, Mo., April 13, 1838.

119He continued friendly to the Church, and his former associates in the Priesthood, however, and in 1846 he was rebaptized in Nauvoo, and went to Great Salt Lake Valley in 1847 as one of the 143 Pioneers. In the year 1858 he settled in St. John, Tooele Co., Utah, and was appointed Bishop when the ward was organized. On the 9th of December, 1861, he died in the house of his brother-in-law, Orson Hyde, in Salt Lake City. After his return to the Church he lived the Gospel to the best of his ability and died in the faith.
