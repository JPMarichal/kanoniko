John A. Widtsoe, Understandable Religion ( 1944)

CHAPTER TWENTY-ONE

THE HEAD OF THE CHURCH

Jesus, the Christ, lived in a small corner of the world. His labors were chiefly with the humble. He was at last crucified between two thieves. The greatness of the world was not His.

So insignificant was He, that the historians of the day scarcely mention Him. He should by all rights have gone down to oblivion.

Indeed, some of His enemies have gone so far as to declare that He did not live; that He is only a legend or myth set up by imaginative people. Strauss of Germany, Renan of France, Brandes of Denmark, all eminent scholars have written books to prove this thesis. [^1] Unfortunately, for the writers, their mode of reasoning would cast serious doubt upon the existence of almost every historical character who lived a hundred years or more ago, from Isaac Newton to Paul Revere.

Despite the humble life of Jesus, His teaching has spread over the earth, until almost one-third of the world's population are his followers. Moreover, out of the Christian nations, with all their faults and failures, has come the progress of the modern world. To deny the reality of the existence of Jesus on earth, is much like declaring that the running stream has no source.

The first Napoleon, selfish and bloody, was not a religious man. With hard eyes he looked at men and history. Yet, he is reported to have said about Christ: "Everything in him astonishes me. His spirit overawes me, and his will confounds me. Between him, and whomever else in the world, there is no comparison . . . I know men; and I tell you that Jesus Christ is not a man. Superficial minds see a resemblance between Christ and the founders of empires, and the gods of other religions. That resemblance does not exist. There is between Christianity and whatever other religions the distance of infinity." [^2]

Hundreds upon hundreds of other trained thinkers have borne similar testimony; and millions of men and women, the kind Jesus loved so well, know not only that He lived, but that His doctrine enlivens the soul, comforts the downcast, and urges all onward to higher levels of conquest.

The world's confusion about Jesus, the Christ, concerns His nature and His place in the plan of salvation. By slow degrees, during the ages of apostasy, interpretations of Jesus and His mission on earth, were set up, that made Him little more than a man-made figure. [^3] His followers have made Him mistakenly a distant, insubstantial personality.

When, at last, the gospel was restored through Joseph Smith the Prophet, the theological fog was lifted. Jesus was revealed, as was done in former days, the central figure of the plan of salvation; but close in origin and purpose to struggling men and women. He became understandable to the mind; and near to our feelings.

The story of Jesus begins in the life before this. In that pre-existent spirit world He was the first begotten of the Father. [^4] Therefore, since we were all begotten by the Father in the spirit world, Jesus becomes our Elder Brother. [^5]

When God, the Father, had prepared the plan of salvation for our benefit, He entrusted the leadership of the work to His eldest Son, whom we know as Jesus the Christ. With the power delegated to him, Jesus was to organize the earth; place man upon it; and guide humanity until they should return to the spirit world, with their earth-won, spiritualized bodies, and trained for eternal progression.

This commission was accepted by Jesus. Thus, He became, in all matters pertaining to the earth and humanity, the official representative of our Father in Heaven.

In conformity with the plan, Jesus, with chosen helpers, created the earth and all things upon it. In the words of the Apostle John: "The worlds were made by him; men were made by him; all things were made by him, and through him, and of him." [^6]

In recognition of this appointment, the plan of salvation, with its body of truth, principles and laws, is called the gospel of Jesus Christ. For that reason, also, we pray to the Father in the name of Jesus.

This commission places Him at the head, always under the Father, of the community of obedient believers in the plan of salvation, known as the Church. Because of this leadership, the Church is called the Church of Jesus Christ, or the Church of the Firstborn. The phrase "of Latter-day Saints" distinguishes the members from those of the early-day Church. The two terms also bring to the fore, the respective relationships of Jesus and His followers, in furthering the cause of the Church.

Among the requirements made of Jesus, as the leader of God's plan, the atonement is of widest importance.

The experiences of earth, that may lead to a fulness of eternal joy, can be won only in an earthly, mortal body. To subject an immortal spirit to mortality, required the breaking of some law, the punishment of which would be death. This, our first parents did, so that the eternal plan might be initiated. A lesser law was broken that a higher law might be honored. Lesser laws are broken in our every day life, so that we may attain a greater good.

The famous fall of man was a necessary part of the plan of salvation. Without it, the purpose of the Lord could not have been accomplished. That raises our first parents above the calumny of being chief sinners on earth, hurled at them by many a preacher. Instead, that makes them worthy of the greatest honor and respect.

This was understood by Adam; for he said, after he was driven out of Eden: "Blessed be the name of God, for because of my transgression my eyes are opened, and in this life I shall have joy and again in the flesh I shall see God." (Moses 5:10) And Eve spoke in like vein: "Were it not for our transgression we never should have had seed, and never should have known good and evil, and the joy of our redemption, and the eternal life which God giveth unto all the obedient." [^7]

Nevertheless, a law was broken. Eternal justice required some atonement. Unless the penalty were paid, the bodies of men would sleep in their graves forever. That would defeat the plan, in part, since our bodies are needed in the hereafter to accomplish the work of eternity.

In accordance with the pre-ordained plan, Jesus undertook to pay this debt. He came on earth in a mortal body, one subject to death. He suffered an earthly death. Then He brought His body out of the grave, no more subject to mortality.

This He did that the bodies of all humankind might be assured of a resurrection from the grave. Saint and sinner, of every color and condition, would recover their bodies by His vicarious act. Every person who has lived on earth will, unconditionally, possess his body forever. He cancelled out the results of Adam's act. Vicarious service is common in everyday life. Look about you. One man may unite the wires, and flood with light the city that lies in darkness. There is no reason why such acts, though they are not fully understood, may not belong to the heavenly as the earthly sphere.

In His constitution, Jesus was different from all other men. He was born of woman, but His father was God. He was the Only Begotten of the Father on earth. [^8] He was both the son of man, and the Son of God. He was both a man and god. Therefore, He could do work beyond the power of man.

Therefore, also, as He hung upon the cross, He suffered pains beyond those of a man. In His own words: "Behold, I, God, have suffered these things for all, that they might not suffer if they would repent . . . which suffering caused myself, even God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to suffer both body and spirit—and would that I might not drink the bitter cup, and shrink—nevertheless, glory be to the Father, and I partook and finished my preparations unto the children of men." [^9]

He gave His life, and accepted His sufferings, voluntarily, for the consummation of His Father's plan. He did no sin; in Him the Father was pleased; His blood was shed that the Father might be glorified; and that all who believe on His name might come to God, and have everlasting life. [^10] The act of His atonement did much else besides providing the resurrection from the grave. It has deep spiritual implications.

Jesus gave "his life a ransom for many." [^11] In that respect all are equal. But, the place, or degree of salvation, that may be attained in the hereafter is determined, under God's merciful justice, by our acts. In that respect we may be unequal. Jesus died that we might recover our bodies, but, by righteous living, these bodies may be used for eternal progression. That is, all are granted eternal existence, but all may not convert that existence into rich and joyous eternal life.

The distinction between mere existence and active life was implied by Jesus when He declared, "I am the resurrection, and the life." [^12] He also said, more specifically, "All that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation." [^13]

Another part of Jesus' earthly mission was to teach anew the truths of the gospel, restore the power of the Priesthood, and re-establish the Church of Christ. Jesus had already set before mankind, at the beginning of man's life upon earth, the truths of the gospel, which must be accepted and obeyed, to win salvation. Men must not be allowed to walk in darkness. The desire of God is that we shall all enjoy the greatest gift in His keeping, that of eternal life. [^14]

After man's successive apostasies from the gospel, Jesus, Himself, while He dwelt on earth, in the meridian of time, laid the gospel message clearly before the people of His day. He conferred on men the lost priesthood. Thereby, His followers might perform with authority the required ordinances of the gospel. He restored all that had been lost. In His own words, He came "to fulfill all, righteousness." [^15] Thus, by His teaching and the gift of authority, He provided the means for carrying on, again, the work of the Lord as required under the plan of salvation.

During His earthly ministry, Jesus taught the gospel, and the spirit of it, in a fulness never before recorded. In the brief records of His life and words, lie the wisdom of heaven and the way to happiness here and hereafter.

In performing this part of His mission, truth and light became the major themes of His teaching. Truth and light are like the two sides of a shield. They are always associated. He declared that "nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest; neither anything hid, that shall not be known and come abroad;" [^16] and that "Ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free." [^17] And He added, "If any man walk in the day, he stumbleth not, because he seeth the light." [^18] and because of His commission, He said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life." [^19]

Always He recognized that the gospel, to have value, must shape human lives. Therefore, He was especially concerned with life, active living. Speaking of His mission to mankind, He said, "I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly." [^20] But, He made it clear that He thought of life, whether here or hereafter, beyond that of mere material things, for He said on one occasion, "A man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things he possesseth;" [^21] and also, "Life is more than meat, and the body is more than raiment." [^22] He urged men to seek the higher life, the life of the spirit; and explained, "The words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life." [^23]

In our day of worship of material things, could any message be more important!

He also explained how that higher life might be secured. "If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments." [^24] "He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life: and he that believeth not the Son shall not see life." [^25] This is the ancient message to every man. To win happiness, he must conform to eternal law.

All this, His atonement, teaching, and sacrificing, was done because of His love for His fellowmen. He recognized the weaknesses of humanity. He sought to guide erring men, His very brethren and sisters, to the truth and light of the gospel, and into eternal salvation. He tried to be the friend, helper, and protector of all. He looked with forgiving, merciful eyes upon the stumblings and risings of mortal beings.

He has ever been our advocate with the Father, to seek from Him forgiveness for our sins. Hear the noble, beautiful words, spoken by the Lord through the Prophet Joseph Smith: "Listen to him who is the advocate with the Father, who is pleading your cause before him . . . who knoweth the weakness of man and how to succor them who are tempted." [^26]

And the exultant cry of Paul the Apostle, "For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord." [^27]

He has never been far from us, but always near to us, in kinship, love, and desire to help us. We may speak to Him directly, for He is our brother. There is no need of an intermediary between us and our Elder Brother.

It is the testimony of multitudes that those who listen to our Elder Brother, and seek help from Him, may more easily solve their problems, and find comfort for their souls.

Jesus, who after His resurrection ascended into heaven, will again come to earth. Then He will come in glory; and with glorious attendants. He will assume control and direction of earthly affairs. Under His personal supervision, the work that He initiated at the beginning of time will be brought to conclusion. During this millennial period Satan will be restrained from broadcasting his evil temptations. The mistakes due to human imperfections will be corrected; the loose ends will be united; the missing parts provided.

At length, the plan having been consummated, Jesus will render an account of His stewardship to the Father, and present the completed task to Him. Then, at last, the children of Adam will enter upon their eternal activities and joys, each in the place assigned under the merciful judgment of the Father.

The faith of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and every member thereof, is placed upon the Lord Jesus Christ. Our knowledge we have received from Him. He is our captain and leader; Jesus, the Savior of the world. [^28] To Him we give our love and devotion. In Him we trust. Our hearts overflow with gratitude for His gifts to us.

There is no lingering fear that He will be defeated. Instead, there is the firm assurance that, in time, His principles and power will govern the earth, and every tongue confess and every knee bow to Him who is the Lord of all.
