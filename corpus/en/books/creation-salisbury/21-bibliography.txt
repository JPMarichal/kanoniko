Frank B. Salisbury, Creation ( 1976)

Bibliography

The following books provide general references to the indicated topics. For the most part, they are books I am familiar with, although there are numerous other texts that would be equally good sources. Books that I have relied upon most are marked with an asterisk.

ASTRONOMY

~Abell, George. Exploration of the Universe. New York: Holt, Rinehart, and Winston, 1969. An outstanding text!

Baker, Robert H., and Lawrence W. Fedrick. Astronomy. New York: Van Nostrand Reinhold, 1971.

Dixon, Robert T. Dynamic Astronomy. Englewood Cliffs, N. J.: Prentice-Hall, 1971.

Hynek, Joseph Allen. Astronomy. Menlo Park, Calif.: W. A. Benjamin, 1972.

Inglis, Stuart J. Planets, Stars, and Galaxies: An Introduction to Astronomy. 3d ed. New York: Wiley, 1972.

~Jastrow, Robert. Red Giants and White Dwarfs. New York: Signet Book, New American Library, 1969.

GEOLOGY

Dunbar, Carl O., and Karl M. Waage. Historical Geology. 3d ed., New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1969.

Flint, Richard Foster, and Brian J. Skinner. Physical Geology. New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1974.

Hurley, Patrick M. How Old Is the Earth? Garden City, N. Y.: Anchor Books, Doubleday & Co., 1959.

Press, Frank, and Raymond Siever. Earth. San Francisco: W. H. Freeman & Co., 1974. An excellent modern text emphasizing plate tectonics.

~Shelton, John H. Geology Illustrated. San Francisco and London: W. H. Freeman and Co., 1966. Beautiful aerial photographs of geologic features.

Spencer, Edgar Winston. The Dynamics of the Earth. New York: Thomas Y. Crowell, 1972.

~Wyllie, Peter J. The Dynamic Earth: Textbook in the Geo-Sciences. New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1971.

BIOLOGY

Curtis, Helena. Invitation to Biology. New York: Worth Publishers, 1972. One of the many introductory texts written for nonscience majors.

~Hardin, Garrett. Nature and Man's Fate. The New American Library, 1959. An excellent summary of Darwin and Mendel, written for the layman.

~Keeton, William T. Biological Science, 2d ed. (1972), and Elements of Biological Science, 2d ed. (1973), both published by W. W. Norton & Company, New York. Two versions of one of the best and most accurate of many biology texts (one for science majors, the other for nonmajors).

~Panter, John H., Jr., Biology Today. Del Mar, Calif.: CRM Books, 1972. Contributions by dozens of authors assembled by an anonymous editor; discusses most modern advances at a fairly sophisticated level.

PALEONTOLOGY

~Colbert, Edwin H. Evolution of Vertebrates. New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1955.

~Jensen, William A., and Frank S. Salisbury. Botany: An Ecological Approach. Belmont, Calif.: Wadsworth Publishing Co., 1972. This is not a paleontology book but a general botany text; nevertheless, what is known about the fossil history of the various plant groups is well summarized (by Jensen—providing me with my education in the field).

~Rensch, Bernhard. Evolution above the Species Level. New York: Columbia University Press, 1959.

Romer, A. S. Vertebrate Paleontology. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1966.

Simpson, George Gaylord. Life of the Past. Yale University Press, 1953.

THEORIES ON THE ORIGIN OF LIFE

Anfinsen, Christian B. The Molecular Basis of Evolution. New York: Wiley, 1959.

Fox, S. W., ed. The Origins of Prebiological Systems and of their Molecular Matrices. New York: Academic Press, 1965.

~Jukes, Thomas H. Molecules and Evolution. New York and London: Columbia University Press, 1966.

Keosian, J. The Origin of Life. 2d ed. New York: Reinhold, 1968.

Miller, Stanley L., and Leslie E. Orgel. The Origins of Life on the Earth. Englewood Cliffs, N.J.: Prentice-Hall, 1974.

~Oparin, A. K. Genesis and Evolutionary Development of Life. New York: Academic Press, 1968.

~Wallace, Bruce. Chromosomes, Giant Molecules, and Evolution. New York: W. W. Norton, 1966.
