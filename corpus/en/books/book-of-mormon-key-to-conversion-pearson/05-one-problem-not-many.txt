Glenn L. Pearson, Book of Mormon, Key to Conversion ( 1963)

One Problem—Not Many

From Hong Kong to San Francisco—the long way around—the letters come with what appear to be myriads of different problems, each needing a different solution. But this is not the case. There is only one problem, and only one solution is needed. This may be a little hard to see; so please read carefully.

4 Missionaries, and others involved in missionary work, seem to have a fixation about problem-solving. They want to have a pat answer to each objection.. They prefer something either intellectual or scriptural which will settle the controversy in a decisive manner. When they do not receive such an answer, they tend to close their minds. But we are asking the reader of this book to hear the matter out.

4 We feel the pat answer is usually the wrong approach for the following reasons:

4 1. It takes too much time to give it.

4 2. It is impossible to learn all the pat answers one would need. Even if he learned all the hundreds he would need today, there will be hundreds more needed tomorrow. The pat answers eventually come from our scholars; but they are always behind in their work.

5 3. It leads to arguments, and this is forbidden. (D&C 19:29-31; 3 Ne. 11:28-30)

5 4. It presumes that we are obligated to prove the gospel. We are not obligated to prove the gospel -only to declare it. Each investigator must prove it for himself. We must not give the impression that we are obligated to answer all the objections that our critics can devise. We must not even pretend that we can answer all their objections. Every man sooner or later is backed up to the wall of faith. And there he must make his stand. Either he will believe if he is good, or doubt if he is evil. (John 3:16-21.) And, if he doubts, only he can do anything about that. It requires repentance. One man cannot repent for another.
