Glenn L. Pearson, Book of Mormon, Key to Conversion ( 1963)

A Positive Approach

It is imperative that we approach our missionary work in a way that will avoid contention. Arguing about interpretations is a poor way to avoid contention. The following dialogue suggests an ideal positive approach to the subject.

52 Missionary: Let me see if I understand you, Fred. You believe or have been taught, that the Bible says that there would be no more revelation after it was completed. Is this a fair presentation of your position?

52 Fred: Yes. That is what I have been taught in my Church.

52 Missionary: I have heard this point of view presented before, and I am familiar with the scriptures that are used to support it. We feel that the passages used are misinterpreted and that the arguments advanced are not consistent with the rest of the Bible. However, I do not wish to argue with you about interpretation. But let me ask you this: if you personally found out for sure that God still does reveal His mind and will and has prophets on the earth today, would this convince you that the Bible could not possibly say that revelation was to cease?

52 Fred: Yes, provided I really knew or believed, as you do, that there is more revelation than just the Bible.

52 Missionary: My purpose in being here is to give you a chance to hear and believe a story about the gospel being restored. I would like to tell you about the visit of an angel of God to Joseph Smith. This angel's name was Moroni, and he was the last prophet of an ancient people who lived on the American continent from about 600 B.C. to about 400 A.D. ...
