This step of the formula often requires some ingenuity. It is usually good to be able to turn to a short and clear answer in one of the four Standard Works or in a book by one of the apostles or prophets of the Church if you are sure that his book represents the official Church position on this particular point.

9 For instance, suppose the investigator has raised a question about our stand on infant baptism. You could turn to Moro. 8 and read a few pertinent verses. Or suppose he has raised a question concerning the validity of immersion instead of sprinkling.. You could turn to 3 Ne. 11 and read verses twenty-three to twenty-eight.

9 But you cannot always turn to a. clear-cut answer in the scriptures or writings of the Brethren. In some cases, the answer hasn't been put into print. In others you cannot remember where it is. But do not be dismayed. This is no problem if you know what the position of the Church is. If you do not know, say so, and find out. But, if you do know, simply declare the position of the Church on your own authority. As Mormon said concerning Alma, his sons, and their brethren, " They preached the word, and the truth, according to the spirit of prophecy and revelation; and they preached after the holy order of God by which they were called. " (Alma 43:2.)

10 Suppose, for example, the investigator has challenged the propriety of using Sunday as our day of worship. You know what the position of the Lord is because you know what we do. If there were a good reason to worship on any other day than Sunday, the Lord would tell the president of the Church. In fact, he very likely would have set this straight at the beginning of the dispensation.
