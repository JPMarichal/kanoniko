Part Two The Nature of Revelation, Inspiration and Scripture

Historical Rejection of Modern Revelation

Joseph Smith, in the Pearl of Great Price, described the reaction of a certain Methodist minister to an account of his first vision. This minister treated his story with "contempt" and told him that such things do not occur in our day. This was typical of the treatment Joseph Smith and other Latter-day Saints received and still generally receive for claiming modern revelation. One of the common experiences shared by missionaries is an investigator who accepts everything but the idea that Joseph Smith was a prophet and the Book of Mormon came the way we say it came.

27 Much of Orson Pratt's Works [^1] was devoted to a defense of the idea of modern revelation, showing that it was a matter of major concern to the Brethren in that day.

27 One of the most interesting stories in the Autobiography of Parley P. Pratt is his account of outwitting a Protestant minister in a debate over modern revelation. [^2]This minister maintained there had been no revelation since the time of the ancient apostles. Elder Pratt asked him how he happened to become a minister. Whereupon the minister claimed to have heard a " vocal voice " calling him to the ministry, thus revealing the inconsistency of his position. On the one hand he claimed all revelation had ceased. On the other, he claimed he had received a revelation to become a preacher. Elder Pratt lost no time pointing this out to his audience.

The Desert News Press, Salt Lake City, 1945.

Autobiography of Parley Parker Pratt, edited by his son, Parley P. Pratt, Third Edition, Deseret Book Company (Salt Lake City: 1938), page 85.
