Glenn L. Pearson, Book of Mormon, Key to Conversion ( 1963)

LDS Claim to Revelation

Unique among the churches of Christendom is the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. For the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in contrast to the time-honored traditions of the Catholic and Protestant world, announced, to all who would listen, from its beginning that God had spoken again, that we have modern prophets and apostles to receive the instructions of God in the affairs of the whole Church and that individuals in the Church-or out of it-can be guided into the paths of truth by that same power which Christ and the apostles promised to all who ever would be saved. (See John 14:26; John 15:16; John 16:7-13; Acts 2:39.) And that power is the power of the Holy Ghost, which, in turn, is the power of revelation. And the proof that we mean what we say is that we have scripture besides the Bible to offer the world.

42 Thousands of members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints can attest to the fact that God speaks to his children in this day as in times of old and that only lack of faith can seal up the heavens and cause the Lord to show his disfavor by ceasing to speak to us. (See Morm. 8 and Morm. 9 and Moro. 10.) All of the gifts of the Spirit and all of the miracles described in the Bible have been witnessed among us. The God of Heaven has revealed to us that Jesus is the Christ, that Joseph Smith was a prophet and that the president of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints today obtained his prophetic calling by the choice and with the full approval of the Lord Jesus Christ, who is the head of this church. All of these revelations, and many others, are real experiences to the thousands who have enjoyed them. And they have brought unity-not disunity-to their recipients.
