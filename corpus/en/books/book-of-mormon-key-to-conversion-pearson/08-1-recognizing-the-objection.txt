Recognizing the objection consists of the missionary restating it in his own words. He will say, " Let me see if I understand you correctly. You feel that it was wrong for members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints to practice plural marriage. Is this what you consider your objection to our message?"

8 One advantage in recognizing the objection or problem is that it shows respect for the other fellow's point of view. Just knowing that you understand him will make him feel more at ease.

8 Another advantage is that it serves as a devise to relieve tension. It slows up the tempo. It breaks the investigator's attack. He now finds himself confronted with a question that requires an affirmative reply or a clarification.

8 Still another advantage is that it saves you from the danger of misunderstanding. You may find you have assumed an objection that was not there. Your investigator may say, "No, I don't object to your practice of plural marriage. I object to your giving it up under pressure from the government. If you believe God commanded it, why did you stop? Should you obey men or God?" (For the answer to this question continue on through the chapter.)

9 When you have properly recognized the problem, you are ready to go on to the next step. Your investigator is waiting and willing to hear your answer to his question.
