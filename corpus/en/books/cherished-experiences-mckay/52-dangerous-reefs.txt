Clare Middlemiss, David O. McKay, Cherished Experiences from the Writings of President David O. McKay ( 1955)

DANGEROUS REEFS

From a general conference address April 8, 1945.

TWENTY-FOUR YEARS AGO when the steamship Marama dropped anchor outside the coral reef that surrounds the Island of Rarotonga, a passenger desiring to go ashore asked the captain why he did not sail nearer the wharf. In answer the experienced seaman mentioned treacherous waters and pointed to an engine of one ship, the Maitai, and to the bow of another, still protruding out of the water—both carrying mute evidence of the danger of anchoring too close to the shore of this coral-bound island. "We anchor here," said the captain, "because it is safer to avoid being dashed to pieces, as were those two vessels, hulls of which lie on those dangerous reefs."

A flippant attitude toward marriage, the ill-advised suggestion of "companionate marriage," the base, diabolical theory of "free sex experiment," and the readymade divorce courts are dangerous reefs upon which many a family bark is wrecked.
