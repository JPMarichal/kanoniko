seek deep to hide their counsel. The wicked work hard to hide their plans from the Lord and others (2 Ne. 28:9).

works are in the dark. Acts of wickedness are conducted secretly, without the knowledge of others.

Who seeth us? The wicked believe that their sinful deeds go unnoticed, but the Lord informs them: "I will show unto them . . . that I know all their works" (JST 29:28).

turning of things upside down/potter's clay. Unrighteous individuals accuse God's people of "stirring up the hearts and minds of people, disturbing the traditions of the fathers (Jer. 16:19-21)." 38 Such was the case in the New Testament era (Acts 17:6), and so it appears to be the case in our own dispensation. Unrighteous individuals believe, falsely, that the work of God's people has no lasting significance, that it has the same permanence as potter's clay, which is easily broken.
