Salvation Comes through Intercession of Christ

SCHeb 7 23Heb 7 24Heb 7 25Heb 7 26Heb 7 27Heb 7 28The contrast—in their ministries of intercession—between the Levitical priests and the great high priest is striking.

SCHeb 7 23Heb 7 24Heb 7 25Heb 7 26Heb 7 27Heb 7 28They were many, he but one. They served during their mortal lives, he continues to plead in immortality.

SCHeb 7 23Heb 7 24Heb 7 25Heb 7 26Heb 7 27Heb 7 28They (as all men are) were sinners: unholy, carnal, defiled; he (and he only) was separate from sinners: holy, harmless, undefiled.

SCHeb 7 23Heb 7 24Heb 7 25Heb 7 26Heb 7 27Heb 7 28They offered daily sacrifices of animals for their own sins and for the sins of the people; he knew no sin, but offered himself once for the sins of the world.

SCHeb 7 23Heb 7 24Heb 7 25Heb 7 26Heb 7 27Heb 7 28They had infirmities, he was perfect. They served as priests, without an oath, under the law; theirs was the Aaronic Priesthood. But he brought the gospel, held the Melchizedek Priesthood, and took his supreme position to intercede forever for his brethren because God swore with an oath that he should he consecrated forevermore.

Heb 7 2525. Save them to the uttermost] Give them exaltation in the highest heaven of the celestial world, where they also shall rule and reign as priests forever in his Father's kingdom.

Heb 7 25Come unto God by him] "No man shall come unto the Father but by me or by my word, which is my law, saith the Lord." (D. & C. 132:12.)

Heb 7 25Intercession] See Commentary II, pp. 269-271.

Heb 7 2828. The word of the oath, which was since the law] The law, administered by the lesser priesthood, came through Moses; over 400 years later, through David, the Lord swore that his Son would have the higher priesthood, which administers the gospel and therefore supplants the law.
