Bruce R. McConkie, Doctrinal New Testament Commentary, vol. 3 ( 1973)

Mosaic Ordinances Nailed to Cross of Christ

Col 2 1010. Ye are complete in him] Men receive all they need for salvation through Christ and his gospel. They need not add thereto the learning of the world, the philosophies of men, or the so-called wisdom of the ages. Salvation is in Christ and his gospel. However beneficial it may be to be learned in the things of the world, still spiritual blessings come through revealed knowledge. The knowledge that saves is the knowledge of God.

Col 2 1111. Circumcision of Christ] A spiritual circumcision, which consists in accepting Christ and living his gospel, of cutting away, not a part of the body, but one's whole carnal nature. The contrast is with carnal or literal circumcision, which had in times past been a symbol of conformity to the law of carnal commandments which God gave Moses to remind Israel of her duties.

Col 2 1212. Buried with him in baptism] See Commentary II, pp. 248-250.

Col 2 1313. By baptism those who are dead in sins, who are spiritually uncircumcised, gain a forgiveness of their sins.

SCCol 2 14Col 2 1514-15. Christ fulfilled the law of Moses, thereby blotting out its ordinances and performances, symbolically nailing them to his cross.

Col 2 1616. 'Accordingly, do not be misled by following the rituals and restrictions of the law of Moses.'

Col 2 1717. 'These performances were only a type and a shadow of Christ and his gospel, and we now have the substance itself rather then the shadow.' (Mosiah 13:27-31.)

Col 2 1818. 'Neither turn aside to worship angels, nor to be puffed up in the things of the world.'

Col 2 1919. 'But be steadfast in Christ, from whom true nourishment and increase come.'

SCCol 2 20Col 2 21Col 2 22Col 2 2320-23. 'If, with Christ, you have left the world, why should you longer be subject to its false doctrines? Rather, forsake them, not so much as touching, tasting, or handling those things which satisfy the flesh.'

Col 2 2323. Will Worship] Worship devised by the will of man; man-made worship; any system of religion which does not come from God. Among some of the Colossians it included the worship of angels, among some modern Christians, the worship of relics or "saints."

Col 2 23Circumcision and the law of Moses] See Commentary II, pp. 134-145.
