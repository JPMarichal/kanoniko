The Spirit and Influence of the Book of Mormon

by W. A. Hyde

In the midst of uncertainties, prophets have been the voices of men of the infinte mind and will. They have revealed in our words, messages of the all-pervading truth. They have interpreted to men the language of the stars, and whether written upon metal, granite, clay or papyrus they have made the enduring record of the word.

247But a book is a lifeless thing, as it lies there on the table, a pound of matter; but if it is opened and read, it becomes not only alive, but before the world it is innocent or guilty. One thought, in olden time, to kill a book by burning it, but there is something in a book that will live through a furnace. It is to us intangible, but it is nevertheless there--it is the spirit of the book.

247God's concern for his children has been such that he has miraculously preserved enough of his written word to guide them to obedience and fife. The Holy Bible has been thus kept, and to the believer in a future life, there is a halo surrounding it that lifts it above other books. One reads it as he would commune with the spirit of the ages. Typography and the bookbinder's art are lost in the presence of its grandeur of truth which now speaks to us. Spokesmen for humanity have poured their essence into these pages, and they have become the voice of universal experience. If, then, speaking for humanity in general, there be any book that may be used as a standard by which we may judge the spirit of all other books, that book is the Bible, for it is the highest expression of truth that the civilized world as a whole, has accepted. In its pages we find the rays that are the basis of the light of truth, liberty, justice and mercy. These are seen to be outlined with unmistakable clearness. They are the heart of the book. You could not take from the Bible one of these elements, and leave it worthy to be called scripture. It would be shorn of so much life. But you can strip it of much else, and still leave it, for the purpose of its mission, unimpaired.

248If this be true, then it is the written standard by which any book that presumes to speak for God must be judged. By this standard we may know something of the Spirit of the Book of Mormon.

248This is the voice of the spirit of the book: [^1]

248That liberty rests in the decrees of God, [^2] and that man may choose and have what he wills; [^3] that liberty is inherent in this Western Hemisphere by reason of God's degree; [^4]that liberty is not compatible with earthly monarchies, [^5]that democracy is advocated, and that operating under this law of choice, was held perhaps the first ejection of sacred history. [^6]There is given an example of constitutional law, [^7]and the principle is established, that all men have the right to worship God as their consciences dictate. [^8]The result of the failure of democracy is shown, [^9] and most important, it is announced that the spirit of freedom is akin to the Spirit of God. [^10]

248It is declared that justice is the operation of a righteous and unfailing law and that, though free, man will be subject to the operations of that law at the great and last day; [^11]that the sinful and unrepentent man must have his just condemnation ; [^12]and that the application of the law is general; [^13]and without favoritism, but that to the uninformed there is no law but the law of mercy; [^14]that according to a man's deserts, shall his reward or condemnation be; [^15]that the redemption of God does not apply to the sins of the unrepentant, [^16]but that it is the prerogative of God that mercy may satisfy the demands of justice. [^17]

248It is taught that God's concern for man is far-reaching and envelops every land and clime, [^18]that the grace and mercy of God are founded in power, and that they are the result of the condescension of God, [^19]and come from the Father, because of the Son; [^20]and that being thus it must be founded upon faith in God, its source, [^21]and upon the repentance of man, [^22]and upon obedience to all of his requirements; [^23]that the grace of God saves man from their sins and not in their sins, [^24]and that it is sufficient for all who are humble; [^25]that the ultimate expression of grace was shown in the atonement and resurrection; [^26]that we would be eternally lost without grace, [^27]but that it is continued and free. [^28]Throughout the book there is the unmistakable lesson that there is a price to forgiveness, but that it is within the reach of all. Mercy shines continually through the rigors of law and commandment. Said the prophet of this infinite tenderness: For I know that the Lord giveth no commandments to the children of men, save he shall prepare a way for them, that they may accomplish the thing which he commanded them. [^29]

249Thus are the primary elements of the spirit of truth unmistakably shown.

249There come to us from the voice of the book, echoes as from some word that we have heard long since and have well understood; they are the whisperings to us of familiar truths. We recognize them, for they are attested by the reasoning of the mind, and the sanction of the spirit within us. They tell us that the end of life is that we shall be born again, [^30]redeemed, and become the sons and daughters of God; that there is no happiness in sin, and that obedience is the way to joy. [^31]It teaches that an element of faith is a recognition of God's superior knowledge, [^32]and how that faith is nurtured and grows in the heart of man; [^33]and how it may become so powerful that nothing can be held from it; [^34]that spiritual gifts to man cease only because of his wickedness, [^35]and that God, to be unchangeable, must be a God of miracles. [^36]It gives full light on the comprehensiveness of the plans of the Almighty, [^37]and shows that all men have been alike to him from the beginning of creations and that his atonement applied as well before as subsequent to his coming in the flesh; [^38]that baptism, necessary now, was always necessary; [^39]that the mission of all prophets has been to testify of the Christ [^40]It teaches that purity of heart alone gives access to God. [^41]And with unanswerable authority is given again, by the Lord himself, the essence of his gospel. [^42]

249With a boldness that has in it a spirit of militancy, the book challenges the world. It invites comparison by laying down a law by which itself is to be judged--that there is nothing which is good save it comes from the Lord, [^43] and that which is evil cometh from the devil. [^44]

249I show you the way to judge, for everything which invites to do good, and to persuade to believe in Christ, is sent forth by the power and gift of Christ, wherefore ye may know with a perfect knowledge that it is of God. [^45]

250There is no book, the Bible not excepted, that tells so plainly and fervently the mission of Christ. [^46]The book challenges the world in its support of the Bible. Said the prophet: This is written for the intent that ye may believe that, and if ye believe that ye will believe this also. [^47]It challenges the world for a better statement of the moral law, and the blessings that come from its observance. [^48]It challenges all men by the witness of the Spirit, if they will read it with open and earnest minds. [^49]It comes to the world with a crystal clearness that challenges the understanding of men.

250It speaks with a spirit which is authoritative. By a law of the operation of the Holy Ghost which it announces, [^50]its light and truth are carried into the hearts of men and women, and many thousands of the honest in heart, have been first thrilled and then convened by its powerful message. The voice of these ancient heralds of the truth, comes to us out of the dust, [^51]speaking as with familiar spirit, and thousands have heard and will still continue to hear its sacred whisperings. They recognize the word, for it is the light of truth, the Spirit of him who is the Author of our salvation.
