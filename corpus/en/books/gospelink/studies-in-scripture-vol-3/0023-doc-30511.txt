# Notes on Contributors

### Notes on Contributors
Robert L. Millet, Kent P. Jackson, Studies in Scripture, Vol. 3: Genesis to 2 Samuel ( 1985)
 
 

Notes On Contributors

ELDER BRUCE R. MCCONKIE, until the time of his death in April 1985, was a member of the Council of the Twelve of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He was sustained as a member of the First Council of the Seventy at the age of 31 in 1946 and was ordained an apostle on 12 October 1972 by President Harold B. Lee. Elder McConkie is the author of the encyclopedic volume, Mormon Doctrine. He has written a three-volume doctrinal commentary on the New Testament, a six-volume masterwork on the ministry of the Messiah, and before his death completed a major study of the Articles of Faith. He and his wife, the former Amelia Smith, are the parents of nine children.

S. KENT BROWN is Professor of Ancient Scripture and Director of Publications of the Religious Studies Center at Brigham Young University, where he joined the faculty in 1971. Brother Brown received his B.A. degree from the University of California-Berkeley and his Ph.D. from Brown University in Biblical Studies. He served for a number of years as Director of the Ancient Studies area of the Religious Studies Center, and most recently as Chairman of the Department of Ancient Scripture. Brother Brown and his wife Gail are the parents of five children.

R. SCOTT BURTON is a doctoral student in Old Testament and Near Eastern Studies at the University of Michigan at Ann Arbor, where he has completed an M.A. degree. Brother Burton is also a part-time instructor at the LDS Institute of Religion adjacent to that institution. He and his wife Estelle are the parents of four children.

GEORGE A. HORTON, JR. is Associate Professor and Chairman of the Department of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. He received his Bachelors and Masters degrees from BYU and his Ed.D. from the University of California at Los Angeles in Curriculum, Programmed Instruction, and Pupil Personnel. Brother Horton has worked as Curriculum Director of the LDS Church Educational System and has served as dean or director of numerous tours to the Holy Land. He and his wife Norma are the parents of seven children.

KENT P. JACKSON is Associate Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University, where he serves as Old Testament area coordinator and as a member of the Editorial Board of BYU Studies. Brother Jackson obtained a B.A. from BYU and his M.A. and Ph.D. degrees from the University of Michigan in Near Eastern Studies. He is a specialist in ancient writings, including ancient scripture. He has published a major book on the language of the ancient Ammonites, as well as numerous articles on both LDS and non-LDS topics. He joined the BYU faculty in 1980. Brother Jackson and his wife Nancy are the parents of five children.

JOHN M. LUNDQUIST is Chief of the Oriental Division of the New York Public Library, New York City. Formerly an Assistant Professor of Ancient Scripture and Anthropology at Brigham Young University, Brother Lundquist received both his masters and Ph.D. degrees at the University of Michigan in Near Eastern Studies. He is Director of the North Orontes Valley Archaeology Expedition at Tell Qarqur in Syria. He is the father of six children.

ANN N. MADSEN is an instructor in the Department of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University, where she has taught since 1977. Subsequent to receiving a B.S. from the University of Utah, she received her M.A. in Ancient Studies from BYU, where she concentrated in Old Testament and Hebrew. Her thesis was a comprehensive treatment of Melchizedek in the scriptures and other ancient literature. She and her husband Truman are the parents of three children.

KEITH H. MESERVY is Associate Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University, where he joined the faculty in 1958. Brother Meservy received his B.A. from BYU and his M.A. from Johns Hopkins University. An acknowledged scholar in Old and New Testament studies, he has inspired students for many years with his depth of insight into scriptural passages. Brother Meservy and his wife Arlene have four children.

ROBERT L. MILLET is Assistant Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Brother Millet received his Ph.D. from Florida State University in Biblical Studies, 19th- and 20th-Century Religious Thought, and Psychology. Before joining the BYU faculty in 1983, he worked with the LDS Church Educational System as a seminary instructor, institute director, and teaching support consultant. Brother Millet and his wife Shauna are the parents of five children.

DONALD W. PARRY is a graduate student in Near Eastern Studies. Brother Parry received his B.A. degree from Brigham Young University in Near Eastern Studies and is currently completing a Masters degree in the same field. He and his wife Camille are the parents of three children.

STEPHEN D. RICKS is Assistant Professor of Hebrew and Semitic Languages at Brigham Young University. He has been employed at BYU since 1981, where he teaches several ancient languages. Brother Ricks received his Ph.D. in Near Eastern Religions at the University of California-Berkeley and Graduate Theological Union. He is a specialist in ancient religions and pre-Islamic Arabian dialects. Brother Ricks and his wife Shirley are the parents of five children.

DAVID R. SEELY is a doctoral student in Ancient and Biblical Studies at the University of Michigan. Brother Seely received a B.A. degree in Classical Greek and an M.A. in Classics from BYU. He also received an M.A. in Near Eastern Studies from the University of Michigan. He has received a number of academic honors and fellowships and has worked as an Editorial Assistant on projects dealing with his field of expertise. Brother Seely and his wife Jo Ann have one child.

DAVID P. WRIGHT is Assistant Professor of Hebrew and Near Eastern Languages at Brigham Young University. Brother Wright received his Ph.D. from the University of California-Berkeley in Biblical Hebrew and Near Eastern Studies. He joined the faculty at BYU in 1984 and now teaches courses in Biblical Hebrew, Mishnaic Hebrew, and Akkadian. Brother Wright and his wife Diane are the parents of four children.
