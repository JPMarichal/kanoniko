# Paul Persecuted, Arrested, Bound

### Paul Persecuted, Arrested, Bound
Bruce R. McConkie, Doctrinal New Testament Commentary, vol. 2 ( 1971)
 
 

Paul Persecuted, Arrested, Bound

SCActs 21 27Acts 21 28Acts 21 29Acts 21 30Acts 21 31Acts 21 32Acts 21 33Acts 21 34Acts 21 35Acts 21 36Acts 21 37Acts 21 38Acts 21 39Is not this scene reminiscent of the babble and confusion, the mobocracy, the spirit of hate and murder which attended the last days of our Lord's mortal life? But why should such things be? Why should one group of religionists persecute another? Suppose some people do have different religious beliefs than others, what of it? Why should men be persecuted for what they believe?

SCActs 21 27Acts 21 28Acts 21 29Acts 21 30Acts 21 31Acts 21 32Acts 21 33Acts 21 34Acts 21 35Acts 21 36Acts 21 37Acts 21 38Acts 21 39The fact that there is religious persecution is one of the great evidences of the existence and influence of Satan. There is no reason to persecute people for their beliefs unless it makes a difference what they believe. Satan, knowing full well that men are saved by believing one set of principles and damned for accepting another, has a personal interest in all religious persecution. By keeping the spirit of persecution alive in the hearts of men, he can hinder and damage the spirit which leads men to piety and worship. And he can then wield the sword of persecution with full strength when he encounters the true religion revealed from heaven. See Acts 5:33-42; 17:1-14.

SCActs 21 27Acts 21 28Acts 21 29Acts 21 3027-30. Paul is here accused, falsely, of taking a Gentile from the outer court of the Gentiles, into the inner court of Israel. Miens, meaning unbelievers in the religion of the Jews, who thus overstepped their bounds were worthy of death according to Jewish law, a law which the Jews were then powerless to enforce.
