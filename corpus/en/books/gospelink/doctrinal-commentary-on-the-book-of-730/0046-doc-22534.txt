### Covenant to Preserve the Record of the Nephites Enos 1:13-18
Robert L. Millet, Joseph Fielding McConkie, Doctrinal Commentary on the Book of Mormon, vol. 2 ( 1988)
 
 

Covenant to Preserve the Record of the Nephites

Enos 1:13-18

13. And now behold, this was the desire which I desired of him- that if it should so be, that my people, the Nephites, should fall into transgression, and by any means be destroyed, and the Lamanites should not be destroyed, that the Lord God would preserve a record of my people, the Nephites; even if it so be by the power of his holy arm, that it might be brought forth at some future day unto the Laminates, that, perhaps, they might be brought unto salvation-

14. For at the present our strugglings were vain in restoring them to the true faith. And they swore in their wrath that, if it were possible, they would destroy our records and us, and also all the traditions of our fathers.

15. Wherefore, I knowing that the Lord God was able to preserve our records, I cried unto him continually, for he had said unto me: Whatsoever thing ye shall ask in faith, believing that ye shall receive in the name of Christ, ye shall receive it.

Enos 1 1416. And I had faith, and I did cry unto God that he would preserve the records; and he covenanted with me that he would bring them forth unto the Lamanites in his own due time.

17. And I, Enos, knew it would be according to the covenant which he had made; wherefore my soul out did rest.

18. And the Lord said unto me: Thy fathers have also required of me this thing; and it shall be done unto them according to their faith; for their faith was like unto thine.

SCEnos 1 13Enos 1 14Enos 1 15Enos 1 16Enos 1 17Enos 1 1813-18. The future of both the Nephites and the Lamanites had been revealed through Lehi and Nephi (see 1 Nephi 12; 1 Nephi 15:5). Enos, witnessing those prophecies coming to pass and fearing that because of their wickedness the Nephites might well be destroyed, sought to obtain from the Lord the promise that a record of their doings would be preserved. Enos's hope was that such a record would come forth to the edification and blessing of the Lamanites and call them back to the faith of their fathers. Thus his prayers became one with the prayers of his fathers who had sought this same blessing for those of future generations.

Enos 1 1313. A record of my people] That is, the Book of Mormon.

Enos 1 1313. By the power of his holy arm] The Book of Mormon declares itself to have come forth by the "gift and power of God" (see title page). The phrase is most instructive-the book is a "gift" of God to all who desire to know him and the saving truths of his gospel. It is also a special treasure to those who are descendants of Lehi or of the family of Joseph. Further, it came forth as only such a book can-by the "power of God." It was written by the power of God, preserved by the power of God, and brought forth by the power of God, and it is to be understood only by the power of God.

Enos 1 1414. The insatiable desire of the Lamanites to destroy a people, their records, and their traditions, evidences a fear of each. Such is an eloquent testimony of the power of such records and of righteous traditions. See also Jacob 7:24.

Enos 1 1515. Ask in faith, believing that ye shall receive] The wisdom of heaven and principles of righteousness govern the answering of prayers. See 3 Nephi 18:20; D&C 88:64; 1 John 5:14.

Enos 1 1515. In the name of Christ, ye shall receive it] How indebted we are to Christ-for it is only in and through his name that things of eternal worth are obtained. We have but one Savior, one atonement, one baptism, one priesthood, and one plan of salvation-the blessings of each come to us in the name of Christ and in none other. The command given our dispensation is: "All things must be done in the name of Christ, whatsoever you do in the Spirit; and ye must give thanks unto God in the Spirit for whatsoever blessing ye are blessed with" (D&C 46:31-32).

Enos 1 1616. This is the Lord's promise that the plates upon which the Book of Mormon were written would be preserved to come forth in a distant day when they could be taken to the Laminates.

Enos 1 1818. Thy fathers have also required of me this thing] In reference to the Book of Mormon prophets who sought the assurance of the Lord that their record would be preserved to come forth to the Lamanites in the last days, the Lord said: "I said ... it should be granted unto them according to their faith in their prayers; yea, and this was their faith-that my gospel, which I gave unto them that they might preach in their days, might come unto their brethren the Lamanites, and also all that had become Lamanites because of their dissensions. Now, this is not all -their faith in their prayers was that this gospel should be made known also, if it were possible that other nations should possess this land; and thus they did leave a blessing upon this land in their prayers, that whosoever should believe in this gospel in this land might have eternal life; yea, that it might be free unto all of whatsoever nation, kindred, tongue, or people they may be. And now, behold, according to their faith in their prayers will I bring this part of my gospel to the knowledge of my people. Behold, I do not bring it to destroy that which they have received, but to build it up. " (D&C 10:47-52.)
