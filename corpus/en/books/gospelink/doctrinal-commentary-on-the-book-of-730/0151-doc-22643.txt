# The Necessity of Order and Authority Mosiah 23:16-18

### The Necessity of Order and Authority Mosiah 23:16-18
Robert L. Millet, Joseph Fielding McConkie, Doctrinal Commentary on the Book of Mormon, vol. 2 ( 1988)
 
 

The Necessity of Order and Authority

Mosiah 23:16-18

16. And now, Alma was their high priest, he being the founder of their church.

17. And it came to pass that none received authority to preach or to teach except it were by him from God. Therefore he consecrated all their priests and all their teachers; and none were consecrated except they were just men.

18. Therefore they did watch over their people, and did nourish them with things pertaining to righteousness.

Mosiah 23 1616, Alma was their high priest] See commentary on Mosiah 18:13, 18.

Mosiah 23 1616. He being the founder] Having laid the foundation for the Church among this people, Alma is properly referred to as its "founder." This is not to suggest that the Church originated with him, any more than the expression "their church" was intended to mean that the Church originated on the initiative of the people rather than with God.

Mosiah 23 1717. It is evident that Alma held both priesthood and keys. Keys are the right of presidency, the authority that controls and directs the activities, functions, and ordinances of the priesthood, thus assuring that the Lord's house will always be a house of order. (See also Articles of Faith 1:5 and D&C 42:11.)

Mosiah 23 1717. He consecrated all their priests and all their teachers] That is, he set them apart to their callings; he ordained them to their offices. See commentary on 2 Nephi 5:26.

Mosiah 23 1717. Except they were just men] See commentary on Enos 1:1.

Mosiah 23 1818. Nourish them with things pertaining to righteousness] Spiritual strength is inseparably connected with righteousness. Many have attempted to find strength or nourishment for their spirit with some form of positive thinking. It need be remembered that the devil and his legions can both take and teach courses in positive thinking and self-assertiveness. That of which the scriptural writ speaks is a power that can be had only in righteousness. (See Jacob 6:7; D&C 121:36-37, 45-46.)
