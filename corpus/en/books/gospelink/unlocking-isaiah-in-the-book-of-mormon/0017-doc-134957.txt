# Great Servants of the Lord in the Last Days

### Great Servants of the Lord in the Last Days
Victor L. Ludlow, Unlocking Isaiah in the Book of Mormon (Salt Lake City 2003)
 
 

Great Servants of the Lord in the Last Days
 

2 Nephi 21/Isaiah 11
 

Stem of Jesse (Christ) shall judge in righteousness— The knowledge of God shall cover the earth in the Millennium— The Lord shall raise an ensign and gather Israel
 

This chapter highlights Isaiah’s skill as a poet, demonstrates his calling as a seer, and reveals the power of his prophetic call. Isaiah describes, in detail, significant events prior to the Second Coming, identifying at least two key figures who will play essential roles before the Savior’s return. Also, Isaiah prophesies of circumstances after the Savior’s arrival, giving us a brief vision of what life will be like during the Millennium.
 

When Moroni appeared to Joseph Smith on September 21, 1823, he quoted this chapter of Isaiah and explained that “it was about to be fulfilled” (Joseph Smith—History 1:40). Through study, pondering, and prayer, we can gain an understanding into this chapter’s importance for us as Latter-day Saints while we look for the signs of its fulfillment in these, the last days.
 

2 Nephi 21:1: “A rod out of the stem of Jesse”
 

The first verse of chapter 21 continues a theme begun in the previous chapter, which ends with a symbolic description of the fate of the wicked. In 2 Nephi 20:33, the Lord reveals that the “haughty” and “high ones” (the wicked and the proud) will be lopped off and hewn down like branches and trunks of trees. While chapter 20 ends on a note of despair, chapter 21 begins with a message of hope. After the wicked have been removed from positions of influence and power, the Lord will raise up righteous servants to fulfill great and important tasks preceding the Second Coming. Instead of trees being hewn down, new branches and growth will come forth.
 

To understand the message contained in verse 1, we must look at each phrase individually. After we are familiar with each separate unit, we can develop a greater understanding of the verse’s collective meaning. The following words and phrases are used by Isaiah to describe key, distinct individuals:
 

Jesse. Jesse is the father of David, the boy who would slay Goliath and become king of Israel. Through Jesse’s lineage came many of the great kings of Israel, including David, Solomon, Hezekiah, and ultimately the King of kings—Jesus.
 

Stem of Jesse. The stem of Jesse is a descendant of Jesse and a rightful heir as the king of Israel. From modern revelation, we learn that this particular descendant of Jesse is Jesus Christ (D&C 113:2). Many scriptures add additional support to this revelation. Matthew 1:1–16 highlights the genealogy of the Savior, tracing Him back through David and Jesse. Similarly, John 7:42 explains that “Christ cometh of the seed of David.” While Christ’s political heritage is notable, His true ancestry is even more impressive, for as John reveals, “God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son” (John 3:16). Thus, Jesus Christ is the son of mortal kings as well as the literal Son of the King of heaven and earth, Heavenly Father.
 

Although the word “stem” does allude to an ancestral relationship between Jesse and Christ, it also has a more symbolic meaning. The term “stem” comes from the Hebrew root geza—the trunk or stump of a tree. The stem, or trunk, is the most powerful portion of a tree, providing support for the network of branches and twigs growing from it. Christ, as the stem of Jesse, acts as the center of strength and nourishment for the figurative branches and twigs (symbolizing His faithful followers) that have developed from His care. We can be part of this network of branches if we choose to be grafted in through the ordinance of baptism and obedience to the commandments of God.
 

The Rod. Isaiah also speaks of a “rod [coming forth] out of the stem of Jesse” (v. 1). The verse identifies this individual as “a branch . . . out of [Christ’s] roots.” A careful study of the scriptures provides helpful information concerning this individual. In the Hebrew texts, the word “branch” is natzar, a word also found in Daniel 11:7. This prophecy from Daniel states that “out of a branch [natzar] of her roots shall one stand . . . , which shall come with an army, . . . and shall prevail.” This branch will be a man of great power. Jeremiah reveals more about the identity of this person: “I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper. . . . In his days Judah shall be saved” (Jeremiah 23:5–6). Similarly, Hosea reports that after the people of Israel live for many years without a king, and after the lost tribes return, they shall “seek the Lord their God, and David their king . . . in the latter days” (Hosea 3:5). Both the stem (Christ) and the rod (the servant) will be welcome sights to the children of Israel in the last days.
 

Modern revelation also provides a revealing look at the possible identity of this servant. When Joseph Smith asked about the identity of the rod, the Lord revealed that he “is a servant in the hands of Christ . . . on whom there is laid much power” (D&C 113:4). Concerning this servant, Joseph Smith reported, “Although David was a king, he never did obtain the spirit and power of Elijah and the fullness of the Priesthood. . . . The throne and kingdom of David is to be taken from him and given to another by the name of David in the last days.”1 This future ruler will be given power from the Lord to perform many great and marvelous things in the events preceding the return of the Redeemer.2
 

Verse 1 provides a revealing look into the identity and life of two pivotal individuals in the last days: Jesus Christ, the Savior of mankind, and a powerful servant of the Lord who will come from the lineage of Jesse to rule in righteousness and play a key role in the preparation for the Lord’s millennial reign.
 

2 Nephi 21:2–5: “And the spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him”
 

This servant, a future ruler of Israel, will do many great things to prepare the world for the Second Coming. However, he will not become powerful on his own. Instead, as we learn in verse 2, his power will come through the Spirit of the Lord. Through the Spirit, this great servant will gain access to heavenly gifts, such as wisdom, understanding, sensitivity, might, knowledge, and fear of the Lord. These powerful gifts will be given to him because of his righteousness and devotion to the Lord.
 

More of this servant’s character is revealed in verse 3. We learn that he will gain the gift of “quick understanding,” which will allow him to judge others as God does—by looking on a person’s heart and not his or her dress, appearance, speech, or actions. This characteristic was given to another descendant of David—his son Solomon. When asked to judge who was the rightful mother of a newborn infant, he judged not by what the competing mothers said or what he saw; instead, he used “quick understanding” given him by the Lord to determine the rightful mother (1 Kings 3:16–28). When the people of Israel heard of this judgment, the scriptures report that “they feared the king: for they saw that the wisdom of God was in him, to do judgment” (1 Kings 3:28).
 

In verse 4, Isaiah uses an effective poetic tool to emphasize an important gospel concept. By using contrasting ideas built around a similar theme, Isaiah helps the reader understand an essential principle of judgment. First, Isaiah reveals that the servant will judge the poor with righteousness and will “reprove with equity for the meek.” Next, Isaiah switches to the other extreme and proclaims that “[the servant] shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked.” The use of extreme opposites in this way is called “antithetic parallelism.” Isaiah uses stark contrast to show that to the meek, God is a tender God of mercy, but to the wicked, God is a firm God of justice. Eventually each of His children must decide whether he or she is for or against our Heavenly Father. There is no in-between with God. Those who choose a “lukewarm” commitment will find themselves cast away from the Lord (Revelation 3:15–16).
 

In the final verse of his servant description, Isaiah uses eloquent imagery to describe the greatness of the servant’s accomplishments. His goodness and equity are so apparent in his actions that he literally wears the manifestations of godly righteousness and faithfulness in the “girdle of his loins” and the “girdle of his reins [waist]” (v. 5). Members of the Lord’s church should endeavor to live their lives in the same manner, so that we can also be “clothed with purity, yea, even with the robe of righteousness” (2 Nephi 9:14).
 

2 Nephi 21:6–9: “The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb”
 

Contained within these four verses is one of the most beautiful and revealing descriptions of what the earth will be like during the Millennium. Unlike most prophets, who speak of events prior to the Millennium, Isaiah gives his readers a glimpse into this wonderful period of life on earth. It is no wonder Nephi quoted these verses to his people and included them in his record for us. In addition, poets, painters, composers, and diplomats through the ages have borrowed from Isaiah’s poetic description of this paradisiacal era.
 

After the Lord returns in glory, the millennial reign will begin. Verse 6 describes how animals will interact with each other during this time. No longer will the prey have to flee from the predator, for “the wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid, and the calf and the young lion and fatling together.” What a vision of peace and beauty Isaiah presents!
 

The temperament of animals will not be the only change during this ideal era. As Isaiah reveals in verse 7, the “lion shall eat straw like the ox.” Elder Bruce R. McConkie, in commenting on this phenomenon, said, “All animals shall mingle together in peace, and the appetites of the carnivorous beast shall be changed so that the grass of the field becomes the common diet of the animal world.”3 Animals will no longer fear each other because of these changes in disposition and diet. All animals will live in a state of peace, no one animal fearing another.
 

Isaiah not only describes the existence of peace among all animals but also foretells an overarching peace that occurs between humankind and animals. Verse 8 records that young children shall play on the hole of asps and the den of cockatrices—both poisonous breeds of snakes. Isaiah’s imagery is powerful—even a child will not fear the poisonous serpent (and the reptiles will not fear active, young boys). When mankind experienced the Fall, Eve and her posterity were cursed that there would be enmity between humankind and snakes (Moses 4:21). By using the snake as an example of peace between mankind and animals, Isaiah is testifying that life will become as it was before the Fall.
 

The Lord taught this same principle to Joseph Smith: “In that day [the millennium] the enmity of man, and the enmity of beasts, yea, the enmity of all flesh, shall cease from before my face” (D&C 101:26). As a reader, one may question what might cause this overarching peace. Isaiah alludes to why it will happen: “the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord” (v. 9). By gaining a true understanding of the Savior and by following His ways, mankind will progress on the path toward peace and harmony. Orson Pratt, an early Apostle of the Church, describes how the knowledge of the Lord will cover the earth:
 

There will be no place of ignorance, no place of darkness, no place for those that will not serve God. Why? Because Jesus . . . will be himself on the earth, and his holy angels will be on the earth, and all the resurrected Saints that have died in former dispensations . . . will be on the earth. What a happy earth this creation will be, when this purifying process shall come, and the earth be filled with the knowledge of God as the waters cover the great deep! What a change!4
 

These images describe a literal peace among all the animals, between animals and humans, and among humankind. This peace on earth will be as natural as the waters that cover the sea (v. 9). The imagery in these four verses is so profound that Nephi repeats it in his writings as he provides inspired commentary on the Isaiah chapters he has quoted (2 Nephi 30:12–15).
 

2 Nephi 21:10–12: “A Root of Jesse, . . . an ensign of the people”
 

Isaiah now moves slightly back in time, from a period when the knowledge of the Lord will fill the earth (during the Millennium) to a time when the knowledge of the Lord will begin to fill the earth (as the gospel is restored). After the great apostasy, the true knowledge of Christ’s gospel dwindled until only a few cold spiritual embers remained. A spark of new faith began to stir again during the Reformation. Religion became an important part of life, and many sought earnestly for religious truth.
 

Isaiah, in verse 10, reveals that another great servant of the Lord, a “root of Jesse,” would come forth when the true knowledge of God and His truths were missing from the earth, and he would “stand for an ensign of the people.” Through him, great things would be done in the establishment of truth and the ushering in of the Millennium. But who is this “root of Jesse”? Once again, modern revelation has provided a valuable key of understanding: The root of Jesse “is a descendant of Jesse, as well as of Joseph, unto whom rightly belongs the priesthood, and the keys of the kingdom” (D&C 113:6). Joseph Smith fulfills both of these requirements: he was a rightful heir of the priesthood (D&C 86:8–10) and was given all the keys of the kingdom throughout his life as part of the restoration of all things.5
 

Verse 11 discusses some accomplishments of the root of Jesse. It states that in his days, the “Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people.” In order for the Lord to do something a second time, He must do it a first time. The first gathering of Israel occurred about six centuries before Isaiah’s time—almost 3,300 years ago—when Moses led the Israelite people out of Egyptian captivity (Exodus 12–14). Through Moses, the Lord freed the physically captive Israelites and led them to a promised land. But this blessing did not come without warning. According to President Joseph Fielding Smith, “Moses, by prophecy, declared to ancient Israel, even before they had the privilege of entering the land of their inheritance, that for their rebellion the tribes of Israel would be driven to the four corners of the earth, but in the last days, if they would humble themselves, the Lord would gather them again.”6
 

Just as Moses prophesied, the tribes of Israel continued in wickedness and were eventually scattered throughout the world. Lehi and his descendants were a part of this dispersion. After centuries of being a scattered people, Israel was promised that they would be brought back together—a process referred to as the “gathering of Israel.”
 

This second gathering began on April 3, 1836, a week after Joseph Smith dedicated the Kirtland Temple. On this glorious Sunday, many prophets of previous dispensations appeared to Joseph and bestowed upon him necessary keys for the building of the kingdom of God. One set of keys that Joseph Smith received that day were the “keys of the gathering of Israel.” These keys were bestowed upon him by the ancient prophet who exercised them during the first gathering—Moses (D&C 110:11).
 

Verse 11 explains that the Israelites will gather from areas of the north (Hamath), east (Elam and Shinar, a major city in Babylon), south (Cush, or Ethiopia), and west (Egypt and Pathros). It is also said that some would come from “the islands of the sea.” The children of Israel would be brought from every corner of the earth, showing that God remembers His promises and is mindful of all His children.
 

Further accomplishments of the root of Jesse are outlined in verse 12: “He shall set up an ensign for the nations.” The ensign to be brought forth is none other than the gospel of Jesus Christ. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints serves as an ensign to the world, inviting all who desire to gain a greater knowledge of the Savior to come and partake. A similar promise is given in 2 Nephi 15:26, where Isaiah promises that the Lord “will lift up an ensign to the nations.”7 President Joseph Fielding Smith spoke of this great ensign:
 

In the little town of Fayette, Seneca County, New York, the Lord set up an ensign to the nations. It was the fulfillment of the prediction made by the Prophet Isaiah. . . . That ensign was The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, which was established for the last time, never again to be destroyed or given to other people. It was the greatest event the world has seen since the day that the Redeemer was lifted upon the cross and worked out the infinite and eternal atonement. It meant more to mankind than anything else that has occurred since that day.8
 

Indeed, the ensign has been established, and the Church continues to grow, gaining approximately a third of a million new members each year.
 

When Moroni appeared to Joseph Smith on September 21, 1823, he told Joseph that the things contained in this chapter were “about to be fulfilled.” While many of the prophecies in chapter 21 have yet to be fulfilled, one has—the root of Jesse has come forth and made it possible for knowledge of the Lord to begin to fill the earth. Through this servant, Joseph Smith, the Lord has brought forth additional scripture, restored the priesthood, clarified doctrinal misunderstandings, and initiated the gathering of Israel.
 

2 Nephi 21:13–16: “Ephraim shall not envy Judah”
 

A powerful prophecy is given in verses 13 and 14, foretelling the reconciliation of the tribes of Ephraim and Judah. Isaiah not only forecasts the glorious reunion of the two factions but also prophesies of their united assault and victory over their enemies.
 

To fully understand the importance of these events, we should gain an understanding of the history of these two nations. Elder James E. Talmage has provided us with an informative summary of the beginning of the feud between these two great tribes:
 

Immediately following the death of Solomon, . . . the nation was disrupted by revolt. The tribe of Judah, part of the tribe of Benjamin, and small remnants of a few other tribes remained true to the royal succession, and accepted Rehoboam, son of Solomon, as their king; while the rest, usually spoken of as the Ten Tribes, broke their allegiance to the house of David, and made Jeroboam, an Ephraimite, their king. The Ten Tribes retained the title Kingdom of Israel though also known as Ephraim. Rehoboam and his adherents were distinctively called the Kingdom of Judah.9
 

Judah had little respect for the tribe of Ephraim during Isaiah’s time. Ephraimites were noted for their false teachings and wild, drunken lifestyle (Isaiah 28:1–8). As the kingdoms of Israel and Judah fought against each other, Ephraim was the dominant tribe in the north while Judah ruled the south (2 Nephi 17:1–9/Isaiah 7:1–9). In the eyes of later Jews, Ephraim was noted for her arrogance and wickedness. Now, in the latter days, Ephraim needs to come forth in humility and righteousness so that Ephraim and Judah can find exemplary strength in each other. And together, they can oppose the enemies of the Lord.
 

Isaiah uses commanding imagery to portray the united power of Judah and Ephraim going forth. Together, “they shall fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines towards the west” and “shall spoil them of the east [Edom, Moab, and Ammon]” (v. 14). These near neighbors to the west and east have provided continual opposition to Israel through the centuries. Isaiah reveals that only when reconciliation is made between Judah and Ephraim will they prevail against their enemies, who will, in turn, become subject to them. The harmony of the two tribes and their combined attributes of spiritual and social leadership ultimately allow them to prevail over the enemies of God’s people and Christ’s church in the last days.
 

In one sense, a union between the tribe of Ephraim and the tribe of Judah developed in 1830 with the coming forth of the Book of Mormon. Ezekiel, an Old Testament prophet, was told by the Lord that two records would come forth for the benefit of mankind: “Take thee one stick, and write upon it, For Judah, and for the children of Israel his companions: then take another stick, and write upon it, For Joseph, the stick of Ephraim, and for all the house of Israel his companions: And join them one to another into one stick; and they shall become one in thine hand” (Ezekiel 37:16–17). The Bible is a record of the people of Judah. On the other hand, the Book of Mormon is a record of the Nephite and Lamanite people, descendants of Ephraim and Manasseh, the sons of Joseph. With the printing of the Book of Mormon, Ezekiel’s prophecy has been fulfilled. The stick of Judah (the Bible) and the stick of Joseph (the Book of Mormon) have come together as sacred scriptures in the last days. While both texts are powerful in their individual testaments of Jesus Christ, a careful study of both sacred records will give us a fuller understanding of their singular message and will help us “fly upon the shoulders” of our common spiritual enemy—Satan.
 

The final verses in chapter 21 describe mighty changes in distant lands that will occur in the last days. With His almighty power, the Lord will “utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea [the delta region of the Nile River]” (v. 15), splitting it into seven streams and allowing people to pass over them “dry shod” (v. 15). A literal fulfillment of this prophecy could mean that the waters of the Nile will be so diminished that its various branches and canals will dry up. Or, it could mean that the Nile waters will be dispersed into so many small diversions that multiple roads and bridges will permit people to cross over the waters by foot. This passage across the Nile is also an allusion to the ancient exodus of the house of Israel, and it reminds the reader of God’s power to deliver His followers from the barriers of physical oppression and the snares of spiritual adversity.
 

Isaiah continues his allusion to the mass exodus of the Israelites from Egypt with another comparison. He prophesies that “there shall be a highway for the remnant of his people . . . from Assyria, like as it was to Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt” (v. 16). Just as the tribes of Israel were led from Egypt to the promised land in the first gathering, the tribes will again gather from their dispersal throughout the world. Assyria is used as a symbolic reference to the enemies of Israel, which could include any political, physical, or spiritual enemies that may impede the gathering process. Through the power of God, the children of Israel will be allowed to travel a “highway” back to their promised land, as they prepare the land, themselves, and others for the return of the one true Messiah.
 

This chapter provides key insights about two important servants of the Lord in the last days. They are major forerunners who help prepare the nations and their inhabitants for the Lord’s glorious millennial era. Isaiah also provides a glimpse of this golden age. Although the precise identity of the first servant (the rod) is difficult to establish, the characteristics of the second servant (the root) are easily identified with the prophet of the Restoration, Joseph Smith. Perhaps that is why the angel Moroni taught the young lad from this Isaiah passage. Joseph Smith and the prophets who have followed him have worked diligently to further the mission of Christ’s church, especially with the gathering of Israel.
 

To complete the gathering of all Israel, the remnants of Ephraim and Judah must work together. Great inroads have been made in removing the enmity between Ephraim and Judah, but more must be done. Elder LeGrand Richards felt that members of the Church can play an essential role in building bridges of understanding between the two tribes: “I do not know how the enmity and the envy between Ephraim and Judah can disappear except that we of the house of Ephraim, who have the custody of the gospel, should lead out in trying to bring to this branch of the house of Israel the blessings of the restored gospel.”10 President Ezra Taft Benson also said, “We need to know more about the Jews, and the Jews ought to know more about the Mormons. When we understand one another, then perhaps you will understand why Ben-Gurion said, ‘There are no people in the world who understand the Jews like the Mormons.’” After talking about Moroni’s visit to Joseph Smith, he added, “From the very inception of this latter-day work, . . . this church has had a deep interest in the remnant of the house of Israel, the descendants of Judah.” After quoting Isaiah 11 and other scriptures, President Benson concluded, “Yes, we understand the Jews . . . because we belong to the same house of Israel. . . . We look forward to the day of fulfillment of God’s promise when ‘the house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel.’”11 As we do all we can to live a good gospel example for the house of Judah, we will see a greater amount of brotherhood develop between Ephraim and Judah. And as Ephraim and Judah work together, world harmony and love can be fostered among all God’s children on earth.
