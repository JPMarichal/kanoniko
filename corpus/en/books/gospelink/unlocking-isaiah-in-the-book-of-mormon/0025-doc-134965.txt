# Isaiah’s Message for Latter-day Saints

### Isaiah’s Message for Latter-day Saints
Victor L. Ludlow, Unlocking Isaiah in the Book of Mormon (Salt Lake City 2003)
 
 

Isaiah’s Message for Latter-day Saints
 

The last major Isaiah passage quoted in the Book of Mormon is the Savior’s citation of Isaiah 54 in 3 Nephi 22. He then admonishes His listeners (and readers) to search the words of Isaiah because they will eventually all be realized (3 Nephi 23:1–3). And, as He had earlier told His Nephite-Lamanite audience, when the words of Isaiah are fulfilled, then God’s covenant with the house of Israel will be fulfilled (3 Nephi 20:12, 46).
 

As covenant Latter-day Saints living in the Dispensation of the Fullness of Times, we have a better opportunity to witness the completion of Isaiah’s prophecies than any earlier generation. In addition, we have the second testimony of the Book of Mormon with its emphasis upon key Isaiah chapters. The Book of Mormon provides valuable prophetic commentary and insightful interpretation from ancient prophets. Coupled with inspired teachings from prophets and apostles of this dispensation, we possess wonderful tools for understanding Isaiah.
 

Isaiah’s message to Latter-day Saints encompasses the whole intent of the holy scriptures. In particular, his words help us achieve the twofold purpose of the Book of Mormon: to teach the house of Israel and to testify to the world about Jesus.1
 

Scriptures teach at least three key elements:
 

• The knowledge of the Lord’s works that He has done (and will yet do) among the children of men, especially His mercy toward them (compare Moroni 10:3).
 

• The covenants of the Lord, which were promised to Abraham, defined by Moses, elaborated upon by Isaiah, and testified to by the Book of Mormon prophets.
 

• The promise that we are not forgotten on this earth, especially in these latter-days.
 

Scriptures testify of Jesus in three significant dimensions:
 

• Jesus is the Christ, the Messiah or Anointed One, foreordained to administer Heavenly Father’s plan of salvation among His children on earth.
 

• Jesus is an Eternal God, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; the God of Isaiah; the God of Nephi, Jacob, and Abinadi who quoted so much of Isaiah in the Book of Mormon; the God of Joseph Smith and the prophets of the Restoration, including our current prophet and mouthpiece for the Lord—Gordon B. Hinckley.
 

• Jesus is a revelator whose light and love manifest His divine nature to all peoples, especially those who seek Him.
 

As you review Isaiah’s messages in the Book of Mormon, you will find that every chapter helps fulfill one or more of these purposes!
 

We can find and know and follow Jesus the Christ through the words of Isaiah as recorded and interpreted in the Book of Mormon. As the prophet Nephi testifies, through the words of Isaiah we are more fully persuaded to believe in the Lord our Redeemer for our eternal profit and learning (1 Nephi 19:23).
 

The author’s personal witness is:
 

• Isaiah is profound. He speaks to all God’s children with messages of eternal importance.
 

• Isaiah is relevant. The Isaiah passages in the Book of Mormon help us recognize God’s teachings and prophecies that have the greatest value for us in the last days.
 

• Isaiah is understandable. With the Holy Spirit as a companion, our reading, pondering, and studying of Isaiah will bear rich spiritual fruits.
 

Isaiah’s ultimate message for Latter-day Saints is, “Sing with joy and testify with strength, you inhabitants of Zion, for great is the Holy One of Israel in your midst.” (2 Nephi 22:5–6/Isaiah 12:5–6.)
