# Notes on Contributors

### Notes on Contributors
Robert L. Millet, Kent P. Jackson, Studies in Scripture, Vol. 2: The Pearl of Great Price ( 1985)
 
 

Notes on Contributors

ELDER BRUCE R. McCONKIE, until the time of his death in April 1985, was a member of the Council of the Twelve of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He was sustained as a member of the First Council of the Seventy at the age of 31 in 1946 and was ordained an apostle on 12 October 1972 by President Harold B. Lee. Elder McConkie is the author of the encyclopedic volume Mormon Doctrine and has written a three-volume doctrinal commentary on the New Testament, as well as a six-volume masterwork on the ministry of the Messiah. He and his wife, the former Amelia Smith, are the parents of nine children.

EDWARD J. BRANDT is Associate Director of the Institute of Religion at the University of Utah in Salt Lake City. He received his Ph.D. in Ancient Scripture from BYU and is a well-known writer to members of the Church through his articles in Church publications. He and his wife Carol are the parents of eight children.

LARRY E. DAHL is Associate Professor of Church History and Doctrine at Brigham Young University. He received his Ed.D. from BYU in 1971 in Public School Administration and Religious Education. Brother Dahl worked for many years for the Church Educational System as an instructor, curriculum writer, and district and division coordinator. He was for a time employed by the Church in Instructional Development and as a staff member of the Melchizedek Priesthood committee. Brother Dahl and his wife Roberta are the parents of nine children.

RICHARD D. DRAPER is an instructor at the Institute of Religion adjacent to the Utah Technical College in Orem, Utah. Brother Draper has been associated with the Church Educational System for a number of years as an institute instructor, as well as a member of the College Curriculum staff. he received his M.A. degree from Arizona State University and concentrated on the American Westward Movement. He is completing a Ph.D. at BYU in Ancient History, with an emphasis on early Christianity. Brother Draper and his wife Barbara are the parents of six children.

JAMES R. HARRIS is Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. He received his B.S., M.A., and Ed.D. degrees from BYU, and has studied at the Oriental Institute of the University of Chicago. Brother Harris came to BYU in 1966 and has taught classes in the Pearl of Great Price for many years. He and his wife Betty are the parents of five children.

KENT P. JACKSON is Associate Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University, where he serves as Old Testament area coordinator and as a member of the Editorial Board of BYU Studies. Brother Jackson obtained a B.A. from BYU and his M.A. and Ph.D. degrees from the University of Michigan in Near Eastern Studies. He is a specialist in ancient writings, including ancient scripture. He has published a major book on the language of the ancient Ammonites, as well as numerous articles on both LDS and non-LDS topics. He joined the BYU faculty in 1980. Brother Jackson and his wife Nancy are the parents of five children.

DEAN C. JESSEE is a research historian for the Joseph Fielding Smith Institute of Church History at Brigham Young University. He has studied the journals, diaries, letters, and histories of Joseph Smith for many years now and has recently edited the monumental work, The Personal Writings of Joseph Smith. He and his wife June are the parents of eight children.

JOHN M. LUNDQUIST is Assistant Professor of Ancient Scripture and Anthropology at Brigham Young University. Both his Masters and Ph.D. degrees were taken at the University of Michigan in Near Eastern Studies. In addition, Brother Lundquist is Director of the North Orontes Valley expedition at Tell Qarqur in Syria. He is the father of six children.

JOSEPH FIELDING McCONKIE is Associate Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. He obtained his Ed.D. from BYU in 1973 and has worked with the Church Educational System as a seminary instructor and institute director. Brother McConkie has also worked as an Army Chaplain. He is the author of a number of excellent LDS works now in print. Brother McConkie and his wife Brenda are the parents of eight children.

ROBERT J. MATTHEWS is Dean of Religious Education and Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Brother Matthews obtained his Ph.D. from BYU in 1968, his doctoral dissertation representing a major textual study of Joseph Smith' translation of the Bible. Dean Matthews was associated for a number of years with Seminaries and Institutes and worked as a seminary and institute instructor, as well as director of academic research. Brother Matthews and his wife Shirley are the parents of four children.

ROBERT L. MILLET is Assistant Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Before joining the faculty at BYU in 1983 he worked with the Church Educational System as a seminary instructor, institute director, and teaching support consultant. Brother Millet received his B.S. and M.S. degrees from BYU in Psychology and obtained his Ph.D. from Florida State University in Biblical Studies, 19th- and 20th-Century Religious Thought, and Psychology. He and his wife Shauna are the parents of five children.

HUGH W. NIBLEY is Professor Emeritus of History and Religion at Brigham Young University. For a time he was the Director of the Institute of Ancient Studies. He began his graduate study in history at UCLA and completed his Ph.D. at the University of California at Berkeley. Brother Nibley has been a prolific writer, is one of the foremost Latter-day Saint scholars, and has been a conscientious defender of the faith. He and his wife Phyllis are the parents of eight children.

MONTE S. NYMAN is Associate Dean of Religious Education and Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Brother Nyman is a recognized authority on the Book of Mormon and for years wrote the institute course of study. He has also written a major work on the life and message of Isaiah the prophet. He received his Ed.D. from Brigham Young University in 1965 and joined the faculty at BYU in 1966. Before coming to BYU Brother Nyman taught in the public schools and also worked with the Church Educational System as a seminary and institute instructor. He and his wife Mary Ann are the parents of eight children.

ALAN K. PARRISH is Assistant Professor of Ancient Scripture at BYU. Before joining the faculty at Brigham Young University in 1984, Brother Parrish worked with the Church Educational System as Director of the Institute of Religion in Cambridge, Massachusetts; prior to that time he directed the Institute at UCLA. He received his Ed.D. from the University of Southern California. He and his wife Marcia are the parents of three children.

H. DONL PETERSON is Professor of Ancient Scripture at BYU and Director of the Pearl of Great Price division of the Religious Studies Center at that institution. Brother Peterson began work with the Church Educational System as a seminary instructor in 1954 and thereafter worked as an instructor in the institutes of religion. He received his Ed.D. from Washington State University in 1965 and has been a member of the faculty at BYU since 1964. He and his wife Mary Lou are the parents of six children.

LARRY C. PORTER is Professor of Church History and Doctrine at BYU and Director of the Church History division of the Religious Studies Center of that institution. Brother Porter received his Ph.D. in the History of Religion at Brigham Young University, his doctoral dissertation representing a major treatment of the history of the Church in the New York and Pennsylvania periods. Before joining the BYU faculty, Brother Porter worked as a seminary instructor and administrator in the Church Educational System. He and his wife LaDawn are the parents of nine children.

STEPHEN D. RICKS is Assistant Professor of Hebrew and Semitic Languages at Brigham Young University. Brother Ricks received his Ph.D. in Near Eastern Religions at the University of California-Berkeley and Graduate Theological Union. He has been employed at BYU since 1981. He and his wife Shirley have five children.

RODNEY TURNER is Professor of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Brother Turner received his A.B. and M.A. degrees at Brigham Young University, his areas of concentration being the Bible, Modern Scripture, and Theology. He received his Ed.D. from the University of Southern California in Educational Administration and Philosophy. In addition to his many years at BYU, Brother Turner has worked with the Department of Seminaries and Institutes of Religion for the Church and is the author of two major doctrinal works. He and his wife Bonnie are the parents of six children.

BRUCE A. VAN ORDEN is a curriculum writer for the Church Educational System of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He is presently completing a Ph.D. at Brigham Young University in American, LDS, and Modern European History, his area of special expertise being late 19th-century LDS history. Brother Van Orden and his wife Karen have four children.
