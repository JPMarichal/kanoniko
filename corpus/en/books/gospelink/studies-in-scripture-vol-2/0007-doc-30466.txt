# The Grand Council (Moses 4:1-4; Abraham 3:22-28)

### The Grand Council (Moses 4:1-4; Abraham 3:22-28)
Robert L. Millet, Kent P. Jackson, Studies in Scripture, Vol. 2: The Pearl of Great Price ( 1985)
 
 

5

THE GRAND COUNCIL

(Moses 4:1-4; Abraham 3:22-28)

JOSEPH F. MCCONKIE

The third chapter of Abraham and fourth chapter of Moses contain our most informative scriptural texts describing the heavenly council at which Christ was called and chosen to be our Redeemer. This is known to us as the Grand Council. It was in this council that Lucifer, who sought the honor accorded to Christ, rebelled and came out in open warfare against God and his beloved son. The texts in both Abraham and Moses are brief and assume a knowledge of the preexistence, foreordinations, and heavenly councils on the part of the reader. Though these doctrines are lost to the theology of the churches of our day they were common to the understanding of the ancient Saints and were well known to those of Abraham's and Moses' day. Indeed we find potsherds from these vessels of truth almost everywhere we start digging into the past. The Old Testament is replete with references to such doctrines, though they have been obscured by the ignorance of Bible translators. They are even more evident in Old Testament apocrypha; they are common to old Jewish sources; we find them woven into Greek thought, and even with surprising frequency in the Quran. They are also common to the New Testament and the writings of the apostolic fathers, though since the middle of the sixth century A.D. they have languished in a papal prison. In A.D. 553 the doctrine of the preexistence of the soul was dropped from Christianity by an edict known as the "Anathema against Origen." This was the work of the Roman Emperor Justinian; the Pope consented under extreme duress. Thereafter these doctrines have quietly slipped from the attention and interest of the religious world.

Knowledge Restored—Line Upon Line

The knowledge of these great truths has been restored to us by Joseph Smith. He first learned of them while translating the Book of Mormon, for therein we have the great discourse of Alma about the foreordination of those who hold the Melchizedek Priesthood. This honor, Alma said, was reserved for those who chose good and exercised "exceeding great faith" in "the first place." Others lost that birthright because of the "hardness of their hearts and blindness of their minds, while, if it had not been for this they might have had as great privilege as their brethren" (Alma 13:3-4). While Joseph Smith was laboring on his inspired revision of the Bible, sometime between June and October of 1830, he restored by revelation an account of how Satan had come before the Father in the Grand Council seeking to be chosen as the savior of mankind. "Behold, here am I, send me," offered Satan. "I will be thy son, and I will redeem all mankind, that one soul shall not be lost, and surely I will do it; wherefore give me thine honor." In contrast to this vainglorious and self-serving offer, we then read the Father saying:

But, behold, my Beloved Son, which was my Beloved and Chosen from the beginning, said unto me—Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever. Wherefore, because that Satan rebelled against me, and sought to destroy the agency of man, which I, the Lord God, had given him, and also, that I should give unto him mine own power; by the power of mine Only Begotten, I caused that he should be cast down; And he became Satan, yea, even the devil, the father of all lies, to deceive and to blind men, and to lead them captive at his will, even as many as would not hearken unto my voice (Moses 4:1-4).

It was approximately at the same time (September 1830) that Joseph Smith, in a revelation describing the fall of Adam, received further detail about the rebellion in the heavenly council. This text also states that Satan sought the "honor" and "power" of God and that he successfully swayed "a third part of the hosts of heaven" to follow him. All such were cast out of heaven, and we are told "there is a place prepared for them from the beginning, which place is hell" (D&C 29:36-38).

The next significant addition to our understanding of this story came as a part of the revelation of the degrees of glory on 16 February 1832. As part of this series of visions the Prophet saw Satan as "a son of the morning" presiding in a position of authority over his fellow spirits in the pre-earth world. He also saw how Satan rebelled against both the Father and Christ and was cast out of that kingdom. The Prophet then recorded how the hosts of heaven wept over the loss of such a great spirit saying, "He is fallen! is fallen, even a son of the morning!" (D&C 76:25-27). 1

This line-upon-line unfolding of the story of the great heavenly council continued with the translation of Abraham 3 sometime between 1835 and 1842. Here with Joseph Smith we learn how God stood in the midst of his spirit offspring in a great heavenly assembly, and viewing them said of those who had distinguished themselves as "noble and great": "These I will make my rulers." Abraham was then told that he was one of those so chosen before he was born. As the revelation continues we are told that among the spirits stood one "like unto God" who spoke to those who were with him saying,

We will go down, for there is space there, and we will take of these materials, and we will make an earth whereon these may dwell; And we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them; And they who keep their first estate shall be added upon; and they who keep not their first estate shall not have glory in the same kingdom with those who keep their first estate; and they who keep their second estate shall have glory added upon their heads for ever and ever (Abr. 3:22-26).

Then, and this without any explanation or introduction, the account states: "And the Lord said: Whom shall I send? And one answered like unto the Son of Man: Here am I, send me. And another answered and said: Here am I, send me. And the Lord said: I will send the first. And the second was angry, and kept not his first estate; and, at that day, many followed after him" (Abr. 3:27-28).

Modern Revelation Unlocks Bible Mysteries

Because of the revelations on this subject given to Joseph Smith and the texts that he has restored to us, Bible passages—the meaning of which have been long lost to the world—are also unveiled to us. We now can read the following passage from Isaiah with considerable meaning:

How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations!

For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north:

I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.

Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the pit (Isa. 14:12-15).

A second passage that takes on considerable meaning comes from the Book of Revelation. It declares that there was a "war in heaven: Michael [meaning Adam] and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels, And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven. And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him" (Rev. 12:7-9). Then John, who apparently saw the same vision seen by Joseph Smith, said: "I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power [authority] of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night" (Rev. 12:10). The Revelator noted that the battle ground for a war that commenced in heaven had shifted to the earth, but the adversaries remained the same. Satan and his army, which consists of "the third part of the stars of heaven" or a third of that heavenly host that joined him in his rebellion, being cast out with him, continue their efforts to get all others to worship him as their Redeemer and their God.

Nature and Purpose of Pre-Earth Life

Combining our texts into one, the following story unfolds: Life did not begin on earth, but rather in a pre-earth experience or spirit state. Here Joseph Smith explained, "God himself, finding he was in the midst of spirits and glory, because he was more intelligent, saw proper to institute laws whereby the rest could have a privilege to advance like himself." 2 Thus he granted to his spirit offspring the godly gift of agency. Now in order for them to have agency the following conditions had to exist: (1) there had to be laws, laws which could be obeyed or disobeyed; (2) there had to be opposition, that is, good and evil, right and wrong; (3) an ability to distinguish between the good and evil, the right and the wrong, had to be enjoyed by those expected to utilize their agency; (4) and of course the unfettered power of choice had to exist. That some used their agency in the pursuit of righteousness, while others did not, is evident from Alma's discourse. At least some, we learn from D&C 76, held positions of authority, and all, it would appear from Abraham 3, had the opportunity to prove themselves "noble and great." By implication those not distinguishing themselves as "noble and great" were something less than that. Indeed many proved themselves both ignoble and base, for the "third part" of the citizenry of this heavenly kingdom were cast out to this earth and eventually to a hell that awaits them.

The purpose of this spirit existence was obviously to train and prepare the spirits for their experience here on earth. In a more recent revelation we are told that the pre-earth life was a place of "lessons" and preparation (D&C 138:56). Many meetings, classes, and councils would have been held. Surely if there was a "grand" council there must have been others from which it was so distinguished. In fact we are told that there was a "law, irrevocably decreed in heaven before the foundations of this world, upon which all blessings are predicated," such that those who were more zealous than others would have so much the advantage in earth life (D&C 130:19-20). To this Abraham added that "they who keep their first estate [that is, those that used their agency wisely and righteously in the preexistence] shall be added upon [meaning, having the privilege granted to them of coming to earth life]; and they who keep not their first estate shall not have glory in the same kingdom with those who keep their first estate; and they who keep their second estate [are wise and righteous during their earthly experience] shall have glory added upon their heads for ever and ever" (Abr. 3:26).

The Plan of Salvation

Thus we learn that the children of God who "keep" both their first and second estates will in "due time receive of his fulness" (D&C 93:19). Such is the doctrine of salvation and such is the purpose of the plan of salvation, which plan was taught to all the hosts of heaven in labored detail. All the laws and ordinances of salvation were "ordained" and known to the heavenly hosts even "before the world was" (D&C 132:11). Hence we find Paul testifying of the hope which we have laid up in heaven, where he said we "heard before" the truths of the gospel taught (Col. 1:5). The scriptural texts already reviewed evidence that the gospel had been taught and the plan of salvation was known to all. How else could the Father meaningfully ask of the assembled spirits "Whom shall I send?" Send where and for what? All knew that for them to continue their progression and have the opportunity to become like God the following conditions had to exist:

1. An earth had to be created, one to which they could come and obtain through the process of birth a tangible or physical body like that of their divine Father.

2. This earth when first created would be paradisiacal, and those first placed on it would not be subject to death nor would they be able to procreate. In order for them to do so they would have to comply with those conditions which would cause them, and all that had been created, to fall from this edenic state to one of a lower nature in which they could be tried and tested, have the privilege of procreation, and in which the body and spirit would be temporarily separated in the experience known to us as death.

3. The effects of this fallen earth could only be overcome through the atoning sacrifice of one free from sin. Further, this Sinless One would have within his nature both the ability to lay down his life and the power to take it up again. This would break the bands of death and its hold on all others and bring to pass the resurrection—the inseparable union of body and spirit.

4. The one ordained to redeem mankind from the effects of the fall must have God as his father, that he might inherit from him the capacity to live forever. From a mortal woman as his mother he would inherit blood, or the ability to die. The one so chosen would thus become the Only Begotten of the Father in the flesh.

5. Through this atoning sacrifice all would be resurrected to a state of immortality and those who had proven themselves true and faithful would obtain the fulness of the Father, or what is spoken of in the scriptures as eternal life.

Without such knowledge, the question of the Father, "Whom shall I send?" and the response of him who was to become our Savior, "Father, thy will be done," would be without meaning.

This marvelous story has often been told poorly in Latter-day Saint circles, with the idea given that the Father, not knowing how to save men and what they must do to become like him, asked for suggestions from the assembled host and that two plans were presented. Such was hardly the case. God, an exalted man himself, hardly needs counsel from his own children on how they are to become like him. His question was not "What shall I do?" but "Whom shall I send?" That is, "Whom shall I send to be your Redeemer in my plan for your salvation?"

The Heavenly Council

What then took place in the grand council? From what has been revealed to us up to this point, we know this much: The Father assembled his spirit offspring and reviewed with them the principles with which they would have to comply and the experiences they would have in order to become like him. Having once again explained the necessity in this plan for a Redeemer, he then asked the question, "Whom shall I send?" In response our elder brother—he whom we know as Jesus the Christ, the firstborn of all spirits—whose birthright it was, came forward and said, "Here am I, send me." He further said, "Father, thy will be done, and glory be thine forever." His expression then was to this effect, "Father I know and understand the eternal plan you have given for the salvation of your children. I accept that plan, I have faith in that plan, and I willingly offer myself as the sacrifice necessitated by that plan. In doing so I seek no honor for myself, the glory be thine forever; I seek only to bless and be blessed with these my brothers and sisters." Then came forth another, he who was known as Lucifer, the son of the morning, saying "Behold, here am I, send me, I will be thy son, and I will redeem all mankind, that one soul shall not be lost, and surely I will do it; wherefore give me thine honor." In effect he then said, "In exchange for their agency, I will save all your spirit children, that they with thee might worship me." Whereupon the Father said, "I will send the first," and the Abraham account reads, "And the second was angry, and kept not his first estate; and, at that day, many followed after him" (Abr. 3:28). By way of explanation the Moses account adds: "Because that Satan rebelled against me, and sought to destroy the agency of man, which I, the Lord God, had given him, and also, that I should give unto him mine own power; by the power of mine Only Begotten, I caused that he should be cast down; And he became Satan, yea, even the devil, the father of all lies, to deceive and to blind men, and to lead them captive at his will, even as many as would not hearken unto my voice" (Moses 4:3-4).

As a confirmation of these accounts, Christ, in his earthly ministry, constantly affirmed his role as the representative of the Father: "I came into the world to do the will of my Father, because my Father sent me. And my Father sent me that I might be lifted up upon the cross" (3 Ne. 27:13-14). "My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work" (John 4:34). "I can of mine own self do nothing: . . . I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me" (John 5:30). "For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me" (John 6:38).

Bible Texts

As suggested at the beginning of this article, the concepts of preexistence, heavenly councils, and foreordinations are everywhere to be found in the scriptures and the literature of antiquity. Let us illustrate this briefly by turning first to Ps. 82:1 which reads as follows in the King James translation: "God standeth in the congregation of the mighty; he judgeth among the gods." The word "mighty" in this verse is a translation of the Hebrew word 'el, which actually means "God." In the New English Bible the verse reads: "God takes his stand in the court of heaven to deliver judgement among the gods." In the Jerusalem Bible it reads: "God stands in the divine assembly, among the gods he dispenses justice." The verse is an obvious companion passage to our primary text in Abraham 3. Surely if God was to stand in the midst of the spirits, as Abraham tells us, and identify some as "noble and great," then others must have been something less than that. A judgment had indeed been made. From the rebellion that was to follow his choice of the Firstborn to be our Redeemer, it is evident that some had become "ignoble and extremely wicked." In the sixth verse of this Psalm we read these words: "I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the most high." One non-Latter-day Saint scholar, after nearly a hundred pages of analysis on this Psalm, concluded that this verse was addressed to the offspring of Deity, who had sinned, and thus come under judgment. He suggested that, in effect, the Father was saying to his rebellious children, "I thought that ye were gods, but since you have proven yourselves unworthy you will now be divested of your divine natures and forfeit the privilege of living in heaven." 3

A contrasting text, in that it emphasizes the blessings promised to the righteous of the pre-earth spirits, is found in the first chapter of Ephesians. Here Paul testifies that the Father of Jesus Christ blessed the "faithful in Christ Jesus" with "all spiritual blessings" in "heavenly places" when they were "chosen" to represent him "before the foundation of the world" (Eph. 1:1-4). In the great revelation on the redemption of the dead, President Joseph F. Smith used the same language to describe the foreordination of the great prophets of this dispensation that was used to describe Abraham's foreordination. He identified Joseph Smith, Hyrum Smith, Brigham Young, John Taylor, and Wilford Woodruff by name, saying that they, along with "other choice spirits," were reserved to come forth in this age to lay the foundation and restore the principles whereby all might be saved. "I observed," he said, "that they were also among the noble and great ones who were chosen in the beginning to be rulers in the Church of God." Continuing he noted, "Even before they were born, they, with many others, received their first lessons in the world of spirits and were prepared to come forth in the due time of the Lord to labor in his vineyard for the salvation of the souls of men" (D&C 138:53-56). 4 Similarly, when Paul testified that God had "set me apart before I was born" (RSV, Gal. 1:15), he was paraphrasing the language of Jeremiah who said the word of the Lord came to him saying: "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations" (Jer. 1:5).

Appropriately the expressions of the Lord to his prophets are used in the scriptures to illustrate these principles. It should not be understood, however, that the principles apply exclusively to them. The blessings and promises of the gospel are not appended to an office or calling. What is true of prophets is true of all who are of the household of faith. "What I say unto one I say unto all," the Lord said (D&C 93:49). Thus in Abraham 2 we read the Lord's promise to Abraham that those called and chosen to hold the priesthood and teach the plan of salvation to all the nations of the earth were destined to come to earth as his seed (Abr. 2:9, 11). Affirming this pre-earth assignment to families and nations, Moses said, "When the most High divided to the nations their inheritance, when he separated the sons of Adam, he set the bounds of the people according to the number of children of Israel. For the Lord's portion is his people; Jacob is the lot of his inheritance" (Deut. 32:8-9).

The Literature of Antiquity

It is marvelous how perfectly an apocryphal work known as the Apocalypse of Abraham (composed near the end of the first century A.D.), found long after Joseph Smith's death, sustains his translation of the Abraham manuscripts and the other revelations we received from him on this subject. This Old Slavonic manuscript contains the following dialogue between Abraham and God as Abraham views in vision a great assembly of spirits yet to be born.

And I [Abraham] said: Primeval One, Strong One, what is this picture of the creature? And he said to me: This is my will in relation to that which has a being in the Council, and it became pleasing before me, and then afterwards I commanded them to be through my word. And it came to pass that as many as I had authorized to exist, before portrayed in this picture, and had stood before me pre-created,—as many as you have seen.

And I said: Ruler, Strong One, Thou Who Wast Before the World, Who are the multitude in this picture, on the right hand and on the left?

And He said to Me: These upon the left side are the multitudes of former generations, and those to come after you. These for judgment and order; these for vengeance and destruction at the end of the world. But those on the right side of the picture are the people chosen for me, separate from the peoples of Azazel. These are those which I have prepared to be born through you and to be called my people. 5

The extent of the idea of foreordinations in apocryphal sources is perhaps best illustrated by the vision of Rabbi Ishmael in 3 Enoch. Ishmael was shown all generations and their doings, both past and future. He saw the rulers of each generation, he saw the shepherds, oppressors, keepers, scourgers, overseers, judges, court officers, teachers, supporters, chiefs, presidents of academies, magistrates, princes, counsellors, nobles, men of might, the elders, and the guides. He saw each generation, their doings and thoughts, from Adam down to the last days, and the time of the anointed prophet who was to be a son of Joseph, and then the coming of the Messiah, son of David and the "fights and wars that God and Magog will fight." 6

Let us take one other example of these doctrines as they are found in our rapidly expanding library of apocryphal works. In this instance, our source is a New Testament work claiming to have been written by Thomas. In it is quoted a rather lengthy allegory from an earlier Syriac source. It is the story of a king's son who is required to leave his father's kingdom, where he enjoyed great wealth, to obtain a pearl. The pearl, quite obviously, is a symbol representing his own soul. His parents take great pains to see that he is provisioned properly for the journey, though before leaving their presence he is required to surrender his splendid robe. This robe, or garment of light, we are told, had been woven to the measure of his stature. He also enters into a covenant with his parents to obtain the pearl and return, that he might once again enjoy their presence and wear his splendid robe. The covenant was written on his heart.

Though the way was hazardous and difficult, an intimate friend, referred to as "anointed one," warns him of the dangers that beset him. Not withstanding this, he soon forgets that he is a king's son and his mission to obtain the pearl. At this point a council is held, attended by his father, mother, brother (the crown prince), and many other great and mighty ones. They determine to send him a letter imploring him to awake and remember who he is and what king he serves. He is encouraged to remember his splendid robe and to so conduct himself that his name might be written in the book of the heroes, and with his brother be an heir to his father's kingdom. Thus reminded, he commences again to obtain the pearl, which he must wrestle from a terrible serpent. This he is able to do only by naming his father's name, that of his brother, and that of his mother. Having obtained the pearl, he flees Egypt, sheds his dirty and unclean garments, and is further guided by the letter. At this point, he is greeted by messengers from his parents who clothe him once more in his royal robe, and he returns as an heir to his father's kingdom. 7

These examples of ancient literature, though they come in their present form from people who did not have the gospel in its fulness, reflect a greater light from an earlier day in which the plan of salvation was known among the Lord's Saints. As such they bear testimony of Joseph Smith—and of the antiquity and divinity of the revelations of Moses and Abraham that were restored in the Pearl of Great Price.

Conclusion

Nowhere is the nature of the plan of salvation better illustrated than in the story of the Grand Council. First it teaches us that there is no salvation in ignorance—all must know the plan and principles by which salvation comes; it dramatizes the absolute necessity of agency—there can be no forced salvation; it affirms that there is but one plan or way by which salvation can come—the plan ordained by the Father; it establishes that in the government of God all service is voluntary, yet it is for him to choose those who will represent him—it is his Church and he runs it; this, in turn, attests that God is our king and that the government of heaven is not a democracy, but a kingdom. Further, it emphasizes that God did not attempt to raise his family without a plan for their salvation and that he frequently sat in council with them and instructed them. The real significance of these conclusions becomes evident when it is remembered that these principles are eternal and that they are as important to governing the Church and families on earth as they were in heaven. Things on earth are to follow the heavenly pattern, "that which is spiritual being in the likeness of that which is temporal; and that which is temporal in the likeness of that which is spiritual," as the Lord told Joseph Smith (D&C 77:2). Such was the instruction of God to Moses on Sinai (Ex. 25:40), for all things approved of God must, he was told, follow the "patterns of things in the heavens" (Heb. 9:23). God did not seek our imput in formulating the plan of salvation in premortality, he does not seek it now. We did not call ourselves to minister in his kingdom then; we do not call ourselves to such a position now; the Church was not a democracy then, it is not a democracy now. It is the Lord who makes the laws, issues the decrees and shows the way whereby we might become like him. And as in all things, Christ, in this heavenly council, set the perfect example for his fellow spirits, responding to the call extended him saying: "Father, thy will be done."
