THE BIRTH AND DEVELOPMENT OF THE PEARL OF GREAT PRICE

H. DONL PETERSON

Introduction

The mid-nineteenth century was a unique and exciting time in church history, due, in part, to four unusual circumstances that had developed in the late 1840s and early 1850s:

1. The Church headquarters had been moved from Nauvoo, Illinois to Great Salt Lake City in the Territory of Deseret. The inclement weather, shorter growing seasons, irrigation and agricultural problems, lack of supplies, transportation problems, Indian relationships, communication problems, financial, governmental, and many other concerns were all very real as the Saints attempted to conquer the arid lands in their new home.

2. The Center of LDS Church population had shifted to England by 1850. The missionaries had met with unbelievable success in Great Britain in the 1840s and 1850s. 1 The following chart is informative. 2

Note that the membership of the British Mission nearly quadrupled in two years between 1840 and 1842. Its rapid growth leveled off in 1844 when the missionaries were needed back home in Illinois to protect their families and properties from mob violence and other threats from those who already had murdered the Prophet Joseph Smith and the Patriarch to the Church, his brother Hyrum, at Carthage.

Even when the Church leaders under extremely difficult conditions were leading the faithful to a new home in the west, missionaries were called to return to England to oversee Church matters there.

The first company of Saints arrived in the Salt Lake Valley on 24 July 1847. As is noted on the chart, while the Saints were building a new civilization in what was then Mexican territory, the Church was flourishing in Great Britain. Between 1840 and 1850 the Church membership had grown from 1,671 to 30,747, a growth rate of over 1,840 percent.

3. Problems with Communications were Extreme. As is noted in the chart, in 1850 Great Britain claimed nearly thirty-one thousand Latter-day Saints while all of Salt Lake Valley, headquarters for the Church, boasted of only slightly over six thousand members. The worldwide membership of the Church in 1850 was about fifty-seven thousand, meaning that over half of the members lived in the British Isles. The balance of them were scattered in various locations in the western states, many were in process of crossing the plains to Utah, and groups were located in several small settlements in the vast, sprawling Utah Territory.

At that time, many dynamic General Authorities were called to Great Britain in order to meet the needs pursuant to the rapid growth of the British Mission. Prominent leaders such as Brigham Young, Wilford Woodruff, Orson Pratt, and Franklin D. Richards were found serving in the British Mission.

4. There was a Shortage of Paper. The Western desert, the new headquarters for the Church, was quite barren of easily accessible timber, a vital commodity in paper-making. What little paper was manufactured or acquired from eastern mills was needed to carry out the official business for the United States Territory of Utah and highpriority needs of the Church. The Church scriptures, missionary tracts, and various record books were not published at Church headquarters for nearly three decades because of paper shortage. It was many years later, in 1869, when the railroad finally united the East and the West, that the needed paper was available from eastern mills.

The attempt of the Saints to maintain a newspaper those first years, The Deseret News Weekly, is a story of courage, frustration, disappointment, and ingenuity. In the 30 November 1850 Deseret News Weekly the following editorial insert is found: "Save your rags, everybody in Deseret, Save your rags. Old wagon covers, tents, quilts, shirts, etc., etc., are wanted for paper. The most efficient measures are in progress to put a paper mill in operation the coming season in this valley, and all your rags will be wanted. Make your woolen rags into carpeting, and save importation." This excerpt is typical of a series of editorial pleadings over a period of years, soliciting the cooperation of the subscribers to supply the much-needed rags to aid in the cause of papermaking.

The Birth of the Pearl of Great Price

England in 1850 was a very compact, highly industrialized country. It was perhaps the most advanced country in the world and one of the great powers. Great Britain had a population of over twenty-seven million, and, by way of contrast, the United States claimed about twenty-three million citizens. Publishing houses were plentiful in England in 1850-51.

The General Authorities resolved the paper shortage in Utah by having the Church literature printed in England. When missionaries would return home after serving a British mission, some would be charged with the responsibility of overseeing the safe arrival of several hundred copies of various Church books back to Church headquarters. Generally, the returning missionaries would leave Liverpool and set sail to New Orleans, sail up the Mississippi to St. Louis, west on the Missouri River to the frontier, and finally across the plains by wagon to Great Salt Lake.

The British Mission Presidents, particularly Elders Orson Pratt and Franklin D. Richards, were responsible for the publication of the following Church works: the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, the Millennial Star (the British Mission publication), the Church Hymn Book, and various missionary tracts. 3 Many of the British Saints were without the Church literature because of its lack of availability, and also because of their own limited finances. The great numbers of recent converts and the famine of Church publications had caused a dilemma.

As President Franklin D. Richards traveled about Britain he realized how anxious the Saints were for additional Church literature. President Richards had preserved some of the Prophet Joseph Smith's early sermons and articles from the Church periodicals published in Ohio, Missouri, and Illinois. These were out of print and not available to the anxious British Saints. The Saints constantly pleaded with President Richards to make these great gospel "pearls" available to them. With the authority he held as one of the Twelve Apostles and as Mission President in Great Britain, Elder Richards chose to make available to the British Saints some of the choice documents he had collected from among the writings of Joseph Smith. He chose to call his little mission publication the "Pearl of Great Price." The title came from the Savior's parable of the same name (see Matt. 13:45-46). The following is the Preface from the first edition (1851) of the Pearl of Great Price.

PREFACE

THE following compilation has been induced by the repeated solicitations of several friends of the publisher, who are desirous to be put in possession of the very important articles contained therein. Most of the Revelations composing this work were published at early periods of the Church, when the circulation of its journals was so very limited as to render them comparatively unknown at present, except to a few who have treasured up the productions of the Church with great care from the beginning. A smaller portion of this work has never before appeared in print; and altogether it is presumed, that true believers in the Divine mission of the Prophet JOSEPH SMITH, will appreciate this little collection of precious truths as a Pearl of Great Price that will increase their ability to maintain and to defend the holy faith by becoming possessors of it.

Although not adapted, nor designed, as a pioneer of the faith among unbelievers, still it will commend itself to all careful students of the scriptures, as detailing many important facts which are therein only alluded to, or entirely unmentioned, but consonant with the whole tenor of the revealed will of God; and, to the beginner in the Gospel, will add confirmatory evidence of the rectitude of his faith, by showing him that the doctrines and ordinances thereof are the same as were revealed to Adam for his salvation after his expulsion from the garden, and the same that he handed down and caused to be taught to his generations after him, as the only means appointed of God by which the generations of men may regain His presence.

Nor do we conceive it possible for any unprejudiced person to arise from a careful perusal of this work, without being deeply impressed with a sense of the Divine calling, and holy ordination, of the man by whom these revelations, translations, and narrations have been communicated to us. As impervious as the minds of men may be at present to these convictions, the day is not far distant when sinners, as well as Saints, will know that JOSEPH SMITH was one of the greatest men that ever lived upon the earth, and that under God he was the Prophet and founder of the dispensation of the fulness of times, in which will be gathered together into one all things which are in Christ, both which are in heaven and which are on earth.

FRANKLIN D. RICHARDS.

15, Wilton Street, Liverpool,

July 11th, 1851.

Note that the original Pearl of Great Price contained: (1) the preface and most of the first six chapters of Genesis from Joseph Smith's translation of the Bible; (2) the entire five chapters from the writings of Abraham; (3) Joseph Smith's translation of Matthew 24; (4) excerpts from five sections of the Doctrine and Covenants; (5) extracts from Joseph Smith's history; (6) the Articles of Faith; (7) the poem entitled "Truth"; and (8) the three facsimiles from the writings of Abraham. As one studies the Table of Contents of the original Pearl of Great Price, it is seen that Elder Richards carefully selected material that would be of great worth to members of the Church.

The First American Edition

The Pearl of Great Price was well received by the British Saints and missionaries alike. It soon became very popular with the Saints in Utah also, as the book was introduced to them by the returning missionaries and emigrating British Saints. In 1878 President John Taylor, acting president of the Church, called Elder Orson Pratt of the Council of the Twelve to prepare the first American edition of this British mission pamphlet. Elder Pratt made several changes. These were: (1) the preface was deleted, since it no longer was applicable; (2) the first two entries in the 1851 Table of Contents were combined in chronological order, and several other verses in Genesis not originally quoted by Elder Richards were added. The Inspired Version of the Bible had been published by the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in 1867 and was the sourcebook for these several changes by Orson Pratt; (3) the revelation on eternal and plural marriage (D&C 132) was added. In 1878 the Church was under heavy attack by the federal officers who were in opposition to the practice of polygamy. It seems apparent that the Brethren wanted the revelation on plural marriage to have as wide a circulation as possible.

The Canonization of the Pearl of Great Price

The 50th Semi-Annual Conference of the Church (October 1880) was a very significant conference historically. President Brigham Young had died in 1877, and during the next three years Elder John Taylor was the acting president of the Church, inasmuch as he was president of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles. The leading quorums were seated in solemn assembly to enact important conference business. Three important items of business were enacted at that conference: (1) President John Taylor was sustained as the third president of the Church, along with his counselors, George Q. Cannon and Joseph F. Smith; (2) the conference accepted the addition of 25 sections to the Doctrine and Covenants; (3) the Pearl of Great Price was accepted as the fourth standard work of the Church. The business of canonizing the new scriptures was conducted as follows:

President George Q. Cannon said: I hold in my hand the Book of Doctrine and Covenants and also the book The Pearl of Great Price, which books contain revelations of God. In Kirtland, the Doctrine and Covenants in its original form, as first printed, was submitted to the officers of the Church and the members of the Church to vote upon. As there have been additions made to it by the publishing of revelations which were not contained in the original edition, it has been deemed wise to submit these books with their contents to the Conference, to see whether the Conference will vote to accept the books and their contents as from God, and binding upon us as a people and as a Church.

President Joseph F. Smith said, I move that we receive and accept the revelations contained in these books as revelations from God to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and to all the world. The motion was seconded and sustained by unanimous vote of the whole Conference. 4

Major Changes Since 1880

In the October Semi-Annual Conference of 1890, the Articles of Faith were read to the assembled Saints by Elder Franklin D. Richards, still a member of the Council of the Twelve after forty years of service. Following his reading of them, they were presented for a sustaining vote. 5 Apparently there had been some confusion as to their scriptural status following their acceptance in 1880; it was felt neccessary to present them once again.

In 1900 Dr. James E. Talmage, formerly a faculty member at Brigham Young University and then serving on the faculty of the University of Utah, was asked by the First Presidency to make some major revisions in the Pearl of Great Price. Dr. Talmage served under the direction of a reading committee composed of General Authorities. He divided the Pearl of Great Price into chapters and verses and added numerous cross-references. He also deleted all the duplications quoted from the Doctrine and Covenants and the poem "Truth," which had been set to music and is now found in the Hymn book. The new format of the Pearl of Great Price was received unanimously by the Church at the October Conference of 1902. 6

Elder Talmage was sustained a member of the Council of the Twelve in 1911. In 1920-21 he served on a committee that made further revisions in the three latter-day scriptures. In 1920 the Book of Mormon was divided into double-column pages, and to the book were added chapter headings, chronological data, revised footnote references, a pronouncing vocabulary, and an index. The committee also prepared for publication the Doctrine and Covenants in double-column pages with chapter headings, revised footnote references, and an index. At the same time, the Pearl of Great Price was organized in conformity with this newly adopted scriptural format. The new edition was published in 1921.

At the April Conference of 1976 President Spencer W. Kimball authorized President Nathan Eldon Tanner, First Counselor in the First Presidency, to read the following statement:

President Kimball has asked me to read a very important resolution for your sustaining vote.

"At a meeting of the Council of the First Presidency and the Quorum of the Twelve held in the Salt Lake Temple on March 25, 1976, approval was given to add to the Pearl of Great Price the following revelations:

First, a vision of the celestial kingdom given to Joseph Smith the Prophet in the Kirtland Temple, on January 21, 1836, which deals with the salvation of those who die without a knowledge of the Gospel; and second, a vision given to President Joseph F. Smith in Salt Lake City, Utah, on October 3, 1918, showing the visit of the Lord Jesus Christ in the spirit world, and setting forth the doctrine of the redemption of the dead."

It is proposed that we sustain and approve this action and adopt these revelations as part of the standard works of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

All those in favor manifest it. Those opposed, if any, by the same sign.

Thank you. President Kimball, the voting seems to be unanimous in the affirmative. 7

These two revelations were the first additions to the Pearl of Great Price since 1878.

In the 1981 revision of the scriptures, the First Presidency and Council of the Twelve decided to delete these two newly canonized revelations from the Pearl of Great Price and add them as sections 137 and 138 in the Doctrine and Covenants. The announcement appeared in the Church News section of the Deseret News, 2 June 1979 as follows:

ADDITIONS TO D&C APPROVED

Joseph Smith's Vision of the Celestial Kingdom and Joseph F. Smith's Vision of the Redemption of the Dead have been transferred from the Pearl of Great Price to become Sections 137 and 138, respectively, in the Doctrine and Covenants.

The 1981 edition of the Pearl of Great Price is significant for other reasons as well. 8 The Pearl of Great Price had lacked a preface since the first edition. A new preface was now included. Some of the titles of the sections of the book were changed to be more consistent and more reflective of the content. What had been called previously "The Book of Moses" was renamed "Selections from the Book of Moses," with a subheading that reads as follows: "An extract from the translation of the Bible as revealed to Joseph Smith the Prophet, June 1830-February 1831." A section in the 1921 edition entitled "Writings of Joseph Smith" contained two chapters: "An Extract from a Translation of the Bible" (commonly called "Joseph Smith 1"), and "Extracts from the History of Joseph Smith, the Prophet" (commonly called "Joseph Smith 2"). These received new titles in the 1981 edition. The inspired revision of Matt. 23:39-24:51 is now called "Joseph Smith—Matthew," and the Prophet's history is called "Joseph Smith—History." Other changes in the 1981 Pearl of Great Price include corrected dates for the revelations contained in the selections from the Book of Moses, 9 and printing of the facsimiles of the Book of Abraham as they appeared in their first edition in the Times and Seasons in 1842. Also, a few corrections were made in the text to bring it in line with the most reliable nineteenth-century sources. 10
