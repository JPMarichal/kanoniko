JOSEPH SMITH'S DEVELOPMENTAL YEARS, 1823-29

(JS–H 50-67)

BRUCE A. VAN ORDEN

Introduction

When seventeen-year-old Joseph Smith, Jr., walked to a large hill about two and one-half miles from his farm home on 22 September 1823, he was hardly prepared or sufficiently mature to translate the Book of Mormon by the gift and power of God. When he first beheld the plates of gold, he thought of how they might aid his family to be relieved of their comparatively indigent circumstances. But through repeated chastisements from the angel and from the Lord, and through numerous harrowing experiences over the next five years, the young Prophet learned how to marshall his seeric gifts and to dedicate his whole heart and soul to the work of the Lord. By the beginning of 1829, Joseph Smith had been sufficently molded in the hands of the Almighty to move forward the translation of the sacred text and to receive heavenly visitations leading to the restoration of the holy priesthood and the Church of Jesus Christ upon the earth. This essay will demonstrate that the years 1823-29 were vital in the preparation of Joseph Smith for his work that lay ahead as the great Prophet and Seer of the Lord in the dispensation of the fullness of times.

Joseph Smith provided his own official account in 1838 of the events of the Restoration. Eventually portions of this account became part of the Pearl of Great Price and are now known as "Joseph Smith—History (JS-H). This essay will comment on that portion of Joseph Smith's history corresponding with JS-H 50-67.

The Visits to Cumorah

Joseph had no difficulty finding the hill "owing to the distinctness of the vision [he] had had concerning it" (JS-H 50). According to Oliver Cowdery, who published an account in 1833 of the Prophet's experiences with Moroni and the plates, "two invisible powers were operating upon [Joseph's] mind during his walk from his residence to Cumorah," 1 the influence of God and the opposing influence of the Evil One. By the time Joseph reached the hill, the power "urging the certainty of wealth and ease in this life" 2 had temporarily won over the mind of the young man. He quickly located the large stone that covered a stone box on the west side of the hill. Joseph had no difficulty unlodging the stone, and before long the precious plates were before his eyes.

As soon as he tried to pick up the plates, he felt a shock through his entire body, depriving him of some of his strength. He tried again but was shocked even more than the first time. With increased exertion he made a third attempt, only to be shocked more severely still. In agony of his soul, he cried out, "why can I not obtain this book?" 3 The angel Moroni once again appeared to Joseph as he had done the previous night. At once Joseph realized his folly of harboring unrighteous desires. The Lord in his mercy was teaching his young Prophet an important lesson. The angel proceeded to show Joseph in vision "the prime of darkness, surrounded by his innumerable train of associates." Moroni declared to Joseph, "All this is shown, the good and the evil, the holy and impure, the glory of God and the power of darkness, that you may know hereafter the two powers and never be influenced or overcome by that wicked one." 4

A Oliver Cowdery reported, Moroni spent several minutes further tutoring the Prophet:

You now see why you could not obtain this record; that the commandment was strict, and that if ever these sacred things are obtained they must be by prayer and faithfulness in obeying the Lord. They are not deposited here for the sake of accumulating gain and wealth for the glory of this world: they were sealed by the prayer of faith, and because of the knowledge which they contain they are of no worth among the children of men, only for the knowledge. . . . No man can obtain them if his heart is impure, because they contain that which is sacred. . . . Now, go thy way, remembering what the Lord has done for thee, and be diligent in keeping his commandments, and he will deliver thee from temptations and all the arts and devices of the wicked one.—Forget not to pray, that thy mind may become strong, that when he shall manifest unto thee, thou mayest have power to escape the evil, and obtain these precious things. 5

Joseph was also told that in the Lord's "own due time" he would obtain the plates. 6 The angel bade him to return to the hill in exactly one year for further instructions. 7

That evening at home Joseph began reciting to his family members his visions with the angel and his subsequent visit to the hill. But his eldest brother Alvin noticed Joseph's fatigue and said, "Now, brother, let us go to bed, and rise early in the morning, in order to finish our day's work at an hour before sunset, then, if mother will get our suppers early, we will have a fine long evening, and we will all sit down for the purpose of listening to you while you tell us the great things which God has revealed to you." 8 Beginning the next evening and for many evenings for the next several years the Smith family eagerly gathered together to hear young Joseph relate the various instructions he had received from the Lord. Joseph warned them that "the world was so wicked that when they came to a knowledge of these things they would try to take our lives." The family heeded his warning and kept the story to themselves. 9

One of the major projects of the Smith family at that time was the building of a substantial two-story frame home. Alvin was superintending the project, motivated by his desire to see "his father and mother once more comfortable and happy." 10 On 15 November 1823, just a few weeks after Joseph had first seen the sacred record, Alvin became seriously ill with some kind of stomach or intestinal disorder. The physician administered calomel, a laxative, which at the time was used as something of a panacea. But the mercurous chloride lodged in his stomach, and he suffered four days before he died on 19 November 1823. Before he expired, he charged the next oldest brother, Hyrum, to finish the family house. With Joseph Alvin pleaded, "I want you to be a good boy, and do everything that lies in your power to obtain the Record. Be faithful in receiving instruction, and in keeping every commandment that is given you." 11

At length the twenty-second day of September 1824 arrived, and it was time for Joseph once again to visit the repository of the plates as directed by the angel. As his mother related, Joseph excitedly approached the hill "fully expect[ing] to carry [the plates] home with him." 12 He supposed that the only thing required of him was to keep the commandments of God, and he sincerely believed that he was doing so. He uncovered the plates, and while reaching in to retrieve them "the unhappy thought darted through his mind that probably there was something else in the box besides the plates, which would be of some pecuniary advantage to him." 13 In this moment of excitement, Joseph laid aside the plates. After covering the box to keep any possible intruders from obtaining its contents, he turned around to pick up the plates again. To his astonishment and alarm, the record was not there. He knelt down and asked the Lord why the plates had been taken from him. The angel Moroni appeared and reminded him that he had not obeyed a previous command that he should not even for a moment allow the plates out of his hands until he had cared for them safely. The angel gave him further instructions and allowed him again to open the stone box, and in the box he saw the plates. He tried again to take them, but was hurled violently back to the ground by an invisible force. When he recovered, the angel was gone, and he wept with great grief. As he had done a year previously, Joseph allowed himself to be temporarily overcome by the powers of darkness and the hope of securing some treasure in the earth. 14

Joseph was greatly troubled when he returned home, fearing that his family would doubt that he had seen the plates. As soon as he entered the house, Joseph was met by his father, who asked if he had obtained the plates. Joseph replied that he had not, but his father persisted and said, "I would have taken them if I had been in your place." Subdued, Joseph could only admit that the Lord would not allow him to take them. The family rallied around Joseph when they heard of his experiences in detail and redoubled their supplications to God in Joseph's behalf. 15

Joseph visited the hill again in September 1825 and received further instructions from the angel, but he still did not obtain the plates. Throughout this period the Prophet often prayed on his own, and from what we can gather from some of his contemporaries, he was blessed with certain visions from individuals who had once dwelt on the western hemisphere and had helped to write the ancient record, the Book of Mormon. For example, John Taylor once declared, "When Joseph Smith was raised up as a Prophet of God, Mormon, Moroni, Nephi and others of the ancient Prophets who formerly lived on this Continent . . . came to him and communicated to him certain principles pertaining to the Gospel of the Son of God." 16 Some of what Joseph learned from these individualized instructions he shared with his family. His mother, Lucy, related:

During our evening conversations, Joseph would occasionally give us some of the most amusing recitals that could be imagined. He would describe the ancient inhabitants of this continent, their dress, mode of traveling, and the animals upon which they rode; their cities, their buildings, with every particular; their mode of warfare; and also their religious worship. This he would do with as much ease, seemingly, as if he had spent his whole life among them. 17

Treasure Seeking and Marriage to Emma Hale

Following the death of Alvin, the most pressing economic concern of the Smiths was the completion of the new family home. While the basic family income was derived from the annual wheat crop of the farm, family members always sought outside employment to support the growing financial needs of the family. "As my father's worldly circumstances were very limited, we were under the necessity of laboring with our hands, hiring out by day's work and otherwise, as we could get opportunity. Sometimes we were at home, and sometimes abroad" (JS-H 55), recorded Joseph Smith in his official 1838 account.

It was on one such employment opportunity "abroad" in 1825-26 that Joseph experienced both grief and happiness. In the fall of 1825, shortly after his annual visit to the hill to speak with the angel, Joseph and his father, Joseph Smith, Sr., hired out with an elderly gentleman, Josiah Stowell (spelled Stoal in JS-H) to dig for an alleged silver mine in northern Pennsylvania. In March 1826 Joseph was arrested on charges connected with this treasure-seeking enterprise and brought to trial. From this experience Joseph carried with him the unfortunate appellations "money-digger" and "glass-looker" the rest of his life. It was also during this time, however, that he met and fell in love with his future wife, Emma Hale, of Harmony, Pennsylvania.

Stowell would not have hired Joseph in the first place had Joseph not already had the reputation of one who could find treasures deep in the earth. 18 Some three years before hiring out with Stowell, Joseph, in company with Alvin, discovered a chocolate-colored, egg-shaped stone while digging a well on a neighbor's farm. Joseph soon learned that he could discern wondrous things with this stone, and it became his "seer stone." He carried it with him the rest of his life and used it for various revelatory purposes. 19 According to Martin Harris, Joseph used the seer stone in addition to the Urim and Thummim to translate the Book of Mormon. 20 But until Joseph learned to channel his seeric gifts, he also believed that he could use the stone to locate buried treasure. This propensity may be in part what he meant when be described his teenage years: "Mingling with all kinds of society, I frequently fell into many foolish errors, and displayed the weakness of youth, and the foibles of human nature; which, I am sorry to say, led me into divers temptations, offensive in the sight of God" (JS-H 28).

Joseph Smith and a few others in the Palmyra vicinity were certainly not the only persons in the 1820's interested in hunting treasure in the earth. Digging for money was neither uncommon nor disreputable. The Palmyra Herald on 24 July 1822 quoted the Montpelier (Vermont) Watchman as saying that "We could name, if we pleased, at least five hundred respectable men, who do, in the simplicity and sincerity of their hearts, verily believe that immense treasures lie concealed upon our green mountains; many of whom have been for a number of years, most industriously and perseveringly engaged in digging it up." 21 Many of the treasure hunters were also deeply religious men. For example, a chief treasure hunter in the area was Willard Chase, who also became a Methodist minister. Josiah Stowell, who hired Joseph for the mine-seeking venture, was a highly regarded member of the Presbyterian Church. Treasure hunting could best be regarded as a fad in the northeastern United States at that time. The difficulty for Joseph Smith was his inability as a teen-ager to distinguish the correct use of the special gifts he recognized the Lord had given him. Evidence of this inability is seen in Oliver Cowdery's account of Joseph's first visit to Cumorah as a seventeen-year-old when he reached into the stone box to obtain the plates of gold. "[Joseph] had heard of the power of enchantment, and a thousand like stories, which held the hidden treasures of the earth, and supposed that physical exertion and personal strength was only necessary to enable him to yet obtain the object of his wish." 22

Josiah Stowell contracted annually to buy some of the Smiths' wheat crop and through this association knew of Joseph's reputation. Stowell had entered into a partnership in 1825 with William Hale, uncle of Joseph's future wife, to dig for what they were convinced was a silver mine abandoned by Spanish explorers and pirates who had covered the mine with earth to hide it. Stowell had even made arrangements with Elizabeth Hale, mother of Emma, who ran an inn in Harmony, Pennsylvania, to board several men. He then went to northern New York to hire them. 23

Joseph Smith was at first skeptical of what he considered Stowell's "vain pursuit," but when Stowell was unrelenting and offered high wages, Joseph, his father, and several neighbors finally conceded to go to northern Pennsylvania. 24 They waited until the harvest was complete and until Joseph had made his annual visit to Cumorah. A letter from Joseph Smith to Josiah Stowell, dated 18 June 1825, has recently come to light. It reveals the superstitious nature that Joseph still retained at that time. It instructed Stowell not to dig for the mine until he had first searched for valuables. Joseph indicated that the mine was probably guarded by a "clever spirit," so he instructed Stowell how to cut himself a hazel stick and with it ascertain the location of the treasure. He told Stowell that he had almost decided to join him in his venture. 25

Joseph Smith and his companions labored as diggers for nearly a month in the hills near Harmony, Pennsylvania. Joseph attempted to use his seer-stone to divine the location of the mine, but to no avail. Finally he became discouraged with the project and prevailed upon Stowell to cease digging (see JS-H 56).

Young Joseph Smith did find another treasure in Harmony, however. The digging company boarded at the home of Isaac and Elizabeth Hale. A twenty-one-year old school-teaching daughter of the Hales, Emma, impressed Joseph with her beauty, intelligence, and dignified reserve. Emma was likewise attracted to this strapping, six-foot-tall, sensitive young man. Unfortunately Joseph did not impress Emma's father, who considered the mine-seeking venture ridiculous and thought Joseph Smith to be lazy and poorly educated. When the treasure-hunting enterprise ended, Joseph stayed in the vicinity to work for Josiah Stowell so that he could continue to see Emma.

Stowell lived up the Susquehanna River a few miles in South Bainbridge, New York. He hired Joseph to work as a farm hand and to cut timber. Joseph Smith, ever a friendly person, developed a close relationship with Stowell at this time as well as with members of the Joseph Knight, Sr. family, for whom he often also worked, who lived three miles away. During the winter of 1825-26 Joseph made frequent trips to Harmony to continue his budding relationship with Emma. He also attended school whenever he could.

In March 1826 Joseph Smith was arrested and brought to trial in Bainbridge, New York. Peter Bridgeman, a nephew of Mrs. Stowell, for some reason had become antagonistic toward Joseph and made out a complaint that Joseph was a disorderly person and an imposter. This was a full four months after operations had ceased in the Stowell "mine." Because diverse accounts of the trial exist, it is difficult to ascertain exactly what occurred there. But all accounts agree that Joseph's stone and its use were widely discussed Apparently Joseph claimed that he had used the stone in the Palmyra area to find lost property, but that he had not solicited this business with Stowell. Joseph Smith, Sr., testified that he and his son were "mortified that the wonderful power which God had so miraculously given . . . should be used only in search of filthy lucre," indicating that the Smiths understood that Joseph's spiritual gift was from the Lord for a serious purpose. 26 In most known accounts of that trial, Joseph was found guilty, although no sentence was recorded. Oliver Cowdery, however, reported that "some very officious person complained of [Joseph] as a disorderly person, and brought him before the authorities of the county; but there being no cause of action he was honorably acquited." 27

We can acknowledge that our first Prophet was engaged as a young man in unprofitable treasure-hunting episodes during the 1820s, when he was still trying to understand his God-given gifts and proper place before his Heavenly Father. To a certain extent he accepted the superstitions of buried treasure in the earth and associated with "money-diggers" and "rodsmen." But when the Prophet was finally allowed to receive the plates in 1827, he was told by the angel Moroni that he must quit the company of money-diggers, for there were wicked men among them. 28 Speaking of this period in the Prophet's life, Oliver Cowdery described Joseph as a "man subject to passion like other men, beset with infirmities and encompassed with weaknesses" 29 (see also JS-H 28). But the important thing for us to recognize is that he gradually overcame his youthful propensities to some forms of foolishness and was molded by the Spirit and the angel's tutoring into a mighty shaft in the quiver of the Almighty.

Following the Bainbridge trial, Joseph's attentions once again turned to Emma. But to his dismay he discovered that he was more unwelcome than ever in the Hale household. This was probably due to the trial and its adverse publicity. Undeterred, Joseph asked for Emma's hand from her father. He was refused. 30

Joseph went back to his family's farm in the summer of 1826 to help harvest the wheat and, of course, to meet again with the angel. According to Joseph Knight, Sr., in whom Joseph later confided the story, Moroni told the Prophet that he could receive the record the following September if he brought with him the "right person." Joseph looked into the seer stone and found the right person to be "Emma Hale." 31

Joseph stayed a little longer at home to help solve family problems concerning the mortgage on their house and also to witness his brother Hyrum's marriage. In December he hurried back to southern New York. Emma visited him occasionally at the home of Josiah Stowell, since Joseph could not easily call on her in Harmony. On 18 January 1827 at the Stowells' home Emma accepted Joseph's proposal and the couple was married that day by a justice of the peace at South Bainbridge, New York (see JS-H 57-58). They then traveled north to Manchester and moved in with the Smith family, where Emma was received warmly by her new in-laws.

Joseph Receives the Plates

Not long after settling in at the farm, Joseph went to Manchester village on business for his father but did not return at the appointed time. This frightened Emma and the other Smiths, because they realized that by now the young Prophet had several enemies. When he finally did arrive late in the evening, he fell into a chair exhausted. His father exclaimed, "Joseph, why are you so late? has anything happened to you? we have been much distressed about you these three hours." 32 Joseph did not respond immediately, but at length he related soberly:

I have taken the severest chastisement that I have ever had in my life. . . .It was the angel of the Lord. As I passed by the hill of Cumorah, where the plates are, the angel met me and said that I had not been engaged enough in the work of the Lord; that the time had come for the record to be brought forth; and that I must be up and doing and set myself about the things which God has commanded me to do. But, father, give yourself no uneasiness concerning the reprimand which I have received, for I now know the course that I am to pursue, so all will be well. 33

From this point on, apparently, Joseph dedicated his all to preparing himself spiritually to be worthy for the great trust that soon awaited him.

On the day before Joseph was scheduled to go to the hill for the plates, Josiah Stowell and Joseph Knight arrived at the Smiths' home to buy their usual load of wheat. Their arrival was providential, since the horse and wagon owned by Knight would come in handy for Joseph and Emma when they went to receive the plates. At about midnight, after the guests had retired for the night, Joseph asked his mother if she had a chest with a lock and key. Lucy did not have one and was concerned lest that would mean her son could not obtain the plates. Joseph replied, "Never mind, I can do very well for the present without it—be calm—all is right." 34 Joseph and Emma then left and were gone with the horse and wagon through the whole night. Joseph left Emma alone at the foot of the hill and disappeared in the darkness. After uncovering the stone box, Joseph once again spoke with the heavenly messenger. Moroni charged him solemnly with the responsibility of the plates. If by any neglect on his part Joseph should lose them, he would be "cut off" (JS-H 59). Joseph returned to Emma with the plates wrapped in his coat. Traveling a short distance, he took the plates into a forest and hid them in a hollowed-out log and covered the hole with pieces of bark.

Joseph and Emma returned after dawn to find Joseph Knight certain that a thief had stolen his horse and wagon. To relieve his mother's anxiety, for she knew full well where Joseph and Emma had been, Joseph privately showed Lucy the Urim and Thummim, which he had also obtained. 35 Joseph also took Joseph Knight aside and happily exclaimed, "It is ten times Better then I expected." He went on to tell his friend the dimensions and appearance of the plates and the nature of the Urim and Thummim. "I can see any thing; they are Marvelus." 36

The next day Joseph went to work on a well for a widow in Macedon, north of Palmyra, to earn money to pay a cabinet maker to build a chest. This was not to be so easy for Joseph; before long some money-digging enthusiasts in the vicinity decided that they wanted to obtain the treasure that they concluded Joseph Smith must have had by then. Lucy remembered, "It appeared that Satan had now stirred up the hearts of those who had gotten a hint of the matter from our friend [Martin Harris] to search into it and make every possible move towards thwarting the purposes of the Almighty." 37

According to his mother, Joseph took the Urim and Thummim with him to work, so that with its use he could ascertain in a moment whether the plates were in any danger. 38 Meanwhile, Joseph Smith, Sr., heard about the group of plotting money-diggers and went among the neighbors to find out more about them. At the first house to which he went he saw the group inside and overheard them devising plans to find "Joseph Smith's gold Bible." The elder Smith quickly returned home and told Emma about what he had heard. He also warned Joseph's wife that they needed to be careful with the record, or they might, like Esau, lose their birthright and blessing. 39 Emma went to Macedon in search of Joseph. Finding him, she informed him what was transpiring. He learned by revelation that the record was yet safe, and together they hurried back home.

Joseph made contact with his brother Hyrum and requested that he obtain a chest with a good lock and key and have it there by the time he (Joseph) should return. Joseph then proceeded to the secret hiding place three miles from the farm, there retrieved the plates, and started for home with them wrapped in a linen cloth. He went through the woods rather than taking the road. As he was jumping over a log, he was accosted from behind and took a blow to his head with a gun. Joseph turned around and knocked his assailant to the ground and ran off. Before he reached home, he was attacked twice more in like manner but was somehow able to elude his enemies. Out of breath, he fell to the ground within sight of his home. He noticed that he had dislocated a thumb. After catching his breath, he entered the house and asked his mother to send for his father, Joseph Knight, and Josiah Stowell to go in search for the assailants. Joseph's youngest brother, Don Carlos, was sent to Hyrum to tell him to bring the chest. Hyrum responded immediately with the chest and Joseph, quite relieved, was able to lock up the plates and hide them away. 40

According to Joseph Knight, who stayed at the Smith home for several days, a number of the treasure hunters came to the house and asked for the privilege of seeing the plates. They tried to bargain with Joseph Smith and offered to be his partners to get full monetary value for the treasure. But Joseph was now through with people of this kind and told them to leave him alone. He and his family remained on an alert guard until November. 41 At that time Joseph was warned of the Lord that a mob was being formed to storm the house. When the treasure seekers arrived. Joseph "halloed as if he had a legion at hand, in the meanwhile giving the word of command with great emphasis; while all the male portion of the family, from the father down to little Carlos, ran out of the house with such fury upon the mob that it struck them with terror and dismay and they fled before the little Spartan band into the woods." 42

Joseph continued to receive other intimations regarding enemy activity toward the plates. He hid the plates in a cooper's shop (a shop for making wooden barrels and tubs) across the street. On a tip from a "glass-looker," the mob ransacked the shop but did not find the record. Joseph could now see that he would have no peace in the Palmyra vicinity and began late in 1827 to make arrangements to go to Harmony, Pennsylvania, where he hoped to he could begin translating the plates, as he had been commanded. A few months previously, Emma's parents, having reconciled themselves to her marriage, had invited the couple to come and stay with them.

Martin Harris and the Anthon Transcript

At this point a prosperous forty-seven-year-old farmer from north of Palmyra entered to proffer needed aid to Joseph and Emma, thus facilitating their safe removal to Harmony. The Smith family had known Martin Harris for several years. Joseph and his brothers had on occasion hired out to work on his 240-acre prime-land farm. Joseph Smith, Sr., in particular, was well-acquainted with Harris. Martin had fought in the War of 1812 and was both elected and appointed by his fellow citizens in Palmyra to numerous civic responsibilities. He was highly respected as an honorable citizen of the community (see JS-H 61). 43 When Martin heard of Joseph Smith's "gold Bible," he lent a sympathetic ear. Joseph learned through revelation that Martin Harris was just the man to assist him in his project. He sent for Martin and told him about the plates, explaining that the Lord expected him (Joseph) to translate them, print the translation, and send it before the world. Joseph asked Martin for monetary assistance and promised that he could retain all the eventual profits from the book. Martin was dubious about young Joseph's claims, but he went to his home and prayed sincerely about them. By the still small voice the Lord revealed to Martin that this was indeed his work, that it was designed to bring in the fulness of the gospel. Martin then gladly covenanted with God to assist in bringing this record to the world. 44

Martin went to Joseph and offered to pay off all the Prophet's debts so he (Joseph) could go as planned to Harmony. He also gave Joseph and Emma the then large sum of $50.00 for their journey. 45 En route to Pennsylvania Joseph and Emma encountered a mob which searched their wagon in vain for the plates, because they were hidden in the bottom of a bean barrel.

After they arrived in Harmony in December of 1827, Emma's father, Isaac, who was still skeptical about Joseph Smith, demanded that he be allowed to see the plates. Joseph, of course, refused. The Smiths could not stay with the Hales under such tense circumstances, so Joseph and Emma contracted with Isaac to buy a nearby house and thirteen acres of land. The young couple moved into their new home, happy at last that they had found the peace they had yearned for. Emma could also deliver her expected baby in comfort.

Joseph began immediately to copy numerous characters from the plates and by means of the Urim and Thummim translated some of them (JS-H 62). By prearrangement, Martin harris arrived in Harmony in February of 1828, having given Joseph sufficient time to get established there. The Lord had revealed to Martin that he was to go to New York City with characters that Joseph would copy from the plates, and that he should show them to learned linguists. 46 Accordingly a manuscript was prepared and Martin returned to Palmyra to prepare for his trip to the metropolis.

Martin probably went via Utica and Albany on his way to New York City in February 1828. 47 It appears that he consulted with Luther Bradish (1783-1863), a student of languages and a New York state assemblyman, in Albany. Perhaps at the suggestion of Bradish he sought out Charles Anthon (1787-1867), professor of classical studies at Columbia College in New York City. Martin understated his man when he described Anthon as "a gentleman celebrated for his literary attainments" (JS-H 64). Indeed Anthon was regarded as probably the greatest classicist in America during his lifetime. Martin talked with still another scholar of languages whom he identified simply as "Dr. Mitchell" (JS-H 65). This person has since been identified as Samuel L. Mitchill, M.D. (1776-1831). Mitchill was a man of many talents and interests and was described by his contemporaries as "a living encyclopedia." At the time that Martin visited him in New York, Mitchill was a vice-president of Rutgers Medical College.

Of the three scholars, it was Charles Anthon who made the biggest impression on Martin Harris, for it was primarily the eventful interview with Anthon that Martin described to Joseph Smith in Harmony. According to Martin, Anthon certified in writing that the characters were of genuine Near Eastern origin, but when he learned that their source was a sealed record delivered by an angel of God, he destroyed that certificate and said, "I cannot read a sealed book" (JS-H 64-65). Latter-day Saints have marveled how this episode fulfilled a prophecy of Isaiah: "And the vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I cannot; for it is sealed" (Isa. 29:11).

At the request of others, Charles Anthon wrote two known letters describing Martin Harris's visit with him. The first was published by E. D. Howe of Painesville, Ohio, in Mormonism Unvailed (1834), and the second was sent to Rev. Thomas Winthrop Coit in Rochelle, New York and was later published in Rev. John A. Clark's Gleanings By the Way (1842). There are conflicts in Anthon's letters with the story of Martin Harris. In his letters, Anthon denied saying that the characters were authentic, and he did not refer to examining a translation. Anthon claimed that he convinced the simple-minded stranger (Harris) that the manuscript was a hoax. But for Martin Harris, his visit to Anthon relieved him of any lingering doubts concerning the veracity of the record. We can therefore conclude that Anthon may have been embarrassed by some of the things which he had said to Harris, especially for that which he had written on a certificate, and he chose to write some inaccurate statements. Furthermore, Anthon wrote these letters to critics of the then emerging Latter-day Saint movement to avoid having his name used in any way to further a religion he dismissed as fanatical.

The Lost Manuscript

After returning the Anthon manuscript to Joseph Smith in Harmony, Martin Harris went home to Palmyra, arranged his affairs, and returned to Harmony on 12 April 1828. While in Palmyra, Martin encountered further difficulty with his wife, who had opposed his association with young Joseph Smith and the "gold Bible" all along. Undeterred, Martin was anxious to aid the Prophet with the translation as a scribe. The pair diligently labored together until 14 June, by which tine Martin had recorded 116 pages of manuscript on foolscap paper (folded writing paper 12 by 15 inches). All the while Martin was anxious to show his wife, his relatives, and his friends something tangible from the project, so he prestered Joseph to allow him to carry the writings home. When Joseph first asked permission from the Lord to allow Martin to do this, he received a negative response. But at length the Lord allowed Joseph to bind Martin in solemn covenant that he would guard the manuscript and not show it except to five specified persons.

Shortly after Martin departed for Palmyra, Emma Smith gave birth to a son that soon died. Emma's own life hung in the balance for about two weeks, so Joseph was totally preoccupied with her care. When Martin failed to return after three weeks, Joseph was beside himself with concern over the manuscript. With his wife's blessings he hurried to his parents' home. En route his soul was filled with "fearful apprehensions," lest through negligence he had fallen into condemnation from the Lord. 48 Alas, Joseph discovered that Martin had not carefully guarded the manuscript. Martin discovered that it had been stolen, probably by his wife or her sympathizers. The Lord later revealed to the Prophet that "wicked men" had stolen the manuscript (D&C 10:8).

Hearing this terrible report, Joseph exclaimed, "All is lost! all is lost! What shall I do? I have sinned—it is I who tempted the wrath of God." 49 Joseph was filled with great lamentation for several hours, and when he departed for Harmony the next day, he and all of the other Smiths felt that the opportunity to bring forth the ancient record "had in a moment fled, and fled forever." 50 His mother described the gloom which filled the Smith household: "The heavens seemed clothed with blackness, and the earth shrouded with gloom. I have often said within myself, that if a continual punishment, as severe as that which we experienced on that occasion, were to be inflicted upon the most wicked characters who ever stood upon the footstool of the Almighty—if even their punishment were no greater than that, I should feel to pity their condition." 51

When Joseph Smith returned to Harmony without the 116 pages of manuscript, he began immediately to pray mightily and humbly for forgiveness for acting contrary to the will of the Lord. Eventually Moroni appeared to him and required that he return the Urim and Thummim, but promised that he could receive the interpreters back on 22 September if he were humble and penitent. 52 Joseph continued his supplications to God for approximately two months and did indeed receive back the power to translate the plates. In the meantime he received a revelation which both chastised him for negligence and setting "at naught the counsels of God" and also comforted him in the fact that he was still chosen to perform the work of translation (D&C 3:4-10). Still later in the summer he received another revelation (D&C 10) informing him that the "lost manuscript" had actually been stolen and that he would be expected to translate other plates, the "plates of Nephi" (D&C 10:40), to replace that which was stolen. By the end of 1828 Joseph once again basked in the glow of the approbation of the Almighty.

Early in 1829 the Lord revealed to Joseph that he would receive a new scribe. 53 This was fulfilled with the arrival of Oliver Cowdery on 5 April 1829 (JS-H 66). In the short space of less than three months, Joseph and Oliver completed the translation of virtually all that is in our present text of the Book of Mormon. It appears that when Joseph was finally prepared and sufficiently mature, very little was allowed by the Lord to stand in his way of translating the plates "by the gift and power of God" (Book of Mormon, Title Page).

Summary

Why was Joseph Smith not allowed to translate the plates "by the gift and power of God" in 1823 when he first saw the sacred record? Apparently because he was not spiritually prepared for such a trustworthy task. As we have seen, due to his family's poverty and his youthful foibles, his mind was sometimes occupied with dreams of monetary gain—often involving superstitions of supernatural money-digging. Gradually in the four years before 1827, Joseph matured and was privileged to receive the plates. In the year between September 1827 and September 1828, the young Prophet was further beset with awesome struggles to hold on to the plates and the interpreters and to test his trustworthiness with these sacred items and the manuscript which came from the translation. Finally he proved his worthiness, showing that he was prepared in every way to take on the mantle of translator. The fruits of the translation of the Book of Mormon were surely all the more sweet to him because of the struggles and sacrifices between 1823 and 1828 which led up to the translation.

It is the firm testimony of this writer that Joseph Smith is a Prophet of God, that the Lord chose to bring forth his great work of the Restoration—the marvelous work and a wonder (Isa. 29:14)—through this young man who was at first not ready for the task. But he had been foreordained from the foundation of this world and called for this mighty work before he was born. Once in mortality, the Lord prepared and developed him to be an instrument in his hands. In like manner, our Father in Heaven patiently works with us, his other humble, naive, and immature servants, to prepare us for other important tasks in his kingdom. We would do well to be as humble, dedicated, and supple to the directions of the Almighty as was Joseph Smith during his developmental years.
