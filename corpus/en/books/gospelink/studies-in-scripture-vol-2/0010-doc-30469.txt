THE RISE OF THE KINGDOM OF SATAN

(Moses 5:16-59)

MONTE S. NYMAN

There are twenty-four verses in the fourth chapter of Genesis which discuss Cain and Abel (vv. 1-24). In the restoration of the text of the writings of Moses through the Prophet Joseph Smith, there are forty-four verses covering the same topic (Moses 5:16-59). Seven of the verses are the same, sixteen verses have restorations in them—nine of which are very significant—and there are twenty-one complete verses restored in the text. With these extra verses and restorations of parts of verses, much of the confusion concerning the account of Cain and Abel, two of the sons of Adam and Eve, is cleared up. The significance of these textual differences is understood best by a comparison of them. The parts italicized in the following comparisons illustrate what was restored by the Prophet Joseph Smith.

The Sacrifices of Cain and Abel

Eve's great hopes for her son Cain were not realized, due to the efforts of Satan and Cain's reluctance to hearken to the Lord.

GENESIS 4:1-2

And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the Lord.

MOSES 5:16-18

And Adam and Eve, his wife, ceased not to call upon God. And Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bare Cain, and said: I have gotten a man from the Lord; wherefore he may not reject his words. But behold, Cain hearkened not, saying: Who is the Lord that I should know him?

And she again bare his brother Abel. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground.

And she again conceived and bare his brother Abel. And Abel hearkened unto the voice of the Lord. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground.

And Cain loved Satan more than God. And Satan commanded him, saying: Make an offering unto the Lord.

In response to Satan's commandment, Cain brought an offering of fruit unto the Lord. Abel also brought an offering, but his was one of the firstlings of the flock. Regarding this Paul wrote, "By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain" (Heb. 11:4). Why was Abel's sacrifice more excellent than Cain's? In addition to Cain's offering being commanded by Satan, it was not in accordance with the Lord's commandment to Adam to offer the firstlings of the flock (Moses 5:5). Abel's offering was an animal sacrifice, and thus it was respected by the Lord (Moses 5:20). A restoration in the Book of Moses shows that Satan knew that Cain's offering would not be respected by the Lord, which, of course, pleased Satan (Moses 5:21). The reason the offering of fruit was not respected was because it was not in similitude of the sacrifice of the Only Begotten (Moses 5:7). Joseph Smith made this explanation.

By faith in this atonement or plan of redemption, Abel offered to God a sacrifice that was accepted, which was the firstlings of the flock. Cain offered of the fruit of the ground, and was not accepted, because he could not do it in faith, he could have no faith, or could not exercise faith contrary to the plan of heaven. It must be shedding the blood of the Only Begotten to atone for man; for this was the plan of redemption; and without the shedding of blood was no remission; and as the sacrifice was instituted for a type, by which man was to discern the great Sacrifice which God had prepared; to offer a sacrifice contrary to that, no faith could be exercised, because redemption was not purchased in that way, nor the power of atonement instituted after that order; consequently Cain could have no faith; and whatsoever is not of faith, is sin. But Abel offered an acceptable sacrifice, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God Himself testifying of his gifts. Certainly, the shedding of the blood of a beast could be beneficial to no man, except it was done in imitation, or as a type, or explanation of what was to be offered through the gift of God Himself; and this performance done with an eye looking forward in faith on the power of that great Sacrifice for a remission of sins. 1

Cain's Rebellion

Cain was angry over the Lord's rejection of his offering. The Lord, however, was not only merciful with him over his first sin but warned him of the future consequences of continuing to follow Satan.

GENESIS 4:7

If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him.

MOSES 5:23

If thou doest well, thou shalt be accepted. And if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door, and Satan desireth to have thee; and except thou shalt hearken unto my commandments, I will deliver thee up, and it shall be unto theem according to his desire. And thou shalt rule over him.

The further warning and consequences given to Cain have been lost from the present-day Bibles. Eight complete verses are added to the text through the revelation to the Prophet Joseph Smith. The Lord explained how Cain would rule over Satan. Cain was warned that his continued compliance to Satan's desires would make him the Father of Satan's lies. As used in this context, a father is the head of a household, so Cain would be the head of Satan's operations upon the earth. This is the first indication in the text of there being an organization of Satan's forces.

Cain was further told that he would be called Perdition. The reason for this title was that he was "also before the world" (Moses 5:24). Perdition is the title given so Satan when he was thrust down from the presence of God for his rebellion (D&C 76:25-26). By Cain becoming the head of Satan's organization, he joined, in a sense, the family of Satan and thus received his name also. As the head man in the flesh, he became the father of those mortals who later joined the organization. Those who so join become the sons of Cain as well as of Satan. This designation of Cain was appropriate in the Lord's eyes, because he too had been present in the pre-mortal councils ("before the world was") and had seen the results of Satan's rebellion. 2 Cain's rebellion would also be a willful rebellion with full knowledge of the consequences. The Lord had spoken to him personally, and if he failed to hearken, he had "rejected the greater counsel which was had from God" (Moses 5:25). Not only would it be said that these abominations were from Cain, but because he had a physical body he would ultimately rule over Satan who had no body. In the words of Joseph Smith: "All beings who have bodies have power over those who have not. The devil has no power over us only as we permit him. The moment we revolt at anything which comes from God, the devil takes power." 3 In spite of these warnings, or perhaps because of them, "Cain was wroth" and no longer listened to the voice of the Lord. The revealed text also discloses that he refused to listen to Abel, "who walked in holiness before the Lord." The text further records the mourning before the Lord of Adam and his wife "because of Cain and his brethren" (Moses 5:26-27). From these verses, we learn of Abel's devotion to the Lord and his brother, as well as another reminder of the fact that Adam and Eve had other children at this time in addition to Cain and Abel. The great sorrow of his earthly parents would, however, increase because of further actions which Cain would take.

The Secret Oath

Another incident not found in Genesis was Satan's plotting with Cain. Since this is Satan's manner of operation, it should be no surprise. Satan would like to keep such information hidden from the general public. Having taken one of his brother's daughters to wife, the two of them loved Satan more than God. In this frame of mind, they were ripe for Satan's proposed secret covenant. It is interesting that the covenant of death not to tell Adam of Satan's promise (to deliver Abel into Cain's hand) was to be sworn "by the living God" (Moses 5:29-30). Such hypocrisy would be expected, as shown by the egotism displayed by Cain after he committed himself to Satan's oath. He bragged that he was "Mahan, the master of this great secret, that I may murder and get gain" (Moses 5:31). Glorying in his wickedness, Cain went into the field and slew his brother Abel. While this terrible act is recorded in Genesis as well as in the book of Moses, the following verse is only in Moses: "And Cain gloried in that which he had done, saying: I am free; surely the flocks of my brother falleth into my hands" (Moses 5:33).

The next fifteen verses are common to both texts, but the Book of Moses has several significant parts of the story retained in it. In response to the Lord's query concerning Abel came Cain's now proverbial response, "Am I my brother's keeper?" (Moses 5:34). The Lord did not answer Cain's question; this had led many to misunderstand the query. Many assume from this statement that everyone is his brother's keeper. However, in the line of stewardship, a father is the keeper of his son—not another son, but a brother should be a helper of his brother. Cain was neither one, but was probably following a pattern of Satan, justifying himself or excusing himself under the guise of a true but inappropriate principle. The Lord ignored Cain's excuse and verified his knowledge of Cain's supposedly secret act by declaring that the blood of Abel "cries unto me from the ground" (Moses 5:35). Justice had to be met, if not in this life in the next. In this case, justice came to the fore and the Lord brought a curse upon Cain.

The curse upon Cain was two-fold. First of all, the earth was not to yield her strength unto the efforts of Cain's tilling of it. It is implied in the two previous verses that this was because of the blood of Abel being absorbed by it. It is further suggested in a subsequent chapter of Moses that this part of the curse also included a loss of the priesthood (Moses 7:8). This is further attested to in v. 23 and Abr. 1:21-27. The full significance of this will undoubtedly be made known at a future time. The second part of Cain's curse was that he would be "a fugitive and a vagabond . . . in the earth" (Moses 5:37). The following story, though raising some puzzling questions, perhaps demonstrates the literalness and continuance of this aspect of the curse. The account is a reconstruction by Abraham O. Smoot of the words of Elder David W. Patten, a member of the first Quorum of the Twelve in this dispensation, relating an event that occurred in 1836.

As I was riding along the road on my mule I suddenly noticed a very strange personage walking beside me. He walked along beside me for about two miles. His head was about even with my shoulders as I sat in my saddle. He wore no clothing, but was covered with hair. His skin was very dark. I asked him where he dwelt and he replied that he had no home, that he was a wanderer in the earth and traveled to and fro. He said he was a very miserable creature, that he had earnestly sought death during his sojourn upon the earth, but that he could not die, and his mission was to destroy the souls of men. About the time he expressed himself thus, I rebuked him in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by virtue of the Holy Priesthood, and commanded him to go hence, and he immediately departed out of my sight. 4

The response by Cain to his being cursed is not given fully in Genesis. The account in the Book of Moses is much more complete and verifies some of the incidents relative to Cain's rebellion discussed earlier in this chapter.

GENESIS 4:13

And Cain said unto the Lord, My punishment is greater than I can bear.

MOSES 5:38

And Cain said unto the Lord: Satan tempted me because of my brother's flocks. And I was wroth also; for his offering thou didst accept and not mine; my punishment is greater than I can bear.

There are some other interesting translations of Cain's response. The Holy Bible from ancient eastern manuscripts renders Gen. 4:13 as follows: "And Cain said to the Lord, my transgression is too great to be forgiven." 5 The Septuagint has the same wording as the Ancient Eastern manuscript but changes the statement into a question: "And Cain said to the Lord God, 'Is my fault too great to be forgiven? If thou castest me out this day from the face of this land and I be hid from Thy presence and be in a state of anguish and terror in the land, it will come to pass that whoever findeth me will kill me."' 6

Perhaps Cain's response was more of a declaration or a question than a complaint. That the question was drawn out more from fear of his own safety than from remorse and fear of loss of salvation is shown from the following verse in the Septuagint (quoted above) and also in a retention in the Book of Moses. This is best illustrated by comparison.

GENESIS 4:14

Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me.

MOSES 5:39

Behold thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the Lord, and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that he that findeth me will slay me, because of mine iniquities, for these things are not hid from the Lord.

In spite of this fear, the curse which came upon him was probably worse than death. The punishment had no end, and his social acceptance as well as his physical prosperity was adversely affected. Perhaps that is why the Lord placed a mark upon Cain (Moses 5:40), to assure that this penalty worse than death would not be cut short.

The next several verses discuss the new geographic location and the genealogy of Cain. The main contribution of the restored text of Moses is that many others joined Cain in his new location, and many other sons and daughters were born to the various families other than those listed in the text.

Secret Combinations Continue

Following the account of Lamech slaying a young man, the Book of Moses has eleven verses which have been lost from Genesis. These verses disclose that Lamech continued the secret combination entered into by Cain with Satan and that Lamech succeeded Cain as Master Mahan (Moses 5:49). Furthermore, one Irad began to reveal Lamech's secret unto the sons of Adam, which caused Lamech to slay him "for the oath's sake" (Moses 5:49-50). This part of the oath is confirmed in the Book of Mormon (Hel. 6:24), as is the fact that secret combinations began with Cain (Moses 5:51; Hel. 6:27).

Although the Lord also cursed Lamech, the spread of secret combinations did not end. Rather, they spread and "began to prevail among all the sons of men" (Moses 5:52-55). Because of these conditions and the failure of men to listen to the voice of God or "believe on his Only Begotten Son," God cursed the earth (Moses 5:56-57). Joseph Smith summed it up with this observation:

The power, glory and blessings of the Priesthood could not continue with those who received ordination only as their righteousness continued; for Cain also being authorized to offer sacrifice, but not offering it in righteousness, was cursed. It signifies, then, that the ordinances must be kept in the very way God has appointed; otherwise their Priesthood will prove a cursing instead of a blessing. 7

On the positive side, Moses 5 concludes with a great declaration:

And thus the Gospel began to be preached, from the beginning, being declared by holy angels sent forth from the presence of God, and by his own voice, and by the gift of the Holy Ghost.

And thus all things were confirmed unto Adam, by an holy ordinance, and the Gospel preached, and a decree sent forth, that it should be in the world, until the end thereof; and thus it was. Amen (Moses 5:58-59).

The fulfillment of this declaration is underway today, but the secret combinations are also prevalent and prospering. Through the Pearl of Great Price we can learn the consequences of following either and hopefully commit ourselves to following the principles of the Gospel.
