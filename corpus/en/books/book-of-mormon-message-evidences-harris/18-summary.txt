Franklin S. Harris, Book of Mormon: Message and Evidences ( 1961)

Chapter 17

Summary

One hundred and twenty-three years ago the Book of Mormon was published in Palmyra, New York. With great daring for that day an edition of 5000 copies was printed. It set forth a translation, by Joseph Smith, with the help of God, of ancient records of peoples who came to the Western Hemisphere from the Near East. It relates the dealings of God with those peoples and the visit of Jesus Christ to them. Because of its contents and the account of how it was produced with divine aid in our day the book immediately attracted considerable attention and controversy and has done so to the present time when it continues to be a best seller.

201The Book of Mormon teaches the universality of God's love for men in time and space. It further teaches that obedience to law is the universal, fundamental law of progress, both temporally and spiritually. The superior person or people, in the sight of God is he who is superior in righteousness and service to God.

201The book advocates and illustrates the operation of freedom of religion, conscience and belief. It has value in understanding the problem of war.

201Three reasons are given for living a good moral life-a life based on the teachings of God to man. First, an impending judgment and a restoration of the effects of past deeds in the life of a person, giving instead not a mistaken idea of a physical hell of fire and brimstone punishment, but an inescapable remorse of conscience until justice and mercy are satisfied. Second, the teaching and experience of Book of Mormon peoples that community prosperity results from obedience to the teachings of God, and despair and chaos from their disregard. Third, happiness of the individual comes from living according to the instructions from God. The book has had a lasting beneficial effect on readers who follow its precepts.

202 The Book of Mormon corroborates and implements the Bible. It is a great witness for the message and doctrines of the Bible, yet giving many Bible doctrines in greater clarity. The book quotes much from the Bible, thus supporting it, but there are frequent differences in which in many interesting cases the Book of Mormon reading has been found in old codices or manuscripts.

202The Book of Mormon describes three migrations to America, two from Palestine about 600 B.C. and another much earlier from the Near East. Though there had been much early speculation about the origin of the American Indian and the ancient American civilizations, and their possible Old World connections, Joseph Smith in the Book of Mormon groups of three peoples did not attempt to correlate them with existing speculations or materials believed to have evidential value. Indeed there was little of value in the Old and New Worlds. It was not until Stephens and Catherwood visited Middle America and published their accounts, and Prescott published his books shortly afterwards, that reliable information began to become available. After a natural conservative reaction against unbridled speculation, careful study is again turning to an examination of evidence for Old World origins and connections of the ancient American peoples and civilizations.

202There is still a great need for continuing research to get more information on the civilizations of Ancient America. There are evidences for racial strains in the American Indian which are non-Mongoloid, and belong to the white group, perhaps to one of the white peoples of the Near East. Traditions of some of the ancient American peoples indicate that they migrated to their present locations from elsewhere, in some cases from across the sea. The change to new environment, and the long time since the Book of Mormon account ended, lead one to expect that great changes took place in the cultures of these peoples. There are evidences suggestive of Israelitish culture. The prevalent feathered serpent symbol in America, in story and art, recall the promised Christ of the Old Testament.

203 The Book of Mormon has been criticized for referring to elephants, horses, steel and the wheel, but excellent evidence for the use of all these has developed, and been accepted by competent scholars, in the last few years, making the Book of Mormon account plausible. But this confirmation is a century after the Book of Mormon was published.

203The use of metallic plates for record keeping such as in the case of the Book of Mormon, was common practice in the ancient world, both Old and New, as a means of preserving important information.

203The Book of Mormon has been criticized for its English style. This seems due, in a considerable measure, to a too-literal translation of a modified Hebrew original and the use of Hebrew idioms. The English seems to be free from anachronisms and other errors inconsistent with the book's origin. Evidence is plentiful showing the variation of literary style of the various historians even though most of the book was first abridged by Mormon and then later translated by Joseph Smith.

203The Book of Mormon is a copy of ancient records and not a novel or fictional account. There are many types of literary techniques and devices in the book, such as narrative, biography, proverbs, epics, arguments. It has had a great inspiration for the sincere reader and has inspired much creative work in the various artistic fields.

203Traditions surviving many centuries after Book of Mormon times indicate that Christ visited America, taught its peoples and left an organized Church, as described in the Book of Mormon. Quetzalcoatl and other culture heroes have many characteristics of Christ, as reported in his Book of Mormon visit, particularly in type of teachings and activities. Traditions of the birth signs and the destruction at the time of death are also present. Christian-type beliefs and practices have been noted by many people.

203Jesus Christ personally ministered to the ancient inhabitants of the Western Hemisphere. The Book of Mormon prophets, long before Christ was born on the earth, had foreknowledge by revelation from God, as did also the people of Israel in Palestine, of the mission and coming of the Messiah in the flesh, and the redemption made possible by his mission. These prophets looked forward to the visit of Christ, and taught the laws of the Gospel while still conforming to the Law of Moses. The resurrected Christ appeared to the Book of Mormon peoples, giving added proof of immortality and literal resurrection. He fulfilled the Mosaic Law, instituted the teachings, ordinances and organization to carry on the Gospel, reaffirming the message of the New Testament. Time and again the Book of Mormon returns to its theme of testifying of Christ and his mission for the benefit of Jew and Gentile.

204The Book of Mormon teaches the experimental approach to religious knowledge. It emphasizes the role of the individual in eternity and the laws which must be obeyed to obtain, by obedience to spiritual laws, the certitudes of religious conviction, and the happiness which everyone seeks. Many have been challenged by this physical evidence for Mormonism and modern spiritual experience to read and test the book. It has had throughout the history of the Church, as it has today, a great power of converting to Christ.

204The Book of Mormon was beyond the unaided power of Joseph Smith to produce. Testimonies of associates and witnesses show their conviction in the truth of Joseph Smith's account of the translation of ancient records. Unfriendly critics were and are confused among themselves. Joseph Smith's story of the coming forth of the Book of Mormon is today consistent with the best information about the book.

204The importance and value of the book is to be judged by its effect on people. Note the effect in the following.

204The test of the scholarly Frenchman, Louis A. Bertrand: [^1]

204"Our conversions (to Mormonism) the fruit of a sincere and persistent conviction, declares better than all we could say, our opinion upon the authenticity, the social and religious importance of this book. No other book, since the Koran, has given birth to a people. The Book of Mormon in the midst of the blaze of the 19th century, has served as foundation to a new people in America. Who can explain this strange social phenomenon of a religious novel accomplishing such a wonder, in presence of the unheard of progress of our age? Giving birth to a people is not such a common thing. From the time of Luther the interpreters of the Bible in both worlds have been able to create sects only, and the philosophers, only systems of philosophy; Joseph Smith is the only one who had laid the foundations of a new societyÂ�.In the midst of the anarchy of opinions, sensible men of all parties must judge of a work only by its results."

205Dr. Samuel R. Davenport wrote: [^2]

205"I believe in the Book of Mormon because I have seen what it has done for others who have had faith in its teachings and commandments."

205In the words of Wm. A. Morton [^3] these results come because:

205"The Book of Mormon teaches faith in God; it strongly urges obedience to his will; it testifies to the divinity of Jesus of Nazareth-that he is the Son of God and the Savior of the world; it declares the truth and the divine origin of the Bible and corroborates its teachings; it pleads for purity in thought, word and deed, and sets heaven as the final goal of life. Then how can straight thinking Christians oppose it?"

205Anyone may get the rewards that President Heber J. Grant [^4] told about William W. Riter:

205"For many years he had read the Book of Mormon through regularly every year. I never dreamed that he would take the time to do so. And he said that he found new inspiration, new uplifting thoughts, that he enjoyed the book, he believed, more each time that he read it than he had previously done. He said that nobody could find anything in that book that was not calculated to uplift mankind, and to improve them; that there was no book that more perfectly inspired a love of the Lord Jesus Christ the Redeemer of the world, by its contents, than did the Book of MormonÂ�."

206Elder Marion G. Romney said in general church conference, 1949: [^5]

206"It is about the Book of Mormon I want to talk today. I do so with just one objective in mind: To get you to read it. I have read it a little, I believe in it, and I love it. I recommend that every person within the sound of my voice read the Book of Mormon. I can testify, as did Nephi, that the things written therein persuadeth all men to do good. It will enrich the life of every person who will read it, unless he is in rebellion against the truth; and in that event it will advise him of his awful fate unless he changes his ways.

206"Very early in my life I became somewhat acquainted with the Book of Mormon. The other day while going through some old records, I found a notebook I had used while in high school in one of the Church academies. In it I had written a short outline of each chapter in the Book of Mormon. I appreciate that training.

206"A few years ago as I began to practice law, members of my family were a little uneasy. They were afraid I would lose my faith. I wanted to practice law, but I had an even greater desire to keep my testimony, and so I decided upon a little procedure which I recommend to you. For thirty minutes each morning before I began the day's work, I read from the Book of Mormon-I read also from all the other standard works of the Church, but I am talking now about the Book of Mormon-and in just a few minutes a day I read the Book of Mormon through, every year, for nine years." The rest of the address is well worth reading.

206At the same conference, Elder Bruce R. McConkie [^6] said:

206"I agree with every word that Brother Marion G. Romney said yesterday. As he has done, I have read the Book of Mormon, prayerfully and carefully, more times than I have fingers; I believe it, sincerely, and wholeheartedly. I know that it is a true witness of Christ and an accurate revelation of the doctrines of Christ."

207This then, is the Book of Mormon, a witness for Jesus Christ and his plan for mankind, a witness that Jesus Christ has through Joseph Smith again established his work in our day. The book invites all to read it and learn for themselves its message of power and inspiration.
