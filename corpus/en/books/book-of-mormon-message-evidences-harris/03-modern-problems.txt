Franklin S. Harris, Book of Mormon: Message and Evidences ( 1961)

Chapter 2

Modern Problems

The Independence Day of the United States is observed on July 4. Traditionally on that day Americans pay tribute to the American nation and to those who have contributed to its freedom, development and greatness, as likewise other nations do on their national holidays. Patriotism plays a large role in this tribute, a tribute remembering great sacrifices in material and human resources in a World War just finished and in present efforts to preserve and extend liberty. With the uncertainty of being able to live together in peace in the future, we need clear thinking and simple real principles as a common basis of understanding between peoples.

15Since in this series of topics we are discussing the Book of Mormon, it may be worthwhile to consider briefly some of the experiences of Book of Mormon peoples with war. Then consider part of the contribution Book of Mormon teachings can make in our job ahead of living together in the world in peace, understanding and activity for the common good.

15The Book of Mormon peoples in three migrations brought their Near East, early Old Testament heritage to America centuries before the time of Christ. These peoples were taught such doctrines as:

15"Neither doth God will that man should shed blood, but in all things hath forbidden it, from the beginning of man.'' [^1]

15"He that hath the spirit of contention is not of me, but is of the devil, who is the father of contention.'' [^2]

15If God does not desire war whence does it come? Ultimately, it comes from the beliefs, manner of living and misconduct of people, [^3] and from anger and hatred of peoples for each other. [^4] The book tells many instances of unrighteous people losing their land through war, [^5] including the statement that the Children of Israel would not have been able to conquer Palestine if the inhabitants had been righteous. [^6] The prediction is made that those who war against the House of Israel will be turned against each other. [^7]

16Is the taking up of arms, or carrying on warfare ever permissible? In ancient America the Nephites, a more civilized people than the Lamanites, were reluctant to take up arms:

16"Notwithstanding their peace amongst themselves, they were compelled reluctantly to contend with their brethren the Lamanites.Â� They were sorry to take up arms against the Lamanites because they did not delight in the shedding of blood.Â� they were sorry to be the means of sending so many of their brethren out of this world into an eternal world, unprepared to meet their God." [^8]

16When the Nephites took up arms it was because they "were compelled for the safety of their lives and their women and children." [^9]

16 Alma records: [^10]

16"It was the only desire of the Nephites to preserve their lands, and their liberty and their church."

16In a letter about 62 B.C., Pahoran, the chief executive wrote to General Moroni:

16"IÂ� seekÂ� toÂ� preserve the rights and liberty of my people. My soul stands fast in that liberty in the which God hath made us free. We will resist wickedness even unto bloodshed. We would not shed the blood of the Lamanites if they would stay in their own land. We would not shed the blood of our brethren if they would not rise up in rebellion and take the sword against us. We would subject ourselves to the yoke of bondage if it were requisite with the justice of God, or if he should command us so to doÂ� He doth not command us that we shall subject ourselves to our enemies, but that we should put our trust in him and he will deliver us. Therefore, my beloved brother, Moroni, let us resist evil, and whatsoever evil we cannot resist with wordsÂ� such as rebellions and dissensions, let us resist them with our swords, that we may retain our freedom. [^11]

17The fine character of General Moroni is given by Alma, [^12] who then explains that: "The Nephites were taught to defend themselves against their enemies, even to the shedding of blood if it were necessary; but never to give an offense, and only to shed blood in their own defense against an enemy."

17The Nephites appointed prophets as leaders of their armies and at times God revealed what course of action to take. [^13] Preparedness was practiced as protection against aggression. [^14] Though God often protected the lives of soldiers in battle, [^15] yet we must keep in mind the words of General Moroni:

17"Do ye suppose that, because so many of your brethren have been killed it is because of their wickedness? Â�If ye have supposed this ye have supposed in vain;Â� there are many who have fallen by the sword; Â� for the Lord suffereth the righteous to be slain that his justice and judgment may come upon the wicked; therefore, ye need not suppose that the righteous are lost because they are slain; but behold they do enter into the rest of the Lord their God." [^16]

18 When the Nephites practiced tolerance, hard work, clean living and worship with honest leadership, the people prospered in peace, except as it was occasionally necessary to defend themselves to preserve their liberty. As with the Nephites we are warned that we can preserve our heritage only through righteous living. [^17]

18As we have seen the Book of Mormon has spoken against war [^18] but what has it said positively to make for peace? What message does the Book of Mormon emphasize which will aid us to live in peace in the world today? Peculiarly, there have been those who thought the Book of Mormon has no particular value.

18Jules Remy, writing in 1860, remarked: [^19]

18"It would be difficult perhaps to refuse to acknowledge the genius but it was true that after the age of fifteen years, he (Joseph Smith) could find in his own inspirations all the woof of this ingenious tale Â�. Joseph tells us himself that he lacked education, and he proves it in every page of the Book of Mormon."

18About the same time, in 1857, T. W. P. Taylder declared: [^20]

18"Were the Book of Mormon authentic, its value would arise from its restoring a fragment of lost history.

18Beyond this it could not claim for regard."

18And a more recent opinion: [^21]

18"The effect of the whole book is disappointing for, outside of the passages quoted, or rather plagiarized from the Bible, there is absolutely nothing uplifting or inspiring."

18Do these comments seem sound when the information already given is considered? Let us review two doctrines of the Book of Mormon and see if they have value and inspiration in promoting peace and understanding in the world today.

19 One of the most important concepts in the book is the universal concern of God with all mankind. This is not a vague sentiment. It may be expressed by saying explicitly, first, that the book's message is for all people who have lived on the earth, [^22] and for all who now live on the earth. Alma says [^23] that a record of the Book of Mormon peoples "should go forth unto every nation, kindred, tongue and people, that they should know the mysteries contained therein." The book makes a similar expression on its title page. Alma [^24] further explains: "We see that God is mindful of every people, whatsoever land they may be in." "The one being as precious in His (God's) sight as any other." [^25] And Nephi avers: "There is one God and one Shepherd over all the earth." [^26] And from Alma:

19"The Lord doth grant unto all nations, of their own nation and tongue, to teach his wordÂ� in wisdom all that he seeth fit that they should have." [^27] The teachings which the Lord gives is "according to the heed and diligence which they give unto him." [^28]

19And note this forceful statement:

19"He inviteth all to come unto him and partake of his goodness; and he denieth none that come unto him, black and white, bond and free, male and female; and he remembereth the heathen; and all are alike unto God, both Jew and Gentile." [^29]Further:

19"He sendeth an invitation unto all men, for the arms of mercy are extended towards them, and he saith: Repent, and I will receive you." [^30] And the fine section from 2 Nephi chapter 29:

20 "Know ye not that there are more nations than one? Know ye not that I, the Lord your God, have created all men, and that I remember those who are upon the isles of the seaÂ� and I bring forth my word unto the children of men, even upon all the nations of the earth? Wherefore murmur ye, because that ye shall receive more of my word? Know ye not that the testimony of two nations is a witness unto you that I am God, that I remember one nation like unto another? Wherefore, I speak the same words unto one nation like unto another. And when two nations shall run together the testimony of the two nations shall run together also.Â� I command all men Â� that they shall write the words I speak unto them; for out of the books which shall be written I will judge the world, every man according to their works.Â� I shall speak unto the Jews and they shall write it Â� unto the Nephites and they shall write it; Â� unto the other tribes of the House of Israel, which I have led away, and they shall write it; and Â� unto all nations of the earth and they shall write it." [^31]

20The Book of Mormon then, teaches the universality of God's concern for men. It further teaches that obedience is a universal fundamental law of progress, both temporally and spiritually. There is no aristocracy in the sight of God except the aristocracy of righteousness. [^32] God is no respecter of persons and is pleased with those obedient to him irrespective of race or heritage. Our race or birth, Jew or Gentile is not important, but what we do with the information and opportunity given us. "For of him unto whom much is given much is required." [^33] This does not mean that God helps and inform all equally because that depends on the ability and willingness to receive.

20For any people to believe that it is the only people in whom God is interested, or helps, or that we have special merit because of our color, race, country or beliefs, that is, that we are inherently superior and loved by God without regard to the lives we live is one of the great fallacies and barriers to peace. This is a fallacy whether in an exploded myth of an Aryan race of supermen, or disguised in more subtle form in our own consciousness. We must not be guilty of such a fallacy.

21"The Lord esteemeth all flesh in one; he that is righteous is favored of God.Â� And he loveth those who will have him to be their God." [^34] The crux of the idea is given by Nephi, [^35] in speaking to his Israelitish brethren:

21"Ye should (not) suppose that ye are more righteous than the Gentiles shall be.Â� Except ye shall keep the commandments of God ye shall all likewise perish.Â�As many of the Gentiles as will repent are the covenant people of the Lord; and as many of the Jews as will not repent shall be cast off; for the Lord covenanteth with none save it be with them that repent and believe in his Son, who is the Holy One of Israel."

21This doctrine of the brotherhood and common spiritual opportunity of man is taught with power and clearness throughout the book, [^36] and taught also in the Bible, [^37] but not as forcefully. [^38] The patriarchs and Israelites preserved and taught God's message, but in the last analysis, they have to be obedient as anyone else does to receive blessings.

22The Book of Mormon has many references pointing out that America is a choice and promised land, [^39] but the requirement of obedience applies to America with even greater force because of its promise. "This is a land which is choice above all other lands; and whatsoever nation shall possess it shall serve God, or they shall be swept off when the fulness of his wrath shall come upon them." [^40]

22What then is the message of the Book of Mormon? In part, it tells that the Book of Mormon peoples were taught to shun war except in preserving their liberty, when they sometimes had to fight for it. [^41] It tells us that God is universal in his desire to aid and draw all mankind to him. It comes as a voice from the past in denying that any people can be superior in the sight of God without being superior in righteousness and service to God. This concept of respect for other peoples, as equals, to whom God has spoken in a different manner, through their own teachers, and the setting of an example of devotion to God-given ideals will do much to remove jealousy, hatred and suspicion from the world.

22These uplifting doctrines show the type of teachings the Book of Mormon would encourage.

22Let us, in whatever part of the world we may live, meet the challenge and set an example to help make the world a peaceful, progressive, and understanding place for all peoples.
