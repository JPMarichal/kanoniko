Chapter 6

The Book of Mormon and the Bible

When the Book of Mormon was published in 1830 it claimed to give the historical and spiritual development of several migrations of Old Testament peoples from the Near East to America. Since the book dealt with their religious experiences and gave an account of the dealings of God with these peoples of Old Testament tradition, and their later church organization, its parallel to the Bible was noticed. It is not surprising then that many statements have been made, though frequently by uninformed and prejudiced people about the Book of Mormon, its nature, purpose, contents and value, particularly in its relation to the Bible.

42Lieut. J. W. Gunnison wrote in 1856: [^1]

42"(The Book of Mormon) is the most successful attempt ever made to imitate the Scriptures, not in its composition, but in its pretensions to be an inspired text.Â� Here is a book that takes fast hold of the minds of hundreds of thousands, and so skillfully arranged on the model of the true Bible, and so garnished with versions and extracts of its text, that it becomes to them a verisimilitude of the Holy Book itself."

42Yet based on only a cursory examination of the book we have such as the following:

42"I have skimmed through the Book of Mormon. Written in exactly the same style as the Bible, it is a rank plagiarism up to the point of insolence," wrote the traveler, Jules Huret. [^2]

42The Book of Mormon anticipated such a reception when it said: [^3]

42"And because my words shall hiss forth many of the Gentiles shall say: A Bible! A Bible! We have got a Bible, and there cannot be any more Bible.

43"But thus saith the Lord God: O fools, they shall have a Bible; and it shall proceed forth from the Jews, mine ancient covenant people. And what thank they the Jews for the Bible which they receive from them? Yea, what do the Gentiles mean? Do they remember the travels, and the labors, and the pains of the Jews, and their diligence unto me, in bringing forth salvation unto the Gentiles? O ye Gentiles, have ye remembered the Jews, mine ancient covenant people? Nay; but ye have cursed them, and have hated them, and have not sought to recover them. But behold, I will return all these things upon your own heads; for I the Lord have not forgotten my people. Thou fool, that shall say: A Bible, we have got a Bible, and we need no more Bible. Have ye obtained a Bible save it were by the Jews?

43"Know ye not that there are more nations than one? Know ye not that I, the Lord your God, have created all men, and that I remember those who are upon the isles of the sea; and that I rule in the heavens above and in the earth beneath; and I bring forth my word unto the children of men yea, even upon all the nations of the earth? Wherefore murmur ye, because that ye shall receive more of my word? Know ye not that the testimony of two nations is a witness unto you that I am God, that I remember one nation like unto another? wherefore, I speak the same words unto one nation like unto another. And when the two nations shall run together the testimony of the two nations shall run together also. And I do this that I may prove unto many that I am the same yesterday, today, and forever; and that I speak forth my words according to mine own pleasure. And because that I have spoken one word ye need not suppose that I cannot speak another; for my work is not yet finished; neither from that time henceforth and forever.

43"Wherefore, because that ye have a Bible ye need not suppose that it contains all my words; neither need ye suppose that I have not caused more to be written. For I command all men, both in the east and in the west, and in the north and in the south, and in the islands of the sea, that they shall write the words which I speak unto them; for out of the books which shall be written I will judge the world, every man according to their works, according to that which is written. For behold, I shall speak unto the Jews and they shall write it; and I shall also speak unto the other tribes of the house of Israel, which I have led away, and they shall write it; and I shall also speak unto all nations of the earth and they shall write it."

44Typical of the type of comments in the old encyclopedias is that from Pierre Larousse's Grand Dictionnaire Universel, [^4] of about 1865:

44"The Book of Mormon is a fraudulent imitation of the Bible, a sort of parody of Genesis, the Books of Kings, the Epistles of the apostles, and the Apocalypse. Not only does one recognize in it very transparent dissimulations but even real borrowings."

44And Stuart Martin: [^5] "It is shown that the Book of Mormon, the authenticity of which was long regarded as unassailable, is only a conglomeration of vain imaginings, intermingled with definite instances of plagiarism from the Bible.Â� Its close imitation to Biblical style is alone suspicious; but there are a great number of instances where direct plagiarism has been employed."

44On the other hand, with further reading, thinking people who became familiar with the book made different comments.

44 Rev. John A. Clark, [^6] who knew of the Book of Mormon before it was published, said: "It fully and cordially admits the truth of the sacred Scripture Â� recognizing the truth and credibility of the sacred Scriptures."

44 Rev. M. T. Lamb: [^7]"It is very readily conceded that the Book of Mormon everywhere professes the warmest attachment of the Bible as the word of God-that it holds it up steadily as the word of GodÂ�. From Bible decisions no appeal is ever taken in the Book of Mormon Bible statements are not disputed or called in question. In fact, the Book of Mormon always pretends to be the handmaiden of the Bible, to be its complement and support. It professes to furnish the clearest proofs of the divine origin of the Bible."

45Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, as a young uninformed writer penned A Study In Scarlet, a story of the Mormons which he later admitted was "sensational and over-colored." But when he had been in Salt Lake City, knew something about the Mormons, and had read the Book of Mormon remarked: [^8]

45"One should take note of the fact that the Mormons have the same regard for the Bible that other Christian denominations have, and that the Book of Mormon is not supposed to supplant it, but rather to corroborate and enlarge it."

45Referring to the personal animosity of bitterly sectarian writers on Mormonism, Phil Robinson, famous British journalist and war correspondent wrote in 1883: [^9] "These men write ofÂ� their (the Mormons) blasphemous repudiation of the Bible, without one particle of information on the subject, except such as they gather from the books and writings of men whom they ought to know are utterly unworthy of credit, or from the verbal calumnies of apostates."

45And the famous German Oriental scholar, Eduard Meyer declared in 1912: [^10]

45"A Bible for America, in absolute harmony with the Old and New Testaments, but at the same time an explanation and fulfillment of their teachings which restore the primitive purity of revelation or the Gospel, that is the thought which runs through the entire Book of Mormon, and in which it is rooted."

45Dr. James E. Talmage [^11] pointed out: "Yet another of the fancies afloat is that of a 'Mormon' Bible. This imaginary volume is known to me only by occasional mention in newspaper columns devoted to sensationalism, or in the expressions of the uninformed. I have never been able to secure a copy. The 'Mormon' Bible is the Holy Bible itself-the version accepted and used by the Christian sects of the day."

46The attitude of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints has been expressed in the eighth Article of Faith:

46"We believe the Bible to be the word of God as far as it is translated correctly; we also believe the Book of Mormon to be the word of God."

46The Bible contains predictions which look forward to the coming forth of the Book of Mormon. Thus the Prophet Ezekiel (37:15-17) tells there was to be a separate record of the house of Ephraim, which should become as one with the record of Judah so that the two would be together. The "stick" or record of Ephraim for Joseph is the Book of Mormon and that of Judah the Bible. [^12] Ezekiel (17:22-23) also seems to predict one of the young sons of a king of Judah going with a migration to a new land and prospering. [^13]

46 Isaiah (29:4) in speaking of a people other than Judah, "And thou shalt be brought down, and shalt speak out of the ground, and thy speech shall be low out of the dust, and thy voice shall be, as of one that hath a familiar spirit, out of the ground, and thy speech shall whisper out of the dust." [^14]

46A detailed discussion of these and other predictions on the Book of Mormon is given by Orson Pratt in Divine Authenticity of the Book of Mormon, [^15] and James E. Talmage, Articles of Faith. [^16]

47 What does the Book of Mormon say about the Bible? It reports that the Bible contains less than the records which the Nephites had, [^17] and "many plain and precious things" [^18] have been left out of the Bible and it seeks to make them known. [^19] The Book of Mormon peoples had records of the prophets back to the beginning. [^20] The comment is made about the Book of Revelations of John "the things which were written were plain and pure and most precious and easy to the understanding of men." [^21] "All scriptureÂ� (is) for our profit and learning." [^22] One of the purposes for which the Book of Mormon was written was to establish the truth of the Bible [^23] that its readers might believe the Bible. [^24]

47What contribution can the Book of Mormon make in establishing the Bible? Let us mention a few of the many ways.

47There is a harmony with the Bible in referring to Old Testament characters and events, the five books of Moses, the early record keeping of the Israelites, the essential unity of most of the book of Isaiah.

47The names of several prophets not found in the Old Testament as we have it today are mentioned in the Book of Mormon. Zenock [^25] is mentioned in five places, Zenos [^26] twelve times and Neum [^27] and Ezias [^28] once.

47The clarification by the Book of Mormon of the Biblical phrase "hell fire" was mentioned in chapter 4.

47Some other doctrines will be discussed in later talks, but for a readily available summary see Verla Birrell, Book of Mormon Guidebook, Clarissa A. Beesley in the Special Interest Handbook for 1946-47, or Seven Claims of the Book of Mormon. [^29]

48M. R. Werner, who from his discussion obviously had not read the book, was to remark in Brigham Young (1925), p. 49, "The Book of Mormon contains no new theological ideas, and very few at all which it has not taken from the Bible." [^30]

48Contrast this with Alexander Campbell, who had read the book carefully and who wrote in the Millennial Harbinger, in 1831, that a main objection to the Book of Mormon was the current and modern controversial points on which the Book of Mormon passes decisions; he lists "infant baptism, ordination, the Trinity, regeneration, repentance, justification, the fall of man, the atonement, transubstantiation, fasting, penance, church government, religious experience, the call to the ministry, general resurrection, eternal punishment, who may baptize, free-masonry, republican government, the rights of man."

48Yet an author was to write in 1851 in The Mormons, p. 36: [^31]

48"The religious matter (is) derived from the Old and New Testaments."

48The Book of Mormon speaks with great clarity as Alexander Campbell observed, on some principles not clear from the Bible as we now have it. If the Bible were clear and unambiguous and complete on all subjects how could there be so many different Christian churches based on it? If the Book of Mormon had not dealt with eternal problems of mankind and the relation to God how could it have any but superficial value to any people who were not of the same time and people it describes?

48As we read the Book of Mormon we are struck by the Biblical style and soon notice reference in the chapter headings to Isaiah, and since the Book of Mormon historians had a copy of Israelite records dating from 600 B.C. it is interesting to compare the two. Can the Book of Mormon make a contribution to a study of the text of the Bible? The contribution would be none if one were to believe with Reverend M. T. Lamb: [^32]

49"The Old Testament, as we find it today, is perfect and complete, all that God designed to be preserved for the world." I am sure he felt the same way about the New Testament.

49But though the Holy Bible is indispensable, yet it is insufficient, as Alexander Campbell discovered, and incomplete as well. There are at least a score of books mentioned in the Bible which are not included in the Bible. [^33] The Book of Mormon indicates that all Scripture is not in the Bible and Book of Mormon. [^34] E. Cecil McGavin has treated the question of the Bible and its text and infallibility [^35] and comparison of Book of Mormon and Bible texts. [^36]

49Let us consider the sections in the Book of Mormon which are also in the Bible. E. D. Howe, [^37] referring to Isaiah, ch. 48 and 49, and 2 Ne. 20 and 21, said:

49"It is a remarkable coincidence that the author of our books should be able to give us an exact copy of those two chapters."

49Actually Howe was careless and superficial when he made that remark. A comparison shows there are many differences between the two texts. There are many differences in all Bible sections also contained in the Book of Mormon. Most of the parallel sections are from Isaiah, of which about one-third of the material in the Authorized Version of King James is in the Book of Mormon, or 433 verses. Of this 199 verses are the same including the italics. The Authorized Version is an excellent translation, though it has the usual errors of copying and many Bible scholars have revised the text of Isaiah in the hope of presenting a clear and understandable reading and at the same time as near the original as possible. In their work they have been guided by versions and available manuscripts and also the Hebrew text itself. In his own translation of the Book of Mormon records, which quote from an original going back to 600 B.C., if Joseph Smith found making a translation to agree essentially with the Authorized Version, and used it, he would only have been doing what translators commonly do. But he made important corrections in places, showing that he actually had the ancient manuscript and followed it where there was not essential agreement. Let us consider some of the evidence.

50In the sections in the Book of Mormon where Isaiah is quoted there are changes which shed light on Isaiah, particularly when considered in the light of other manuscripts. Building upon a previous thesis by Sidney B. Sperry, the master's degree thesis of H. Grant Vest, The Problem of Isaiah in the Book of Mormon, Brigham Young University (1938) [^38] and Sidney B. Sperry, Our Book of Mormon, chs. 14, 15, 16, consider the 234 verses which are different. The following examples may be noted:

50Comparing Isa. 2:16 with 2 Ne. 12:16, we see that the Book of Mormon reads: "And upon all the ships of the sea, and upon all the ships of Tarshish, and upon all pleasant pictures." The Greek Septuagint includes the phrase "and upon every ship of the sea," which the Authorized Version leaves out, but the Septuagint omits "and upon all the ships of Tarshish," which the Authorized Version includes, and words the third phrase differently. The Book of Mormon contains all three phrases, the Authorized Version and the Greek Septuagint two each. This is an excellent example of what often happens in translation and copying. This indicates that the Hebrew lost the first phrase which the Greek Septuagint preserved, and the reverse was the case on the last phrase.

50To Isa. 29:6 the Book of Mormon (2 1 Ne. 27:2) reads "and with" for "and" in four places. The Syriac reads as the Book of Mormon suggests. In some cases the Book of Mormon text adds "and," which in Hebrew is represented by a single character "waw," inferring there has been an omission from the present Hebrew text, the addition is confirmed by the Septuagint and the Syriac. Examples are found in Isa. 3:14 (2 Ne. 13: 14); 48:13 (1 Ne. 20:13) 50:9 (2 Ne. 7:9) 51:18 (2 Ne. 8:18). In Isa. 48:5 (1 Ne. 20: 5) the Book of Mormon adds "and" which is literally what the Hebrew reads. The Authorized Version translators used "even" in English. "In Isa. 14:4 (2 Ne. 24:4) the Book of Mormon adds 'And it shall come to pass in that day,' which is without support in the Hebrew. But of striking interest is a similar reading in Codex Alexandrinus (now in the British Museum), 'and thou shall say in that day.' The latter is not found in (Codex) VaticanusÂ�." [^39]

51The Codex Alexandrinus is now in the British Museum and Joseph Smith did not have access to it. Nor was the Codex Alexandrinus used in preparing the Authorized Version because it arrived in England 17 years too late in 1628 A.D. [^40]

51"In Isa. 2:20 (2 Ne. 12:20) where the Book of Mormon reads 'he hath made' for 'they made' the reading is confirmed by Codex Alexandrinus which renders 'he made.' In Isa. 51:15 (2 Ne. 8:15) the Book of Mormon revises the Authorized Version 'His name' to read 'my name' and interestingly these readings are found in the Septuagint and Latin." [^41]

51In Isa. 5:5 (2 Ne. 15: 5) the Book of Mormon adds "I will" making the clause read "and I will break down the wall thereof." This reading is precisely that of the Septuagint which renders "and I will pull down its walls."

51"In Isa. 5:7 (2 Nephi 15: 7) the Authorized Version translators render the Hebrew 'but behold' which is literally 'and behold' as suggested in the Book of Mormon. To Isa. 29:21 the Book of Mormon in 2 Ne. 27:32 adds the phrase "and they" to the beginning of the verse. The Septuagint and Syriac both read the same as the Book of Mormon. [^42]

52For Isa. 51:15 the Book of Mormon (2 1 Ne. 8:15) revised the Authorized Version "his name" to read "my name" and interestingly the Book of Mormon form is found also in the Septuagint and the Latin.

52These changes show that the Book of Mormon writers actually had a copy of the early Old Testament records, and is valuable in studying the text of the Bible, since it dates from 600 B.C. and until recently the oldest Hebrew manuscript of any considerable portion of the Old Testament was the ninth century A.D., and the earliest versions and relatively complete codices date from the fourth century A.D.

52These studies of the text a century after the Book of Mormon was published dispose of the unsubstantiated guesses of critics about the relation of the Book of Mormon and Bible texts.

52 Nephi (2 1 Ne. 25) makes some comments and explanations on Isaiah.

52It is interesting to recall the statement made by Joseph Symes, editor of the Liberator, of Melbourne, Australia: [^43]

52"I submit the Book of Mormon is the best attested Bible in the world, that the Jewish-Christian Bible has no evidence at all in comparison with it."

52The relation of the Book of Mormon to the Bible has always been of considerable interest. As Nels Anderson remarks: [^44]

52"Mormon missionaries might have been content to rest their case on the Book of Mormon. Sectarian preachers constantly challenge them in this wise: 'If this new book is in harmony with the teachings of the Bible then prove to us from the Bible that your doctrines are true.' Before many years the Mormon elder delighted in nothing more than a challenge from a minister for a public debate on the Bible."

53However, we should keep in mind the words of the late President Joseph F. Smith on the relation of the Book of Mormon to the Bible and usefulness in missionary work: [^45]

53"In later years, our elders have depended more upon the Bible to establish the truth of the Gospel, and to some extent the Book of Mormon has been neglected in the missionary field. We have lost track of the fact that God has said, through his prophet, that the Book of Mormon should be a witness for the truth of the Bible, and should establish the truths of the Bible (1 Ne. 13) Â�. The honest in heart will as willingly receive a truth from the Doctrine and Covenants or the Book of Mormon as from the Bible. If a person does not want the truth, the evidence in the Bible that may be brought to bear, while it may silence him, will not change his position nor cause him to receive the truth. It is the Spirit of God, after all, which works the change in the human soul."

53It is the reading of the Book of Mormon, and the corroborative evidence which it gives as a staunch friend and ally in supporting and confirming the Bible, which has made the Latter-day Saints, revere, prize and use the Bible to a degree probably unequalled by any modern educated group of like size in the world.

53In this day of skepticism it serves as a witness to confirm the Old Testament, and as we shall see in a later chapter, also the story of the Gospels in the New Testament. The reading and study of the Book of Mormon gives more power and clearness to the vital message which the Bible has for man today.
