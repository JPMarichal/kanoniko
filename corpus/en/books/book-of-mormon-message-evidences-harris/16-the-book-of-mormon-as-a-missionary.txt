Franklin S. Harris, Book of Mormon: Message and Evidences ( 1961)

Chapter 15

The Book of Mormon As a Missionary

The Book of Mormon on its title page and in the message it contains claims to be a witness for Jesus Christ, and the love of God for all mankind. Its purpose is to bring people to accept Jesus as the Christ. [^1] The most important part of the book tells of the visit of Jesus Christ to ancient America and the teachings he gave in clarity and great power to those people. But the Book of Mormon also supports the Bible in the Bible's teachings of the message of Christ and his mission as the Redeemer of the world. The Book of Mormon teaches of man's present obligation to fellow man, to God, and to God's authorized representatives.

168Joseph Smith tells us he translated this ancient account "by the gift and power of God," and he founded a church in 1830-the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints-in which the Book of Mormon became accepted as scripture in addition to the Bible. Now this Book of Mormon was translated and published in a day when most Christian people believed that the canon of scripture was full and no more was to be revealed either in records or otherwise. These people were challenged by the Book of Mormon's claims to be a record of the direct dealings of God with an additional section of humanity, and its witness for Jesus Christ. The world of 1830 and since has been provoked by the claims of the Church which published the book. Such a book then, as we have seen previously, did arouse great attention and controversy.

168Much more important than the book's role in provoking discussion of its claims is its role in carrying conviction to its readers of the divinity of Christ and his work for mankind. Did and does the book make converts to its message and the church which teaches it? That is the key to its value and of much greater importance than its usefulness as an ancient history of part of mankind, or even of its confirmation of much Biblical history and teaching, or yet its appealing elaboration in clarity of many doctrines not agreed on by the Christian world. [^2] Did and does it bring men to accept Christ and join his Church?

169The greatest value of the Book of Mormon is then its value as a witness for Christ and its effectiveness in convincing people of his message to a point where they will try to live his teachings and commandments.

169Only a small fraction of the experiences of people revealing the influence which the Book of Mormon has had in making converts and instilling testimony is to be found in written literature. Many thousands of stories could be told, and who has not heard many such accounts in such places and times as people tell their most precious experiences, particularly in the fast meetings, (often called testimony meetings) of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints?

169Let us first notice the philosophy of religion, the treatment of religious knowledge and doubt and how to obtain religious convictions as related in the Book of Mormon. The book itself seems to advocate a personal, experimental approach to religious convictions.

169Nephi, the first historian in the book, was told by his father Lehi, of a vision in which Lehi found that the enjoyment of the love of God is more desirable than anything else. [^3] Nephi's brothers spoke of these things as "thou art like unto our father, led away by the foolish imaginations of his heart." [^4] Nephi, however, wanted to know what his father had seen in the vision, so he went directly to the Lord and it was made known to Nephi. [^5] When Nephi's brethren could not understand what Lehi had told them of the vision, Nephi relates: [^6]

170 "For he (Lehi) truly spake many great things unto them, which were hard to be understood, save a man should inquire of the Lord; and they being hard in their hearts, therefore they did not look unto the Lord as they oughtÂ�." Then Nephi asked his brothers:

170"Have ye inquired of the Lord? And they said unto me: We have not; for the Lord maketh no such thing known unto us. Behold, I said unto them: How is it that ye do not keep the commandments of the Lord? How is it that ye will perish, because of the hardness of your hearts? Do ye not remember the things which the Lord hath said? If ye will ask me in faith, believing that ye shall receive, with diligence in keeping my commandments, surely these things shall be made known unto you."

170Note that Nephi said it was not just idle asking, but it involved a sincere desire for knowledge, shown in part by obedience to information already known.

170 Enos approached God directly, in experimental fashion, to get knowledge and to obtain forgiveness for mistakes and he received them. [^7]

170The Book of Mormon tells of a great many spiritual experiences among its peoples. It further emphasizes the relation between faith and the exercise of spiritual gifts. [^8] Miracles do not in general convert. [^9]

170The great teacher and thinker Alma, the younger, tells of the joy which the humble seeker of happiness may obtain. [^10] Alma in his great discourse on faith, [^11] in which he compares faith to the growth of a seed, says in part: [^12]

170"If ye will awake, and arouse your faculties, even to an experiment upon my words, and exercise a particle of faith, yea, even if ye can no more than desire to believe, let this desire work in you, even until ye believe in a manner that ye can give place for a portion of my wordsÂ�. Neither must ye lay aside your faith, for ye have only exercised your faith to plant the seed that ye might try the experiment to know if the seed was good."

171And immediately after, Amulek continued: [^13]

171"He hath exhorted you unto faith and patience, yea, even that ye would have so much faith as even to plant the word in your hearts, that ye may try the experiment of its goodness."

171Christ told Moroni, the last historian: [^14]

171"He that believeth these things which I have spoken, him will I visit with the manifestations of my Spirit, and he shall know and bear record. For because of my Spirit he shall know that these things are true; for it persuadeth men to do good."

171And one of the most important statements in the book is the assertion by Moroni [^15] that a test can be made of the Book of Mormon itself whether it be of God. This test is the central theme of the stories which follow.

171"When ye shall receive these things, I would exhort you that ye would ask God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Christ, if these things are not true; and if ye shall ask with a sincere heart, with real intent, having faith in Christ, he will manifest the truth of it unto you, by the power of the Holy Ghost. And by the power of the Holy Ghost ye may know the truth of all things."

171This challenge to a sincere, personal test, individually made, was the same basis of part of the Mutual Improvement Associations theme for 1946-47, quoting from Brigham Young: "Let everyone get a knowledge for himself that this work is true."

171President Heber J. Grant [^16]on this subject, said:

171"Experimental knowledge is the very best knowledge in the world; and any man who will believe what he is told to believe, in one of the closing paragraphs of the Book of Mormon, and who, after reading it, will ask God for a knowledge of its divinity, and live worthily of that knowledge will receive itÂ�.

172"I rejoice in the wonderful spirit of the Book of Mormon. I believe that it is one of the greatest missionaries in the hands of an elder that it is possible for him to have. I believe that no man can open that book and read it with a prayerful heart, and ask God, in the name of Jesus Christ, for a testimony regarding its divinity, but what the Lord will manifest unto him by His Spirit the truth of the book itself. And God has performed it; He has done it in thousands of cases. There is a mark of divinity on this book; and I maintain that no man can read, for instance, the thirty-sixth chapter, the comments of Alma to his son Helaman, without receiving an impression of this kind. It is claimed by some that this book was written as a novel. I maintain that a man ought to have his head tapped for the simples who would undertake to say that any one would be idiotic enough to write a book like the Book of Mormon as a novel, hoping to sell it to the people."

172In the Book of Mormon, Ammon after a very successful missionary trip to the Lamanites expresses in a hymn of praise the spiritual satisfaction that comes from such work. Part of the hymn [^17] follows:

172Behold, how many thousands of our brethren has he (God) loosed from the pains of hell; And they are brought to sing redeeming love, And this because of the power of his word which is in us, Therefore have we not great reason to rejoice?

172Yea, we have reason to praise him forever, For he is the Most High God, And has loosed our brethren from the chains of hell. Yea, they were encircled about with everlasting darkness and destruction; But behold, he has brought them into his everlasting light, Yea, into everlasting salvation;

173 And they are encircled about with the matchless bounty of his love; Yea, and we have been instruments in his hands of doing this great and marvelous work.

173Therefore, let us glory, yea, we will glory in the Lord; Yea, we will rejoice, for our joy is full; Yea, we will praise our God forever.

173Behold, who can glory too much in the Lord? Yea, who can say too much of his great power, And of his mercy, and of his long-suffering towards the children of men? Behold, I say unto you, I cannot say the smallest part which I feel."

173"It is its deep message which converts its readers. Those who read the volume in 1830 cared little for history or archaeology, but the philosophy answered their inner needs. The great pity is that so many became lost in debate over the origin of the book to see clearly its message." [^18]

173After the Church was organized in 1830, for about seven years the Book of Mormon was the only important Church literature, and was used by the elders as practically the only missionary. There is one copy which had a particularly interesting history as told by Joseph Smith's mother, Lucy Mack Smith. [^19]

173Within three months after the Church was organized, Samuel H. Smith had gone out as the first missionary. He left a copy at the home of John P. Greene, a Methodist preacher, who said he would take a subscription paper on his preaching mission and if he found anyone who wanted a copy he would give his name to Samuel when he got back. Samuel H. Smith tells the story:

173"When I arrived at Mr. Greene's, Mrs. Greene informed me that her husband was absent from home, that there was no prospect of selling my books, and even the one which I had left with them, she expected I would have to take away, as Mr. Greene had no disposition to purchase it, although she had read it herself and was much pleased with it."

174 Samuel was impressed, however, to leave the book with Mrs. Greene.

174"I made her a present of it, and told her that the Spirit forbade my taking it away. She burst into tears, and requested me to pray with her. I did so, and afterwards explained to her the most profitable manner of reading the book which I had left with her; which was, to ask God, when she read it, for a testimony of the truth of what she had read, and she would receive the Spirit of God, which would enable her to discern the things of God. I then left her, and returned home."

174According to Mrs. Smith when Mr. Greene returned home he was told what Samuel had said about reading the book and Mrs. Greene requested him to read it.

174"This, he for a while, refused to do, but finally yielded to her persuasions, and took the book, and commenced perusing the same, calling upon God for the testimony of his Spirit. The result of which was, that he and Mrs. Greene were in a short time baptized. They gave the book to Phineas Young, Mrs. Greene's brother, who read it, and commenced preaching it forthwith. It was next handed to Brigham Young, and from him to Mrs. Murray, his sister, who is also the mother of Heber C. Kimball's wife. They all received the workÂ� and rejoiced in the truth thereof. Joseph Young was at this time in Canada, preaching the Methodist doctrine; but, as soon as Brigham became convinced of the truth of the gospel, as contained in the Book of Mormon, he went straightway to his brother Joseph, and persuaded him to cease preaching Methodism and embrace the truth, as set forth in the Book of Mormon, which he carried with him."

175Some readers of the Book of Mormon accepted it on brief examination, but some, like Brigham Young, must have felt like Chateaubriand, [^20] who in discussing Christianity said, in 1802:

175"Is our religion afraid of the light? A great proof of its heavenly origin is that it permits the most severe and minute examination of reason.Â� We are no longer in a time when it is good to say: 'Believe and do not examine.'"

175Brigham Young [^21] listened to the reports and testimony of already converted friends and later related:

175"I weighed the matter studiously for nearly two years before I made up my mind to receive that Book. I looked at it on all sides. All other religions I could fathom Â� but this new one I reasoned on month after month, until I came to a certain knowledge of its truth. Had this not been the case I never would have embraced it to this day. I wished time sufficient to prove all things for myself."

175Thomas B. Marsh [^22] on a trip from his home in Boston, Massachusetts, heard about the Book of Mormon in Lyonstown, New York. He altered his journey and obtained from Martin Harris at the printing office in 1829 the first sixteen pages of the Book of Mormon which had just been struck off the press. After talking to Oliver Cowdery he returned home highly pleased with the information he had received. He showed his wife the sixteen pages which he had obtained and she also believed it to be the work of God. After the Church was organized the Marsh's moved to Palmyra, in September, 1830, and were baptized.

175Reverend Orson Spencer was a graduate of two colleges and extensively known particularly in the New England states as a Baptist preacher. He had a congregation in Massachusetts which included the governor of the state. His brother Daniel called the nature and origin of the Book of Mormon to his attention. Reverend Spencer bought a copy. He later describes his reactions in 1842: [^23]

176"I read diligently the Book of Mormon from beginning to end, in close connection with the comments of Origen Bachelor, Laroy Sunderland, and Dr. Hurlbut, together with newspapers and some private letters obtained from the surviving friends of Mr. Spaulding, the supposed author of the book. I arose from its perusal with a strong conviction on my mind, that its pages were graced with the pen of inspiration."

176He was converted and with great sacrifice joined the Church. He said:

176"I only ask the friends of pure religion to read the Book of Mormon with the same unprejudiced, prayerful and teachable spirit that they would recommend unbelievers in the ancient scriptures to read those records."

176Orson Hyde was an active Campbellite minister in Ohio in 1830 when several Mormons visited that neighborhood with the Book of Mormon. By request of his members he read part of the book and preached against it. But feeling that he had done wrongly, he decided that he would oppose it no more until he had made further investigation. He went to Kirtland, Ohio, and after diligent inquiry became convinced of its truth and was baptized. [^24]

187 "Knowledge is not sufficient to permit us to say: I know the Book of Mormon is an inspired book. There must be a spiritual confirmation which comes to the individual through obedience, humility, faith, prayer, and the Holy Ghost. If we comply with these spiritual principles then there shall come to us an individual testimony which was promised by Moroni centuries ago." [^51]

187The test given by Moroni, it is to be noted, promises conviction to such readers to be given by the Holy Ghost. The book in its accounts of the lives of men such as Nephi, Enos, Alma, Moroni and others holds out a promise that in this or any other day, individuals may approach God directly, personally, in what may be called the experimental method, to find for themselves the certitudes of religion, based on a sincere, honest approach by one obedient to the commandments of God so far learned. [^52] That such convictions of spiritual knowledge have been obtained is the witness of thousands, many of whom have sacrificed greatly for the satisfaction which comes from spiritual peace and understanding. The Book of Mormon has been and is a powerful physical witness of the mission of Christ. It offers rich spiritual rewards for the reader because it is a missionary for Jesus Christ.
