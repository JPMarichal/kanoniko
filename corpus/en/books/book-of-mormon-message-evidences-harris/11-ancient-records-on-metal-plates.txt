Franklin S. Harris, Book of Mormon: Message and Evidences ( 1961)

Chapter 10

Ancient Records on Metal Plates

A few years ago a conversation between a college student and a friend led to a discussion of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The comment was made by the college student that the Church was composed of fine people and that the Church seemed to have a good influence on the lives of its members, but that he wished they would leave out the story about Joseph Smith and the golden plates, from which the Book of Mormon was translated. This young physical science student with the common assumption that "seeing is believing" asked the friend:

98"Do you believe the Book of Mormon could have been written on gold plates?"

98The friend answered, "Yes."

98The student then asked, "Have you seen the gold plates?"

98"No."

98The student continued by asking, "Then why do you think it was originally written on gold plates?"

98The friend replied by asking a question, "Have you seen the standard meter bar?"

98 "No."

98"Well, why are you asking me about the gold plates then?"

98The student could not answer his question. It was no more reasonable to question the gold plates which he had not seen, but which others had seen and described, and whose translated text was available, than it was to question the existence of the international meter bar at SÃ¨vres in France. But the bar which he had not seen has been used to calibrate the standards of length for the world including the familiar yardstick. The common yardstick is available to all for measurement, and the printed Book of Mormon is available for all to read, though few have seen the originals of either. Though the Book of Mormon plates are not now available yet there are many such plates in the world as we shall see.

99In a day when paper is the common material for record keeping, we do not expect to get our newspapers, magazines and books on metal plates. It is commonplace, however, to see metal signs, identification tags, cornerstone plaques and monuments with writing on metal for preservation. Before printing was developed when important information was to be preserved against the various destructive forces of nature, the most durable materials available were used, metals and stone. Let us see if such record keeping on metals was a practice with ancient peoples and therefore if it seems reasonable that the Book of Mormon as an important history might be recorded on metal.

99In our survey let us start in the Orient.

99In the British Museum, in London, I remember seeing on exhibition twenty-five silver plates, approximately eighteen by two inches, bound together by thongs. This was a Pali manuscript beautifully engraved in Singhalese characters, giving Buddha's first sermon at Benares India. By the side of the silver plates is a very thin gold plate with another attached, about two by nine and a half inches, both plates beautifully engraved with a letter in Javanese characters, probably from one of the native princes. A picture has been published of the Pali plates. [^1]

99One of the oldest gold plates ever found was made in very early times indeed.

99Menkure [^2] of 2800 B.C., builder of the third pyramid at Giza, in his tomb bad only a blue sarcophagus containing the remains of a man and no treasure except some golden tablets "inscribed with characters of a language which nobody could understand."

100 The "pseudo-hieroglyphic" inscriptions on bronze tablets found by Dunand [^3] in Lebanon at Byblus, date from about the 18th century B.C. The Demotic Chronicle [^4] of Egypt was kept originally on plates.

100G. A. Barton [^5] shows the Boss of Tarkondemos and says: "This 'boss' consisted of a round silver plate, in form like half an orange, which must have covered the knob of a staff or a dagger. This had been described by Dr. A. D. Mordtmann in the Journal of the German Oriental Society in 1872. The original was then in possession of Alexander Jovanoff, a numismatist of Constantinople who had obtained it in Smyrna. The 'boss' bore in its center a figure of the peculiar Hittite form flanked on both sides by writing in the Hittite characters, while around the whole was an inscription in the cuneiform writing of Assyria."

100About 1254 B.C. after a long war between the Hittites of Syria and the Egyptians, a treaty was concluded by Rameses II of Egypt and Khattu-sil, king of the Hittites. A translation of the Egyptian copy given by the famous scholar the late A. H. Sayce, [^6] reads:

100"This is a copy of the contents of the silver tablet which the great king of the Hittites, Khattu-sil, had caused to be made and which was presented to the Pharaoh by the hand of his ambassador Tal-tesub and his ambassador Re-mes, to propose friendship with the king Ramessu-Miamun" (Rameses II)

100In the same book, [^7] Dr. Sayce tells us that the oldest writing material of the Hittites was metal plates with the characters hammered through to the front surface from the back side.

100Seck [^8] reports that the kings of Egypt used inscribed tablets of gold, silver and copper to honor their gods and J. H. Breasted [^9] gives an example with Rameses III:

101"I made for thee great tablets of gold in beaten work engraved with the great name of thy majesty, bearing my praises-I made for thee great tablets of silver in beaten work, carved with the graver's tool, bearing the decrees and the inventions of the houses and temples which I made (in) Egypt."

101The University of Chicago Oriental Institute [^10] has a gold tablet found at the source of the Tigris referring to Shalmanezer III (reigned 859-825 B.C.) which reads: "Shalmanezer, prefect of Eubil, priest of Assur, son of Assur-nasir-pal, priest of Assur, grandson of Tukulti-Urta, priest of Assur, conqueror from the upper sea to the lower sea, even the Sea of Chaldea which they call the bitter sea, who marched to Hittite land, who brought it under sway to its farthest border: to Babylon, Borsippa Kuha I went; I offered sacrifices."

101Brown [^11] notes that a discoverer "found under one of the stones of the palace of the Chaldean monarch Sargon a box containing votive tabletsÂ� The tablets were of gold, silver, copper, lead and tin."

101Sargon King of Assyria, 722-705 B.C., in his Annals [^12] repeats over and over that he kept his records on plates of metal tablets of gold, silver, bronze, and lead.

101Not long after the migration of Lehi from Jerusalem about 600 B.C., described in the Book of Mormon, Darius I, between 518 and 515 B.C., buried gold and silver memorial tablets at Persepolis in Iran. Discovered undisturbed in 1933, [^13] these tablets setting forth the limits of his kingdom were over thirteen inches square and .6 inch thick with a text in three languages, Old Persian, Elamite and Babylonian. Just like another Rosetta stone.

102The oldest Greek records are inscriptions carved in stone or engraved on metal surfaces. The treaties between Greek states were frequently engraved on bronze plates and attached to the walls of temples. [^14] I have seen a number of such bronze plates in the National Museum in Athens.

102In the British Museum [^15] there is also an Egyptian gold plate two by six inches giving in Greek characters the dedication of a temple to Osiris by Ptolemy Eurgetes and Berenice (242-222 B.C.)

102Polybius [^16] mentions that the treaties between Rome and Carthage written on plates were in his time still preserved in Rome.

102In the Evkaf Museum in Istanbul, Turkey, I remember seeing some silver plates four and one-half by three inches in a group about an inch thick. This was a part of the Koran, sacred to the Moslems, engraved in beautiful fine script. (Museum No. 1440)

102In recent years a considerable number of copper plates, inscribed, perforated and linked together with metal plates have been found in India.

102An example of those listed by Hugh Nibley [^17] is the Kesarbeda Plates. These plates consist of three copper plates strung together on a copper ring the circumference and diameter of the ring are 7.4 and 2 inches respectively. The plates measure about 7.5 inches in length and 1.5 broad. All plates have writing on both sides. These plates date from about 324 A.D. and give the governing conditions of a royal charter.

102Family records were kept anciently at the house of the ruling family [^18] as we learn from adventures of the Egyptian envoy to Byblus (1090-1085 B.C.) and from the Amarna tablets (14th century B.C.) in Palestine the same practice and the same type of records were later kept in Rome at the time Lehi left Palestine.

103 During its entire history the Book of Mormon has been criticized for an account having been written on gold plates, and telling that some of its people had taken from Jerusalem about 600 B.C. a set of brass plates giving Israelite history and prophecy up to that time. (1 Ne. 3:3.) [^19] This has been strenuously objected to as unhistorical and unreasonable as almost any Mormon missionary can testify. [^20] L.A. Bertrand [^21] expresses it:

103"When the Book of Mormon came to reveal to the world that these migrating peopleÂ� had the custom of engraving their records on tablets of gold or copper to leave to posterityÂ� the wise laughed heartily at these absurdities.'

103 John Hyde, Jr. [^22] early claimed:

103"The Jews did not use plates of brass at that time."

103As did also D. H. Bays: [^23]

103"The Hebrews never kept their records on 'plates of brass.'"

103However, the famous traveler Sir Richard F. Burton, [^24] when this was mentioned to him brushed it aside with the remark:

103"As regards the golden plates it is contended that the Jews of old were in the habit of writing upon papyrus parchment and so on, not upon metal, and that such plates have never been found in America. But of late years Himyaritic inscriptions upon brass tablets have been forwarded from Yemen (in Arabia) to the British MuseumÂ�."

103Let us consider some of the writing practices of the Israelites, [^25] in which we are greatly interested.

103In Ex. 39:30 we read: "And they made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote upon it a writing, like to the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD." And in Jer. 17:1, [^26] "The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron, and with the point of a diamond; it is graven upon the tablet of their heart." The late J. M. Sjodahl [^27] remarks after a discussion of these two passages: "That proves beyond a question that the Israelites were familiar with engraved tablets, for otherwise the words of the prophet would have been unintelligible to them."

104Recently a letter in Hebrew written in the twelfth century B.C. on a copper or bronze plate has been found in Palestine. [^28]

104In the Smith and Goodspeed translation of Isa. 8:1-2 Isaiah telling of the instructions the Lord gave him says:

104"Then said the Lord to me, Take a great tablet, and write upon it in easily legible characters, "Speeding is the spoil, hastening is the prey. And get me a reliable witnesses to attest the writing, Uriah the Priest, and Zechariah, the son of Jeberechiah." Adam Clarke in his Commentary remarks on this section: "I have a metal-line mirror found in Herculaneum which is not above three inches square. The prophet is commanded to take a mirror, or brazen polished tablet, not like these little hand mirrors, but a large one; large enough for him to engrave upon it in deep and lasting characters Â� with a workman's graving tool, the prophecy he was to deliver."

104The Book of Maccabees 8:22 in the Apocrypha states:

104"And this is a copy of the writing which they (the Roman Senate) wrote back again on tablets of brass, and sent to Jerusalem, that it might be with them for a memorial of peace and confederacy." In chapter 14:16-18 it is recorded that the Spartans wrote to Simon on tablets of brass, to renew the friendship which they had had with Judas and Jonathan his brethren.

105From Josephus compared with Pliny, Richard Watson [^29] tells us "The Hebrews went so far as to write their sacred books in gold." According to John Kitto [^30] lead plates were also used. [^31]

105From a multitude of documented evidences in Metal Record Plates in Ancient Times by Ariel L. Crowley, [^32] we can only note three samples [^33] and part of his conclusion.

105"It is written in the Secrets of Enoch [^34] that sacred books were written by Adam, Seth, Enos, Cainan, Mahala-leel, Jared and Enoch, for the benefit of their posterity. The ancient rabbinical tradition is that Tubal-cain, the hammerer of brass, [^35] made the plates on which the books were inscribed." [^36]

105"When Abraham bought his burial place, the field and cave of Machpelah, from the sons of Heth, monuments were erected, according to tradition, bearing plates of brass engraved with the terms of the accord. These plates are said to have continued in place and legible until the time of David, to whom the promises of Abraham so written were pointed out when he came to assault Jerusalem." [^37]

105The Jebusites, descendants of Abimelech, King of Gerar, according to an ancient story, built a strong fortress in Zion, on top of which he placed two monuments with metal plates attached. These plates were engraved with the terms of his covenant with Abraham. This covenant was respected as long as any member of the house of Abimelech remained alive. [^38]

106Mr. Crowley concludes, after an extended presentation of evidences, "It is clear that according to all tradition in Israel, tablets of gold and brass were kept, inscribed with holy writings from antiquity far antedating Moses, and thereafter."

106So much for the Old World. [^39] Let us consider the New World and see what metallic objects have been preserved. I say preserved because most of them have been destroyed.

106In the regions of high civilization, Mexico and Peru, gold was found in abundance, and the Book of Mormon plates had the appearance of gold. We remember that the Inca of Peru bought his ransom from the Spaniards by filling a room seventeen by twenty-two feet to a depth of nine feet with gold objects, and in Mexico, Montezuma, the ruler, served food on elaborately worked gold platters as large as shields.

106Unfortunately there are but a few precious metal objects which were not melted down, or for that matter any type of writings, that were not destroyed by the conquerors. The melting of this gold is a great loss to civilization. Were that these objects had been saved for us like the gold casket and other materials from King Tut-ankh-amen's tomb, now in the Cairo Museum, or the objects from Monte Alban, now in the Museo Nacional in Mexico City.

106However, many metallic plates, mostly found since 1830, are now known. Various materials have been used. A few years ago, Dr. Warren K. Moorehead [^40] found copper plates in Georgia. Adair [^41] tells of five copper and two brass tablets among the Tuccabatchey Indians which according to tradition were given them by God.

107 One of the most interesting finds was made near Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1847, when a gold plate was discovered three or four inches in length and about three-quarters of an inch in width. Two plates fastened together with pins were beautifully engraved with ancient raised characters which seem to be mostly ancient Egyptian, according to Dr. Wise, a learned rabbi of the Jewish synagogue there and editor of a Hebrew paper who examined the plates. Though the present location of the plates is unknown, fortunately there was a facsimile printed in the Millennial Star. [^42]

107Other gold plates have been noted. Padre Gay [^43] mentions the selling to some European antiquarians by the Mexican Indians of some very thin plates of gold. These thin plates had evidently been worked with the hammer, and the plates engraved with ancient hieroglyphs their ancestors had been able to preserve.

107In Colombia, it has been reported [^44] that the Indian graves contained plates of gold and tablets of clay with various curious characters.

107Torquemaria [^45] tells of ancient Americans inscribing on metal, Clavijero, de Solis and de la Vega [^46] tell of the use of gold plates in ancient America.

107More striking, Elders Melvin J. Ballard and Rey L. Pratt, [^47] in the museum in Lima, Peru, saw a stack of gold sheets almost identical in size with the Book of Mormon sheets, about seven by eight inches and thin as paper. The whole stack was nearly an inch thick, not a character on either side, but just gold sheets as though ready for a scribe to write.

107There are other sets of plates of the same kind. Levi Edgar Young reports: [^48] "Beautiful gold plates were collected by the museums of Europe before World War I. The writer saw in the Royal Museum of Berlin, some years ago, a large and beautiful assortment of gold plates, some of them circular and others rectangular or square. Some were as thin as ordinary sheets of paper and showed careful workmanship. There was no writing on them, but they were found in the old temples of the Mayas and were made ages ago. Of recent date the writer was shown the collection of gold plates now in the collection of the Museum of the American Indian in New York City. The plates were about twelve inches long by ten inches wide and as thin as ordinary paper."

108In the deYoung Museum in Golden Gate Park in San Francisco, California [^49] there are on exhibit two thin plates of gold from ancient Colombia, South America. Each plate is about five inches wide, twelve inches long and about one-fifteenth inch thick.

108The Book of Mormon records which Joseph Smith translated he described [^50] as "engraved on plates which had the appearance of gold, each plate was six inches wide, and eight inches long, but not quite so thick as common tin."

108On the basis of the quotations and remarks given it is seen that the ancients frequently preserved their writing of sacred literature. as well as other important information, on metal plates both in the Old World and the New, Israelites and American Indians. Although the student of our story could not now see the plates from which Joseph Smith translated the Book of Mormon he could in our day have seen plates in the museums of the Old and New World which would dispel any doubt that he might have had that historians of ancient America, with a Near East heritage, and with important records to preserve would use just such metal plates for preserving their history as Joseph Smith tells us were used in the Book of Mormon.
