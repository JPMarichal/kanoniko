Franklin S. Harris, Book of Mormon: Message and Evidences ( 1961)

Chapter 3

A Modern Message

Since mankind first came on the earth we have had serious problems, but none so difficult as the problems of man's relation with man. These troubles we shall have as long as there are people on the earth. The accumulated knowledge of man's experience should, however point the way toward a solution of some of them. Let us look at one from the point of view of the Book of Mormon peoples, their experiences, and the teachings of their inspired leaders.

23What is the most important problem facing statesmen and the common man in working out a long range program for peace? In the opinion of Dr. Gladys A. Reichard, professor of anthropology, Barnard College, Columbia University, [^1] "tolerant and sympathetic understanding between races and creeds with the objective of elevating the levels of minority groups is the paramount issue."

23From the other side of the world, Zaki Ali, an Indian Moslem, [^2] says the world badly needs at present: equality, justice for all, civic freedom, toleration and good fellowship.

23What does the Book of Mormon say about tolerance?

23In the first place the historians of the Book of Mormon records were themselves free from arrogance. They were aware of their own limitations. [^3] Likewise the priests and teachers of those ancient peoples believed themselves to be no better than other people.

23"The priest, not esteeming himself above his hearers, for the preacher was not better than the hearer, neither was the teacher any better than the learner; and thus they were all equal, and they did all labor, every man according to his strength." [^4]

24Some of the most absolute monarchs felt that way, such as the beloved King Benjamin, who told his people:

24"I am like as yourselves, subject to all manner of infirmities in body and mindÂ� Even I, whom ye call your king, am not better than ye yourselves are; for I am also of the dust.'' [^5]

24 Alma in refusing to be king said:

24"Ye shall not esteem one flesh above another." [^6]

24King Mosiah sent forth a proclamation among the Nephites: "There was a strict command throughout all of the churches that there should be not persecutions among them, that there should be an equality among all men; that they should let no pride nor haughtiness disturb their peace; that every man should esteem his neighbor as himself, laboring with their own hands for their support. [^7]

24Among the Nephite people there was a definite law protecting men in their freedom of speech, freedom of belief and freedom of conscience, but punishing crime. This is a point of view we espouse today, but there is still much discussion, (for example, Freedom of Inquiry and Expression, written by a group of experts and published by the American Academy of Political and Social Science in 1938.)

24Among the Nephites as recorded in the Book of Mormon "there was no law against a man's belief; for it was strictly contrary to the commands of God that there should be a law which should bring men on unequal groundsÂ�. If a man desired to serve God, it was his privilegeÂ� but if he did not there was no law to punish him. But if he murdered he was punished unto death; and if he robbed he was also punished; and if he stole he was also punishedÂ� for there was a law that men should be judged according to their crimesÂ�." [^8]

25The Jaredite people protected prophets against persecution, [^9] and Korihor, an anti-Christ of the Nephites, was given protection of the law, even though he strongly attacked beliefs which the majority of the people held to be most sacred. [^10] Yet the law gave him freedom to speak.

25The enforcement of the law against misconduct of various types gave protection to the Nephite people and discouraged crime, [^11] and the government was maintained. [^12]

25The problem of whether we should curtail and, if so, how far, those who would destroy our beliefs and government has been greatly argued in our day, for example in the Readers Digest [^13] and continues today.

25Because we accept a people as friends, and permit them to live in peace does not mean that we adopt their views or beliefs, or because a people are good it does not follow their beliefs are necessarily true. Nor does tolerance mean indifference to concepts we believe are wrong.

25As discussed previously in Chapter 2, the Book of Mormon teaches that God is trying to help all peoples, and that he is pleased by those who are righteous and obedient to him, irrespective of heritage. Yet somehow very few people in the world are free from the idea that they are superior, and that their people or race is superior. For example, a group of people called the Zoramites in the Book of Mormon [^14] thanked God that they were chosen of Him and not like their brethren.

26 The unconscious ideas that we all have can be illustrated by a story.

26A few years ago an American university professor was returning from a trip to the Orient. As the boat was nearing the Golden Gate at San Francisco, he was standing at the rail talking with a Chinese friend met on the voyage. Said the American: "Isn't it interesting to think of all the different races of mankind?"

26"Yes," said his Chinese friend, "there is the white race, the red race, the brown race, the black race, and then there are the flesh-colored people."

26Who of us does not think that his color, habits, beliefs and customs are the natural ones?

26Now let us look at a special case of tolerance and persecution.

26The Book of Mormon peoples included some descendents of Judah, but were mostly of Joseph and pre-Abraham. The book tells us:

26"Ye need not any longer hiss, nor spurn, nor make game of the Jews, nor any of the remnant of the House of Israel; for behold the Lord remembereth his covenant unto them, and he will do unto them according to that which he hath sworn. [^15]

26"Do (the Gentiles) remember the travels, and the labors, and the pains of the Jews, and their diligence unto me, in bringing forth salvation unto the Gentiles? O ye Gentiles, have ye remembered the Jews, mine ancient covenant people? Nay; but ye have cursed them, and have hated them, and have not sought to recover them. But behold, I will return all these things upon your own heads; for I the Lord have not forgotten my people. Thou fool, that shall say: A Bible, we have got a Bible, and we need no more Bible. Have ye obtained a Bible save it were by the Jews Â� What thank they the Jews for the Bible which they received from them? [^16]

26As Shakespeare says in the famous Shylock speech in The Merchant of Venice:

26 "Hath not a Jew eyes? Hath not a Jew hands, organs, dimensions, senses, affections, passions? fed with the same food, hurt with the same weapons, subject to the same diseases healed by the same means, warmed and cooled by the same winter and summer, as a Christian is?"

27Professor E. A. Hooton remarks, [^17] showing the foolishness of some even in our day:

27"One of the things that used to puzzle me as a child was the fact that we were taught to regard the children of Israel as a wholly magnificent people up to the time of the birth of Christ, and thereafter as a thoroughly ornery lot." A few pages later (p. 236) he points out that the dominant type of nose popularly associated with the Jews does not originate with Semitic-speaking peoples at all.

27Professor Anton J. Carlson has written: [^20]

27"All the present evidence indicates that the human beings now inhabiting the earth are one species, irrespective of their color and form. But this knowledge has not yet succeeded in eliminating the ancient and ever-recurring superstition or belief in this or that 'superior race.' We admit superior individuals and, for varying times, superior families, but I know of no clear evidence of superior groups within the same species, given the same environment, food and educational opportunities."

28It has been very apparent, particularly before and during the last war that those groups who were spreading anti-Semitic propaganda on the American continent, were linked to the same forces which had to be fought with bullets abroad, and were preaching the same type of intolerance.

28On the importance of tolerance and freedom, and the continuing fight to maintain them, Ham Fisher has written: [^21]

28"(We) must crush all efforts from within and without which are foreign to our 'promise of liberty for all' and make it treason to preach hatred of any man because of his race or creed within our borders, or 'democracy' and the 'Fourth of July' will become a memory.Â� Fight the breeders of hate and intolerance as you would a pestilence which destroy you."

28We recently fought a world war for liberty and freedom, yet in the daily press reports from the world, we can see the same old devilish forces at work to destroy the peace and prosperity of the human family.

28For a peaceful world we must be as successful as the Swiss have been within their own borders in promoting cultures and mutual tolerance of the groups of people which they have.

28We cannot have peace in the world today until we have tolerance, peace and understanding as exemplified by the great Nephite Alma, [^22] when he said:

28"There was no law against a man's belief; therefore, a man was punished only for the crimes he had done; therefore, all men were on equal grounds."
