Chapter 38

What the Doctrine and Covenants Teaches about Jesus Christ

The formal title of this chapter is "What the Doctrine and Covenants Teaches about Jesus Christ." However, the title could be "What the Lord Has Told Us about Himself, as Recorded in the Doctrine and Covenants." Such a title change would be appropriate because most of the information in the Doctrine and Covenants is spoken by the Savior in first person singular or in second person. He introduces himself as "your Lord," "your Savior," "the Great I Am," and so on. He says of himself, "I am merciful and gracious," "I will redeem you," "I will reveal all mysteries to you," and so on. While the name Doctrine and Covenants sounds formal and official, the Lord's choice of language is inviting and shows how close he wants to be to his Saints. He calls them his friends and talks to them "after the manner of their language" so they can understand. He tells them about himself and what their relationship is to him.

This chapter will review the following six areas:

Item 1: The Scriptures Are Primarily Testimony Rather Than History

The scriptures are by nature testimonies. Even the four Gospels, which we tend to think of as histories of the life of Jesus Christ, are actually testimonies rather than biographies.

Jesus lived on this earth for thirty-three years. The books of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John combined deal with only thirty-one days of those thirty-three years. That is, only thirty-one days of Jesus' mortal life are specified in the total of those four books. That would be a very meager biography or life history for anyone but especially for someone as important as the Savior. These records do, however, give considerable information as to who Jesus is, including the facts of his parentage and the circumstances of his birth; why he came to earth; what he taught; what he did; what he suffered; and the details of his Atonement, death, burial, Resurrection from the dead, and ascension into heaven. These are the things pertaining to a testimony. Other topics, such as eating habits, favorite foods, eye color, hair color, style of clothing, tone of voice, physical build, and the size of the house he lived in are relatively unimportant when compared to the doctrine.

The Scriptures Testify of Christ

The main purpose of the scriptures is to testify of Jesus Christ. Unfortunately, some people get so involved with their learning and scholarship that they tend to worship the product more than the Producer. Paul spoke of people who worship the creature more than the Creator (see Rom. 1:25). For example, a scientist may study the things that God has made: the world of nature, the earth, water, plants, minerals, trees, animals, and even man. While these are legitimate and honorable subjects of study, true religion, with loftier goals, shift's man's focus from nature up to nature's Author. In this way, people become acquainted not only with the creation but with the Creator. Religion involves God and his laws. It does not ignore the earth, but it does not limit itself to physical things.

Many of the Jewish rulers became so attached to their study of the scriptures that they worshiped learning about the scriptures. They failed to see that they were rejecting the very person of whom the scriptures were written and with whom they should have been acquainted by means of the scriptures. These people thought eternal life was found in the scriptures themselves and in the individual's knowledge and interpretation of the scriptures. They worshiped the law of God more than God. Ironically, at the same instant they were praising the scripture, they were rejecting the Giver and the Object of the scripture: Jesus. The Savior chided them in John 5:39-40: "Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life."

I think when we read this passage we often put the accent on the wrong words. Let me show you the way I think it was said. We have to start with the preceding verse. Jesus says to the Jewish rulers: "Ye have not [God's] word abiding in you: for whom he has sent, him ye believe not. Search the scriptures, for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me." In other words, he said the scriptures were the source for information about Christ.

The purpose of the scriptures is to testify of and teach about Christ. The Doctrine and Covenants does this and is as much a witness and testament of Jesus Christ as any of the standard works. The Doctrine and Covenants offers more than words; it also offers the Spirit.

Item 2: The Quiet Promises

I have found it helpful if the reader places his own name in the stead of the name printed in the revelation. This would not be acceptable in every case, but often it is just right, and it enables us to sense more fully what the Lord is saying.

The Lord makes hundreds of promises in the Doctrine and Covenants that are so casually stated that unless the reader is alerted to them, they almost go unnoticed. I call these the "quiet promises." They are "quiet" because they are usually only a few words. They are also personal and private, in contrast to the great public displays like the Second Coming, the Judgment, earthquakes, the burning of the earth by fire, and so on. Only by searching do we begin to "hear" some of the quiet promises. Yet when we do hear them, they almost overshadow the others in importance because the Lord is speaking to us of our personal and current daily needs. In some of these promises we may find the Lord for ourselves and discover that he is interested in us as individuals. While it is reassuring to know that the purposes of God will be accomplished for the masses of mankind, each of us needs individual attention and personal experiences with the Lord. It is in these quiet promises that we can substitute our own name for those given in the revelation. For example, the Lord said to Oliver Cowdery, "Seek not for riches, but for wisdom, and behold, the mysteries of God shall be unfolded unto you, and then shall you be made rich. Behold, he that hath eternal life is rich" (D&C 6:7). This could just as well have been said to any or all of us. Put your name in front of it, and the promise is yours. The Lord is saying it to you. And again, "Be faithful and diligent in keeping the commandments of God, and I will encircle thee in the arms of my love" (D&C 6:20). Other such references include D&C 6:12; 11:12; 19:23, 38-41; 38:39; 39:7, 23; 112:10.

Item 3: Names and Titles of Jesus

The Doctrine and Covenants gives at least sixty-six names and titles of Jesus Christ. For the most part these are things Jesus has said about himself in English. They are terms the Savior has used to identify himself in the revelations. These titles help us to have confidence that he can and will fulfill his promises. In the Doctrine and Covenants we have first-person, original, first-generation information from the Lord Jesus Christ himself. Item 4: Statements about the Mission of Jesus Christ

The revelations contained in the Doctrine and Covenants are a strong witness and testimony of Jesus Christ, carefully outlining his mission in the premortal, mortal, and postmortal times. The Doctrine and Covenants generates faith and confidence in the Savior. There is not a single word, not even a hint, that he is lacking in anything, either in knowledge or in power or in mercy or in love; not in character nor in physical or intellectual ability. Much of what the Doctrine and Covenants says about Jesus is also contained in one or more of the other standard works, but it is necessary that it be revealed anew in our own dispensation. These revelations are forthright declarations from the Lord himself, most of them in first-person language, telling of his mission, who he is, and what that means to us. We read in D&C 93:19 that the Lord has given us the revelations so we would know what we worship and how to worship. Here are some things that the Doctrine and Covenants says about Jesus Christ:

[^14] The Father bore an audible record that Jesus is the Son of God. [^15] After his baptism, he received all power from the Father. [^16] He knows all things. [^22] These Apostles will assist him in the final Judgment of mankind. [^23] He did no sin; [^24] he bled at every pore and made a perfect atonement through the shedding of his blood [^25] both for the Fall of Adam [^26] and, upon repentance, for the individual sins of mankind. [^27]

[^29] He was crucified, [^30] died, and rose from the dead on the third day [^31] with a tangible body. [^32] Between his death and Resurrection he entered the spirit world and organized a mission among the righteous to enable the gospel to be taught to the remainder of the spirits in the spirit world. [^33]

He now reigns in the heavens [^34] until he descends in a pillar of fire [^35] to reign on earth for a thousand years. [^36] He will judge every man according to his works and the deeds he has done. [^37] He requires that we take upon us his name, [^38] take up our cross and follow him, [^39] and pray to the Father in his name. He promises the righteous that he is in their midst, [^54] even though they may not be able to see him. All of the above concepts about the Savior are taught at least once in the Doctrine and Covenants. Let us now read some excerpts that define and illustrate the mission of Jesus Christ. D&C 20:17-28 gives a summary statement outlining several points in the plan of salvation, particularly that Christ's Atonement was necessary because of the Fall of Adam:

"By these things we know that there is a God in heaven, who is infinite and eternal, from everlasting to everlasting the same unchangeable God, the framer of heaven and earth, and all things which are in them;

"And that he created man, male and female, after his own image and in his own likeness, created he them;

"And gave unto them commandments that they should love and serve him, the only living and true God, and that he should be the only being whom they should worship.

"But by the transgression of these holy laws man became sensual and devilish, and became fallen man.

"Wherefore, the Almighty God gave his Only Begotten Son, as it is written in those scriptures which have been given of him.

"He suffered temptations but gave no heed unto them.

"He was crucified, died, and rose again the third day;

"And ascended into heaven, to sit down on the right hand of the Father, to reign with almighty power according to the will of the Father;

"That as many as would believe and be baptized in his holy name, and endure in faith to the end, should be saved—

"Not only those who believed after he came in the meridian of time, in the flesh, but all those from the beginning, even as many as were before he came, who believed in the words of the holy prophets, who spake as they were inspired by the gift of the Holy Ghost, who truly testified of him in all things, should have eternal life,

"As well as those who should come after, who should believe in the gifts and callings of God by the Holy Ghost, which beareth record of the Father and of the Son;

"Which Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are one God, infinite and eternal, without end."

D&C 18:10-13, 23-25, tells of the worth of souls:

"Remember the worth of souls is great in the sight of God;

"For, behold, the Lord your Redeemer suffered death in the flesh; wherefore he suffered the pain of all men, that all men might repent and come unto him.

"And he hath risen again from the dead, that he might bring all men unto him, on conditions of repentance.

"And how great is his joy in the soul that repenteth! . . .

"Behold, Jesus Christ is the name which is given of the Father, and there is none other name given whereby man can be saved;

"Wherefore, all men must take upon them the name which is given of the Father, for in that name shall they be called at the last day;

"Wherefore, if they know not the name by which they are called, they cannot have place in the kingdom of my Father."

D&C 19:1-5 explains who Jesus is and that he will judge the world:

"I am Alpha and Omega, Christ the Lord; yea, even I am he, the beginning and the end, the Redeemer of the world.

"I, having accomplished and finished the will of him whose I am, even the Father, concerning me—having done this that I might subdue all things unto myself—

"Retaining all power, even to the destroying of Satan and his works at the end of the world, and the last great day of judgment, which I shall pass upon the inhabitants thereof, judging every man according to his works and the deeds which he hath done.

"And surely every man must repent or suffer, for I, God, am endless.

"Wherefore, I revoke not the judgments which I shall pass, but woes shall go forth, weeping, wailing and gnashing of teeth, yea, to those who are found on my left hand."

In D&C 19:16-20 Jesus describes the pain he suffered in Gethsemane:

"For behold, I, God, have suffered these things for all, that they might not suffer if they would repent;

"But if they would not repent they must suffer even as I;

"Which suffering caused myself, even God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to suffer both body and spirit—and would that I might not drink the bitter cup, and shrink—

"Nevertheless, glory be to the Father, and I partook and finished my preparations unto the children of men.

"Wherefore, I command you again to repent, lest I humble you with my almighty power; and that you confess your sins, lest you suffer these punishments of which I have spoken, of which in the smallest . . . degree you have tasted at the time I withdrew my Spirit."

D&C 29:40-48 states again the relation between the fall of Adam and the atonement of Jesus Christ:

"Wherefore, it came to pass that the devil tempted Adam, and he partook of the forbidden fruit and transgressed the commandment, wherein he became subject to the will of the devil, because he yielded unto temptation.

"Wherefore, I, the Lord God, caused that he should be cast out from the Garden of Eden, from my presence, because of his transgression, wherein he became spiritually dead, which is the first death, even that same death which is the last death, which is spiritual, which shall be pronounced upon the wicked when I shall say: Depart, ye cursed.

"But, behold I say unto you that I, the Lord God, gave unto Adam and unto his seed, that they should not die as to the temporal death, until I, the Lord God, should send forth angels to declare unto them repentance and redemption, through faith on the name of mine Only Begotten Son.

Item 5: What Does the Doctrine and Covenants Tell Us about Jesus That We Would Not Know if We Had Only the Other Scriptures?

Let us now consider a few things that the Lord has revealed about himself in the Doctrine and Covenants that we would not otherwise know. That is, these are things not revealed about Jesus in the other standard works, or they are so dim that no one could understand them without the help of the Doctrine and Covenants. First of all let us reaffirm the fact that the Doctrine and Covenants testifies of the truth of the Bible and the truth of the Book of Mormon and all the things that are contained therein about the Savior. The Doctrine and Covenants contains several statements linking the Atonement of Christ to the Fall of Adam (see 20:16-26; 29:34-46), which is also good Bible, Book of Mormon, and Pearl of Great Price doctrine. But in addition there are the following things about Jesus that I think are quite unique to the Doctrine and Covenants. In most cases these things are exclusive to the Doctrine and Covenants, but in some instances they are clarifications and extensions of things that are mentioned vaguely in the Bible.

The Doctrine and Covenants Is a Source of Doctrinal Knowledgeabout Jesus Christ and the Godhead

The Doctrine and Covenants, in company with the Book of Mormon, Pearl of Great Price, and Joseph Smith Translation of the Bible, will answer every doctrinal question having to do with the nature of Christ and the Godhead that was raised by the Nicene Council and every council held by apostate Christianity since that time. We have perhaps often thought of the Doctrine and Covenants as a source of administrative directives and laws for Church government and missed the doctrinal message and witness for Jesus Christ that it truly is.

Item 6: The Prophet Joseph Smith Is the Greatest Witness for Jesus Christ

Inasmuch as the Prophet Joseph Smith was the recipient of nearly all the revelations in the Doctrine and Covenants, plus many others not in the Doctrine and Covenants (for example, the First Vision), it naturally follows that Joseph Smith is the greatest witness for and greatest Apostle of Jesus Christ who has lived on this earth in the past 1,900 years.

[^3] The Doctrine and Covenants gives many names and titles for Jesus Christ. Alpha and Omega (19:1) He saves animals and other forms of life, in addition to men (see 29:23-25; 77:2-3; 101:23-24). 93:15.
[^4] Specific statements in the Doctrine and Covenants declare the mission of Jesus Christ. Alphus and Omegus (95:17) He gives revelations "after the manner of the language" of the people or of the prophet who receives the revelation (see 1:24). 38:3; 76:24; 93:9-10.
[^5] The Doctrine and Covenants reveals information about Jesus that cannot be found in any other standard work. Beginning and the End (38:1) This earth is destined to become a celestial kingdom (see 88:17-19, 25-26) and will be Christ's (see 130:9). That is, he will dwell on the earth and will govern it. 43:34; 88:42-63.
[^6] The Prophet Joseph Smith is the greatest witness for Christ in 1,900 years. Beloved Son (93:15) Jesus will return to earth and partake of the sacrament with the righteous of all dispensations (see 27:4). This is alluded to in a very sketchy manner in the New Testament but not with the clarity of the Doctrine and Covenants. 38:1.
[^7] Bridegroom (33:17-18) Jesus will be clothed in red garments at his second coming (see 133:48). This is alluded to in Isa. 63 but is so unclear that the meaning is lost. 38:1.
[^8] Christ (38:4) The Savior will not come in the form of a woman or as a man traveling on the earth (see 49:22). 95:7.
[^9] Christ the Lamb (76:85) There are at least five titles or names applied to Jesus in the Doctrine and Covenants that are not given elsewhere: Son Ahman (see 78:20); Lord of Sabaoth, which means Creator of the first day (see 95:7); stem of Jesse (see 113:1-2); stone of Israel (see 50:44); and Endless (see 19:10). Being Creator of the first day probably also has something to do with his creation of many worlds. 88:42.
[^10] Christ the Lord (19:1) The revelations show that Jesus is interested in real estate purchases, the size of buildings, the number of rooms, printing presses, temples, hotels, jails, books, curriculum, schools, tanneries, chariots, mules, canals, and other daily things, including proper food and drink and the fragrance and beauty of flowers (see 59:18-20). He is not only interested in Jerusalem, Rome, Egypt, Babylon, Corinth, and Athens but in Palmyra, Cumorah, Kirtland, Far West, Liberty, Adam-ondi-Ahman, Nauvoo, Independence, Salt Lake City, New York, Albany, and Boston. 19:24.
[^11] Creator of the first day (95:7) Jesus' visit to the spirit world between his death and Resurrection is explained, outlining what he did and did not do there (see 138). Contrary to what one would think from 1 Pet. 3:18-20 or even D&C 76:73, we learn that Jesus did not go among the wicked to teach them personally. Detailed knowledge of Jesus' spirit world ministry is found only in the Doctrine and Covenants and not in any other standard work. 93:4, 11.
[^12] Creator of worlds (38:3; 76:24; 93:9-10) There is an emphasis in the Doctrine and Covenants on Jesus' knowledge, majesty, and greatness. He says he looked "upon the wide expanse of eternity, and all the seraphic hosts of heaven before the world was made." He also said, "all things are present before mine eyes" and I "knoweth all things" (38:1-2). 93:12-13.
[^13] Deliverer from death (138:23) Jesus is the God of Enoch and his brethren (see 45:11) and has taken the Zion of Enoch to his own bosom (see 38:4). 93:7.
[^14] Endless (19:10) Jesus received a fulness of truth, even all truth (see 93:26). 93:15.
[^15] Eternal King (128:23) There is an indication that Jesus was born in the flesh in this world on 6 April (see 20:1). 93:15.
[^16] First and the Last (110:4) The Bible and Book of Mormon speak of Jesus as the light of the world, but the Doctrine and Covenants extends that thought further and says he is "a light which cannot be hid in darkness" (14:9). 93:17.
[^17] Firstborn (93:21) The concept of going from grace to grace and receiving grace for grace, which is barely hinted at in John 1:16-17 is extended in the Doctrine and Covenants to show that not only does man progress that way but Jesus progressed to perfection the same way (see 93:12-20). 38:2.
[^18] God (19:16-18) The Light of Christ permeates the immensity of space (see 88:7-13) and is the light, the controller, and the power of the sun, stars, moon, and the planets. 133:26.
[^19] God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (136:21) Christ has promised that all who keep his commandments and obey his voice shall see his face (see 88:67-68; 93:1). 20:22.
[^20] God of Enoch (45:11) The celestial law is the law of Christ (see 88:21). That is, the fulness of the gospel is the law of Christ and is designed to get people into the celestial kingdom. The gospel is the celestial law. 20:22.
[^21] Good Shepherd (50:44) Jesus and his Father established the law of the holy priesthood before the foundation of the world (see 132:11, 28). This law of the holy priesthood governs all of the ordinances pertaining to salvation and exaltation in the celestial kingdom. 62:1.
[^22] Great I Am (38:1) He gave a revelation authorizing and explaining the nature of celestial and plural marriage and justifying it as a righteous practice under controlled conditions (see 132). 45:8.
[^23] Holy One (78:16) He commanded that there should be a school for the Brethren called the School of the Prophets (see 88:127). 29:12.
[^24] Holy One of Zion (78:15) He commanded that temples should be built to his name for the salvation of the living and the dead (see 124). 45:4.
