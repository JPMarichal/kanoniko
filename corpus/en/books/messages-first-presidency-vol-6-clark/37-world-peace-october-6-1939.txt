According to the files of President J. Reuben Clark, Jr., President bark circulated copies of the Church Section of the Deseret News containing this message of the First Presidency to leading and influential officials of the government of the United States. The accompanying talk of President Clark on "America As The Peacemaker" and "Foundations of Peace" by Apostle John A. Widtsoe were also circulated to these officials by President Clark. The entire Church Section for October 14, 1939, should be read and studied in connection with this message.

Among other prominent governmental officials President Clark sent copies to the Department of State of the United States, of which he was a former officer; to Senators William E. Borah and Hiram Johnson, of the United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations; and to former President of the United States, Herbert Hoover.

The correspondence ensuing from this submission, preserved in the files of President Clark, seems to give ample evidence that not only the short message of the First Presidency read by President Grant (but in all likelihood written by President Clark) but also the addresses of President Clark and Apostle Widtsoe were considered at the time as the official position of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints on war and peace.

The cover letter which uniformly accompanied President Clark's submission of printed copies of these documents to national leaders read as follows:

"Thinking that you might find some interest in it as representing the Church view toward the present war, I enclose herewith the Church Section of our Church paper for Saturday, October 14, which contains an announcement made by the First Presidency, as also addresses delivered by the President of the Church, his two counselors, and a member of the Council of the Twelve.

The response of Senator William E. Borah to these documents was typical of many others. He wrote November 7, 1939, on the letterhead of the Senate, Committee on Foreign Relations.

"Dear Clark:

"Thank you for the Deseret News with your and other speeches in it. I am always, Clark, pleased and always benefited by your addresses upon these subjects, and I hope you will not permit me to miss any of them. I thought your address particularly fine by reason of the magnificent moral tone one finds in it. I still hold to the faith, as it seems you do, that there was Divine guidance in the building of this government and in the shaping of its policies in its young constructive era.

"Let me hear from you often. Please remember me to President Grant and other friends in Salt Lake. "Very respectfully, (signed) "William E. Borah."

FIRST PRESIDENCY'S MESSAGE ON WORLD PEACE

The long-threatened and dreaded war has broken out. Its end and fruition await now the measure of God's infinite wisdom, justice, and mercy.

The divine law on the taking of human life was proclaimed at Sinai and in the Garden. This law, we declare, is equally binding upon men and upon nations. It embraces war.

We further declare that God is grieved by war and that He will hold subject to the eternal punishments of His will those who wage it unrighteously.

Appeal to Leaders

We affirm that all international controversies may be settled by pacific means if nations will but deal unselfishly and righteously one with another, We appeal to the leaders of all nations and to the people themselves thus to mend and adjust their differences, lest the vials of God's wrath be poured out upon the earth, for He has said He will visit his wrath upon the wicked without measure.

We call the unrighteous of the world to repentance-a forsaking of sin and a returning to righteousness, for the Lord has said:

"I, the Lord, am angry with the wicked. I am withholding My Spirit from the inhabitants of the earth.

"I have sworn in My wrath and decreed wars upon the face of the earth, and the wicked shall slay the wicked and fear shall come upon every man." (D&C 63:32-33.)

Condemn War

We condemn all war's foul brood-avarice, greed, misery, want, disease, cruelty, hate, inhumanity, savagery, death.

We earnestly implore all members of the Church to love their brethren and sisters, and all peoples whoever and wherever they are; to banish hate from their lives, to fill their hearts with charity, patience, long-suffering, and forgiveness. The Master said:

"Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good for them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you." (Matt. 5:44.)

We ask the Lord to overrule the plans and designs of man that this war shall not spread to countries not now involved, and especially that America shall escape the material and spiritual ravages of war.

We humbly pray God to bring to all bereft and grieving mothers the sweet consolation of His Spirit, to the widow robbed of her helpmeet a faith that God will help her in her lonely struggle for a livelihood for her children, to those fatherless children a will to help their mother in her fight for their welfare and existence, and to peoples everywhere an increased desire and determination to "renounce war and proclaim peace, and seek diligently to turn the hearts of the children to their fathers and the hearts of the fathers to the children; . . . lest," saith the Lord, "I come and smite the whole earth with a curse, and all flesh be consumed before me."

We pray that the spirit now raging in men's hearts of hate, of exploitation, of desire to dominate may be supplanted by the spirit of reconciliation and forgiveness, that in obedience to principles of righteousness and of justice this war without further bloodshed and suffering may be brought to an early close.
