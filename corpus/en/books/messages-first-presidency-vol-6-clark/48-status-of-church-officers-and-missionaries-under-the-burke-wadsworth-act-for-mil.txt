Status of Church Officers and Missionaries under the Burke-Wadsworth Act for Military Service, December 31, 1940

1940-December 31-Original circular letter, L.D.S. Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.

After a furious debate in the United States Congress, "the first peace-time draft in American history," the Burke-Wadsworth Act, was passed by Congress and signed by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on September 16, 1940. This letter reports the attempts to clarify the status of L.D.S. Church officials and missionaries under the provisions of the act. It states that the Church will not knowingly call young men on missions with a view to having them avoid the prescribed service to their country.

Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints Office of the First Presidency Salt Lake City, Utah December 31, 1940 Presidents of Stakes Bishops of Wards Presidents of Branches Presidents of Missions

Dear Brethren:

On December 6th we sent a formal communication to Major H. A. Rich, Acting State Director, United States Selective Service, Utah State Headquarters, in which we set out briefly the views of the Church concerning the meaning which should be attached to the phrases "regular minister of religion" and "duly ordained minister of religion" as those phrases are used in the Selective Service Act and Regulations.

In that letter we listed the following as falling within our understanding of the meaning of the phrases quoted:

The First Presidency

Quorum of the Twelve Apostles

First Seven Presidents of Seventies

Presiding Bishopric of the Church

Presidents of Stakes and their Counselors

Bishops of Wards and their Counselors

Presidents of Branches and their Counselors, in Stakes and Missions.

Regularly ordained missionaries, with formal certificates from the First Presidency, who are performing missions principally in areas away from the regular Church organizations and in the regular missions of the Church, so long as they are in their fields of labor.

As to the missionaries it was stated in our letter to Major Rich:

"The Church feels that while these young men are performing these regular missions, from the time that they are called until they are released Â�. (a period of from two to three years), they properly belong within the exempt classification indicated by the ActÂ�.

"The Church does not ask that these men be exempt from the operation of the Selective Service Act after they have been regularly released from their missions; the Church feels that they then become fully responsible under the law for service in our Military forces.

"It may be said that the Church has adopted the principle that it will not knowingly call young men on missions with a view to having them avoid service, nor will it prolong for that purpose the regular period of service for such missionaries."

We are now in receipt of a letter from Major Rich which informs us that he has taken this matter up with the law officers in Washington, who have felt that they could not make a ruling at this time on our request covering these various groups, inasmuch as the law precludes blanket classifications, and that the State Directors should make individual rulings on each case as the facts of each case are presented to the Board of Appeals for review.

It was suggested that pursuant to this ruling the persons falling within the classifications indicated above are in a position to claim deferment as ordained ministers and that if their claim for deferment is disallowed by the local boards they are in a position to prosecute an appeal to the Board of Appeals.

It is our understanding that already the Utah State Headquarters has issued instructions to Local Boards within the State of Utah that missionaries are within the purview of the law relating to ordained ministers.

It was pointed out in our letter of December 6th to Major Rich that the great bulk of our officers, with the exception of our missionaries, are probably not covered by the present age levels for registration; that is, "between the ages of 21 to 36."

Faithfully yours, HEBER J. GRANT, J. REUBEN CLARK, JR., DAVID O. MCKAY, First Presidency.
