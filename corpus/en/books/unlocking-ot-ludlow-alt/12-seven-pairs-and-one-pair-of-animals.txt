(Gen. 7:2)

Moses recorded that Noah took one pair of most animals, but that he took seven pairs of the "clean" animals. These would be clean animals according to Moses' definition. (See Lev. 11.)

These domesticated animals would be necessary for Noah's family to provide sacrifices and sustenance after the flood, and yet they would also become a prey to the wild animals released after the ark was opened. The larger numbers of these "clean" animals would provide food for both man and beast.

Also, the number seven was symbolic of wholeness. This number showed up repeatedly in Noah's episode--he not only collected seven pairs of clean animals, but he had seven days to load the ark, it floated seven months before it rested on Mount Ararat, and Noah waited seven days between each time he released a bird.
