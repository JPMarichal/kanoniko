(Gen. 16:2)

Numerous ancient Near Eastern records demonstrate the practice of a barren wife (usually after at least seven years without a child) giving a second wife to her husband. Even in a patriarchal society the husband did not automatically have the right to acquire a second wife without the first wife's permission. The fact that Sarah set such a noble example with this practice led to perhaps the only place in the scriptures where a law was named after a woman. We read about the laws of different kings and the law of Moses, but the law of plural marriage is called the "law of Sarah." (D&C 132:65.)
