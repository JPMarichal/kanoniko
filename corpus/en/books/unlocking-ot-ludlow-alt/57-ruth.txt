The story of Ruth occurred during the period of the judges (about 1150 B.C.) but was not written in its present form until centuries later (about 500-400 B.C.). According to early Jewish tradition as recorded in the Talmud, the story was originally written by Samuel. In its present form, it is a compact, complete literary work. It has a plot, identifiable characters, suspense, and a moral.

Throughout the story, Naomi and Ruth faced danger in the form of drought, death, and hunger. Finally, they rested secure in the home of Boaz. More importantly, their emotional and social welfare were rewarded through their posterity. Spiritual values were also gained when God's hand in the life of this one family was made manifest. An Israelite and a Moabite had their faith rewarded and they became models for a righteous community and links in the noble heritage of David, Solomon, and Jesus. (See BD "Ruth.")
