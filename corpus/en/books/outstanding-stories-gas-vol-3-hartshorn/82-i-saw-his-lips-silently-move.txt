Leon R. Hartshorn, Outstanding Stories by General Authorities, vol. 3 ( 1974)

"I Saw His Lips Silently Move"

A few years ago, I was having dinner in a cafeteria in Los Angeles. While I was eating, a boy carrying a tray sat down at the table next to me. My attention was attracted to him because it was rather unusual to see an unaccompanied boy in such a place.

He placed his napkin on his lap, and then he took the gum he was chewing and stuck it under the table. As I saw him do this, I wondered what kind of training this young man had had. Then suddenly he bowed his head in the attitude of prayer. I saw his lips silently move. How quickly my feelings changed! How proud I was of that young man. How proud I am of you I have been in your cafeterias and I have seen you do the same thing—not the gum, but the prayer that was offered before you ate.

I remember on one occasion when President George Albert Smith was visiting the welfare ranch in Southern California. Eddie, the son of our ranch superintendent, took a tray of food over to the guest cottage for the President. After coming out of the cottage, Eddie sat down on the step. He heard the President, who was all alone, ask the Lord to bless the food and express his appreciation for it and for those who had sent it. This made a great impression on Eddie, and it does on all of us, to think that the Prophet of the Lord talks with God about little things, as well as the great problems of the kingdom.

"Pray Always," BYU Speeches of the Year, October 15, 1963, p. 6.
