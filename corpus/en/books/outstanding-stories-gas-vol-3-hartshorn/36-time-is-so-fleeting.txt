Leon R. Hartshorn, Outstanding Stories by General Authorities, vol. 3 ( 1974)

"Time Is So Fleeting"

Some time ago I read a story that had a profound influence upon me. I cannot recall its source. . . . Anyway, it was about a young man who had committed a serious crime and was languishing in jail, awaiting execution in payment for the crime (if that will pay it). During his last night on earth, of course, he did not sleep. Dawn found him standing at the window of his cell, holding the bars, looking out. He could see over the prison walls and behold the countryside around about and the sky.

As the sky brightened with the dawning of the day, a changed seemed to come over him—a change of values, a change of appreciation for what he had hitherto considered commonplace, unworthy of his notice. He observed the rich, brown earth, and the leaves of the trees and the bright green fields and the sky. He gripped the bars of his cell and stared intently at the scene which lay before him, but which had been unnoticed through his lifetime. Tears came to his eyes, and he realized for the first time the glory and the magnificence of the world about him. As the muffled footsteps of the jailors could be heard on their way to the cell, he still stood at his window, transfixed by the unspeakable beauty of the dawning of the day and the rising of the sun.

Of course, the beauty and the wonder of it had been there all during his twenty-three years. He had simply let time run out on him until now it was too late to fully enjoy it. I doubt if he is the only one who has failed to make the most of his opportunities and appreciate while there is still time to do so.

"Time is so fleeting," said Hans Christian Andersen, "that if we do not remember God and learn of him in our youth, age may find us incapable of thinking of him."

"The Endowment Time," BYU Speeches of the Year, March 14, 1962, pp. 3-4.
