Leon R. Hartshorn, Outstanding Stories by General Authorities, vol. 3 ( 1974)

"Mother Made Every Shirt I Wore"

My parents taught me how important the family is. Father was a hard worker and a busy man, but he always had time for his boys. He was a carpenter and learned the trade from grandfather.

Our father taught us to work with our hands as he built houses. We didn't have any girls in the family, so we also learned to iron clothes, and wash dishes and mop floors, and do a lot of girl-kinds of things, because our parents believed in work. They taught us how to work.

We didn't have very much. Mother made every shirt I wore until I went to high school. My first bike was used, and without fenders. Dad got new tires and painted it for me.

Mother fixed my lunch for school because we could not afford to buy lunch. The sandwiches were made with homemade bread, and it seemed like they were about three inches thick. In high school I used to sit on the curb and eat, and wish that I could at least have thin slices of store bread. Now I long for that good homemade bread.

We didn't go on vacations often, but we did go for rides together. We were taught that we could do a lot of things that weren't expensive. We were taught that we could be a strong family on little money. The greatest thing we had in our home was love, and I think that was the catalyst for it all.

Church News, section of the Deseret News, April 22, 1972, p. 14.
