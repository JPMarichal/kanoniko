Leon R. Hartshorn, Outstanding Stories by General Authorities, vol. 3 ( 1974)

ELDER JAMES A. CULLIMORE

Biographical Sketch

Elder James A. Cullimore, Assistant to the Council of the Twelve, is a native of Lindon, Utah; he was a funiture business executive and Church leader in Oklahoma City before moving to Salt Lake City for his present assignment in 1966.

Elder Cullimore's assignment as an Assistant to the Council has been as supervisor of the missions in Asia.

Having lived in Oklahoma City for some thirty years, he served as a branch president for fourteen years and as president of the West Oklahoma District of the Church for nine years. He became the first president of the Oklahoma Stake when it was organized in 1960. A short time later, he was called by the First Presidency as president of the Central British Mission with headquarters in Warwickshire, England.

Elder Cullimore was born January 17, 1906, to Albert Lorenzo and Luella Keetch Cullimore. His great-grandfather was an English convert to the Church in 1837.

As a young man he served a mission in California. He was graduated from Brigham Young University, where he was student body president. He did graduate work at the New York School of Retailing in 1931. That same year he married Grace Gardner in the Salt Lake Temple.

He began his furniture business career as a buyer for Gimbel Brothers in New York, and later the Mandel Brothers in Chicago. He started his own furniture business in 1946.

The Cullimores have three children: a son, Kelvyn, in Miami, Oklahoma, and two married daughters, Luella, in Calgary, Alberta, Canada, and Nancy, who lives in Sandy, Utah.
