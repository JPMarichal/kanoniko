ELDER STERLING W. SILL

Biographical Sketch

Elder Sterling W. Sill, Assistant to the Council of the Twelve Apostles, is a prominent businessman, educator, popular speaker, and prolific writer. He is a retired insurance executive.

He is the author of fourteen books on religious and social themes. Since April 17, 1960, he has been the speaker over Radio KSL on the longest continuous program on the air, "Sunday Evening on Temple Square," which is now in its forty-fifth year (it began May 22, 1926). He is the speaking voice of two weekly radio broadcasts which together are released over more than 370 United States radio stations.

Titles of Sterling W. Sill volumes include: Leadership I, Leadership II, The Glory of the Sun, The Upward Reach, The Law of the Harvest, The Way of Success, What Doth It Profit, The Miracle of Personality, The Quest for Excellence, The Power of Believing, The Three Infinites, The Strength of Great Possessions, Making the Most of Yourself, and The Keys of the Kingdom.

Elder Sill served for eleven years as a member of the Board of Regents of the University of Utah and for two terms was chairman of the board. The esteem in which he is held is indicated in the action of the university in awarding him an honorary LL.D. degree and naming a building, the Sterling W. Sill Home Living Center, in his honor.

He was born March 31, 1903, in Layton, Utah, to Joseph A. and Marietta Welling Sill. He received his early training in Davis County schools, Utah State University, and the University of Utah. He taught school for two years.

Elder Sill entered the insurance business in 1927, rising to assistant manager of the Salt Lake City office of the New York Life Insurance Office in 1932 and manager in 1933. Under his direction the intermountain general office became the largest office of the entire company in actual sales volume. On January 1, 1940, he was appointed inspector of agencies for seven western states. On September 27, 1934, he was awarded the C.L.U. degree from the College of Life Underwriters in Philadelphia, the first Utahn to earn this degree.

He was president of the Salt Lake Exchange Club in 1938 and of the Salt Lake Association of Life Underwriters, 1935-36. In 1960, he was awarded the Carnegie Hero Medal by the Carnegie Hero Foundation of Philadelphia.

In Church activity he filled a mission in the Southern States, 1924-26. He was appointed to the high council of the North Davis Stake in May 1931, and served until he moved to Salt Lake City in January 1933. Other Church positions include: bishop of the Garden Park Ward, 1936-46; high council of Bonneville Stake, 1946-51; member of the Sunday School general board, 1951-54. He was called to serve as an Assistant to the Council of the Twelve April 6, 1954.

He married Doris Mary Thornley September 4, 1929, in the Salt Lake Temple. They have two sons and one daughter.
