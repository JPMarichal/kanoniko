James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 2 ( 1965)

Instructions to Presidents of Stakes and others, April 9, 1879

1879-April 9-Original document in L. John Nuttall Papers, Brigham Young University Library, Special Collections.

President Taylor had met the Stake Presidents in his office for a formal instruction meeting in February 23, 1878. Here again the Stake Presidents were called together for instructions in their duties. Some of the same instructions and background of the previous year are here repeated. There had evidently been some changes in the Presidencies of Stakes in the interim, judging by the two lists of those present.

More emphasis is laid in this message than in the previous instructions on handling cases of disloyalty to the Church. The pace of the anti-polygamy persecution was beginning to step up and the lines of loyalty were becoming more sharply defined.

Historically, it is interesting that in these instructions to Stake Presidents, President Taylor seemingly is suggesting several "outs" for brethren who might be prosecuted for polygamy. He suggests as possible alternatives: (a) being called on a mission; (b) settlement in Arizona; (c) settlement in Mexico until Utah can become a State in the Union.

Also significant is President Taylor's instructions that "cast iron rules suitable for all cases" cannot be laid down, but the Stake Presidents were to "Get the Holy Ghost; follow its teachings, by and by it will become a principle of revelation, and you will have wisdom given you in all cases."

INSTRUCTIONS to Presidents of Stakes and others, at a meeting held in the office of President John Taylor.

The Presidents of the Stakes and others met in the President's Office.

Present,-President John Taylor, C. C. Rich, E. Snow, F. D. Richards, Geo. Q. Cannon, B. Young, J. F. Smith, Moses Thatcher; Elders D. H. Peery, Geo. Teasdale, W. H. Dame, Abram Hatch, Jos. E. Taylor, C. O. Card, F. Spencer, P. D. Lyman, Ed. Partridge, J. N. Hinckley, J. B. Maiben, W. G. Smith, M. D. Hammond, A. O. Smoot, W. W. Cluff, A. M. Cannon, W. R. Smith, E. A. Box, A. Call, F. M. Lyman, T. J. Jones, Henry Sunt, H. H. Cluff, Ward E. Pack, Geo. Reynolds, J. R. Murdock, W. B. Preston, John Morgan.

Meeting opened by singing: "Come Let Us Anew" &c.

Prayer by Elder Geo. Q. Cannon.

Prest. Taylor said,-The organizations that we have now, are quite similar to those that existed when there was only one stake of Zion. In those days the First Presidency were also the presidency of that one stake. The present organizations are similar, with the exception that one extended to all the world, whilst the jurisdiction of the present presidents only extend to their several stakes. You, as presidents, preside over every organization of the priesthood in your stake as Joseph Smith did in all the world. He spoke of the responsibility of the calling of the presidents of Stakes, and of the important part, politically and otherwise, the Saints will shortly have to take. We have to take the lead in all things; Israel must be the head; to do so, we must be united and sustain the Kingdom of God.

There has been a little tremor of late among some, that owing to the lack of water fearing that our crops this year would be short. He dare prophecy in the name of the Lord: if the Saints would be prayerful and faithful and exercise mighty faith, they would have all the rain that was needed. Warned the brethren against being tyrannical in their course as presidents among the people, but counseled them to be fatherly and godlike in their administrations, and Heaven's blessings would attend them. He then referred to the anti-polygamy law, and said, We cannot afford to let go any of the principles Heaven has revealed, neither need we be blatant nor defiant. Shall we stop keeping God's commandments? No. But we must take a prudent course. If people would get married he did not know that he was going to try to help it. He much preferred marriage to whoredom, even if whoredom was according to law. Thought he would get out a form for the presidents to observe when recommending persons for plural marriage. Wished in future that recommends for plural wives be signed by the Presidents of Stakes. If members of the Church go to law with one another, it becomes your duty to labor with them, and if they do not withdraw their suits, cut them off the Church. Spoke of the advantages of the Church Councils (High Councils, Bishop's Council, &c) over those of the land; but if men carry out things fraudulently the presidents have a perfect right to cut them off the Church, then they can be proceeded against by the law. He should hold the presidents of Stakes responsible if they gave men recommends who were not subject to the order of the Church, who would not pay their tithing, or who were transgressors. Some brethren had taken wives lately; he did not wish to publish it everywhere, and if there was any chance of their being prosecuted, he advised the brethren to give a hint to "get," in a wise and prudent way. We might give them a mission, or they might go over into Arizona, or further still, into Mexico; and by and by we may be a State, and then if brethren are persecuted in the Territory, let them come home into our State. Spoke of engaging certain lawyers and paying them out of a general defense fund raised by the Church, and not let every man who might be prosecuted bear all the weight and expense incidental to a prosecution.

Spoke of Statistical and Financial reports that were needed at this office. Said, he intended to instruct the Clerks in the Trustee in Trust office to notify each bishop when orders were drawn against them, at the Church office, so that they could be kept advised of the orders standing against the amount of produce &c, they had on hand.

Bro. Cannon referred to Statistical reports; said they need only be read at Quarterly Stake Conferences twice a year. Spoke of the great necessity of all accounts being accurate.

Bro. E. Snow spoke of the necessity of harmony between the president of Stakes and Bishop Hunter's Agent, and the desirability of the president of Stake making himself well acquainted with the financial affairs connected with the tithing matters in his stake, so that when any communications are made to the general authorities they can be made conjointly by the president and the Bishop's Agent.

In answer to a question of Prest. W. G. Smith, Prest. Taylor said, that twice a year was often enough to present the general authorities of the Church at the Stake Conferences.

E. Snow,-Instructed the brethren that it was not necessary to await the action of a conference to ordain a man an Elder, Priest, Teacher or Deacon. The action of the Ward, or of the bishop, was all that was necessary in these cases. It was different in the cases of ordination to the Seventies and High Priests Quorums. These required the consideration of the Apostles.

Bro. Jos. F. Smith read a blank that he suggested be given by the presidents of Stakes, in cases of plural marriage, and the signature of the president instead of the bishop would signify to the proper persons of what was intended. One to be given to the lady, the other to the brother. For instance:-

Bro. Smith also explained that where men broke the law of the land they should be handled by the law of the land; the Church can deal with them afterwards.

Prest. Taylor said, in answer to Prest. Murdock, that in all ordinary cases, ordinations should be made with the sanction of the ward or branch, with which the brother was connected; also that delinquents in the payment of local option school taxes should be taken before the bishop, and the matter be adjusted by his court, it would depend upon the circumstances of the case; if the bishop should proceed against such a man for his standing, if he still refused. Could not lay down cast iron rules suitable for all cases. Get the Holy Ghost; follow its teachings, by and by it will become a principle of revelation, and you will have wisdom given you in all cases.

In answer to a question he said, It was the privilege of a bishop to suspend from standing a man holding the Melchisedec priesthood, who was doing wrong, and then report him to his quorum or to the High Council, the Lesser Priesthood, or lay members who are immediately under his control, he could cut off for transgression, the quorums can also withdraw fellowship from their own members but cannot sever from the Church; the High Council attends to that duty.

Dismissed with prayer by Elder B. Young.

A short meeting of the Council of the Apostles was held immediately after the above meeting.
