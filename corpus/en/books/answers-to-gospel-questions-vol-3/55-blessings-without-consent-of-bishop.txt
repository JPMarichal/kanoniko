Joseph Fielding Smith, Answers to Gospel Questions, vol. 3 ( 1960)

52

Blessings without Consent of Bishop

Question: "In our adult class we were discussing the right to officiate in the priesthood and the necessity of obtaining the consent of the bishop before performing certain ordinances. Some of the members of the class were of the opinion that in cases of emergency an elder could name a child or bless the sick without first obtaining permission from his bishop. One father told of his going to a distant town from his own stake to bless and name his new grandchild at the request of his daughter. Does the Church permit the right to officiate in these ordinances without consent from such local authority?"

Answer: If an elder in the Church could not perform any duty such as administering to the sick, naming a child when it faces death shortly after birth, or give comfort to someone in distress until he had received the consent from his bishop there would be confusion, and those in distress could die without such blessings before such consent could be given.

Any father who holds the priesthood could bless and name his own child without consulting the bishop of his ward, but the order of the Church would require that the child be taken before the elders of the Church for a blessing, which would he done in the regular ward meeting so that the records of the Church would be complete.

No one could refuse a grandfather, who holds the Melchizedek Priesthood, from blessing his own grandchild. However, afterwards it would be taken before the meeting where the record is made.

When, in an emergency, a blessing is given to a baby that is not expected to live until it can be taken to the meeting, the elder who blessed the child should report the facts to the bishop of the ward.
