Joseph Fielding Smith, Answers to Gospel Questions, vol. 3 ( 1960)

49

Withdrawal of the Priesthood

Question: "Whenever a man holding the priesthood has grieved the Spirit of the Lord and it is withdrawn from him to the extent that we may say 'Amen to the priesthood or the authority of that man,' and the individual in question is then asked to ordain someone else to the priesthood, does the person upon whom hands are laid actually receive the same?"

Answer: If it is possible for this ordinance to be performed by a faithful man, it is well. However, circumstances may be such that there is no possibility for the ordinance to be performed again. Then what can be done? The Lord has given us a key to such a situation in the Doctrine and Covenants. It is as follows:

And this ye shall know assuredly—that there is none other appointed unto you to receive commandments and revelations until he be taken, if he abide in me.

But verily, verily, I say unto you, that none else shall be appointed unto this gift except it be through him; for if it be taken from him he shall not have power except to appoint another in his stead. (D. & C. 43:3-4.)

In such cases, and as you know we have had them, the authority possessing the keys must ratify what has been done, because the guilty person has not been excommunicated. Therefore, from force of circumstances, we must accept the action in this manner performed. We also are justified, because the guilty person has been authorized by a higher authority who did not know the true condition. Certainly as soon as the fault is discovered, an end is given to any priesthood activity by such an individual.
