Joseph Fielding Smith, Answers to Gospel Questions, vol. 3 ( 1960)

56

Were Revelations Suppressed?

Question: "A friend of ours attending a Catholic investigators' class was told that any revelation Joseph Smith claimed to have received was never made known to the public for at least ten years after he supposedly had received it. They say there were several people during Joseph Smith's time who claimed to have visions which caused many comments from newspapers and such, but there is no reference to Joseph Smith's vision. Can you tell me where I can find any proof that their statements are false?"

Answer: I suppose the time will never come until the millennium when people quit telling false stories about the Church.

The revelations in the Doctrine and Covenants were made known immediately after they were given and many of them were published in The Evening and Morning Star, The Messenger and Advocate, and other publications, shortly after they were received. Some of them were given in the presence of a group of brethren, like that known as Section 88. They were collected and published in 1833 which was less than three years from the organization of the Church.

Supposing they had not been published until years afterwards? Would that make them untrue? There was nothing published about the Savior until after his crucifixion. By the same doctrine as propounded by the Catholic instructor, people should reject all of the Four Gospels and all that they contain, since they were not written until many years after the Savior's death.
