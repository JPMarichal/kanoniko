Joseph Fielding Smith, Answers to Gospel Questions, vol. 3 ( 1960)

54

Rebaptism of Nephi 3

Question: "In our last theology lesson in our study of the Book of Mormon, we read that when Jesus Christ made his personal appearance to the Nephite people on this continent, he gave to Nephi power to baptize (3 Nephi 11:21). We remembered that following the preaching of Samuel, the Lamanite prophet, many did believe and went unto Nephi and were baptized (Helaman 16:3-4). If Nephi already had authority to baptize, why was it necessary for Christ to give him that power again? Was it merely to clarify and settle disputations among them, (3 Nephi 11:28) or was there another reason?"

Answer: There is nothing strange in the fact that when the Lord came to the Nephites, Nephi was baptized and so was everybody else although they had been baptized before.

The Church among the Nephites before the coming of Christ was not in its fulness and was under the law of Moses. The Savior restored the fulness and gave to them all the ordinances and blessings of the gospel. Therefore, it actually became a new organization, and through baptism they came into it. (III Nephi 9:15-22; 11:10-40; 12:18-19; 15:4-10.)

REBAPTISM FOR ENTRANCE INTO THE CHURCH

We have a similar condition in this dispensation. The Prophet Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery were baptized by command of the Angel John the Baptist. (P. of G. P., Joseph Smith 2:68-72.) Several others were baptized before the organization of the Church. However, on the day the church was organized, all who had been previously baptized were baptized again, not for the remission of sins, but for entrance into the Church. In each case the reason was the same. (See Smith, Doctrines of Salvation, Vol. II, p. 336.)
