Kathleen Rawlings Buntin, When a Loved One Dies ( 1988)

HEALING

I've saved the most important thing about grief for last because it is the best. When you are working through the hardest times in your life, Heavenly Father and Jesus are the best friends you can have. You can tell them anything, even things you don't think you can tell to anyone else.

No matter what you are feeling, Jesus has felt it all. Are you sad and lonely? He was often sad and lonely. (Isaiah 53:3.) Do you feel angry? So did He! When He found the holy temple full of animals and dishonest people, He braided a whip and drove them out. (Mark 11:15; Luke 19:45; John 2:15.) He also taught about the dangers of being angry for no reason and without control. (Matthew 5:22.) Are you ever scared or worried? Are there things you need to do that seem too hard for you? He had those feelings. The night before He died, He went to the Garden of Gethsemane to pray. He knew what was going to happen and He prayed that He might not have to do it. (Luke 22:42.) But because He loved us so much, He did all of those things for us even though it was so hard to do.

Jesus told the Prophet Joseph Smith that no matter how bad things seem, He, the Lord, had experienced it all. (D&C 122.) Whatever you are feeling, however you are hurting, He has felt that way, too. He knews that these painful times can help you grow and be more like Him. He loves you. When you are hurting the most, you can go to Him in prayer and know you are never alone.

How marvelous it is to know that even while we are grieving we are part of His great eternal plan.

Jesus said, "I am the resurrection and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die." (John 11:25-26.)
