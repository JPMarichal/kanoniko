The Case Against Mormonism, January 29, 1916

1916-January 29-Millennial Star 78:123, February 24, 1916; Improvement Era 19:464, March, 1916.

The Case Against Mormonism is one of the very few books, if not the only book, written by a non-Mormon ever to receive a formal public expression of approval and commendation from the First Presidency and a recommendation for use by missionaries in the missionary system of the Church.

The author of the book was actually Mr. J. C. Romans, a professional writer from New York City. Robert C. Webb was one of Homan's pen names. He had earlier defended the Mormon position in a series of articles in the Deseret News in 1912-13 when the Pearl of Great Price was under attack by a "jury" of scholars solicited by the Rt. Reverend F. S. Spalding, Episcopal Bishop of Utah. He was paid the regular newspaper rate for the series.

The Case Against Mormonism is a defense of the claims of the Book of Mormon.

AN EXPRESSION OF APPROVAL

The Case Against Mormonism is a book of one hundred and fifty-seven pages, written by Robert C. Webb, and published by L. L. Walton, New York City. In this little work, many of the misrepresentations under which the Church and our people have suffered are analyzed in a masterly way by the able author, who, as specified by himself on the title pages, is a non-'Mormon.' We commend this book to our missionaries in the field, to the earnest inquirers and investigators whom they may meet, and to the Latter-day Saints generally. JOSEPH F. SMITH, ANTHON H. LUND, CHARLES W. PENROSE, First Presidency, Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Salt Lake City, Utah, January 29th, 1916.
