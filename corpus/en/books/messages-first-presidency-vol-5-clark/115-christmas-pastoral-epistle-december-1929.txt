Christmas pastoral epistle, December, 1929

1929-December-Millennial Star 92:17-19, January 9, 1930; Der Ster 35:17-20, January 15, 1930; Liahona, the Elders' Journal 27:337-339, January 7, 1930.

This annual Christmas message notes the coming one hundredth anniversary of the Church and comments simply that "We are an hundred years nearer the time of the advent of the Redeemer of the world."

GREETINGS FROM THE FIRST PRESIDENCY OF THE CHURCH OF JESUS CHRIST OF LATTER-DAY SAINTS

To our brethren and sisters and friends throughout the world:

More than one hundred years ago-before the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints was organized-the following message was addressed to the world:

"A great and marvelous work is about to come forth unto the children of men.

"Behold, I am God; give heed unto my word, which is quick and powerful."

Shortly after this, the Lord again said:

"Hearken ye people from afar; and ye that are upon the islands of the sea, listen together.

"For verily the voice of the Lord is unto all men, and there is none to escape; and there is no eye that shall not see, neither ear that shall not hear, neither heart that shall not be penetrated.

"And the voice of warning shall be unto all people, by the mouths of my disciples, whom I have chosen in these last days.

"And they shall go forth and none shall stay them, for I the Lord have commanded them."

One thousand eight hundred twenty-nine years before that time the greatest epochal event in the history of the world had occurred. Christ our Lord, the Son of God, had been born at Bethlehem of Judea, His birth heralded by the angel of the Lord, and heavenly hosts, who declared the new-born Babe to be Christ the Lord, the Redeemer of the world.

Of the infancy, the sojourn in Egypt and ministry of Jesus we have but brief account. The message of peace and good will which He brought to the House of Israel was rejected by His own people. He suffered persecution, and was finally crucified because the new truths which He revealed were in conflict with the old law by which Israel, from the time of their deliverance from the Egyptian captivity, under the leadership of the prophet Moses, had been governed.

He arose from the tomb, the First-fruits of the resurrection, thus making possible the redemption of the human family from the penalty of death which had been pronounced upon them.

During His ministry He plainly indicated to His disciples that His mortal mission was to be of short duration, but He promised that at a later date He would come again in His glory, accompanied by holy angels, to sit upon the throne of His power and to judge the world.

Asked by His disciples when the time of His glorious appearance would be, and for a sign by which they might identify it, He said:

With the passing of the present Christmas-tide a century will have elapsed since the opening of the present Gospel dispensation, and the organization of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. We are an hundred years nearer the time of the advent of the Redeemer of the world.

In humility His disciples have carried the message of the restored Gospel to the people of the world. It has been preached in every civilized nation, and testimony borne that the Lord has again manifested Himself to His children who are here upon earth, has restored the everlasting Priesthood, and caused the Church of Christ to be re-established.

Thousands of honest-hearted people have accepted the testimony of these humble messengers and been made partakers of the blessings and joy which the Gospel brings. Truly, from an obscure and modest beginning, a marvelous work and wonder has been accomplished.

During this period the voice of the Lord, by His Spirit, has been constantly speaking to the pure-in-heart; while the voice of thunderings and lightnings, of earthquakes, tempests, the sea heaving beyond its bounds and the constant recurrence of war, warn the people of the world that the day of the coming of the Lord is nigh.

By the voice of the Lord, spoken through His servants, and by signs and wonders which are constantly manifested, He is calling His children to repentance. He pleads with them to forsake sin and return to His service, that they may be delivered from the tragic judgment which their own wicked acts will bring upon them.

To the Latter-day Saints everywhere we send our greeting, and blessing, and our humble prayer that the Spirit of the Lord may be increased upon you, that you may have strength to resist the temptations of the world. Put your houses in order, honour the laws of God and obey and uphold the civil laws of the country in which you reside. Keep your lamps trimmed and burning, that when the cry shall go forth: "Behold, the bridegroom cometh," as He will come, we may all be prepared to meet Him.

To the people of the world we say: All hail! We greet you as brothers. We exhort you to come unto Christ and be made perfect through Him. Let us unite in a universal determination to establish peace on earth, and good will and brotherhood among men; that the words of the angel who declared the birth of the Lord upon the hills of Judea one thousand nine hundred thirty years ago may become an accomplished fact, and peace on earth and good will to men be firmly established. God speed the day when the nations of the world shall "beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruning hooks;" when "nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more."

Even so come quickly Lord Jesus. HEBER J. GRANT, ANTHONY W. IVINS, CHARLES W. NIBLEY.
