GREETINGS FROM THE FIRST PRESIDENCY

Once again it becomes our great pleasure to extend through the columns of The Christmas News, hearty greetings not only to the Latter-day Saints, but to all humanity. The joyous Christmastide is upon us. The season when the great heart of fellowship and brotherly love throbs in sympathy with the unfortunate and in joyous acclaim with those who have been bounteously blessed, has reappeared. The year, as with those that preceded it, has brought its disappointments and sorrows, as well as its abundant cause for thanksgiving and praise. The work of the Church has been onward and upward. Especially in war-stricken Europe has the testimony of the Truth found lodgment in the hearts of men, and many have been led to see the light of the Gospel who, under more prosperous conditions, were seemingly unable to humble themselves to receive its teachings. While the great war left ruin and desolation in its wake, it caused many to reflect on the real purpose of life and to "accept with meekness the engrafted word." Thus the Titanic struggle, entailing terrible suffering and enormous sacrifice, is not without its sanctifying influence to those who remain and who are left to fight their battles under its handicap.

Reports from all the Missions, both at home and abroad, are filled with hope and encouragement. The prejudice at one time so universal against the Latter-day Saints, is fast being dispelled and our people are beginning to receive their due meed of praise for what they are accomplishing in the world. In the matter of alleviating distress and suffering the Church and its people have done much during the past year. No call of distress has gone unheeded. Almost as a unit the Latter-day Saints have rallied to the support of every worthy cause and have given of their substance unsparingly that the cry of the afflicted might not ascend to our Heavenly Father in vain. We commend the Latter-day Saints for their faithfulness in this respect, and for their interest in all that concerns the welfare of mankind. After all, this is the mission that has been assigned us, to warn the nations of impending judgments, to preach the Gospel of the Redeemer, whose birth we at this season commemorate, and to invite all to come unto Christ and receive the benefits of His glorious atonement. "Mormonism," so-called, is in the world for the world's good. Its missionary system has no other purpose than to bless and benefit. It has no quarrel with the creeds and sects of the day. It stands for peace, the peace of God "which passeth understanding." It is ever ready to do good in every possible way. It pleads for a return to the faith "once delivered to the Saints," believing that such a course will save humanity from the sins of the world and eventually exalt men in the presence of God, the Father, and Jesus Christ, the Son, "Who is the Light and Life of the World."

On this hallowed anniversary, when men turn their hearts and thoughts to the time when the Star of Bethlehem led the Wise Men to the Child in the Manger, we extend to the Latter-day Saints our sincere thanks for their hearty cooperation in the varied activities of the Church, and invoke upon them the blessings of God for their devotion to His cause. May they continue to be prospered in all that they set their hands to do in righteousness, and may the spirit of the Christmas season distill upon them as the dews from heaven and bring comfort and joy to their souls, strengthening their testimonies of the Truth and encouraging them to press on in the great work of the Lord. May they enjoy in their homes all the comforts of life and the satisfaction which comes from a knowledge of duty well done and a harmonious relationship with Him who is the Giver of every good and perfect gift. Thus will they be able to resist temptation and to live, as nearly as may be, "by every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God." HEBER J. GRANT, CHARLES W. PENROSE, ANTHONY W. IVINS, First Presidency.
