James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 5 ( 1971)

Court case on Church property in Tahitian Mission, November 24, 1922

1922-November 24-Original letter. Reed Smoot files, Brigham Young University, Archives, Provo, Utah.

Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Office of the First Presidency Salt Lake City, Utah Nov. 24th, 1922. Hon. Reed Smoot, United States Senator, Washington, D.C.

Dear Brother Smoot:

We realize that you are burdened to death and we do not know that it will be possible, through the officials there, to give any instructions to the American consuls to help us in a matter that has arisen in the Tahitian Mission.

We are enclosing copies of two letters from President O. B. Peterson of the Tahitian Mission, dated October 18th, which give a full account regarding our property on the island of Tubuai. The case will come up before the courts shortly after the first of January and if it is possible for any of your friends to make any suggestions to the consuls there to assist us in getting a fair deal, we will appreciate it, but if this is something that you feel we ought not to burden the officials in Washington with, it will be alright with us. We are grateful to the American Consul for the help which he has already rendered and he undoubtedly is willing to do all that he can for us. We are in hopes that our peaceful possession for so many years will entitle us to retain the ownership of this property. Perhaps the customs of the country are more or less responsible for our not securing a title, and thinking that peaceable possession was all that was necessary.

If anything can be done of course your early attention will be appreciated, seeing that the matter comes up in January.

Ever praying for your welfare and with best wishes we are, Sincerely your brethren, HEBER J. GRANT, CHARLES W. PENROSE, ANTHONY W. IVINS, First Presidency.
