James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 5 ( 1971)

Suppression of improper movies, April, 1921

1921-April-Original telegram. Reed Smoot files, Brigham Young University, Archives, Provo, Utah.

Whether or not the First Presidency had asked Senator Smoot to intercede with Mr. Hays in "suppressing the arbuckle and other improper films," is not evident in the Reed Smoot files. The background of the story, however, can be reconstructed, in general, as follows:

The Mr. Hays referred to was undoubtedly William Harrison Hays, American moving-picture executive. According to the Columbia Encyclopedia (1950):

"Will H. Hays . . . served (1918-1921) as chairman of the Republican National Committee. He was (1921-22) Postmaster General under President Harding. As president (1922-1945) of the Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America, he administered the moving-picture moral code-popularly called the 'Hays Code'-which was promulgated by agreement of the leaders of the motion picture industry." (p. 869.)

While the Columbia Encyclopedia does not specifically mention the Arbuckle films by name, it does comment as follows on films, and particularly "sex-appeal" films (which the Arbuckle films were), in these words:

"Criticism by educational, religious and other groups and the fear of organized censorship induced the industry to establish (1922) an organization for self-regulationÂ�. The taste in movies has in general run to the sensational, as in Western films, the 'sex-appeal' films of the 1920s, and gangster movies in the 1940s." (p. 1337.)

"Fatty" Arbuckle was known popularly at the time as a sex pervert and his films, though "mild" when compared with the "sex-appeal" films being shown in 1970, were nevertheless objectionable to religious and educational groups, including The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, hence the telegram commending and supporting Mr. Hays in their suppression.

Salt Lake Utah Sen R. Smoot

The first presidency appreciate highly what Mr. Hays has done in suppressing the arbuckle and other improper films. HEBER J. GRANT.
