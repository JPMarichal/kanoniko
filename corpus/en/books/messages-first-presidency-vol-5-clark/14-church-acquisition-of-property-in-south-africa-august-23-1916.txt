James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 5 ( 1971)

Church acquisition of property in South Africa, August 23, 1916

1916-August 23-Original letter. Reed Smoot collection, Brigham Young University Library, Archives, Provo, Utah.

Senator Reed Smoot had been continuously re-elected to the United States Senate since 1904. His assistance was needed on a matter of acquiring Church mission property in South Africa and clearance through the British Consul. A similar letter sought his help in connection with the Swiss Mission.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Salt Lake City, Utah, August 23rd, 1916. Senator Reed Smoot, Washington, D.C. Dear Brother:

The Church is acquiring some property in South Africa and order to procure the title we are sending a certified copy of the law of Utah in relation to corporations sole and also a copy of the articles of incorporation of the "Corporation of the Presiding Bishop of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints," to whom we desire to have the property conveyed.

These papers have been duly certified by the Secretary of State, but the solicitors there ask that the authority of the Secretary be vouched for by the British Consul, so we are sending them to you for certification by such British officials as will render them valid in South Africa.

Please give the matter your immediate attention and have the papers forwarded to the following address: Nicholas G. Smith, Main Road, Mowbray, Cape Colony, South Africa. Your Brethren, JOSEPH F. SMITH, ANTHON H. LUND, CHARLES W. PENROSE, First Presidency.
