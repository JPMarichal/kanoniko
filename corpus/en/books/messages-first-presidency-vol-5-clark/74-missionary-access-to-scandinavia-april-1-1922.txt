James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 5 ( 1971)

Missionary access to Scandinavia, April 1, 1922

1922-April 1-Original letter. Reed Smoot files, Brigham Young University, Archives, Provo, Utah.

Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Salt Lake City, Utah April 1, 1922. Hon. Reed Smoot, United States Senate, Washington, D.C.

Dear Brother Smoot:

Your letter of February 19th was received during my absence from the City.

The advice you give with reference to the manner of making out passports for missionaries going to the Scandinavian countries, I consider sound and have communicated the same to the Presidents of the Danish and Swedish missions with instructions to observe same.

I notice that you have about reached the conclusion that it will be necessary for you and Brother Widtsoe to visit the Scandinavian countries and endeavor to remove the prejudice that exists in the minds of their government officials in order to make it possible to send missionaries to these countries.

As I wrote you from Santa Monica a few days ago I certainly consider it wise that you make the trip with President Harding to Alaska. After you return if you still consider it necessary that this trip to Scandinavia be made, I shall be glad to have you bring the matter to my attention. I had hoped that we would be able to settle our differences with these countries without making this trip and I shall be glad if developments in the meantime may be such as to obviate the necessity.

We are having a good deal of success in getting missionaries into the other foreign countries and I can not help but feel that Lord will open the way to send them into the Scandinavian countries in due time.

Thanking you for the prompt attention you gave this matter and ever praying for your welfare, I am, sincerely and affectionately Your friend and brother, HEBER J. GRANT.
