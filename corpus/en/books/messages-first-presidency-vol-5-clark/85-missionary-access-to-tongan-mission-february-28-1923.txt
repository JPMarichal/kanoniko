James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 5 ( 1971)

Missionary access to Tongan Mission, February 28, 1923

1923-February 28-Original letter. Reed Smoot files, Brigham Young University, Archives, Provo, Utah.

A number of letters in the Smoot file in the early 1920's show evidence of considerable difficulty on the part of the L.D.S. Church in maintaining adequate missionary forces in a number of foreign countries, both in the Pacific and in Europe, due to governmental regulations denying missionaries passports or entry visas. Senator-Apostle Smoot was influential in smoothing out many of these problems.

Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Office of the First Presidency Salt Lake City, Utah Feby. 28th, 1923. Hon. Reed Smoot, U. S. Senate, Washington, D.C.

Dear Brother Smoot:

We are in receipt of a letter from President Mark V. Coombs of the Tongan Mission, from which we quote as follows:

"My anxiety is I shall soon have to release Elders Dunn and Hansen and then others will follow them home, and we will be left without an adequate force of Elders to carry on the work. These people who are good hearted and generous to a fault, are very jealous one to another, and will not hold together in any kind of an organization unless a white Elder supervises."

You will understand that the reason Brother Coombs is anxious about this matter is that there is an embargo against sending new missionaries to the field, and we are giving you this information that you may realize the importance of getting as prompt action as possible in having this embargo removed. We realize that you have this matter in mind, but are furnishing this information that you may know just what the situation is.

Ever praying for your welfare, we are, Sincerely your brethren, HEBER J. GRANT, CHARLES W. PENROSE, ANTHONY W. IVINS, First Presidency.
