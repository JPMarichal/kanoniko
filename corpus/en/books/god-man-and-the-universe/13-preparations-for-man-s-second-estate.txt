Hyrum L. Andrus, God, Man, and the Universe ( 1968)

11

Preparations For Man's Second Estate

Then the Lord answered Job out of the whirlwind, and said, . . .

Gird up now thy loins like a man; for I will demand of thee, and answer thou me.

Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou has understanding.

Who hath laid the measures thereof, if thou knowest? or who hath stretched the line upon it?

Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened? or who laid the corner stone thereof;

When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?—JOB.

The spirit children of the Man of Holiness existed in the spirit world for an extended period before the time came for them to receive bodies of flesh and bones and to be proved in the endowments of physical life. Since the task of clothing organized spirits with tabernacles of flesh and bones, with that of proving them in a probationary period, was of major significance to those concerned, certain important preparations were necessary. A physical world had to be created, and a master plan capable of dealing with the complex task had to be formulated.

The Grand Council Of The Gods

The Gods Met to Decide Upon a Plan

As the time approached for the Father's spirit children to receive the endowments of physical life, the Man of Holiness called a council, known as the Grand Council of the Gods, to consider the preparations that would have to be made. Those in attendance included the leading spirits in the pre-earth spirit world—the noble and great ones. Having shown these great intelligences to Abraham, the Lord said unto him: "Abraham, thou art one of them." [^1] Of the Grand Council of the Gods, Abraham wrote:

. . . there stood one among them [i.e., the noble and great spirits] that was like unto God, and he [i.e., the pre-earth Christ] said unto those who were with him [i.e., the noble and great spirits]: We will go down, for there is space there, and we will take of these materials, and we will make an earth whereon these [i.e., the remainder of the Father's spirit children] may dwell;

And we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them. [^2]

The fact that the noble and great spirits were called Gods in the pre-earth sphere of existence is made apparent in Abraham's record. As the activities of the creation got underway, Abraham reports that the one like unto God said to the noble and great spirits, "Let us go down." The record then states: "And they went down at the beginning, and they, that is the Gods, organized and formed the heavens and the earth." [^3]

In a previous chapter it was noted that the reason Christ became God and is called God is that for others He is the source of the divine attributes and powers that lead to eternal life—to that state where man may be made like God in the possession of the divine attributes and powers of deity. Christ became the Son of God by receiving these divine attributes and powers of life, or glory, from His Father; and when He was empowered to develop them in others, He became their God. [^4]

The same principles on a lower echelon of authority probably applied to the noble and great spirits in the pre-earth sphere. Joseph Smith spoke, for instance, of Moses and other great prophets of his spiritual stature and declared that they were "sons of God who exalt[ed] themselves to be Gods, even from before the foundation of the world." [^5] From this it would seem that under the authority of the Father and the Son, the noble and great spirits were given the keys and power to develop the divine nature of God in lesser spirit beings; and as the source of the divine nature for others, they were called Gods. [^6]

Joseph Smith again expressed the view that the noble and great spirits who participated in the creation of the earth were gods when he said of the Grand Council of the Gods: "In the beginning, the head of the Gods [i.e., the Man of Holiness] called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it." [^7] That the head of the Gods was the exalted Man of Holiness seems evident from the Prophet's reference to the head as "the Father of the Gods." [^8] He also explained: "The head God brought forth the Gods in the grand council." [^9] The gods in the Grand Council were children of the head God.

A statement made by the latter-day Seer seems to indicate that the mighty sphere Kolob was the place of meeting for this august council. [^10]

Biblical Evidence of the Grand Council of the Gods

The Prophet contended that the original Hebrew account of the creation and placement of life on the earth agreed with Abraham's account. Said he:

I shall comment on the very first Hebrew word in the Bible; I will make a comment on the very first sentence of the history of creation in the Bible—Berosheit. I want to analyze the word. Baith—in, by, through, and everything else. Roch—the head, Sheit—grammatical termination. When the inspired man wrote it, he did not put the baith there. An old Jew without any authority added the word; he thought it too bad to begin to talk about the head! It read first, "The head one of the Gods brought forth the Gods." That is the true meaning of the words. Barurau signifies to bring forth. If you do not believe it, you do not believe the learned man of God. . . . Thus the head God brought forth the Gods in the grand council.

I will transpose and simplify it in the English language. . . . The head God called together the Gods and sat in grand council to bring forth the world. The grand councilors sat at the head in yonder heavens and contemplated the creation of the worlds which were created at the time. [^11]

The fact that the Gods contemplated the creation of more than one world implies that leaders representing spheres other than our spirit world may have been present at the Grand Council of the Gods. Such an assumption is in harmony with Joseph Smith's declaration that Jesus holds "the keys of the universe." [^12] It also finds support in scriptural statements which indicate that Christ's powers of creation and redemption extend beyond this earth. But though these points are of general interest, a revelation plainly states that God's canon of revelation to man presently is restricted to that which concerns this earth. [^13] Revelations concerning the Grand Council of the Gods and events related thereto are similarly restricted.

Procedures of the Grand Council of the Gods

In all their transactions, the Gods in the Grand Council operated upon theocratic principles. In the decisions they were expected to make, Christ proposed: "Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever." [^14] Within that true theocracy the free agency of each individual was strictly upheld, and members of the Council were at liberty to express their views on the subjects that lay before them. But in addition to sustaining freedom, true theocracy exalts intelligence and gives deference to truth. Instead of acting strictly upon democratic principles, the Gods in the Grand Council therefore subordinated their views to those of the Father, Elohim.

There are several reasons why theocratic procedures were used in the Grand Council of the Gods: First, all spirits in the pre-earth sphere had obtained their organization as spirit beings from the Man of Holiness and were subordinate to Him in this respect. Second, the truth and power which the spirit children of the Father had acquired since the time of their organization as spirits had also been given to them by Him. Third, the power by which the physical earth was to be created would have to be obtained from the Man of Holiness and exercised according to His divine will. Fourth, the new physical endowments which the spirit children of the Father would receive on earth would be given to them from Him as the ultimate source of physical life for them. Finally, the further endowments of truth and power which they could acquire in the great experience that lay before them would have to be given to them by the Father, Elohim, through Christ.

Since the Man of Holiness had created worlds without number before this earth was organized, it seems possible that His spirit children who were to come here had acquired a general knowledge of the means by which they could obtain physical bodies and be proved in a period of mortal probation. To an inquirer, John Taylor wrote of the pre-earth spirit state: "As thy spirit beheld the scenes transpiring there, and thou grewest in intelligence, thou sawest worlds upon worlds organized and peopled with thy kindred spirits who took upon them tabernacles, died, were resurrected, and received their exaltation on the redeemed worlds they once dwelt upon." [^15]

But the degree to which unembodied spirits possessed detailed knowledge of the nature and requirements of physical life and mortal probation may be questioned. Though pre-earth man was in the presence of God, he existed under a law that was adapted to his state and condition of life, where his agency was maintained and his right to determine his future was held inviolate. An example of the policy of God concerning lesser intelligences is found in a revelation which speaks of angels and explains: "They reside in the presence of God, on a globe like a sea of glass and fire, where all things for their glory are manifest, past, present, and future, and are continually before the Lord." [^16]

The future that God knows is not revealed in its unrestricted details to the angels, for it is written that they know not the day nor the hour of Christ's coming to this earth in glory. [^17] This appointed time is known only by the Father. The statement that "all things for their glory" are manifest to the angels seems to carry the connotation that knowledge of the past, present, and future is revealed to them only as it is necessary for their progress in glory. [^18] The same policy may have applied to the spirit children of the Father in their pre-earth state.

When the noble and great spirits met with the Man of Holiness in the Grand Council of the Gods, they recognized that the Father, Elohim, was their source of life and truth and power, and that any further endowments of life and power which they might receive would have to be obtained from Him. In receiving these further endowments of life and power, the requirements of eternal truth would have to be upheld. The Father knew the way His spirit children would have to acquire the new endowments of life they desired to receive and the experiences they would have to undergo to obtain a fulness of glory and power in the celestial world. Consistent with these facts, the procedures of true theocracy were therefore utilized.

It was the will of the Father that His spirit children should obtain bodies of flesh and bones in a fallen spiritual state and under conditions of opposition in which they might be proved in their new endowments of life. [^19] Such a probationary state would allow them to determine for themselves how they would use their new physical endowments, and it would permit them to demonstrate their ability to walk by faith in obedience to the will of God. Being away from the direct influence of God's glory and power, they could be proved under conditions where they would not be as strictly accountable for their actions as they would be in the full presence of God. Yet they could still determine for themselves the degree to which they would develop in acquiring the divine nature of God, or they could disobey the laws of life and thereby turn away from God.

Lucifer's Counter-proposal to the Expressed Will of the Father

Among those who were present in the Grand Council of Gods was an influential spirit named Lucifer. [^20] Scripturally, he is said to have been a "Son of the Morning," a powerful spirit who held "authority in the presence of God." [^21] Yet Joseph Smith also characterized him as being "selfish, ambitious and striving to excel." [^22]

There was general agreement among the Grand Councilors that the spirit children of the Father should acquire physical bodies in a fallen state, away from the direct influence of God's glory and power. Members of the Council also agreed that a savior was to be appointed with power to bring man back into the presence of God, crowned with immortality and eternal life. [^23] But when it came to the question of how man was to be redeemed and who should be the redeemer, there was a serious difference of opinion registered. Jehovah, the Firstborn of the Man of Holiness, sought to uphold the will of the Father in all things. But Lucifer offered a counter-proposal to the expressed will of the Father.

Within the Father's expressed will and the counter-proposal presented by Lucifer were fundamental differences of principle that set the stage of conflict between good and evil which resulted in a struggle of the greatest magnitude among the spirit children of Elohim. This conflict has since been extended to this earth with devastating effects. To Moses, God explained the opposing issues. Lucifer, the Lord declared,

. . . came before me, saying—Behold, here am I, send me, I will be thy son, and I will redeem all mankind, that one soul shall not be lost, and surely I will do it; wherefore give me thine honor. But, behold, my Beloved Son, which was my Beloved and Chosen from the beginning, said unto me—Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever.

Wherefore, because that Satan rebelled against me, and sought to destroy the agency of man, which I, the Lord God, had given him, and also, that I should give unto him mine own power; by the power of mine Only Begotten, I caused that he should be cast down; and he became Satan, yea, even the devil, the father of all lies, to deceive and to blind men, and to lead them captive at his will, even as many as would not hearken unto my voice. [^24]

The Father's proposal and Lucifer's counter-proposal differed, first, regarding the issue of man's free agency in mortality. Lucifer suggested a plan of false security that would have suspended man's freedom on earth. Not one soul would be lost as a result of the mortal experience, for without their agency there would be no risks involved, and all would return to the presence of God. On this point, Joseph Smith explained: "The contention in heaven was—Jesus said there would be certain souls that would not be saved [if man was given his free agency on earth]; and the devil said he could save them all, and laid his plans before [the members of] the Grand Council, who gave their vote in favor of Jesus Christ." [^25]

Second, Lucifer's counter-proposal in denying free agency would have thwarted to a great extent the Father's desire to make earth life a probationary period in which challenge and reward were meaningful factors in human experience. Man could not be proved in his new physical endowments, for without agency he could not determine for himself whether he would obey the will of the Father in all things. Had the Father's proposal to make earth life a probationary period been set aside, a future judgment where each individual would be rewarded according to merit also would have been impossible.

Third, the lust for power was hidden skillfully within Lucifer's proposal. He sought for the honor of the Father, which is His power, [^26] as it was to be centered in the Only Begotten Son; and the desire to excel worked within Lucifer as a fever. Having set forth his counter-proposal to the expressed will of the Father, he said: "Surely I will do it; wherefore give me thine honor." [^27]

Finally, Lucifer would have subverted the principles of eternal truth, equity, and reciprocation upon which celestial society is founded. It is required that all who receive organization and life from the Father consecrate themselves and all that they possess back to Him. In this way, the principles of eternal justice and reciprocation are sustained; and in return, men become heirs in celestial society. [^28] But whereas Lucifer selfishly sought for himself all the glory to be derived from the work to be accomplished by means of the Father's honor or power, the Firstborn expressed His desire to sustain the heavenly order of consecration with the simple declaration, "Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever" [^29]

Lucifer's proposal to the Grand Council of the Gods stood in vivid contrast to the one set forth by the Father. A decision had to be made, for when the Father inquired, "Whom shall I send?" both Jehovah and Lucifer replied, "Here am I, send me." In making the required decision, it was important that the Father uphold truth and consistency. He therefore said, "I will send the first." But because He chose Jehovah, Lucifer "was angry, and kept not his first estate." [^30] When frustrated in his design, Lucifer's ambitious nature exploded into rebellion. "He sought for things which were unlawful," Joseph Smith observed, and consequently he fell. [^31]

The War In Heaven

The General Conference of all Spirits

Having concluded their deliberations, the Gods returned to report to the others of the Father's spirit children. Undoubtedly all who resided in the spirit world were invited to attend the great convocation that was then held. But apparently there were some who did not immediately accept the proposal which the Gods had formulated in accordance with the will of the Father, and others rejected it and openly opposed it. The leader of the opposition was Lucifer. Following the rejection of his proposal by the Grand Council of the Gods, he defied the decision of that body and recruited supporters to his cause, and "at that day many followed after him." [^32]

The Nature of the Pre-earth Conflict

It is not known whether Lucifer was permitted to lay his proposal before the General Conference of all Spirits. But it is known that he promoted his views in the spirit realm and they became general knowledge and were favorably accepted by many. In the great conflict of opinion that followed, the Son of the Morning and his supporters employed insinuations, accusations, and half-truths to obscure and oppose the eternal verities upon which the Father's proposal was founded. The War in Heaven was a struggle for power. Apparently it was an ideological conflict, in which the work of the opposition tended to destroy the principles of life or glory within themselves and others. Instead of building upon the powers of truth and of life and strengthening them in others, Lucifer became the great "accuser of the brethren." [^33]

The War in Heaven centered largely around the issue of freedom vs. coercion—an issue as deep and as meaningful as the foundations of life itself. The basic principles upon which organized existence is sustained were involved. In the final analysis it was a question of the existence or the disintegration of organized life. Mature independence under law, as has been shown, is prerequisite to the existence of all truth and intelligence. Each individual therefore must stand free, act maturely, and cleave to truth if he is to sustain his organized existence. If carried to its logical conclusion, Satan's proposal would have made every organized being a non-entity, since it advocated the practice of principles destructive to the basis of organized life. "All organized existence is in progress," said Brigham Young, "either to an endless advancement in eternal perfections, or back to dissolution." [^34] Orson Pratt observed:

If the agency of man were destroyed, he would only act as he is acted upon, and consequently he would merely be a machine; and his actions would have neither merit nor demerit, so far as he was concerned, and could neither be punished nor rewarded, and would produce neither misery nor joy. Destroy the agency of man, and you destroy the main-spring of his happiness. Again, take away the agency of man, and you deprive him of his intelligence; for intelligence is the original force or cause of actions; it is a self-moving force; and all actions resulting from such a force, must necessarily be free. If, therefore, the agency of man or his freedom of action be destroyed, you destroy his self-moving force; and if you deprive him of such force, you deprive him of intelligence; therefore agency is essential to the very existence of intelligence. This truth is clearly revealed in a revelation given to Joseph the SEER, which reads as follows:—"All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, to act for itself, as all intelligence also, otherwise there is no existence. Behold, here is the agency of man." [^35]

Coercion is a perversion. By nature it is contrary to the formula of life. Truth and intelligence diminish, to the degree that compulsion is exercised, when they are acted upon by coercive powers. Joseph Smith once sanctioned the thought that "an hour of virtuous liberty on earth is worth a whole eternity of bondage." [^36] When reduced to fundamentals, the law upon which life exists supports this conclusion. Unlike the Puritan fathers, the Prophet did not look upon the idea of man's freedom as "the intellectual mistake of giving the finite a veto upon the infinite." [^37] Instead, he declared: "The cause of human liberty is the cause of God." [^38] In supporting life, God must promote mature independence, for life is dependent upon independence. It is the law of existence—of truth's existence, of man's existence, and even of God's existence.

God's Judgment Upon Satan and His Hosts

The foregoing principles played a vital part in the struggle that decided the fate of God's spirit children in their first estate. Having openly sought for coercive power in the face of eternal verities, Lucifer and his host of perverse disciples were "thrust down from the presence of God and the Son." [^39] Thus, "a third part of the hosts of heaven" were turned away from God; and "because of their agency," they were allowed to "become perdition." Coercive measures could not be employed to prevent them from making such a decision. Consequently, the rebellious spirits "were thrust down, and thus came the devil and his angels." [^40]

While wrapped in heavenly vision, Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon beheld the fall of Lucifer and wrote:

And this we saw also, and bear record, that an angel of God who was in authority in the presence of God, who rebelled against the Only Begotten Son whom the Father loved and who was in the bosom of the Father, was thrust down from the presence of God and the Son, and was called Perdition, for the heavens wept over him—he was Lucifer, a son of the morning. And we beheld, and lo, he is fallen! is fallen, even a son of the morning!

And . . . we beheld Satan, that old serpent, even the devil, who rebelled against God, and sought to take the kingdom of our God and his Christ—wherefore, he maketh war with the saints of God, and encompasseth them round about. [^41]

From this statement it is plain that, as a result of transgression, Lucifer and his followers suffered a spiritual death. They were "thrust down from the presence of God and the Son," [^42] which means that they were severed from the intelligent powers of God's glory. By rebellion they destroyed the divine truth and light within themselves until, as apostate beings devoid of the glory they had acquired in their first estate, they were unfit to reside longer in the presence of God. Joseph F. Smith, nephew of the Prophet, likened the spiritual death which they suffered to that which Adam suffered as a result of his transgression in the Garden of Eden, and which those who become sons of perdition on earth will suffer. [^43]

As fallen beings devoid of glory, Lucifer and his hosts became spirits of relative darkness. Though the elements of light or spirit of which they are composed enable them to appear at times to mortals as "angels of light," Parley P. Pratt explained that "there is more or less of darkness about them." [^44] To man in his present fallen state they may manifest some degree of light, but when compared with the glory of God their light is almost negligible. Satan's lack of glory is illustrated in an experience which Moses had. First Christ appeared to the man of God in the office and power of the Father. Then Satan appeared, and Moses exclaimed: "I could not look upon God, except his glory should come upon me, and I were strengthened [i.e., transfigured] before him. But I can look upon thee in the natural man. Is it not so, surely?" [^45]

In addition to being severed from the glory and power of God, Joseph Smith explained that Lucifer's "punishment is that he shall not have a tabernacle." [^46] His followers were restricted in like manner, for they too had failed to prepare for the endowments of physical life and the added glory that could thereby be acquired in the resurrection. The Lord had observed as the Grand Council of the Gods commenced, "They who keep their first estate shall be added upon [i.e., given the endowments of physical life]; and they who keep not their first estate shall not have glory in the same kingdom with those who keep their first estate." [^47]

Lucifer's Mission

Because of the position Lucifer took and sought in unrighteousness to sustain, he became the "father of lies," or perdition. [^48] But God in His eternal plan, utilizes the energies of Satan and his followers, even though their efforts are directed toward destruction. The Prophet observed that Lucifer was given "a mission to fill—to take possession of this earth, [and] cajole and deceive its inhabitants who had been sent by the Father to test their integrity." [^49] A revelation also explained that Satan is permitted "to deceive and to blind men, and to lead them captive at his will, even as many as would not hearken unto my [i.e., God's] voice." [^50] The prophet Mormon referred to the word of God as leading "the man of Christ in a straight and narrow course across that everlasting gulf of misery which is prepared to engulf the wicked." [^51] Herein lies the test of man's mortal probation.

In the work of deception, Lucifer is a master artist. His power, Joseph Smith contended, "has been and still is much underrated." [^52] He said: "The devil has great power to deceive; he will so transform things as to make one gape at those who are doing the will of God." [^53] The Prophet referred to Lucifer as a skillful orator and as "a being of great power," [^54] who currently manifests a "smooth, sophisticated influence" among men. [^55] Exclaimed the latter-day seer: "Think of a spirit who was sufficiently courageous to measure strength with his Father and Elder Brother, defying their authority, and when cast from heaven for his rebellion could have a retinue of one-third of its innumerable hosts to follow him." [^56] So great is Satan's power that "his influence can only be rebuked by the great Jehovah, who restrains his power only when attempting a greater destruction than was allowed his majesty." [^57]

The Grand Organizational Council

Faithful Spirits Organized in Preparation for Earth Life

After the War in Heaven was over, a third major assembly was held, in the form of a Grand Organizational Council. Those who then convened under the direction of the Father were His spirit children who had kept their first estate and were to receive the endowments of physical life on earth. This included those spirits who may have manifested lesser degrees of faithfulness without openly rebelling against God, or who may have halted or hesitated for a time but finally joined in sustaining the proposals of the Father. [^58] All those who finally sustained the proposal of the Father were there.

Joseph Smith explained that in this great assembly, arrangements pertaining to our mortal probation were made "previous to our advent on earth." [^59] Of the Grand Organizational Council he again observed: "The Father called all spirits before Him at the creation of man, and organized them. He (Adam) is the Head, and was told to multiply." [^60]

Although the spirits who kept their first estate were privileged to come to earth, consistency required that consideration be given to the circumstances under which they would enter mortality. Some spirits had acquired great gifts and talents in the various spheres of endeavor. Some had been more attentive than others in attaining those eternal truths and powers that lead to immortal glory. Still others had developed great ability for leadership in the respective spheres of social life and organization. [^61] Because there would be a carry-over of aptitude from the spirit world to the mortal state of existence, it would not be right to send spirits into the world without giving proper consideration to their several abilities and desires. To disregard such matters would be to set aside justice and the rewards of merit. Such a policy might also hamper the valiant in their efforts to do good on earth, for a noble individual requires a noble cause. But in order to recognize and give due consideration to the differences thus existed among the several spirits, a master plan was necessary to assure that each individual would be sent to earth at a time and under circumstances which would best fulfil the will of God concerning his life in this probationary state.

The First Organization in Heaven

In referring to the Grand Organizational Council, Joseph Smith said: "At the first organization in heaven we were all present, and saw the Savior chosen and appointed and the plan of salvation made, and we sanctioned it." [^62] This was not the Grand Council of the Gods, for we were not all present at that council; nor was the plan of salvation presented in that council to all the Father's spirit children and "sanctioned" by them. Final ratification of the proposed program of life and salvation did not take place until after the War in Heaven and those who sanctioned it were those who kept their first estate.

A thorough study of the Prophet's thought suggests that the "first organization in heaven" to which he alludes in the statement above took place in the Grand Organizational Council. The "first organization" must therefore have determined in a general way the major family, group, and natural relationships that would be developed on earth, as the spirit children of the Father entered into physical life. In spirit life, they were the sons and daughters of the Man of Holiness; and as such, they were members of His spirit family. But they were about to enter into a new stage of life and receive the endowments of life that are related to the flesh. In doing a new family relationships were to be developed that could, by faithfulness to the divine plan of life and salvation, endure forever. Otherwise, modifications of this program would of necessity have to take place. The "first organization" would therefore be subject to revisions or further organizations, depending upon the faithfulness of each individual during his mortal probation. But to begin, the Grand Organizational Council determined in a general way the families, nationalities, etc., with which the spirit children of the Father were to be identified in the flesh. Apparently this was the "first organization" to which the Prophet referred in the statement above.

How specific and detailed the pre-earth organization was is not presently known. Evidence that such an organization was formed, however, is suggested by the following: Abraham was chosen before his birth to the patriarchal mission that he fulfilled on earth. [^63] Prior to the birth of Esau and Jacob, it was declared prophetically that the older would serve the younger. [^64] Mary was appointed to be the mother of Jesus long before Christ's birth on earth. [^65] The name of Joseph Smith's father was previously determined; and it was known that the Prophet would be his son, and that the latter-day Seer would be called after the name of his father. [^66] It would also seem that the family difficulties which Martin Harris experienced during the translation of the Book of Mormon were foreknown, for thousands of years before these difficulties occurred, the Lord provided a way to counter their effects upon the latter-day work. [^67] John Taylor wrote publicly to a woman who made inquiries on the subject that she had chosen her parents and her mate before coming to earth, and that the children that were to be born unto her were also designated. [^68]

Although an organization of the spirit children of the Man of Holiness was carried out at this early period—called the "first organization in heaven," it has been indicated that this organization was conditional in nature and subject to modifications, depending upon how each individual exercised his free agency on earth. It may be that the conditional nature of this early organization and God's foreknowledge of the waywardness of man in mortality made detailed arrangements at this early period unwise and unprofitable. But whether or not this is true, the fact remains that the Lord works within the framework of man's free agency, issuing commandments according to that which is best under given circumstances. In a revelation, God said: "Wherefore I, the Lord, command and revoke, as it seemeth me good; and all this to be answered upon the heads of the rebellious." [^69] If a man fails to obey the commandments of God, that which is promised on a conditional basis goes unfulfilled, and a new commandment or organization, with a new promise, is given. Until a man demonstrates the reality of his faith by obedience, God acts toward him according to his potential, reserving to Himself how, in His infinite wisdom and foreknowledge, He will bring to pass His designs in the earth.

The contingent nature of the "first organization in heaven" was clearly implied in a statement by Brigham Young. Some time after Joseph Smith's death, President Young reported that the martyred prophet appeared unto him and instructed:

Tell the people to be humble and faithful, and be sure to keep the Spirit of the Lord and it will lead them right. . . . They can tell the Spirit of the Lord from all other spirits; it will whisper peace and joy to their souls; it will take malice, hatred, strife and all evil from their hearts; and their whole desire will be to do good, bring forth righteousness and build up the kingdom of God. Tell the brethren if they will follow the Spirit of the Lord, they will go right. Be sure to tell the people to keep the Spirit of the Lord; and if they will, they will find themselves just as they were organized by our Father in Heaven before they came into the world. Our Father in Heaven organized the human family, but they are all disorganized and in great confusion. [^70]

Continuing, President Young explained:

Joseph then showed me the pattern, how they were in the beginning. This I cannot describe, but I saw it, and saw where the Priesthood had been taken from the earth and how it must be joined together, so that there would be a perfect chain from Father Adam to his latest posterity. Joseph again said, "Tell the people to be sure to keep the Spirit of the Lord and follow it, and it will lead them just right." [^71]

The assumption that there was a first organization in heaven of a contingent nature implies that modifications of that organization might be necessary as the spirit children of the Father came to earth. For the first organization to find actual expression on earth, all people in mortality would have to seek the guidance of the Holy Spirit to direct their lives in accordance with the will of God. In this event, there would be no significant alterations of the pre-earth arrangements. Those who were chosen to stand at the head of major family associations in the flesh would then enter into the relationships that had been planned. And under the guiding influence of the Holy Spirit, men in general would either form those associations that were planned for them or that were left to the exercise of their agency on earth. In either event, the will of God would be expressed in a given generation. This, in turn, would lay the foundation for His will to be expressed in future generations. But since men in general do not seek the guidance of the Holy Spirit, and since they do many things by their agency which are contrary to the will of God, modifications of the original plan of the Father are necessary.

The master plan that was formulated in the spirit world was an expression of the Father's will concerning the several generations of men that would live on the earth. If Joseph Smith called these initial arrangements the "first organization," it follows that their actual implementation on earth, with any required modifications, may be designated as a second organization. But in a more enduring sense, it was the Father's intent to effect an organization that would find its expression in mortality and be perpetuated in eternity. To this end, family associations which are formed in mortality must be sanctified by the powers of the gospel of Jesus Christ and sealed by the authority of the Holy Priesthood. If such associations are not sealed, the contracting parties cannot continue eternally the relationships they form on earth. [^72]

The sealing together of family units by the power and authority of the Holy Priesthood and the designating of future rights and blessings under the Holy Priesthood is the final and most vital of all the organizations within the divine plan that pertains to this earth. Only those relationships that are sealed by the Priesthood will remain intact after the resurrection; and each generation of the righteous, having been given rights and blessings in the Priesthood and sealed together into one great patriarchal structure, will constitute the eternal family order of this earth within the celestial kingdom.

In directing the attention of the Saints to the final Grand Council that will be held sometime in the future, in which the mold of the divine patriarchal order for this earth will be set for eternity, Joseph Smith spoke of their responsibility to organize themselves and the faithful of all ages under the law and authority of God, that the work they accomplish and seal by the authority of the Holy Priesthood might be approved by that great and final organizational council. As the Prophet spoke of the need to prosecute this work in the way ordained by the Lord, he said:

It will be necessary, in the Grand Council, that these things be testified to by competent witnesses. Therefore let the recording and witnessing . . . be carefully attended to from this time forth. If there is any lack, it may be at the expense of our friends; they may not come forth. [^73]

According to the views which the Prophet expressed, a series of organizations was necessary before those who keep both their first and second estates will be organized in the flesh into a great and divine family structure that will endure in eternity. The first organization toward this end was made in the spirit world before man was placed upon the earth.

The Doctrine of Election and Foreordination

In the Grand Organizational Council, many spirits were appointed to fill important missions on earth. Chief among these were the noble and great spirits who had attained to the title and authority of gods in the pre-earth state. From among these, there were those who were appointed to hold the keys to the Holy Priesthood in the several dispensations of gospel power and truth which were to be revealed on earth. As God stood among these noble and great spirits, He said: "These I will make my rulers." [^74]

Michael the Archangel led the forces of righteousness and of freedom in the War in Heaven for the spirits who were to come to this earth. When the Grand Organizational Council convened, he was chosen "to open the ways of the world." [^75] That is, he was chosen to initiate the fall of the earth from the place it first occupied in the presence of God to a temporal state. In becoming the man Adam, [^76] he was also selected to be the great progenitor of physical life on earth. Finally, he was appointed, under Christ, to be the father of the divine spiritual attributes and powers of life that lead eventually to eternal life and salvation for all those of his posterity in the flesh who would obey the plan of redemption. [^77] In performing these functions, he opened "the ways of the world." [^78]

To such an extent were men foreordained to perform righteous works on earth that Joseph Smith observed: "Every man who has a calling to minister to the inhabitants of the world was ordained to that very purpose in the Grand [Organizational] Council of heaven before the world was." [^79] Noah "was born to save seed of everything, when the earth was washed of its wickedness by the flood." [^80] Having shown Abraham the noble and great spirits of the pre-earth state, God declared to the Father of the Faithful: "Abraham, thou art one of them; thou wast chosen before thou wast born." [^81] Abraham's calling, as was previously mentioned, included his appointment to preside as a patriarch within the divine family order of the Gospel, as well as his ordination to preach the Gospel to men in his day. Moses was foreordained to redeem the Israelites from Egyptian bondage. [^82] Before Jeremiah was formed in his mother's womb on earth, God knew him and ordained him to be "a prophet unto the nations." [^83] John the Baptist was chosen before birth to prepare the way before the Lord of Life; and the Twelve whom Jesus chose on earth were called to be His apostles prior to the commencement of their earthly experiences. [^84] Among these, John the Apostle was ordained before he was born to receive and write the great apocalypse. [^85] And God promised to "raise up" Joseph Smith, the latter-day Seer, to "bring forth" the word of truth in the last and greatest of all gospel dispensations. [^86] But chief among those who were then chosen was Jesus Christ, who, as a lamb without blemish, "was foreordained before the foundation of the world" to redeem the sons of men from their fallen mortal state. [^87]

Men are Called of God by Prophecy

Concerning the foreordination of righteous individuals in the Grand Organizational Council to bear the Holy Priesthood on earth, Joseph Smith declared in an official statement: "We believe that a man must be called of God by prophecy, and by the laying on of hands, by those who are in authority to preach the Gospel and administer in the ordinances thereof." [^88] In order for a man to be called of God by prophecy, he must be called by a prophetic declaration (that is, foreordained in the Grand Organizational Council) before he actually receives the appointment on earth. Having been foreordained according to the foreknowledge of God, the promised appointment is then conferred upon the individual by the laying on of hands of those who have authority to act in such matters under the Spirit of revelation and of power. Alma explained the true procedure:

. . . this is the manner after which they were ordained—being called and prepared from the foundation of the world according to the foreknowledge of God, on account of their exceeding faith and good works; in the first place being left [on earth] to choose good or evil; therefore they having chosen good, and exercising exceeding great faith, are called with a holy calling, yea, with that holy calling which was prepared with, and according to, a preparatory redemption for such. [^89]

Alma stated further that the Holy Priesthood is a system of divine power and authority that has its center in the Son of God, and it is patterned after the order of the Son of God. The Holy Priesthood therefore draws upon the divine intelligence and foreknowledge of God in extending its rights and powers to those who become the sons of God. For this reason, Alma said that men who are ordained to this holy order on the earth are "called and prepared from the foundation of the world according to the foreknowledge of God." But though they are foreordained to bear the Holy Priesthood, justice and equity must govern the actual ordination of men to these sacred rights. As those who have been forordained to callings in the Priesthood come to earth, they are left to choose good or evil; and it is only when they have chosen good and exercised great faith that they are actually given the promised appointment in the flesh. In this way, Alma observed that all men are placed on "the same standing" with each other, and the blessings and callings of God are given without favor to the obedient. [^90] Here may be seen the challenge that is involved in man's mortal probation.

Although each person who receives a calling in the Priesthood on earth was appointed to that office or responsibility in the Grand Organizational Council, by the foreknowledge of God and by the Spirit of prophecy, his free agency on earth is not impaired. Every man who is born on earth is free either to accept or reject the guidance of the Spirit of God in his life; and only by responding to the influence of the Spirit can he be led to receive the previously appointed office or calling in the flesh.

Neither does a person who was foreordained in the Grand Organizational Council come to earth with a guarantee that having received that calling here, he will fulfil its obligations in righteousness.

Finally, regardless of the appointment a given individual may have received in the Grand Organizational Council, no man comes to earth with a prior guarantee of his final destiny in the resurrection. In discussing the doctrine of election in the flesh, Joseph Smith declared: "Unconditional election of individuals to eternal life was not taught by the Apostles." [^91] It follows that no man's final destiny in eternity was guaranteed or irrevocably set in the Grand Organizational Council. The above points indicate that the prophetic declarations made in the Grand Organizational Council concerning each individual who was foreordained to receive a calling in a Priesthood on earth was not "the more sure word of prophecy" of which the scriptures speak and which Joseph Smith explained. [^92] The latter is a more sure prophetic declaration than that which was expressed in the Grand Organizational Council, and it has to do with the final rewards in eternity that are given to those who prove themselves in the flesh and are sealed on earth by the power of the Holy Priesthood to receive the designated blessing in the resurrection. [^93] Given with a guarantee that the promised blessing will ultimately be realized, with some possible reservation, [^94] it is a more sure prophetic pronouncement than that which was expressed in the Grand Organizational Council.

With the completion of the preparations that were made in the Grand Organizational Council, the spirits who kept their first estate were ready to enter into their second estate and receive the endowments that pertain to the flesh.

Summary

Revelations given to the Prophet Joseph Smith and ideas he expressed concerning them reveal the preparations that were made before the spirit children of the Man of Holiness could receive physical bodies on a physical earth. The Father, Elohim, called a council, known as the Grand Council of the Gods, to which the noble and great spirits were invited. This great assembly was conducted according to true patriarchal and theocratic principles. Members of the Council met to consider the Father's will. But Lucifer presented a counter-proposal based upon coercion, a denial that earth life should be a probationary state with challenge and reward as meaningful factors in human experience, and a subversion of the eternal principle of consecration. This plan was rejected, and the councilors formulated one in accordance with the Father's will. But when they presented their proposal to the General Conference of all Spirits, opposition arose under Lucifer's leadership. A war occurred in heaven. Lucifer and his followers were thrust down from the presence of God into a state of spiritual darkness and denied the privilege of obtaining physical bodies. Meanwhile, they are permitted to tempt and try those who come to earth, and to lead into captivity those who do not hearken to the Lord's will.

After the War in Heaven, the Father called together those spirits who kept their first estate and organized them in preparation for earth life. Many were appointed to fulfill missions on earth, and the doctrine of election [^95] and foreordination has its basis in the actions of this Grand Organizational Council. Only after the work of this council was complete were those who kept their first estate ready to come to earth and receive the physical endowments of life.

The view can also be taken that it is more sure than the contingent promises that were made in the spirit of prophecy in the Grand Organizational Council. These early promises were not sealed, guaranteed, or made sure. But when a man by faithfulness in earth life makes his calling and election sure to promised blessings in the resurrection, these blessings are sealed upon him by the power of the Holy Priesthood. The promise of such blessings is a more sure word of prophecy; that is, more sure than the contingent word of prophecy which was expressed in the preordination of men in the Grand Organizational Council. This conclusion harmonizes the doctrine that in receiving the appointments of the priesthood on earth, man is called of God by prophecy; and having proved himself in his mortal probation, he may then make his calling and election sure to blessings in the resurrection and receive the more sure word of prophecy that is associated with the administration of the sealing powers of the Holy Priesthood.
