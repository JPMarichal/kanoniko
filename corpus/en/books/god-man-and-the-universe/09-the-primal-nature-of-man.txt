The Primal Nature of Man

What is man, that thou art mindful of him?—PSALMS.

Both religious and philosophical thinkers have speculated about man and his primal nature. Most schools of philosophical thought, if not all, deny that man had any form of conscious existence before earth life, and they seek to analyze and express the nature of man primarily from the standpoint of this temporal sphere. But with the restoration of the knowledge of God and of the cosmos there came also a restoration of the knowledge of man.

Latter-day revelation declares that man existed as a conscious entity before his birth into mortality, and that the fundamental elements of life within man are coeval [^1] with God. These "first principles," the Prophet taught, had no beginning, neither will they have an end. [^2] Having affirmed that "man was also in the beginning with God," a revelation said of the primal life within him: "Intelligence, or the light of truth, was not created or made, neither indeed can be." [^3] In an address delivered at Washington, D.C., Joseph Smith reportedly stated: "I believe that the soul is eternal; and had no beginning; it can have no end." [^4] And as he contrasted this view of man with traditional Christian thought, the Prophet argued that it is absurd to maintain that the life within man can be made eternal by creation. "Anything created," he declared, "cannot be eternal." [^5]

The Primal Life Or Intelligence Of Man

Man an Eternal Entity

To view the primal life within man as having existed from eternity, the Prophet contended, gives man more dignity and honor than is generally accorded him by those who have pondered the subject of his existence. He said:

I have another subject to dwell upon, which is calculated to exalt man. . . . It is associated with the . . . soul—the mind of man—the immortal spirit. Where did it come from? All learned men and doctors of divinity say that God created it in the beginning; but it is not so; the very idea lessens man in my estimation. I do not believe the doctrine; I know better. . . .God has told me so; and if you don't believe me, it will not make the truth without effect. . . . I am going to tell of things more noble.

We say that God Himself is a self-existing being. Who told you so? It is correct enough; but how did it get into your heads? Who told you that man did not exist in like manner upon the same principles? Man does exist upon the same principles. God made a tabernacle and put a spirit into it, and it became a living soul. (Refers to the Bible.) How does it read in the Hebrew? It does not say in the Hebrew that God created the spirit of man. It says, "God made man out of the earth and put into him Adam's spirit, and so became a living body."

The mind or the intelligence which man possesses is co-equal [i.e., coeval] with God himself. I know that my testimony is true. . . .

It is logical to say that the intelligence of spirits is immortal, and yet it has a beginning? The intelligence of spirits had no beginning, neither will it have an end. That is good logic. . . .

I take my ring from my finger and liken it unto the mind of man—the immortal part, because it had no beginning. Suppose you cut it in two; then it has a beginning and an end; but join it again, and it continues one eternal round. So with the spirit of man. As the Lord liveth, if it had a beginning, it will have an end. . . .

Intelligence is eternal and exists upon a self-existing principle. . . .

The first principles of man are self-existing with God. God himself, finding he was in the midst of spirits and glory, because he was more intelligent, saw proper to institute laws whereby the rest could have a privilege to advance like himself. The relationship we have with God places us in a situation to advance in knowledge. He has power to institute laws to instruct the weaker intelligences, that they may be exalted with Himself, so that they might have one glory upon another, and all that knowledge, power, glory, and intelligence, which is requisite in order to save them in the world of spirits. [^6]

In this statement the Prophet was speaking of the individual life and personality within man, not merely of the elements out of which man is organized. The following points may be drawn from his explanation: First, the mind of man—the central principle of life or intelligence within him—is a living entity. Joseph Smith refers to "the mind of man" by the singular pronoun "it." Second, the mind or the central primal intelligence of man, as a separate and distinct personality, is immortal in that it had no beginning. It is as old as God. Third, like God, man (that is, the primal life or intelligence of man as well as the elements from which he is organized) exists upon a self-existing principle. "The first principles of man are self-existing with God," the Prophet declared. This means, to stress the central point of his explanation, that "intelligence is eternal and exists upon a self-existing principle." [^7]

Two Stages in Man's Pre-earth Existence

Joseph Smith divided the pre-earth existence of the life within man into two general periods of time: (1) the period immediately prior to physical birth, during which man's spirit existed as an organized personage composed of a pure and fine substance called spirit, and (2) an earlier period antedating the time of the spirit's organization and continuing back in eternity without beginning. Little is known of the nature of life in the earliest of these two periods of existence.

Scriptural Statements Concerning Man's Primal Life

There are but three statements in modern scripture that speak directly about the nature of the primal life of man. These statements which concern the life within man prior to the time the human spirit was organized are:

For man is spirit. [^8]

Ye were also in the beginning with the Father, that which is Spirit, even the Spirit of truth. [^9]

Man was also in the beginning with God. Intelligence, or the light of truth, was not created or made, neither indeed can be. [^10]

From these scriptural statements it appears that the primal life within man is associated with that pure substance called spirit in such a way that it is equated with spirit. The first scripture above does not say that "man is a spirit"; instead, it states that "man is spirit." [^11] He is not composed merely of physical elements, but the primal life within man seems to consist of properties of life or intelligence that are associated with spirit. This view finds support in the second statement: "Ye were also in the beginning with the Father; that which is spirit, even the Spirit of truth." Again the primal life within man is identified as "that which is spirit", and the basic substance or principle of life is said, in turn, to be "the Spirit of truth." [^12] In the third statement, the primal life within man is designated as "the light of truth." Since "light" and "spirit" are interchangeably used as terms in the scriptures and since, as has been observed in the preceding chapter, they may be said to be "one," the expression "the light of truth" is rightly equated with "the Spirit of truth." The revelation from which these statements are taken apparently makes them synonymous with the primal "intelligence" of man. Consequently, the primal life within man is said to be "intelligence, or the light of truth." [^13] The primal life within man therefore seems to be equated with the intelligence that is inherently associated with spirit.

Clarification by Prominent Brethren

Joseph Smith and his initimate associate corroborated the above conclusions. They taught that the primal life or intelligence within man is inherent within that pure substance called spirit. The Prophet asked: "Is it logical to say that the intelligence of spirits is immortal and yet that it [i.e., the primal intelligence of spirits] had a beginning? The intelligence of spirits had no beginning, neither will it have an end." [^14] In this statement, the Prophet is not referring to that substance called spirit, but to the intelligence or life that is directly associated with it. That intelligence had no beginning, being coeval with the pure and fine substance with which it is associated.

Parley P. Pratt wrote of the primal intelligence of man:

It was never created, being an inherent attribute of the eternal element called spirit, which element composes each individual spirit [i.e., each organized spirit being], and which element exists in an infinitude of degrees in the scale of intellect, in all the varieties [of intelligence or life] manifest in the eternal God, and thence [down] to the lowest agent, which acts by its own will. [^15]

Three points of doctrine are apparent from Elder Pratt's explanation: First, the primal intelligence or life of man is inherent within spirit. Second, the human spirit is organized from that pure and fine substance. Third, spirit exists in an infinitude of degrees in the scale of intellect in all the varieties of intelligence that are manifested in God down to the lowest agent which has life and acts by its own will.

Orson Pratt also referred to the primal life of man as being "a part of that great substance of life, or Spirit," that is "intelligent, immortal, and eternal." [^16] "The capacities [i.e., the intelligent properties or attributes] of all spiritual substance are eternal as the substance to which they belong," he declared. "There is no substance in the universe which feels and thinks now, but what has eternally possessed that capacity." [^17]

Evidently Orson Pratt also understood that the primal life within man consisted of the life that was inherent within a given portion of that substance called spirit.

Brigham Young said:

There is an eternity of life, from which we were composed by the wisdom and skill of superior Beings. . . .

The life that is within us is a part of an eternity of life, and is organized spirit, which is clothed upon by tabernacles, thereby constituting our present being, which is designed for the attainment of further intelligence. [^18]

Brigham Young's statement that the life within man is part of an eternity of life and is organized spirit is in harmony with Orson Pratt's view that man's primal life was part of that great substance of life, or spirit, that is in itself intelligent, immortal, and eternal. In turn, both statements above are in harmony with Parley P. Pratt's explanation that the primal intelligence of man is an inherent attribute of spirit, and that this pure substance which went into the composition of man's organized spirit exists in an infinitude of degrees in the scale of intellect.

Man's Central Primal Intelligence a Personal Entity

Though man's spirit is organized from a pure and fine substance which possesses certain properties of life, Joseph Smith seems to have taught that within each individual spirit there is a central primal intelligence (a central directing principle of life), and that man's central primal intelligence is a personal entity possessing some degree of life and certain rudimentary cognitive powers before the time the human spirit was organized. As has been indicated, Joseph Smith's statement quoted at some length in the forepart of this chapter seems to stress this point. This view may also be extrapolated from a revelation, part of which has been quoted previously in this chapter, which says:

Man was also in the beginning with God. Intelligence, or the light of truth, was not created or made, neither indeed can be.

All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, to act for itself, as all intelligence also; otherwise there is no existence. [^19]

In studying these verses together as related statements in the over-all explanation, two points of doctrine should be noted: First, the fact that the term "intelligence" is used to designate the primal life of man implies that that primal life possessed some basic elementary powers of cognition. Why call it "intelligence" if the name does not describe that which is characteristic of it? Intelligence, not some inanimate thing, is said to be in the beginning with God; it exists on a self-existing principle; and it cannot be created or made. The revelation is speaking of something more than incognitant matter; it is speaking of that which pertains to life and personality—the individual life and personality of man.

Second, the last verse in the passage above indicates that the primal intelligence or life of man, as well as the intelligence and truth which is in all things, can exist only when it is independent in the sphere in which God has placed it. [^20] If the central primal intelligence of man was independent in its sphere during the period of its pristine existence, it may be argued that it was separate from other living things. Independence implies separateness. This assumption, in turn, implies that the central primal intelligence of man must have been a distinct entity possessing some elementary, rudimentary properties of life. In its pristine state, the central primal intelligence of man therefore must have possessed properties of personality and individuality—the rudimentary characteristics of an independent, uncreated, self-existing being. Again, it seems proper to speak of man as a separate entity before the time his spirit was organized in the pre-earth life. If so, the central principle of life within him may properly be designated as man's central primal intelligence.

The two verses of the revelation quoted above also indicate that the central primal intelligence of man (with all life-possessing elements that went into the composition of his organized spirit) was in some respects dependent upon God in its pristine state. If all truth and intelligence were independent in the sphere in which God had placed them, it follows that God ordained and sustained the laws that governed all forms of intelligence in their pristine state. The primal intelligence of man, therefore, was not independent of God; it was independent in the sphere in which God placed it. Speaking of lesser as well as of greater kingdoms of life, a revelation states: "Unto every kingdom is given a law; and unto every law there are certain bounds also and conditions. [^21] All beings who abide not in those conditions are not justified." There are "beings" of life within the lesser kingdoms of life in the cosmos, and they are subject to the law of God.

Even though the central primal intelligence of man may have been a living entity with some rudimentary qualities and powers of life, Latter-day Saint thought holds that eventually it was organized in union with other elements of spirit (with their inherent and acquired intelligence) to form a spirit personage.

The Organization Of The Human Spirit

Pre-earth Man a Spirit Child of God

The organization by which the central primal intelligence, or ego, of man was united with other life-possessing elements of spirit to make the pre-earth human spirit was accomplished by a process of conception and birth. In that primeval birth, the organized spirit of man became literally the offspring of divine parents—the Man of Holiness and His glorified and exalted companion.

According to Latter-day Saint thought, filial relationships in man's pre-earth state were literal and meaningful. Man is the offspring of heavenly Parents. Eliza R. Snow, the Mormon poetess and a wife of Joseph Smith, wrote:

O my Father, thou that dwellest In the high and glorious place; When shall I regain thy presence, And again behold thy face? In thy holy habitation Did my spirit once reside? In my first primeval childhood Was I nurtured near thy side? For a wise and glorious purpose Thou has plac'd me here on earth, And withheld the recollection Of my former friends and birth: Yet oft times a secret something Whispered you're a stranger here; And I felt that I had wandered From a more exalted sphere. I had learn'd to call thee father Through thy spirit from on high; But until the key of knowledge Was restor'd, I knew not why. In the heav'ns are parents single? No the thought makes reason stare; Truth is reason—truth eternal Tells me I've a mother there. When I leave this frail existence— When I lay this mortal by, Father, mother, may I meet you In your royal court on high? Then, at length, when I've completed All you sent me forth to do, With your mutual approbation Let me come and dwell with you. fn

Birth is more than an organization. It is the passing on of traits and characteristics from one organized being to the subordinate being who is formed in the process of conception. For this reason it may be that as the human spirit was formed, it had forged into it the divine traits and characteristics of deity. John Taylor wrote that the fundamental principle of life within man was "a spark of Deity, struck from the fire of his eternal blaze, and brought forth in the midst of eternal burnings." [^22] Being conceived and born of heavenly parents, the newly organized spirit was a child of God—the offspring of Deity, with the rudimentary characteristics of Deity and the potential of becoming eventually like its eternal parents.

Having been instructed by the Prophet, Joseph Smith's associates stressed that each organized spirit was "born and begotten by our Father and our God" [^23]—the exalted Man of Holiness. To support their view of the literal fatherhood of God and the actual pre-earth brotherhood of man, Latter-day Saints cite the declarations of the Apostle Paul, that man is "the offspring of God" [^24] and that God is "our Father." [^25] Again, sacred literature declares: "We have had fathers of our flesh which corrected us, and we gave them reverence: shall we not much rather be in subjection unto the Father of spirits, and live?" [^26] It is with this view of things that Latter-day Saints pray, "Our Father which art in heaven." [^27]

The firstborn of the Father's spirit children was a noble son known as Jehovah, and also revealed as Jesus Christ. [^28] Of His relationship to man in that primeval birth, the Redeemer said to the Prophet in a revelation: "I was in the beginning with the Father, and am the Firstborn," [^29] Here the revelations of Joseph Smith have an affinity with biblical testimony which refers to Christ as "the beginning of the creation of God," [^30] and as "the firstborn of every creature." [^31] As the risen Lord came from the tomb, He expressed the fact that man's relationship to the Father was similar to that which He possessed when He said to Mary Magdalene: "Go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and to your Father; and to my God, and your God." [^32]

Biblical Testimony of Pre-earth Life

Latter-day Saints often point to the fact that the Bible bears testimony repeatedly of the pre-earth existence of Jesus Christ and of man. At the commencement of his Gospel, John wrote of Christ: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." The apostle then testified: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth." [^33] Of Himself, Jesus declared: "I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me." [^34] Again: "What and if ye shall see the Son of Man ascend up where he was before?" [^35] Still again: "I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father." [^36] Finally, to the Father, He prayed: "And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was." [^37] Yet Jesus was born a child on this earth. Sometime between conception and birth there was a union of His organized spirit, as a conscious living entity, with the physical tabernacle which He received. The fact that Christ represented such a union of organized spirit and gross matter suggests to Latter-day Saints that the same is true of man.

Biblical evidence also indicates to Latter-day Saints that man had a conscious pre-earth existence. "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee," God declared to Jeremiah; "and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations." [^38] The fact that Christ's disciples understood that man had a pre-earth existence is evidenced by the question they asked the Redeemer as they came upon a man who had been blind from his birth. "Master," they inquired, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?" How could a man sin and thereby be born blind as a condemnation for his act if he did not live before mortal birth? Jesus confirmed the assumption that such a thing is possible. When He replied: "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him." [^39]

The Form of Organized Spirits

Latter-day revelation clarifies that the organized spirit of man resembled in form and stature the physical tabernacle which the spirit occupies on earth. Indeed, the physical tabernacle that man receives in mortal birth conforms, in general, to the image of the organized spirit. [^40]

As has been shown, Latter-day Saint thought holds that Jesus Christ, like man, was an organized spirit before He was born of Mary in Bethlehem of Judea. As a spirit personage, Jesus manifested Himself to an ancient prophet many years before He came to earth and received a physical tabernacle. The pre-earth Christ then said:

Seest thou that ye are created after mine own image? Yea, even all men were created in the beginning after mine own image. Behold, this body, which ye now behold, is the body of my spirit; and man have I created after the body of my spirit; and even as I appear unto thee to be in the spirit will I appear unto my people in the flesh. [^41]

The pre-earth Christ revealed that His spirit body was in the same general form as the physical body which He would receive on earth. According to a revelation to Joseph Smith, the same is true of man and of other forms of life; that which is spiritual is in "the likeness of that which is temporal; and that which is temporal in the likeness of that which is spiritual; the spirit of man in the likeness of his person, as also the spirit of the beast, and every other creature which God has created." [^42]

Parley P. Pratt referred to the organization of the human spirit from that pure, fine, life-possessing substance called spirit. He said:

What are they, if they are not flesh and bones? . . . Of what are they composed? . . . Why, they are organized intelligences. What are they made of? They are made of the element which we call spirit, which is as much an element of material existence as earth, air, electricity, or any other tangible substance recognized by man; but so subtle, so refined is its nature, that it is not tangible to our gross organs. . . . [Spirit is] a substance so holy, so pure, so endowed with intellectual attributes and sympathetic affections, that it may be said to be on a par, or level, in its attributes with man.

Let a given quantity of this element, thus endowed, or capacitated, be organized in the size and form of man, let every organ be developed, formed, and endowed, precisely after the pattern or model of man's outward or fleshly tabernacle—what would we call this individual, organized portion of the spiritual element?

We would call it a spiritual [i.e., a spirit] body, an individual intelligence, an agent endowed with life, with a degree of independence, or inherent will, with the powers of motion, of thought, and with the attributes of moral, intellectual, and sympathetic affections and emotions.

We would conceive of it as possessing eyes to see, ears to hear, hands to handle; as in possession of the organ of taste, of smelling, and of speech.

Such beings are we, when we have laid off this outward tabernacle of flesh. [^43]

Man's Primal Life Organized not Tabernacled

In the statement above, Elder Pratt is describing an organizational process in which pure, fine, life-possessing elements were organized to form the human spirit, with the necessary organs and limbs to give the organized spirit the form and stature man now possesses. Elder Pratt is not describing a tabernacling process in which the central primal intelligence of man was clothed upon with a tabernacle of spirit that conformed to the form and stature of that primal intelligence. [^44] Brigham Young agreed with this view when he observed that the life within man "is a part of an eternity of life, and is organized spirit." [^45] These conclusions are in harmony with Abraham's vision of the pre-earth world, in which he beheld that the spirits of men had been "organized." [^46]

Life in All Parts of the Human Spirit

Since pre-earth man was organized from spirit and each element of spirit in that organization possessed certain inherent and acquired properties of life, it must follow that there was life throughout the organized spirit. For this reason, Orson Pratt observed:

Some suppose that all our intelligence is in the head. I do not believe any such thing. . . . If the spirit is composed of innumerable particles possessing knowledge or intelligence, we argue that it is diffused through the system in which it dwells. For if the parts of the spirit had individually no knowledge, then they would not have any knowledge collectively. [^47]

This does not mean that the same kind and degree of life existed in all areas of the organized spirit. As Parley P. Pratt has stated, the substance called spirit "exists in an infinitude of degrees in the scale of intellect." [^48] Presumably the elements out of which the human spirit was formed possessed varying degrees of life; and in being organized under the direction of the central primal intelligence of man, they were probably placed within the organized being according to the intelligence they possessed.

A Union of Life in the Human Spirit

From that which has been said in the foregoing paragraphs, it appears that when the human spirit was organized, the elements that composed that organization were able to act and feel and think in free and open union with each other, [^49] under the direction of the central primal intelligence and under a higher law than that which had governed them in their separate and individual state. Living things have the capacity to feel and act in sympathy with each other when there is an element of compatability in their individual natures, when truth is the foundation of their union, and when the benefits of union accrue justly to all who are within the organized system. This fact is demonstrated in the common associations of life from the relationships of mutual friendship to those that prevail in the home and society. The Holy Spirit of God may also have quickened life and promoted union within the organized human spirit. Under the direction of the central primal intelligence the same feelings, thoughts, emotions, and affections were than made to pervade every part of the organized spirit.

Benefits of Organization

Brigham Young and several other leading authorities who had been associated with Joseph Smith expressed the fact that both "the physical and spiritual [i.e., the pre-earth spirit] organization of a human being conferred . . . additional powers or benefits on the creature thus organized." [^50] With the collective organs and powers of the organized spirit under its command, the central primal intelligence of man could progress in ways that had theretofore been denied it. Power and a greater facility to progress are the results of proper organization. These benefits included the power of thought as man now possesses it. Brigham Young said: "The origin of thought was planted in our organization at the beginning of our being. . . . Thought originated with our individual being, which is organized to be as independent as any being in eternity." He thus explained that "matter . . . can be organized and brought forth into intelligence, and to possess more intelligence, and to continue to increase in that intelligence." [^51]

Spirits Unequal

Evidence in modern scripture indicates that spirits were unequal at the time of spirit birth and afterward, although each organized spirit as the offspring of deity had the potential of becoming like God.

The assumption that spirits were unequal at the time of spirit birth is deduced, essentially, from two major points of evidence, the first of which is that the central primal intelligence of man was independent in its sphere of action before it was organized in union with other elements of spirit to become a spirit personage. [^52] Such a state of independence implies that there was a variation of development prior to the time of spirit birth. To be independent in a given sphere is to have agency; and where agency exists, variety develops, resulting in differences and gradations of being. Parley P. Pratt observed:

It may be inquired, why God made one unequal to another, or inferior in intellect or capacity. To which I reply, that he did not create their intelligence at all. It never was created, being an inherent attribute of the eternal element called spirit. [^53]

The second point of evidence is found in a statement the Lord made to Abraham. "These two facts do exist," Jehovah the pre-earth Christ explained, "that there are two spirits, one being more intelligent than the other, there shall be another more intelligent than they; I am more intelligent than they all." [^54] This gradation extended back to a time without beginning. The Lord declared: "If there be two spirits, and one shall be more intelligent than the other, yet these two spirits, notwithstanding one is more intelligent than the other, have no beginning; they existed before, they shall have no end, they shall exist after, for they are gnolaum, or eternal." [^55]

Though the above statement does not indicate that there was an organization of the spirits of men and that in this sense spirits had a beginning, Abraham later states that the Lord had shown him the "intelligence" (or spirits) [^56] that had been organized, which indicates that there was a beginning to their existence as organized spirits. But with apparent reference to the primal life within organized spirits, the Lord declared in the last statement cited in the preceding paragraph that, notwithstanding one spirit was more intelligent than another, they had "no beginning." [^57] Since this is a general statement on the gradation of the intelligence of spirits, without regard for their organization as spirits, it apparently applies to the period before as well as after the organization of the spirit of man in its primeval birth. The basic point in the Lord's explanation to Abraham is that there was a gradation of intelligence among spirits, the life element of which had no beginning, and that this gradation existed before as well as after spirit birth.

The fact that Jehovah was the Firstborn of the Father's many spirit children can hardly be said to be a coincidence. Out of all the spirit children which were born to the Man of Holiness in the pre-earth state, why should Jesus be the Firstborn if not by merit and by design? As later chapters and volumes in this study will indicate, there is great importance placed upon the role of the firstborn son in the divine patriarchal order of society which prevails in the presence of God. The fact that Christ was the Firstborn therefore seems to indicate, first, that a gradation of intelligence prevailed before the time of spirit birth, and second, that Jesus merited the distinction that went with being the Firstborn.

Having made it plain that there was a gradation in the intelligence of spirits in the pre-earth life, the Lord showed Abraham that "among all these [spirits] there were many of the noble and great ones." God saw that these spirits "were good." [^58] As will be shown hereafter, the noble and great spirits in the pre-earth state attained to such intelligence and power that they were called gods. [^59] More will be said of them in this role in later chapters.

God's Spirit Children Placed Upon a Spirit World

Parley P. Pratt expressed the view that after God's spirit children were born they were "matured in the heavenly mansions, trained in the school of love in the family circle, and amid the most tender embraces of parental and fraternal affection." There each individual spirit "lived and moved as a free and rational intelligence, acting upon its own agency, and, like all intelligence, independent in its own sphere." In due time, however, "worlds and mansions composed of spiritual element" were organized, "adapted to their existence and enjoyment," upon which to "transplant them." [^60]

Elder Pratt's statement suggests that sometime after their primeval birth, the spirit children of God left the immediate physical environs of the Father to dwell upon a spirit world adapted to their condition and state of progress. Joseph Smith may have been referring to this change when he explained: "The organization of spiritual and heavenly worlds, and of spiritual and heavenly beings, was agreeable to the most perfect order and harmony: their limits and bounds were fixed irrevocably, and voluntarily subscribed to in their heavenly estate by themselves." [^61] The fact that the spiritual and heavenly beings to which the Prophet referred voluntarily subscribed to the limits and bounds that were set for them on the spiritual and heavenly worlds they went to occupy implies that such a change as mentioned by Elder Pratt may have taken place. [^62]

Though the spirit children of the Father may have changed their residence to a newly organized spirit world that had been created for them, they were still in the presence of God, enveloped by His glory and power; and by means of that divine and pervading intelligence, they were in direct visual and auditory communion with Him.

Man's First Estate

Man's spirit existence is designated scripturally as his "first estate." [^63] During this period, the spirit children of the Father were given continual opportunities to mature in the divine attributes and powers of eternal truth; and, by faithfulness, they were endowed with the Father's glory to the degree they were capable of receiving and utilizing it. It was God's purpose, according to the Prophet, "to institute laws whereby the rest [of the intelligences] could have a privilege to advance like himself." The latter-day seer explained:

The relationship we have with God places us in a situation to advance in knowledge. He has power to institute laws to instruct the weaker intelligences, that they may be exalted with Himself, so that they might have one glory upon another, and all that knowledge, power, glory, and intelligence, which is requisite in order to save them in the world of spirits. [^64]

That they might better be instructed and directed, the spirit children of God were organized under the leadership of the more noble and great ones among them, who were given authority in the presence of God to minister to their fellow spirits. [^65]

Spirits and Glory

The pre-earth spirit of man resided in the presence of the Father, amid the glory of that exalted personage. John Taylor observed that the organized spirit of man "dwelt in eternal light." [^66] And to an inquirer he wrote in somewhat poetic terms: "Knowest thou not that thou art a spark of Deity, struck from the fire of his eternal blaze, and brought forth in the midst of eternal burnings?" [^67]

As explained above, one of the major objectives of God in the plan of life was to glorify His children with those powers of life that are designated as "intelligence, or . . . light and truth," [^68] and to this end the several organizations in the over-all plan of life were directed. Though this goal could not be achieved in its fulness in the pre-earth state, or until the spirit children of the Man of Holiness received the further organizations and endowments of the flesh, developments were then made toward the final attainment of the desired objective. By obedience to the laws of God, spirits were able to acquire a significant degree of glory during the period of their first estate.

It is possible for a spirit personage to be a brilliant or radiant being for two reasons: First, light and spirit are inseparably associated; and since pre-earth man was composed of organized spirit, he was a being of light. Orson Pratt therefore made reference to "the light that enters into the composition of the spirit of man." [^69] Second, by their obedience to the divine plan of life which the Lord gave them, the spirit children of God were endowed to some degree with the truth and light of God's glory. For these two reasons righteous spirits may be personages of transcendent brilliance, depending upon the degree to which they have attained the glory and power of God. Joseph Smith thus observed that in manifesting himself unto man a righteous spirit cannot "come but in glory." Since an organized spirit does not possess a tabernacle of flesh and bones by which the light may be hidden, the Prophet explained that this "is the only way it can appear." [^70]

The fact that pre-earth spirits vary in the glory which they attained is evident from a vision which was given to Lehi in which he saw the coming of Christ and His apostles to begin their ministry on earth. Nephi reported of his father's vision:

. . . it came to pass that he saw one descending out of the midst of heaven, and he beheld that his luster was above that of the sun at noon-day. And he also saw twelve others following him, and their brightness did exceed that of the stars in the firmament. [^71]

Unembodied spirits can also lose the glory they once acquired. Lucifer was probably called a "Son of the Morning" because of the glory and power he possessed. But he fell "from the presence of God" because of rebellion and lost the glory which he had attained." [^72] When Lucifer manifested himself unto Moses, the latter observed: "I could not look upon God [i.e., the pre-earth Christ] in the office and power of the Father [^73], except his glory come upon me, and I were strengthened before him. But I can look upon thee in the natural man." [^74] Because of the distinctions in glory that exist among spirits, Parley P. Pratt explained:

. . . an unembodied spirit, if it be a holy personage, will be surrounded with a halo of resplendent glory, or brightness above the brightness of the sun.

Whereas, spirits not worthy to be glorified will appear without this brilliant halo, and although they often attempt to pass as angels of light, there is more or less of darkness about them. So it is with Satan and his hosts who have not been embodied. [^75]

Although the spirit children of God varied in the intelligence and power they acquired, each according to his desires and interests, they eventually began to level off in the scale of development. According to the divine plan it was necessary for them to be clothed with physical tabernacles so that they might undergo a new series of experiences designed to elevate them further in the scale of life.

Summary

Joseph Smith held that man existed as a conscious entity before birth into mortality, and that the fundamental elements of life within him are coeval with God. He taught that the central principle of life or intelligence within man is a single entity and that its pre-earth existence can be divided into two general periods of time: (1) the period immediately prior to earth life and (2) an earlier period antedating the time of the organization of the human spirit and continuing back to a time without beginning. Primal life is spoken of as being "Spirit, even the Spirit of truth" and "intelligence, or the light of truth." [^76] From authoritative statements on the subject, it appears that the primal life within man is directly associated with that pure substance called spirit, as an inherent attribute within spirit. Eventually the central primal intelligence of man was organized, by means of a primeval birth, with other elements of spirit (with their inherent and acquired intelligence) to form a spirit personage with a form and stature such as man now possesses. In that birth, the organized spirit of man became the literal offspring of divine parents—the Man of Holiness and His glorified and exalted companion—and it had forged into it the divine traits and characteristics of deity. Christ was the firstborn in that primeval birth, and others of the human family were His younger brothers and sisters. By that organization, the central primal life within man was given greater power and facility to progress. In man's first estate, his organized spirit existed amid the glory and power of God in conditions adapted to his state of progress. As an organized being, a spirit could acquire some measure of glory. By disobedience a spirit could also lose glory. But to acquire the full glory and power of God it was necessary for the spirit children of the Man of Holiness to be given physical bodies and be proved in a state of mortal probation.
