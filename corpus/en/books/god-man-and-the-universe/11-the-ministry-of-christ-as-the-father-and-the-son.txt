Hyrum L. Andrus, God, Man, and the Universe ( 1968)

The Ministry of Christ as The Father and The Son

For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and . . . his name shall be called Wonderful, Counseller, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.—ISAIAH.

Among the important insights revealed in the new dispensation are those that give greater light concerning the ministry of Christ as the Father and the Son. These insights are meaningful only when they are viewed in the light of God's glory and power. Not only is a knowledge of the divine nature of God an important key by which man may know how to worship and what to worship, [^1] but it is a vital factor in giving him an understanding of the relationship that Christ has with His Father and by which Christ manifests Himself as both the Father and the Son.

The preceding chapter states that having partaken of the divine nature of the Man of Holiness, Jesus was appointed to develop the living attributes and powers of the Father's glory in others. For this reason He became both God and Father (that is, the Father in certain relationships of life) to man. But in the divine plan of life, Christ is the Son of God in spirit life, in physical life, and in the attributes and powers of eternal life. Jesus therefore may be referred to properly as both the Father and the Son; and He manifests Himself to man both as the Father and the Son.

Basic Factors Concerning The Fatherhood of Christ

Christ's Heirship the Basis of His Fatherhood

Being the Firstborn of the Man of Holiness in pre-earth spirit life, Christ became the Heir of His Father; and as such, He was given the right to inherit all that that exalted being possesses. [^2] This privilege came to Him naturally as His birthright in the spirit family of the Man of Holiness. In speaking of this earth, Joseph Smith said: "Jesus Christ is the heir of this kingdom." [^3] William W. Phelps referred to the Firstborn as "the heir of all things." [^4] And Brigham Young said: "Jesus Christ, the first-begotten of the Father, of all the rest of the children, and of all they possess, alone is the lawful heir." [^5]

Christ's role as the Father seems to have commenced in the pre-earth life when He was appointed to be the Heir of His Father. [^6] Having inherited [^7] that which the Man of Holiness possesses, including His glory and power, and having been appointed to give these things to His younger brothers and sisters in the spirit family, Jesus then made it possible for others to become joint-heirs with Him in the Father's kingdom, by inheriting through Him that which the Man of Holiness possesses. In this way, a younger member of the pre-earth spirit family of Ahman could inherit that which Christ had previously inherited. With this view, Joseph Smith spoke of man becoming "a joint-heir of the heirship of the Son." [^8] In fulfilling His obligation as the Heir of the Father, Jesus commenced to act as both God and Father to others in the divine relationship of life that leads to immortal glory. His position as the Heir of His Father thus became the basis of His Godhood and of His divine Fatherhood.

Christ Manifested as the Father and the Son

Because there was centered in Christ the divine mind, glory, and power of the Father, Elohim, Jesus had a dual purpose in coming to earth. To Nephi, the grandson of Helaman, He declared: "Behold, I come unto my own, to . . . do the will, both of the Father and of the Son—of the Father because of me, and of the Son because of my flesh." [^9] Those who beheld Him on earth therefore beheld the Father as well as the Son. That is, they beheld a being who exactly resembled the Man of Holiness in physical features and stature, [^10] and in whom dwelt the divine nature of the Father, Elohim. Jesus thus explained: "No man knoweth the Son, but the Father; neither knoweth any man the Father, save the Son, and they to whom the Son will reveal himself; they shall see the Father also." [^11] Again: "All things are delivered to me of my Father; and no man knoweth that the Son is the Father, and the Father is the Son, but him to whom the Son will reveal it." [^12]

Jesus was conceived in the flesh with the powers and attributes of eternal life within him; [^13] and having matured in both the physical and the divine attributes and powers of His Father's nature, it was said that He was in the brightness of His Father's glory and the express image of His person. [^14] Jesus was the living manifestation of God—even of the Father—unto men. When Philip exclaimed, "Lord, shew us the Father, and it sufficeth us," Christ declared:

Have I been so long time with you, and yet hast thou not known me, Philip? he that hath seen me hath seen the Father; and how sayest thou then, Shew us the Father? Believest thou not that I am in the Father, and the Father in me? the words that I speak unto you I speak not of myself; but the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works. [^15]

As indicated in the previous chapters, Jesus bore witness to Philip of the relationship in glory which He had with His Father. Christ was in the Father, and the Father was in Him; and because the mind, glory, and power of the Man of Holiness dwelt in the Son, Christ was the Father. If Philip had known this, would he have exclaimed, "Lord, shew us the Father?" for the Father was manifest to man in the person of Jesus. Christ therefore explained, "The words that I speak unto you I speak not of myself: but the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works." [^16]

The questions may be asked, From whence did Jesus obtain power on earth to heal the sick and raise the dead? From whence came His power to comand and control the elements? And from what source did He obtain divine intelligence to confound the learned and speak the words of life to all ages of time? The scriptures indicate that by His divine conception in the flesh, there was planted within Christ the Holy Spirit (or mind), power, and glory of His Father. Because the Father was in Christ and Christ was in the Father in the indwelling relationship of eternal life, the words that Jesus spoke were not His own. It was the Father within Him that did the works which He performed on earth. [^17]

Christ as the Father and the Son is the One God of Scripture

As the Father and the Son, Christ is the one God to whom the scriptures make reference. As a branch of the House of Israel in ancient times, the Nephites had great respect for the writings of Isaiah who expressed the doctrine that the Redeemer would be born as a Son but would be called the Mighty God and the Everlasting Father. [^18] The Nephite prophet Abinadi explained this doctrine in the following prophetic statement:

. . . I would that ye should understand that God himself shall come down among the children of men, and shall redeem his people. And because he dwelleth in flesh he shall be called the Son of God, and having subjected the flesh to the will of the Father, being the Father and the Son—the Father, because he was conceived by the power of God; and the Son, because of the flesh; thus becoming the Father and Son—and they [i.e., the indwelling mind, glory, and power of the Father tabernacled in the flesh as the Son] are one God, yea, the very Eternal Father of heaven and of earth.

And thus the flesh becoming subject to the Spirit, or the Son to the Father, being one God, suffereth temptation, and yieldeth not to the temptation, but suffereth himself to be mocked, and scourged, and cast out, and disowned by his people. . . . Yea, even so he shall be led, crucified, and slain, the flesh becoming subject even unto death, the will of the Son [i.e., Christ's will as pertaining to the flesh] being swallowed up in the will of the Father [i.e., the will of the indwelling intelligence of the Father]. And thus God breaketh the bands of death. . . . [^19]

God himself came down among men in the person of the great Redeemer. Since the Son without the glory and power of the Father is not God, it follows that it was Christ as the Father and the Son who manifested Himself among men. Because he dwelt in the flesh He was the Son, [^20] for He was subordinate to His Father in acquiring the endowments of physical life. But He was conceived by the power of God, and thereby the divine nature of the Father, Elohim, was planted in embryo within Him on earth. Thus Christ was the Father and the Son; and they—the mind, glory, and power of the Father embodied in the flesh as Christ—were one God, even the very Eternal Father of heaven and of earth. Here is the one God to whom reference is made throughout the canon of scripture.

Expressions Of Christ's Fatherhood

The Scope of Christ's Fatherhood

In the normal processes of life, sons in turn become fathers. But as the Son of God in all of the three basic stages of life, Christ is not the father of all men in all aspects of the divine plan that lead to eternal life. In spirit life, both Christ and man were the offspring of the Man of Holiness, and Jesus is revealed as the Firstborn and the Heir of the Father.

In physical life, Christ was the Son of God, while all others who come to this temporal sphere are born of mortal fathers. For this reason, Jesus was revealed only as the Son of God in the flesh and not as the Father of all men in this relationship of life. Adam is the common father of all men in physical life. [^21]

Finally, Jesus was the Son of God in the divine attributes and powers of eternal life. But having received the quickening, regenerating, and life-endowed Spirit from the Man of Holiness, as the Firstborn and Heir of that exalted being, Christ was appointed to be the light and the life of the world—the light of life to all others of His Father's spirit children. In this sense Christ, as the Son of God, is in turn the Father of all men. The attributes and powers of the Father's divine nature were developed first in Jesus, making Him the Son of God in glory; [^22] and from Him, as their most immediate source, those divine attributes and powers of life were extended to others. Having become the Son of God in glory, Christ is the Father of all others in this divine relationship of life.

Christ Speaks as the Father

There are various ways in which Christ manifests Himself to man as the Father. He possesses the right by divine investiture of authority to reveal and express to others the divine truth which He receives from the Man of Holiness. In speaking as the Father, Jesus is not acting merely as a legal representative of the Man of Holiness in manifesting that superior being unto others, as one person may act with legal power of attorney for another. Because the divine mind, glory, and power of the Father dwell in Christ, [^23] the Man of Holiness is in Him; and the Father manifests Himself in a literal sense unto others through Christ, who has authority to speak and act as that primary being. [^24]

In fulfilling His divine commission as the God of this earth, Jesus speaks at times for Himself as the Son of God; at other times He speaks for the Father, as though He were the Father, with no apparent effort to clarify His identity to the contrary. But it is Christ who is speaking and expressing Himself in both capacities. All things have been delivered unto Him of His Father, [^25] so that no man can come unto the Father except through Him. [^26] This means that all revelations given to man in his mortal state are given through Christ. Though the Father, Elohim, may manifest Himself unto man in His own person, He honors the divine commission that He has given to the Son. Consequently the Man of Holiness has never dealt directly and personally with man in mortality except to bear witness of His Son. John's testimony as given in Joseph Smith's Inspired Revision of the Bible declares: "And no man hath seen God at any time, except he hath borne record of the Son; for except it is through him no man can be saved." [^27] For example, in Joseph Smith's First Vision, the Father, Elohim, took the lead in addressing the Prophet and declared, as He introduced Jesus: "This is my Beloved Son, Hear Him!" [^28] Christ then answered Joseph's inquiry.

Other illustrations of Christ speaking as the Father may be found in modern scripture. In a revelation to Joseph Smith, He declared: "Thus saith the Lord; for I am God, and have sent mine Only Begotten Son into the world for the redemption of the world, and have decreed that he that receiveth him shall be saved, and he that receiveth him not shall be damned." Though it seems that the Father is speaking, the revelation concludes with the declaration: "Behold, I am Jesus Christ, and I come quickly." [^29] Christ therefore was speaking as the Father. In another revelation, the Lord introduced Himself as "Jesus Christ, your Redeemer, the Great I AM." But toward the close of the revelation, He spoke of the fall of Adam and explained: "I, the Lord God, gave unto Adam and unto his seed, that they should not die as to the temporal death, until I, the Lord God, should send forth angels to declare unto them repentance and redemption through faith on the name of mine Only Begotten Son." [^30] Again Christ was speaking as the Father and the Son.

A revelation to Joseph Smith reported that Christ appeared to the ancient patriarch Enoch as the Father and the Son and revealed His future mission on earth. When Enoch beheld the earthly ministry of Christ and His subsequent ascension into heaven, he exclaimed to the personage who stood before him: "Forasmuch as thou art God, and I know thee, and thou hast sworn unto me, and commanded me that I should ask in the name of thine Only Begotten, . . . wherefore, I ask thee [i.e., the Father] if thou [i.e., Christ, the Father and the Son] wilt not come again on the earth." The divine being whom Enoch addressed as the Father then promised: "As I live, even so will I [i.e., Christ] come in the last days, in the days of wickedness and vengeance, to fulfil the oath which I have made unto you concerning the children of Noah." [^31]

This principle of divine investiture of authority is used similarly on a lower level by angels who have a right to speak and act as Christ in fulfilling the duties of their ministry. Though the angel who gave the revelation to John on the isle of Patmos twice enjoined the beloved Apostle not to worship him, he nevertheless spoke as if he were Christ, stating: "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last." [^32] In like manner, Stephen reported that the Lord spoke unto Moses out of the burning bush, declaring: "I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." Yet the early Christian martyr continued his report by stating that the message was actually delivered to Moses "by the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the bush." [^33] When Jehovah withdrew in person from leading rebellious Israel through the wilderness, He sent an angel to take His place and warned the Israelites: "Beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for my name is in him." [^34] Jehovah would speak to Israel by and through this angel, for the Lord continued: "But if thou shalt indeed obey his voice, and do all that I speak; then I will be an enemy unto thine enemies, and an adversary unto thine adversaries." [^35]

Christ the Father of Heaven and Earth

Jesus is the Father of heaven [^36] and of earth, and of all things therein because the divine creative and quickening powers of life within all things are centered in Him. In each of the before mentioned stages of life man has a father. Elohim is the Father of the organized spirit of man. The physical attributes and powers of man are received from his mortal parents. And Christ is the Father of the quickening powers of truth, light, and life that dwell within man. Jesus is the Father because He is "the light, and the life, and the truth of the world." [^37] And in a revelation to Joseph Smith, Christ stated: "I am the true light that is in you, and . . . you are in me; otherwise ye could not abound." [^38]

To be the father of the divine quickening and sustaining powers of life by which all things are organized and given life is a parental function as vital and meaningful as being the father of physical attributes and endowments of life.

Since Christ is the source of the divine powers of life within all things, the Nephite prophets repeatedly testified that He is the Father of heaven and of earth, and of all things therein. Nephi said of the coming of the Redeemer:

. . . when the day cometh that the Only Begotten of the Father, yea, even the Father of heaven and of earth, shall manifest himself unto them in the flesh, behold, they will reject him. . . . [^39]

Of Christ, King Benjamin declared:

And he shall be called Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Father of heaven and earth, the Creator of all things from the beginning; and his mother shall be called Mary. [^40]

Having declared that the glory and power of the Father embodied in Christ constituted "one God, yea, the very Eternal Father of heaven and of earth," [^41] Abinadi admonished some corrupt priests:

And now, ought ye not to tremble and repent of your sins, and remember that only in and through Christ ye can be saved?

Therefore, if ye teach the law of Moses, also teach that it is a shadow of those things which are to come—

Teach them that redemption cometh through Christ the Lord, who is the very Eternal Father. [^42]

After Abinadi had been martyred for his testimony of Christ, King Limhi observed:

. . . because he said unto them that Christ was the God, the Father of all things, and said that he should take upon him the image of man . . . and that God should come down among the children of men, and take upon him flesh and blood, and go forth upon the face of the earth—

And now, because he said this, they did put him to death. . . . [^43]

As the birth of Christ drew nigh, Samuel the Lamanite prophet went forth proclaiming repentance to the people, saying:

. . . for this intent have I come upon the walls of this city, . . . that ye might know of the coming of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Father of heaven and of earth, the creator of all things from the beginning. . . . [^44]

Christ said to Moroni as He spoke of the sacred revelations that were given in ancient times and their manifestation to man the last days:

. . . in that day that they [i.e., men in the latter days] shall exercise faith in me, saith the Lord, even as the brother of Jared did, that they may become sanctified in me, then will I manifest unto them the things which the brother of Jared saw, even to the unfolding unto them all my revelations, saith Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Father of the heavens and of the earth, and all things that in them are. [^45]

An illustration of the Nephite view of Christ as the Father of heaven and of earth is found in the teachings of Amulek at the city of Ammonihah. As he and Alma were proclaiming the Gospel to the people of that city, they were accosted by a group of lawyers. Zeezrom, the chief figure among them, interrogated Amulek: "Thou sayest there is a true and living God?"

Amulek answered, "Yea, there is a true and living God."

Zeezrom then questioned, "Is there more than one God?"

Amulek replied, "No."

"How knowest thou these things?" the lawyer inquired.

Amulek said, "An angel hath made them known unto me."

Zeezrom asked, "Who is he that shall come? Is it the Son of God?"

Without hesitation, the Nephite teacher answered, "Yea."

After a discussion on the saving mission of Christ, Zeezrom finally inquired: "Is the Son of God the very Eternal Father?"

And to this question Amulek replied: "Yea, he is the very Eternal Father of heaven and of earth, and all things which in them are; he is the beginning and the end, the first and the last; and he shall come into the world to redeem his people." [^46]

The teachings of Amulek reflect the doctrine expressed consistently throughout the Nephite Scripture, that for man on earth there is but one God, and that this God is the Son of God; and the Son of God is the very Eternal Father of heaven and earth and of all things therein.

Christ the Father of Eternal Life

In a higher and more select spiritual relationship, Jesus is the Father of those who acquire the living, regenerating, and sanctifying powers that lead to eternal life. In their general or elementary manifestations, these divine powers quicken the life within all things upon the earth. But by exercising living faith in Christ and by obeying His Gospel, man may attain the powers of the Holy Spirit in their higher and more intelligent manifestations; and in receiving these living powers which constitute the glory of God, the faithful become the sons and daughters of Jesus Christ. By following the great Redeemer in the spiritual regeneration that leads to eternal glory and power, [^47] they become in a literal sense new creatures in Christ, and He becomes their Father in this glorious state of existence. [^48]

Though Jesus was the Son of a glorified Father who had power to plant within Him in His conception in the flesh that divine substance of truth, light, and life which is called glory, men in general are conceived in their fallen mortal state of parents who do not have power to plant the divine nature of God within their offspring at conception. To obtain the divine attributes and powers of God unto eternal life, men in general must subscribe to the articles of adoption (i.e., the First Principles and Ordinances of the Gospel) by which they may become sons and daughters of Jesus Christ. Jesus then becomes the Father of those who truly believe in Him, by developing His glory and power within them; and by continuing to mature in this new and glorious stage of life, the faithful may grow up to receive a fulness of the divine stature or image of Christ. Jesus referred to this aspect of His divine Fatherhood when He declared to an ancient prophet: "I am the Father and the Son. In me shall all mankind have light, and that eternally, even they who shall believe on my name; and they shall become my sons and my daughters." [^49]

The divine plan by which the faithful may become sons and daughters of Jesus Christ—sons and daughters of God—in the attributes and powers of eternal life will be discussed in detail in the next volume of this work. The infinite atonement which Christ made is the foundation of this divine plan of life. A revelation states that Jesus "so loved the world that he gave his own life, that as many as would believe might become the sons of God." [^50]

The view that Christ's Fatherhood is founded upon His atonement was stressed by Abinadi in an inspired commentary upon Isaiah's declaration concerning the earthly mission of the Lord. Having explained that Christ came to earth as both the Father and the Son, the Nephite prophet said of Him and His atoning mission:

. . . who shall declare his generation? For he was cut off out of the land of the living; for the transgressions of my people was he stricken. And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death; because he had done no evil, neither was any deceit in his mouth. Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise him; he hath put him to grief; when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the Lord shall prosper in his hand. He shall see the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied; by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities. Therefore will I divide him a portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death; and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bore the sins of many, and made intercession for the transgressors. . . .

And now I say unto you, who shall declare his generation? Behold I say unto you, that when his soul has been made an offering for sin he shall see his seed. And now what say ye? And who shall be his seed?

Behold I say unto you, that whosoever has heard the words of the prophets, yea, all the holy prophets who have prophesied concerning the coming of the Lord—I say unto you, that all those who have hearkened unto their words, and believed that the Lord would redeem his people, and have looked foward to that day for a remission of their sins. I say unto you, that these are his seed, or they are the heirs of the kingdom of God. For these are they whose sins he has borne; these are they for whom he has died, to redeem them from their transgressions. And now, are they not his seed? [^51]

In making the atonement, Christ opened the way into a new stage of life and became the Father of all who receive eternal life. [^52] But man must be born into the kingdom of heaven to acquire the divine attributes and powers of this new state of life. Those who are truly born again become members of the family of Christ and take upon themselves the name of Jesus Christ. When the people of King Benjamin covenanted to fulfil the requirements of the Gospel, the Nephite ruler gave them the name of Christ and explained: "Because of the covenant which ye have made, ye shall be called the children of Christ, his sons, and his daughters; for behold, this day he hath spiritually begotten you; for ye say that your hearts are changed through faith on his name; therefore, ye are born of him and have become his sons and his daughters." [^53] By revelation, the Lord also explained to Joseph Smith:

Hearken and listen to the voice of him who is from all eternity to all eternity, the Great I AM, even Jesus Christ—

The light and the life of the world; a light which shineth in darkness and the darkness comprehendeth it not;

The same which came in the meridian of time unto mine own, and mine own received me not;

But to as many as received me, gave I power to become my sons; and even so will I give unto as many as will receive me, power to become my sons. [^54]

This statement indicates that man must be a recipient of divine power—the power of the Holy Spirit—which is given to him from Christ if he is to become a son of the great Redeemer. Jesus gives divine power of truth and light to those who truly receive Him, that they may become His sons. Man cannot enter into that new stage of life that leads ultimately to celestial glory unless he is regenerated and transformed by the power of Jesus Christ. Only when he receives the transforming powers of the Holy Spirit is man truly a son of God, established on the path that leads to eternal life. [^55]

Clarifications Concerning Christ As The Father

Man Should Pray to the Father, Elohim, in the Name of Christ

Having begun to see the relationship in glory which Christ has to His Father, some may query: If Christ is the Father, to whom should man pray? The scriptures declare that man should call upon the Father, Elohim, and worship that primary being in the name of Jesus Christ. [^56] The fact that Christ manifests Himself as the Father in the ways indicated in this chapter does not alter this commandment. But in order to see and appreciate this fact, it is necessary to keep certain points in mind:

First, the Man of Holiness is the ultimate source of divine truth and power for all things within His vast domain. He resides in His heavenly abode in the midst of His glory and power; and man should worship Him in Spirit and in truth by communing with Him through the medium of His divine intelligence or glory which pervades all things.

Second, the Father, Elohim, has extended His divine nature to Christ and developed it within Christ, and through Him the Man of Holiness is revealed to others. Because the mind, glory, and power (the Spirit, glory, and power) of the Father, Elohim, dwell in Christ, these two separate glorified beings are made perfectly one in intelligence and power; and they are intuitively in tune with each other—in perfect intelligent and visual union with each other. That which the Father, Elohim, knows and perceives and has power to do the Son knows and perceives and has power to do. For these reasons Christ is able to speak and act and be as the Father. But more than this, it is the Father who dwells in Christ who speaks and acts. [^57] As previously indicated, the works of the Father were manifest on earth through Christ. [^58]

Third, the fact that the glory of the Father, Elohim, dwells as an intelligent substance in Christ, manifesting in Him and through Him the mind, glory, and power of the Man of Holiness, suggests that under some circumstances there may be an extension (not an alteration) of the established procedure of prayer. If the Father is in Christ in the way that has been indicated above, it may be said in one respect that the Father is in two places. He exists in His own individual person; and by the fact that His divine mind, glory, and power center in Christ, the Man of Holiness is in Christ, making that secondary being also the Father. Nevertheless the Father, Elohim, and Jesus are two separate corporeal beings.

In light of the glorified relationship that exists between the Man of Holiness and His Only Begotten Son, the question may be asked, Is man always required to address the person of the Father, Elohim, directly in the name of Jesus Christ, or may he on some occasions address as the Father that secondary being in whom dwell the glory and power of the Man of Holiness? Scriptural precedents seem to indicate that the latter approach is permissible under proper circumstances. It has been shown, for example, that the patriarch Enoch addressed the Father in Christ in the name of the Only Begotten Son. [^59]

It should be stressed that Enoch was addressing the Father in his supplication, even though it was the Father that dwelt in Christ and was manifest through Christ. He was not praying to Christ alone, or to Christ as the Son. "Thou art God," the partiarch declared to the personage who stood before him, "and . . . thou hast . . . commanded me that I should ask in the name of thine Only Begotten." [^60] Thus the established formula of prayer remained the same, even though there was an extension of that formula in recognition of the fact that the glory of the Man of Holiness dwelt in Christ and was manifest through Him.

The Book of Mormon reports a similar incident which occurred during Christ's ministry to the Western hemisphere. Having obeyed the Gospel and become His sons and daughters, the Nephites prayed "unto Jesus, calling him their Lord and their God." [^61] That this was not a general practice, however, is indicated by the statement which Christ made to His Father as He acknowledged their prayers. "They pray unto me," He reverently observed, "because I am with them." [^62] It therefore may be suggested that when Jesus manifests Himself personally to man, revealing the glory and power of God unto him in a direct manner, man may address the Father in Christ or pray to Christ as the God of this earth. But this latter procedure seems to be reserved for such special occasions. Nevertheless, such an approach is not a departure from the established order of prayer, but an extension of that order to suit the situation. On such occasions, Christ is the most accessible source of the Father. Why should not those who understand the doctrine of deity and man's relationship to God address the Father in Christ? For as Jesus implied in His statement to Philip, He is the living revelation and manifestation of the Father unto men. [^63]

The Holy Ghost Speaks for Christ—the Father and the Son

Since Jesus Christ is the light and the life of the world and since, in addition, He is the Father of the regenerating powers of life that lead to celestial glory, it must follow that the spiritual powers that are manifest unto man through that personage called the Holy Ghost are centered first in Christ and are dispensed by His authority as the God of the earth. This means that the power which the Holy Ghost manifests as a member of the Godhead is the power of Jesus Christ, or the power that the Man of Holiness has centered in Christ and which the Father, Elohim, reveals through Christ. For this reason Jesus said of the third member of the Godhead:

He shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you. All things that the Father hath are mine: therefore said I, that he shall take of mine, and shall shew it unto you. [^64]

As the third member of the Godhead, the Holy Ghost has power to speak as Christ—the Father and the Son—in witnessing, testifying, and dispensing the truth and power of Christ unto man. As an illustration of this point, consider the following report of His manifestation unto Adam: "And in that day the Holy Ghost fell upon Adam, which beareth record of the Father and the Son, saying I [i.e., the Father and the Son] am the Only Begotten of the Father from the beginning, henceforth and forever, that as thou hast fallen thou mayest be redeemed, and all mankind, even as many as will." [^65] In bearing record of the Father and the Son, the Holy Ghost declared: "I am the Only Begotten of the Father." [^66]

In revealing Himself to man, Christ may do so through the Holy Ghost, who has authority to manifest Jesus both as the Father and the Son. To the Saints on the day the Church was organized in this dispensation, Christ explained that the truth and power which they received from God were given unto them "through me by the Comforter." [^67] This apparently means that when Christ speaks as the Father and the Son in some revelations, [^68] it is actually the Holy Ghost manifesting the will of Christ as the Father and the Son.

This does not mean, however, that the Holy Ghost is confined to the role of bearing testimony of Christ as the Father, or of the Father in Christ, or of Christ as the Father and the Son. The mission of the Holy Ghost is to guide man into all truth, [^69] and the fundamental truth concerning the Father, Elohim, and the Son is that they are two separate and distinct corporeal beings. But it is given unto Christ to reveal the Father; and it is through the Holy Ghost that Jesus reveals knowledge pertaining to Himself and the Man of Holiness unto man on earth.

In Christ Dwells the Fulness of the Godhead

Joseph Smith taught the doctrine that in Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. [^70] The term "Godhead" in Latter-day Saint literature generally has reference to the three divine personages—the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost—who hold the keys of life and power over the earth. But the statement that in Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily means that in Him is centered the full intelligence, power, and authority of the three personages in the Godhead; and that under Christ's authority the truth and power of the three personages are dispensed to men on earth. [^71] For this reason, the word "Godhead" is used at times as a singular term denoting Christ and the divine essence or substance of truth and power that is centered in Him. Parley P. Pratt wrote that the Light of Christ in its several and varied manifestations is "continually flowing from the Godhead throughout all His creations." [^72] In a letter to William Frye describing the scroll or record of the patriarch Joseph, [^73] Oliver Cowdery observed: "The representation of the Godhead—three, yet in one, is curiously drawn to give simply, though impressively, the writer's views of that exalted personage." [^74]

A similar view is expressed in the Book of Mormon when Amulek (while stressing that there is but one God, even Jesus Christ, the very Eternal Father of heaven and earth) declares that in the judgment men will be "arraigned before the bar of Christ the Son, and God the Father, and the Holy Spirit, which is one God, to be judged according to their works." [^75] The singular phrase "is one God," as distinct from the plural phrase "are one God," which Amulek would have used to denote three distinct persons, implies that the singular person, Christ, has centered in Him the full intelligence, power, and authority of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost. For Amulek to have meant otherwise would have been a denial of his positive assertion that there is but one God. It would also deny the doctrine consistently taught in the Book of Mormon that Christ is "the great Jehovah, the Eternal Judge of both quick and dead." [^76] The scriptures nowhere state that the three separate personages of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost will judge mankind, for all such matters are committed into the hands of Christ. [^77]

There are other instances in modern scripture where the singular phrase "is one God" is used with apparent reference to the power and authority of the Godhead which are centered in Christ. As Nephi explained the plan of life and salvation, he concluded: "And now, behold, this is the doctrine of Christ, and the only and true doctrine of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost which is one God, without end." [^78] In speaking of the final state of a righteous individual, Mormon wrote that it will be "given unto him to dwell in the presence of God in his kingdom, to sing ceaseless praises with the choirs above, unto the Father, and unto the Son, and unto the Holy Ghost, which is one God, in a state of happiness which hath no end." [^79] The three witnesses, in attesting to the divine authenticity of the Book of Mormon, concluded their testimony with the statement: "And the honor be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, which is one God." [^80] Finally, the Prophet wrote by revelation, that "the Holy Ghost . . . beareth record of the Father and of the Son; which Father, Son, and Holy Ghost is one God, infinite and eternal." [^81]

(The Book of Mormon also states that "the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Ghost are one." [^82] Here the apparent meaning is that by means of the glory they possess, these three separate and distinct personages are as one mechanism. The Son obeys the Father in all things; and Christ possesses the full glory and power of the Man of Holiness. By means of His divine nature, the Father is therefore in the Son; and the Son is in the Father. The Holy Ghost speaks not of Himself, nor does He manifest His own power to man; but that which He receives from the Father and the Son He reveals to others. He is subordinate to Christ; yet He is one with the Father and the Son in all that He does. In this way, the persons of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost are one. They are united in purpose. But their union has its basis in the glory which the Father centers in Christ and which Christ extends to the Holy Ghost.)

The fact that Christ has received the Father's glory is sufficient reason why the fulness of the Godhead is centered in Him and why He is the God of this earth and the Father of all things that dwell thereon. [^83] Since the power and authority which the Holy Ghost possesses are derived from Christ, that power and authority do not add to that which Jesus receives from the Father. The fulness of the Father centers in Jesus, which in itself constitutes all the rights, glory, and power of the Godhead.

The view of Christ as Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end—that in Him center all the fulness of the Godhead, including the names of the respective personages of deity—is expressed in the injunction that man should be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, i.e., in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost. Jesus instructed the Nephites:

Verily I say unto you, that whoso repenteth of his sins through your words and desireth to be baptized in my name, on this wise shall ye baptize them—Behold, ye shall go down and stand in the water, and in my name shall ye baptize them. And now behold, these are the words which ye shall say, calling them by name, saying:

Having authority given me of Jesus Christ, I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. Amen.

And then shall ye immerse them in the water, and come forth again out of the water. And after this manner shall ye baptize in my name. [^84]

To be baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost is to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, in the full concept of the power and honor of His divine majesty and Godhead. The words of the sacred covenant of baptism by which man initially accepts Christ thus express the central doctrine that has been considered in this chapter.

Summary

According to the revelations given to the Prophet Joseph Smith, Christ is both our God and, in some relationships of life, our Father because He is the heir of the Man of Holiness. Having inherited that which the Father, Elohim, possesses, including His glory and power, and having been appointed to give these things to His young brothers and sisters in the spirit family of Ahman, Jesus then made it possible for others to become joint-heirs with Him in the Father's kingdom. In extending the divine nature to others, Christ speaks and acts as the Father. Because the divine mind, glory, and power of the Father, Elohim, dwell in Christ, the Man of Holiness can manifest Himself to man through Christ. For this reason Jesus has the dual purpose of doing the will both of the Father and the Son; and in the revelation of Himself in this dual capacity, He is the one God revealed in the scriptures. Christ speaks the word of truth to man, and here He acts as the Father. He extends His glory in its elementary manifestations to organize and quicken the life within all things, and here He is the Father of heaven and earth and all things therein. To those who exercise living faith in Him, Jesus gives the higher spiritual powers that renew and regenerate life; and here He becomes the Father of all those who attain eternal life. For this reason, in particular, Christ made the atonement, that others might become His sons and daughters in celestial glory. Nevertheless, the Father, Elohim, is the primary source of life in the divine program, and man should worship the Man of Holiness in the name of Jesus Christ. As a divine being, the Holy Ghost is subordinate to Christ and reveals to man the truth and power which the Father, Elohim, has given to Christ. In Christ center the full powers, attributes, and authority of the Godhead; and for this reason, the term "Godhead" refers at times in a singular sense to Christ.
