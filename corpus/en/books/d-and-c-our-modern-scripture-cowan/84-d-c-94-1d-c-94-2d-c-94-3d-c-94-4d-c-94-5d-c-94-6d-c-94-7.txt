Richard O. Cowan, Doctrine and Covenants, Our Modern Scripture ( 1984)

D&C 94-96: The Building Up of Kirtland

During the spring of 1833, the brethren were concerned with the building up of the city of Kirtland. These three revelations were given at that time to explain various phases of the work. D&C 95 encouraged Church members to respond favorably to the appeal for funds to build the temple. D&C 96 settled a disagreement by assigning the bishop to take charge of certain properties held by the Church in Kirtland.

148Three Important Buildings. During the previous December, the Lord had directed the erection of the Kirtland Temple. (See D&C 88:119.) Now, however, the brethren were giving more attention to the erection of two other buildings, a "house for the presidency" and a printing establishment, to be located respectively on the first and second lots south of the temple, each building having upper and lower rooms measuring 55 by 65 feet. (See D&C 94:3-12.) Even though the Lord approved these plans, the buildings were not to be erected until he gave further commandments concerning them. (See D&C 94:16.)

149 D&C 95 directed the elders' attention again to the temple, which was the most important building they should construct. The temple was to be built following the same general pattern as the two buildings above, the lower room being devoted to sacrament, fast, and other worship meetings, and the upper room being used for the "school of mine apostles." (D&C 95:13-17.)

149Several uses and purposes of the temple were suggested in these revelations. The apostles were to be prepared for their ministry by receiving an endowment of power from on high. (See D&C 95:4, 8.)

149The Lord's "Strange Act" (D&C 95:1-4). The phrase "strange act" apparently is an allusion to Isa. 28:21. To the world, for example, it may seem strange for the Lord to chasten and rebuke those whom he loves; nevertheless, this is done for their own good.
