Analysis and Summary

As one scholar notes,

The millennial era does not end, as one might expect, with the earth and its inhabitants moving smoothly into eternal glory. Instead, it ends in horror. With a saddened heart, the prophet Mormon records how a small group of people revolted from the Church around AD 150 (4 Ne. 1:20). By that time, wealth had become both widespread and abundant. Some could not handle it. They grew in pride and that drove them to want greater wealth and, for some, greater power. Ostentation began to abound. The effect on the society as a whole was twofold: goods and substance were no longer held common, and class distinction was initiated and promoted. Ironically, at the same time there was a proliferation of churches. However, the record shows that these were not built up to serve God but for getting gain, and lots of it. Analysis and Summary

According to the model found in Revelation, only after Michael destroys Satan does the final judgment commence. With the blindness of satanic delusion gone, all men and women, both small and great, will stand to face Christ. John shows us two judgment scenes and two bases of judgment.

The first to be judged are the righteous. All these will be âjudged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their worksâ (20:12). The basis of their judgment will consist of what is recorded in the books on earth and in heaven. John sees two sets of records. The first he refers to as âbooks,â and the second as âthe book of life.â Speaking of these two sets of records, Joseph Smith wrote, âThe books spoken of must be the books which contained the record of their works, and refer to the records which are kept on the earth. And the book which was the book of life is the record which is kept in heavenâ (D&C 128:7). He went on to explain that John actually had reference to priesthood sealing ordinances in which

whatsoever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatsoever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Or, in other words, taking a different view of the translation, whatsoever you record on earth shall be recorded in heaven, and whatsoever you do not record on earth shall not be recorded in heaven; for out of the books shall your dead be judged, according to their own works, whether they themselves have attended to the ordinances in their own propria persona, or by the means of their own agents, according to the ordinance which God has prepared for their salvation from before the foundation of the world, according to the records which they have kept concerning their dead. (D&C 128:8)

Those people are, then, the ones who accepted the gospel while either on earth or in the spirit world and were sealed by the power of the priesthood. They will come under the Lordâs domain and receive everlasting life.

After the first resurrection is complete, John sees the sea (the old home of chaos and misery), along with death and hell, give up the dead that are in them. The time of the second resurrection has come. Those upon whom this judgment comes are they whose names are found in neither the books on earth nor those in heaven. The sealing power never covered them, and therefore most of them âremain separately and singly, without exaltation, in their saved condition, to all eternity; and from henceforth are not gods, but are angels of God forever and everâ (D&C 132:17). There is, however, one last group who will not even qualify to be Godâs angels. Painting with a very broad brush, the Lord has declared, âBefore the earth shall pass away, Michael, mine archangel, shall sound his trump, and then shall all the dead awake, for their graves shall be opened, and they shall come forthâyea, even all. And the righteous shall be gathered on my right hand unto eternal life; and the wicked on my left hand will I be ashamed to own before the Father; wherefore I will say unto themâDepart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angelsâ (D&C 29:26â28).

A somber note ends this declaration, but for the vast majority of people, neither sorrow nor lamentation cast a pall on the joy of the moment. This judgment does not mark the beginning of torment, but its end. The misery of death and the pain of hell cannot endure the divine flame emanating from grace and love. All who pay the uttermost farthing shall come out of captivity into a kingdom of glory (Matt. 5:26).

Nonetheless, John concludes this vision with a note of warning: he whose name âwas not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fireâ (20:15). There are those who deny grace and reject Christ, who, in their rebellion, refuse the power of repentance and spurn the love of God. The Savior spoke of the âeternal fire prepared for the devil and his angelsâ (Matt. 25:41). Gehenna was not prepared for men, only for demons.59 But those people who become devils and angels to the devil must partake of that punishment. This is the second death. It is visited only upon those who can go through the purifying fires of hellâwhere all but the most hardened find humility and acceptanceâand remain âfilthy still.â These are âvessels of wrath, doomed to suffer the wrath of God, with the devil and his angels in eternityâ (D&C 76:33). Becoming like Satan, they have no redemption. Anyone who âbreaketh a law, and abideth not by law, but seeketh to become a law unto itself, and willeth to abide in sin, and altogether abideth in sin, cannot be sanctified by law, neither by mercy, justice, nor judgment. Therefore, they must remain filthy stillâ (D&C 88:35). These are not resurrected until the very end. When they are resurrected, deathâspiritual deathâimmediately reclaims them.

Conclusion

This portion of Johnâs vision, though very abbreviated, teaches some important principles. Among them, it teaches that the power of the first resurrection only reaches those of the past and present ages who are Christâs at the time of his coming. That moment is critical. Those who must seek a little time while they get their lives in order will find that the words âI come quicklyâ indeed mean âI come suddenly, even before you are ready.â These, like the five foolish virgins, may find, when they finally are ready, that the door to celestial glory has been shut. As the whore Babylon provided the foil that strengthened and prepared the Lambâs bride for her marriage, so too will Satanâs work after the Millennium provide the resistance that will build the spiritual muscle of those in Godâs kingdom on earth. One can imagine, as Satanâs deceptions capture many and he builds his forces to huge proportions, that the faith of the Saints will be tried to the core. That trial, however, will strengthen their trust and obedience in the Lord. The result of that extreme faith will be the heavenly fire that consumes their enemies and ushers in celestial glory. Thus, their faith, as with those before the Millennium, will be fully vindicated.

Johnâs vision drives home another point: the binding of Satan does more than merely give hope for the future. It speaks to the present. It says that there is no âultimate dualism.â The power of evil, though lethally real now, has no place in the future. It exists and operates at any time only by the permission of God.60 When he determines that it will end, it will end. There will be no argument, no retort, no rebuttal, only the irresistible and overpowering explosion of divine fire in which all corruption will be eternally consumed and evil forever incarcerated. Not a whisper of confutation will overreach or penetrate through the massive and frozen walls of Gehenna.61

Analysis and Summary

According to the model found in Revelation, only after Michael destroys Satan does the final judgment commence. With the blindness of satanic delusion gone, all men and women, both small and great, will stand to face Christ. John shows us two judgment scenes and two bases of judgment.

The first to be judged are the righteous. All these will be âjudged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their worksâ (20:12). After the first resurrection is complete, John sees the sea (the old home of chaos and misery), along with death and hell, give up the dead that are in them.
