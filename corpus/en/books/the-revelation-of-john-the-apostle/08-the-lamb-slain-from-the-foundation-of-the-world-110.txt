Analysis and Summary

This unit (chapters 4 and 5) discloses the glory, power, and authority of the Father and the Son and the awe in which heaven holds them. Its primary function is fourfold: first, to introduce the Lamb; second, to declare his worthiness and preparation to execute the will of God; third, to show his investiture as Godâs agent; and finally, to show that all righteous creation stands behind the Lambâs execution of Godâs will.65

In both chapters, God sits as a majestic, motionless, and silent figure, coming into direct contact with humankind neither with revelation nor with providence. He operates exclusively through his Son. Thus Jesus is the Word (see John 1:1â3) and Will of God. The point of the chapter is that the Lamb was able to do what no one else could: comprehend and execute the will of the Father. The four hymns of chapters 4 and 5 also show the ever-expanding appreciation for the effect of the Lambâs work. This unit (chapters 4 and 5) discloses the glory, power, and authority of the Father and the Son and the awe in which heaven holds them.
