Modern revelation confirms the view that angels will be a vehicle for the latter-day destruction. In doing so, it adds depth and understanding to Johnâs words. God commissioned the four angels and gave them power over the earth to save life and to destroy. They also hold sealing powers for both Hades and heaven. These angels have, as it were, two faces. They are not only destroying angels but also saving angels. What makes the difference is how people respond to the message they dispense. They have long since left their home in heaven and are now at work upon the earth.11 Their main period of operation is the sixth seal, but their ministry reaches into the seventh. Thus, angelic administration combines with mortal to assure the worldwide preaching of the gospel during this time.12 Only after they fulfill that commission will they unleash the winds of destruction.13

During the sixth seal, the fullness of his gospel will be restored to the earth, and both natural and supernatural forces will combine to see that it spreads to all lands and cultures. The world has already rejected the gospel many times, but during the sixth seal it will be in a position where one more rejection will assure its doom. This is because the gospel, preached with tremendous power, will not be easily ignored. Those in authority over the nations who reject it will not do so because they could not know the truth of it, but because they refuse to do so. Therefore, the sixth seal will heighten the ideological conflict between the forces of good and evil. The conflict will not be resolved during the sixth seal but the battle lines will be clearly drawn.

Some near eastern societies squared a number to amplify its symbolic meaning. Thus, 144 suggests a fullness of priesthood authority. But God is not satisfied with that. He gives the image a superlative quality by multiplying by 1,000, representing perfection.33 In this way he shows the strength and breadth of the priesthood in the latter days, in this dispensation which is, indeed, the dispensation of the fullness of times. It is also of note that the Romans used mille (1,000) to represent a very large number of objects. In that case, Johnâs figure could also point to the vast number whom God will call to his service at this time. One thing is sure, during the period dominated by the 144,000, the fullness of the priesthood will operate (see D&C 124:28). Chapter 6, anticipating the outpouring of the wrath of God, asked the question, âWho shall be able to stand?â John answers that question in chapter 7: those who have been sealed against destruction and have washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb.
