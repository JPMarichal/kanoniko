Introduction

As the songs of adoration cease, the Lamb begins the task only he can do. One by one he breaks the seven seals attached to the scroll of destiny. As he does so, John beholds in vision a series of dramatic events. They derive from the content of the seals themselves. Each seal symbolizes a set portion of earthâs history: âThe first seal contains the things of the first thousand years, and the second also of the second thousand years, and so on until the seventhâ (D&C 77:7).1 Of course, the history of the earth does not divide easily into exact thousand-year increments. The length of each era varies. These eras are in fact what we in Latter-day Saint parlance call âdispensations.â2

The breaking of the seals does not open any portion of the document. The scroll will not open until the seventh seal breaks. All must wait until then for the will of God to be fully executed. Before that day, evil will rule. Judgment, however, fully fueled by the indignation and wrath of God, will be executed; it must simply wait until the time of the seventh seal. For a discussion of the scroll and the seals, see Translation Notes on 5:1.

The throne vision (4:1â6:17; 8:1â9:21; 11:1â19) forms a single unit that acts as the first of two preludes to the Second Coming, which does not occur until chapter 19. Mortal history begins in this chapter with the opening of the seals and concludes with the opening of the seventh in 8:1. The opening of the last seal, which is seemingly anticlimactic since it is followed by a mere silence in heaven, actually serves to introduce the plagues signaled by the seven trumpet blasts wherein Godâs wrath is finally unleashed in preparation for the Lordâs Second Coming.3 There are two interludes that break up the unit. The first, 7:1â17, shows Godâs preparation of the righteous for the final events, and the second, 10:1â11, reveals the role John is to play in the whole.

The Lord uses horses and their riders to symbolize specific events of the first four thousand years. Horse symbolism is present in the Old Testament. But in Revelation, God modifies it for his own purposes. Even so, it still echoes the two visions of Zechariah, one of which depicts a horsemen with multiple horses, and the other depicts four chariots (Zech. 1:8â11; 6:1â8). In Zechariahâs vision, however, both sets of horses were patrol squads whose purpose was to report on the peaceful condition upon the earth. Like Johnâs, his horses were of different colors but they corresponded to the different winds or points of the compass.4 In Revelation, the horses also take on their usual symbolic nature as steeds of war, but their four colors indicate a significant aspect of each millennium: conquest (white), bloodshed and war (red), plague and famine (black), and death (pale or greenish yellow).5

Though each seal represents a span of about a thousand years in this apocalyptic depiction of earthâs history, the vision with its imagery focuses on but one aspect of that era. The horse and the horseman represent that aspect. Taken together, they show the major developments in each millennium that contributed to and shaped earthâs history. They also help explain why the world will end as it does.

Analysis and Summary

The sixth seal introduces the reader to the modern era. As the Lamb breaks the seal, a terrible scene of cosmic proportions meets the readerâs eye. A tremendous earthquake rocks the planet, affecting every mountain and island. The sun ceases to shine, the moon hangs blood red, and some âstarsâ fall from heaven and blast the earth.

In ancient rabbinic thought, cosmic order depended on mankindâs obedience to God. The rabbis taught that when humankind becomes so wicked as to attempt to set up their own system against Godâs, the constellations would abandon their harmony and the universe would begin to disintegrate.89 The Revelatorâs point seems to be that humankind cannot sin without influencing heaven and earth.90 The cosmos will, indeed, echo back their unholy deeds.

Some of Johnâs readers may have seen a similarity between his descriptions of the cosmos in turmoil and popular apocalyptic belief that had its roots in earlier prophetic portrayals of the end of time. They would have understood that not every detail was to be taken literally.91 For example, the moon will not become blood, though it will take on a sanguine hue.92 It must constantly be kept in mind, however, that behind the symbol stands reality. The cosmos really will react to events on earth and the earth itself will act with great convulsions.

Biblical literature regularly associated the earthquake with divine judgment. When God revealed his presence on Mount Sinai, âthe whole mount quaked greatlyâ (Ex. 19:18). Isaiah stated that men would hide in caves from the terror of the Lord âwhen he ariseth to shake terribly the earthâ (Isa. 2:19). âFor thus saith the Lord of hosts,â warned Haggai, âyet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land; and I will shake all nationsâ (Hag. 2:6â7).93 Thus, by use of the earthquake, the Lord shows his incredible power as he continues to warn the nations. Such power will be sufficiently terrifying to drive men to hide rather than face vengeance. Johnâs forceful words dramatically convey the great natural disasters that bring the sixth millennium to a close and act as forerunners to the final catastrophes.94

The revelation lists seven parts of the cosmos that are affected: the whole earth, the sun, the moon, the stars, the heavens, every mountain, and every island. Likewise, a threat hits seven classes of men: kings, magnates, generals, the rich, the powerful, slaves, and freemen. Such numbering seems deliberate on Johnâs part. It emphasizes the idea of completeness; no enemy of God, no matter how high- or lowborn, whether in heaven or on earth, will escape the wrath of the Lamb.95 He will move in his fury when patience ceases to be a virtue: âAnd it shall come to pass, because of the wickedness of the world, that I will take vengeance upon the wicked, for they will not repent; for the cup of mine indignation is full; for behold, my blood shall not cleanse them if they hear me notâ (D&C 29:17).

Though much of Revelation is symbolic, behind that symbolism there are actual events. The Lord revealed to Joseph Smith the events that were destined to occur as the sixth seal closed. He said, âBefore that great day shall come, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon be turned into blood; and the stars shall refuse their shining, and some shall fall, and great destructions await the wickedâ (D&C 34:9; see also D&C 88:87; Isa. 34:4). The moon wonât literally turn to blood, but it will appear red; stars wonât literally fall to the earth, but there will be destructive meteor showers; and so on. Elder McConkie states, âThe events of that day shall be so unprecedented and so beyond human experience, that the prophets are and have been at an almost total loss for words to describe those realities pressed in upon them by the spirit of revelation.â96 The threat of the awesome desolation of the world will bring all to their knees.

Another event will transpire of such consequence that only symbolic language can describe it: the heavens splitting (6:14). John gives the sky the attributes of a flexible material that can be withdrawn by being rolled up like an ancient scroll. Just what is meant here is unknown,97 but a disclosure seems likely. According to the Doctrine and Covenants, after the folding up of the heavens there shall be an unfolding in which âthe face of the Lord shall be unveiledâ (D&C 88:95).

Many will interpret the signs falsely, believing that the Second Coming has arrived. The end, however, is not yet (Matt. 24:6; see also Luke 21:9; JSâM 1:23). The mistaken notion of the wicked may actually feed their further rebellion, for when the troubles cease for a short season, they may believe that the events were not judgments at all and therefore refuse repentance, becoming even more hardened.

Conclusion

With the breaking of each seal, Revelation portrays a major event that characterizes an important aspect of each dispensational period through the fifth. When John arrives at the sixth, his pace slows, and he gives more detail. He notes not just one event, but seven. It seems fitting that his rapid narrative should slow at this point. This is the moment just before the millennium begins that acts as its preparation period. It is the time when the Lord will reach out in terrible majesty and shake the very foundations of the earth in an attempt to break the hardheartedness of mankind. The Lord explained his purpose in these words:

God is giving these signs as a warning to the modern inhabitants that the end is near. They are designed to encourage repentance,98 and the Lord will try everything from promises to punishments to bring people to him. But once all is tried, the retribution will follow: he âwould have savedâ them, but now they are left to be damned. It is little wonder that the wicked will cry âto the mountains and rocks, Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lambâ (6:16).

Looking at this same time period, an angel prophesied to Nephi, saying,

Therefore, the Lord will use the sixth seal to call people to repentance in ways that will shake the earth to its very core. Having been given every opportunity, unrepentant men and women will feel the wrath of the Lamb. These verses, however, show clearly that these plagues are only preliminary in nature. They announce the coming eschaton, or final events, but are not a part of it. Their severity, as bad as it is, is but a gentle prelude to what is coming when the inhabitants of the earth face the full wrath of God.

The fullness of that wrath is revealed in chapters 8 and 9. But what of the righteous? What happens to them as the world is gripped in the mailed fist of divine justice? Introduction

As the songs of adoration cease, the Lamb begins the task only he can do. One by one he breaks the seven seals attached to the scroll of destiny. As he does so, John beholds in vision a series of dramatic events. They derive from the content of the seals themselves. Each seal symbolizes a set portion of earthâs history: âThe first seal contains the things of the first thousand years, and the second also of the second thousand years, and so on until the seventhâ (D&C 77:7).1 Of course, the history of the earth does not divide easily into exact thousand-year increments. The breaking of the seals does not open any portion of the document. The scroll will not open until the seventh seal breaks. All must wait until then for the will of God to be fully executed. Before that day, evil will rule. Judgment, however, fully fueled by the indignation and wrath of God, will be executed; it must simply wait until the time of the seventh seal. For a discussion of the scroll and the seals, see Translation Notes on 5:1.

The throne vision (4:1â6:17; 8:1â9:21; 11:1â19) forms a single unit that acts as the first of two preludes to the Second Coming, which does not occur until chapter 19. Mortal history begins in this chapter with the opening of the seals and concludes with the opening of the seventh in 8:1. The opening of the last seal, which is seemingly anticlimactic since it is followed by a mere silence in heaven, actually serves to introduce the plagues signaled by the seven trumpet blasts wherein Godâs wrath is finally unleashed in preparation for the Lordâs Second Coming.3 There are two interludes that break up the unit. The first, 7:1â17, shows Godâs preparation of the righteous for the final events, and the second, 10:1â11, reveals the role John is to play in the whole.

The Lord uses horses and their riders to symbolize specific events of the first four thousand years. Horse symbolism is present in the Old Testament. But in Revelation, God modifies it for his own purposes. Even so, it still echoes the two visions of Zechariah, one of which depicts a horsemen with multiple horses, and the other depicts four chariots (Zech. 1:8â11; 6:1â8). In Zechariahâs vision, however, both sets of horses were patrol squads whose purpose was to report on the peaceful condition upon the earth. Like Johnâs, his horses were of different colors but they corresponded to the different winds or points of the compass.4 In Revelation, the horses also take on their usual symbolic nature as steeds of war, but their four colors indicate a significant aspect of each millennium: conquest (white), bloodshed and war (red), plague and famine (black), and death (pale or greenish yellow).5

Though each seal represents a span of about a thousand years in this apocalyptic depiction of earthâs history, the vision with its imagery focuses on but one aspect of that era. The horse and the horseman represent that aspect. Taken together, they show the major developments in each millennium that contributed to and shaped earthâs history. They also help explain why the world will end as it does.

Analysis and Summary

The sixth seal introduces the reader to the modern era. As the Lamb breaks the seal, a terrible scene of cosmic proportions meets the readerâs eye. A tremendous earthquake rocks the planet, affecting every mountain and island. The sun ceases to shine, the moon hangs blood red, and some âstarsâ fall from heaven and blast the earth.

In ancient rabbinic thought, cosmic order depended on mankindâs obedience to God. The rabbis taught that when humankind becomes so wicked as to attempt to set up their own system against Godâs, the constellations would abandon their harmony and the universe would begin to disintegrate.89 The Revelatorâs point seems to be that humankind cannot sin without influencing heaven and earth.90 The cosmos will, indeed, echo back their unholy deeds.

Some of Johnâs readers may have seen a similarity between his descriptions of the cosmos in turmoil and popular apocalyptic belief that had its roots in earlier prophetic portrayals of the end of time. They would have understood that not every detail was to be taken literally.91 For example, the moon will not become blood, though it will take on a sanguine hue.92 It must constantly be kept in mind, however, that behind the symbol stands reality. The cosmos really will react to events on earth and the earth itself will act with great convulsions.

Biblical literature regularly associated the earthquake with divine judgment. When God revealed his presence on Mount Sinai, âthe whole mount quaked greatlyâ (Ex. 19:18). Isaiah stated that men would hide in caves from the terror of the Lord âwhen he ariseth to shake terribly the earthâ (Isa. 2:19). âFor thus saith the Lord of hosts,â warned Haggai, âyet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land; and I will shake all nationsâ (Hag. 2:6â7).93 Thus, by use of the earthquake, the Lord shows his incredible power as he continues to warn the nations. Such power will be sufficiently terrifying to drive men to hide rather than face vengeance. Johnâs forceful words dramatically convey the great natural disasters that bring the sixth millennium to a close and act as forerunners to the final catastrophes.94

The revelation lists seven parts of the cosmos that are affected: the whole earth, the sun, the moon, the stars, the heavens, every mountain, and every island. Likewise, a threat hits seven classes of men: kings, magnates, generals, the rich, the powerful, slaves, and freemen. Such numbering seems deliberate on Johnâs part. It emphasizes the idea of completeness; no enemy of God, no matter how high- or lowborn, whether in heaven or on earth, will escape the wrath of the Lamb.95 He will move in his fury when patience ceases to be a virtue: âAnd it shall come to pass, because of the wickedness of the world, that I will take vengeance upon the wicked, for they will not repent; for the cup of mine indignation is full; for behold, my blood shall not cleanse them if they hear me notâ (D&C 29:17).

Though much of Revelation is symbolic, behind that symbolism there are actual events. The Lord revealed to Joseph Smith the events that were destined to occur as the sixth seal closed. He said, âBefore that great day shall come, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon be turned into blood; and the stars shall refuse their shining, and some shall fall, and great destructions await the wickedâ (D&C 34:9; see also D&C 88:87; Isa. 34:4). The moon wonât literally turn to blood, but it will appear red; stars wonât literally fall to the earth, but there will be destructive meteor showers; and so on. Elder McConkie states, âThe events of that day shall be so unprecedented and so beyond human experience, that the prophets are and have been at an almost total loss for words to describe those realities pressed in upon them by the spirit of revelation.â96 The threat of the awesome desolation of the world will bring all to their knees.

Another event will transpire of such consequence that only symbolic language can describe it: the heavens splitting (6:14). John gives the sky the attributes of a flexible material that can be withdrawn by being rolled up like an ancient scroll. Just what is meant here is unknown,97 but a disclosure seems likely. According to the Doctrine and Covenants, after the folding up of the heavens there shall be an unfolding in which âthe face of the Lord shall be unveiledâ (D&C 88:95).

Many will interpret the signs falsely, believing that the Second Coming has arrived. The end, however, is not yet (Matt. 24:6; see also Luke 21:9; JSâM 1:23). Joel notes these events happen not at but before the coming of the Lord (Joel 2:31; see also D&C 34:9). The mistaken notion of the wicked may actually feed their further rebellion, for when the troubles cease for a short season, they may believe that the events were not judgments at all and therefore refuse repentance, becoming even more hardened.

Conclusion

With the breaking of each seal, Revelation portrays a major event that characterizes an important aspect of each dispensational period through the fifth. When John arrives at the sixth, his pace slows, and he gives more detail. He notes not just one event, but seven. It seems fitting that his rapid narrative should slow at this point. This is the moment just before the millennium begins that acts as its preparation period. It is the time when the Lord will reach out in terrible majesty and shake the very foundations of the earth in an attempt to break the hardheartedness of mankind. The Lord explained his purpose in these words:

And again, the Lord shall utter his voice out of heaven, saying: Hearken, O ye nations of the earth, and hear the words of that God who made you. O, ye nations of the earth, how often would I have gathered you together as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, but ye would not! How oft have I called upon you by the mouth of my servants, and by the ministering of angels, and by mine own voice, and by the voice of thunderings, and by the voice of lightnings, and by the voice of tempests, and by the voice of earthquakes, and great hailstorms, and by the voice of famines and pestilences of every kind, and by the great sound of a trump, and by the voice of judgment, and by the voice of mercy all the day long, and by the voice of glory and honor and the riches of eternal life, and would have saved you with an everlasting salvation, but ye would not! Behold, the day has come, when the cup of the wrath of mine indignation is full. (D&C 43:23â26)

God is giving these signs as a warning to the modern inhabitants that the end is near. They are designed to encourage repentance,98 and the Lord will try everything from promises to punishments to bring people to him. But once all is tried, the retribution will follow: he âwould have savedâ them, but now they are left to be damned. It is little wonder that the wicked will cry âto the mountains and rocks, Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lambâ (6:16).

Looking at this same time period, an angel prophesied to Nephi, saying,

Wo be unto the Gentiles if it so be that they harden their hearts against the Lamb of God. For the time cometh, saith the Lamb of God, that I will work a great and marvelous work among the children of men; a work which shall be everlasting, either on the one hand or on the otherâeither to the convincing of them unto peace and life eternal, or unto the deliverance of them to the hardness of their hearts and the blindness of their minds unto their being brought down into captivity, and also into destruction, both temporally and spiritually, according to the captivity of the devil, of which I have spoken. (1 Ne. 14:6â7)

Therefore, the Lord will use the sixth seal to call people to repentance in ways that will shake the earth to its very core. Having been given every opportunity, unrepentant men and women will feel the wrath of the Lamb. These verses, however, show clearly that these plagues are only preliminary in nature. They announce the coming eschaton, or final events, but are not a part of it. Their severity, as bad as it is, is but a gentle prelude to what is coming when the inhabitants of the earth face the full wrath of God.

The fullness of that wrath is revealed in chapters 8 and 9. But what of the righteous? What happens to them as the world is gripped in the mailed fist of divine justice? The question with which John closes this section of the vision has real weightâwho will be able to stand? The answer is in chapter 7.
