A Matter of Words

Question: "Why did Joseph Smith, when translating the Book of Mormon, write in 3 Nephi 25:5-6 the same words to the prophecy of Malachi 4:5-6 after the Angel Moroni appeared to him and told him three different times a different wording of it?" (Question asked by member of Jehovah's Witnesses.)

Answer: First of all let us consider some of the history of the "Jehovah's Witnesses." I have followed them for many years and know something of their origin, and how they have changed their name from time to time.

This organization is the brain-child of Charles Russell at Allegheny, Pittsburgh about 1872. He was a member of the Congregational Church, but becoming dissatisfied with some of the doctrines of that church broke away and started on his own. They went by the title— "Zion's Watch Tower Society," then they were known as "International Bible Students." It was after the death of Mr. Russell, when "Judge" Rutherford took command, that they hit upon the name, Jehovah's Witnesses. They used to teach that there were millions now living who would never die. We have not heard so much about this in more recent years. They have had no revelations, no divine guidance and assumed the title without any divine appointment. They are very combative and energetic, but with no revelation by which they assumed their title.

POINT RAISED BY JEHOVAH'S WITNESSES

Now the point that they have raised is, "Why did Joseph Smith when translating the Book of Mormon write in 3 Nephi 25:5-6 the same words that were given to Malachi 4:5-6; and then when Moroni appeared to him, he quotes the following:

Behold I will reveal unto you the Priesthood, by the hand of Elijah the prophet, before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord. . . .

And he shall plant in the hearts of the children the promises made to their fathers, and the hearts of the children shall turn to their fathers. If it were not so, the whole earth would be utterly wasted at his coming. (Joseph Smith 2:38-39.)

Did not the Prophet Joseph Smith state clearly that Moroni quoted this scripture differently from how it was given in the Book of Malachi, and in the Book of Mormon? Is there any law divine or otherwise, that would compel Moroni to follow the exact words of Malachi? One beautiful thing about the interpretation of Moroni is that it is clearer and adds the thought that if Elijah did not come, the whole earth would be wasted at the coming of the Lord. The words of Moroni are clearer and exactly what one could expect, written in this later time.

There is a statement in the prophecy of Isaiah about one making another an offender for a word. Does this not appear like a needless offense? Moroni merely quoted these words with an interpretation suited to the times.

A MATTER OF HISTORY

Now let me call your attention to a matter of history and ask a simple question or two.

Is the prophecy of Malachi true? If so, has Elijah been here to restore the priesthood according to the promise? Is there any other church, or religious organization unto whom Elijah came to bring this great restoration? Have you ever heard of one of them, including "Jehovah's Witnesses," that has ever made the claim or that they have had him come to them to restore these authorities? If not, is it not a fact that before the great and dreadful day shall come, these authorities of turning the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the children to the fathers MUST BE REVEALED? Everything in the world points to the fact that the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord draws near! Now here is a bit of history that should be important to "Jehovah's Witnesses," falsely so-called and to Catholics and Protestants alike.

On the third day of April, 1836, in the Kirtland Temple, in Ohio, Elijah did come to Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery and conferred upon them the authority spoken of by Malachi and Moroni, saying:

Behold, the time has fully come, which was spoken of by the mouth of Malachi—testifying that he [Elijah] should be sent, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord come—

To turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the children to the fathers, lest the whole earth be smitten with a curse—

Therefore, the keys of this dispensation are committed into your hands; and by this ye may know that the great and dreadful day of the Lord is near, even at the doors. (D. & C. 110:14-16. )

AMPLE EVIDENCE OF ELIJAH'S APPEARANCE

There is ample evidence in the world that Elijah did come and restore this authority. The hearts of thousands of the children have turned towards their dead fathers and they are compiling the records of their dead, as never before, so that we can obtain them and go into the temples and perform the necessary work for their dead fathers that their dead fathers may be saved in the kingdom of God. If any one desires to make a research they will discover that before the year 1836, there was practically no research for the dead, but since that time there are tens of thousands who are making their researches. Their hearts have turned to their fathers. They do not know why, but nevertheless this is the absolute fact. We are doing the work in our temples for the dead—we the children, for the fathers who died without a knowledge of the gospel, and who never had the opportunity to believe. The Lord in his justice has decreed that even the dead must have an opportunity to have the gospel taught to them. That is why our Savior went to the world of spirits and carried the message of salvation to them. We are performing this great work for the dead and it is the Latter-day Saints who are laboring so that there will be many who will not be lost at Christ's coming.
