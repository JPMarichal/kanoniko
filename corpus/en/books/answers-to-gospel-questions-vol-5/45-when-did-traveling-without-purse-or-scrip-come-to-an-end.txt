When Did Traveling without Purse or Scrip Come to an End?

Question: "I am interested in knowing when and by what official decree the tradition of Mormon elders performing their work without purse or scrip came to an end?"

Answer: In the early days from 1830 upward for many years all the missionaries of the Church traveled without "purse or scrip," and wonderful success was achieved. Missionaries traveled in all parts of the United States and Canada in this manner; they crossed the Atlantic Ocean and commenced the work in Great Britain and on the continent of Europe. In more recent years this method of proselyting has not been insisted upon universally in the Church. One reason being that many states have passed laws compelling those so engaged to have money in their pockets.

CHURCH HAS NEVER TAKEN ACTION ON METHOD

However, the Church has never taken action changing this method of proclaiming the gospel. There have been some who have traveled without purse and scrip during all the years, but this has been more or less optional, except in certain countries where there would come a conflict with the law to travel in this manner.

It is true that there has been a revival of this practice in more recent years, and far more of the missionaries are carrying on their work without money than has been the case for several years immediately preceding; but there has not been any change of policy in the matter. In other words at no time were the missionaries compelled to travel without purse and scrip, and at no time have they been compelled to travel with funds in their pockets.
