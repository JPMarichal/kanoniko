Ed J. Pinegar, Especially for Missionaries, vol. 2 ( 1997)

Positive Labeling

We also must get to know the person we're communicating with. We've got to smile and say something like, "It is so nice to see you." Then follow that up with a genuine "How are you doing?" Next, we start a gospel conversation. That is the greatest thing you will learn to do on your mission: start a gospel conversation. When I meet a person at the grocery store, or knock on someone's door, or even just see someone on the street, I make it a habit to approach them and say, "Excuse me, would you be so kind and friendly as to help me in regards to a few questions that might be a blessing to your life?" Do you know what they do? They think, "Oh, I'm kind and friendly! Oh, you need help?" Do you see it? If you label a person and say, "You are kind, you are friendly," they act that way.

It's important when we communicate that we raise the level of esteem of the person we're communicating with. But our comments must be genuine and honest. Let me give you an example. I was a substitute as a basketball player during high school. I knew I was good, but the coach just didn't think I was good enough. My senior year, however, we got a new coach, and he said, "Ed, you will be my center and my captain."

Ta-dahh! He labeled me. He communicated to me and labeled me with a positive label. Guess what happened? Ed Pinegar led the region in scoring and was All-State and was selected to be on the All-Star Shrine team for the state of Utah. Why? Because a coach believed in me and communicated that to me by labeling me with a positive label.
