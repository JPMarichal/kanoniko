When the eighteen-year-old Godbe arrived in Great Salt Lake City in 1851, the Mormon settlement was four years old. Striving and ambitious, he quickly tried to establish himself. One account has him forming a partnership with another enterprising young Mormon, Benjamin Hampton. Perhaps as early as 1853, these two men secured control of a strategic Bear River ferry located near the present-day villages of Collinston and Fielding in Box Elder County. It is unclear how the men secured this prized real estate, which straddled growing north-south and east-west trade routes.1

More important was Godbeâs commercial activity. During the last part of his trip to Great Salt Lake City, Godbe had traveled with merchant Thomas S. Williams. Idiosyncratic, combative, and utterly untamed, Williams had swarthy skin and, when riding on the range, impossibly tousled hair. During such times, even his wife found it difficult to separate Williams from the Indians with whom he sometimes rode. Still, Williams was an able (some said brilliant) man, and shortly after the Saintsâ arrival in the Salt Lake Valley, he had established himself as one of Utahâs first merchants.2

Williams liked Godbe and offered him a job in his Salt Lake City store. Two or three years later, this opportunity expanded further. Godbeâs trip did more than nurture another lifelong dream; it gave him the chance to see more of the world and possibly the opportunity to start his own business.
