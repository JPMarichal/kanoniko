It was not easy for Mormons to measure William Godbe. Outwardly he seemed to be an ideal nineteenth-century Saint. It was not easy for Mormons to measure William Godbe. Outwardly he seemed to be an ideal nineteenth-century Saint. He got things done, a trait important to pioneering Mormons who valued practical achievement far more than religious meditation. Godbe was an entrepreneur; he liked to bring a task to successful completion. Achievement was more important to him than money or position, which he appeared to regard as social trusts to be used to aid others. Ironically, and at least in theory, Godbeâs entrepreneurial spirit and sense of stewardship had much in common with the ideals of Brigham Youngâs Zion, the very thing Godbe railed so strongly against.

Yet behind the pleasing public exterior of Godbe lay other personality traits less congenial to the traditional faith.
