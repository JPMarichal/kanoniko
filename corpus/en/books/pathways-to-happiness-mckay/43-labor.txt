David O. McKay, Pathways to Happiness ( 1957)

* Labor

LABOR UNIONS

YOU should know the attitude of the Church regarding efforts of some so-called labor organizations in coercing members of our Church into unions. I think we need not quibble. We have no apology to offer. It is un-American when five per cent of this nation attempts to force 95 per cent along a particular line of action. It is undemocratic. That is just what is being done, however. I sympathize with labor, too. So do you. We are in favor of paying the highest wages that the employer or the business man employing labor can afford to pay. There is nobody in this Church who wants to cut down labor, but we do resent the un-American attempt to coerce one of our young boys who has given two years of his life to the Church and who has returned to work: "You can't work on this job unless you pay the dues and join our union."

It isn't right. No matter what difficulties we may face, let us stand for what is right. It is not a matter of reducing wages; it is just a question of having individual liberty. We will make no discrimination against a man who is or is not a member of a union, but request that a good day's work be given for compensation received. No man shall be discriminated against in regard to these things.
