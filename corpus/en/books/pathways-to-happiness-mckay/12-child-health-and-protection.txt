David O. McKay, Pathways to Happiness ( 1957)

* Child Health and Protection

THE greatest blessing that can come to a home is a wellborn child. Homes that rear healthy, moral, welltrained children constitute the bulwark of organized society.

The greatest asset of a nation is its children. The highest obligation of a government is to train properly and protect adequately childhood and youth. Intelligently to discharge this obligation should be the first concern of town boards, city commissions, state legislatures, and the law-making bodies of Congress. Unless more serious thought and wise united action are directed to this momentous question, modern civilization is likely to be submerged in threatening difficulties.

The White House Conference on Child Health and Protection set up some "minimum standards" to apply to every child in the United States:

Upon the training these children receive depends the future of the nation. The question of whether or not they enter tomorrow well equipped to take their places in handling the social, political, industrial, and moral problems of the state must be answered by the men and women who have the responsibility of guiding their destinies today.

These problems involve the integrity of the home, the safety of the community, the giving to every child a fair and equal start in his life's race with his fellows, and the bequeathing to the next generation an inheritance of physical health, intellectual strength, and moral tendencies that will assure the perpetuity and progress of the human race.
