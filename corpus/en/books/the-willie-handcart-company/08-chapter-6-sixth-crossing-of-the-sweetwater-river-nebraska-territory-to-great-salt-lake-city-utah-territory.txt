The trail from the Sweetwater River to the top of Rocky Ridge is just over three miles long and has a rise in elevation of more than 750 feet for roughly a 5 percent grade. However, it is not a consistently rising grade. Instead, it has several steep portions and one portion where it actually drops in elevation for some distance before turning uphill again. It is hard to find a day that the wind does not blow on Rocky Ridge. The wind was blowing snow down the hill and into their faces as the Saints trudged up toward the ridge top and then onward for miles. Wind chill was a real factor fighting against them. The actual temperature on October 23 is not known, but it was certainly less than 32Âº F. One clue as to how cold it was on October 23 comes from the record of the Abraham O. Smoot wagon train, which arrived at Fort Bridger on October 23. It reads in part, âit snowed hard on 19th, 20th, all day & it was very cold, thermometer down to zero part of the time, left our camp at 11 a.m. on Thursday 23rd & reached Fort Bridger.â8

At this lower temperature and wind speed, frostbite could occur in a little more than 30 minutes. Many of the people were on the trail for hours, with a few coming in well after dark. The trail from the Sweetwater River to the top of Rocky Ridge is just over three miles long and has a rise in elevation of more than 750 feet for roughly a 5 percent grade. Rhoda had walked the entire distance, only to die one hill away from their destination.19 Rhodaâs death was the 40th since October 20. She was the final Willie Handcart Company death.

The bishops in the Salt Lake Valley were ready to receive the Saints. Hundreds of people came out to welcome the company. Margaret Dalglish, age 29, saw to it that she had a fresh start in Salt Lake City. With the valley in sight, she took out what was left of her 17 pounds of provisions and pushed her handcart off the road and down the canyon ravine. All that she owned in the world was in her arms as she entered the valley.20

After the loss of many of our oxen, west of Fort Kearney, we hunted for them some four days & then sent two young men back on the road towards Missouri River to see if they could find them. Mary Hurren, age 7, one of the girls who jumped rattlesnakes near the North Platte River, arrived in the Salt Lake Valley with frozen feet. Two doctors told her parents, James M.
