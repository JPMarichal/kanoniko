C. Wilfred Griggs, Paul R. Cheesman, Scriptures for the Modern World ( 1992)

Notes on the Contributors

Elder Neal A. Maxwell

Elder Neal A. Maxwell, now a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, was a member of the Presidency of the First Quorum of the Seventy when he delivered this address. He served as Commissioner of Education for the Church Educational System from 1970 to 1976; from 1974 to 1976 he also served as an Assistant to the Quorum of the Twelve. Before 1970, Elder Maxwell held a variety of administrative and teaching positions at the University of Utah, including executive vice-president, and he earlier served as a legislative assistant to United States Senator Wallace F. Bennett of Utah. He is the author of many articles and books, including Look Back at Sodom, an imaginative reconstruction of the fall of Sodom, and Of One Heart, a similar treatment of the city of Enoch.

H. Donl Peterson

H. Donl Peterson was born in Lehi, Utah, and earned bachelor's and master's degrees at Brigham Young University. He taught in the LDS seminary program for five years and in the institute program for an additional five years. After receiving a doctorate in education at Washington State University, he joined BYU's faculty in 1964, where he currently is professor of religious education and Pearl of Great Price director in the Religious Studies Center. Author of A Study Guide for the Pearl of Great Price and Moroni—Ancient Prophet, Modern Messenger, he has contributed many articles and chapters to Church publications.

Jo Milgrom

Jo Milgrom holds a Ph.D. from the Graduate Theological Union at Berkeley, California, where she is now visiting assistant professor in theology and the arts. She has taught at the San Francisco Art Institute and in the overseas program of the Hebrew University at Jerusalem, and has served as consultant at the Skirball Museum of the Hebrew Union College of Los Angeles and as Scholar-in-Residence at the Brandeis Bardin Institute of Los Angeles. Dr. Milgrom has been awarded fellowships from the Institute of Jewish Life (1974, 1975) and NDEA Fellowships (1966, 1967).

Jacob Milgrom

Jacob Milgrom, a professor in the Department of Near Eastern Studies at the University of California at Berkeley, has held positions with the University of California Study Center, at the Hebrew University in Jerusalem (1969-71), and with Virginia Union University's graduate school of religion in Richmond, Virginia (1955-65). Professor Milgrom received the D.H.L. degree in 1953 from the Jewish Theological Seminary in New York City. A foremost authority on the book of Leviticus, he has received many awards, including the Rabbi Solomon Goldman Award for Literary Creativity (1973) and a Guggenheim Fellowship (1978), and was elected a Fellow of the American Academy for Jewish Research (1978). His publications include Studies in Levital Terminology (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1970), Cult and Conscience: The Asham and the Priestly Doctrine of Repentance (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1976), and over one hundred articles in scientific journals and encyclopedias.

S. Kent Brown

S. Kent Brown, director of the ancient studies area of Brigham Young University's Religious Studies Center, has taught early Christianity, New Testament, Hebrew, Old Testament, and the Intertestamental period. Professor Brown has a Ph.D. from Brown University in biblical studies (1972). Special honors include James Foundation Fellow at Brown University, Fellow of the National Endowment for the Humanities and the Council on Library Resources at Brown University, and Fellow of the American Research Center in Egypt.

Victor L. Ludlow

Victor L. Ludlow is an Old Testament specialist at Brigham Young University. He received a Danforth Fellowship for graduate studies at Brandeis and Harvard and received his Ph.D. from Brandeis University, where he majored in Near Eastern and Judaic studies. Dr. Ludlow directed the BYU Study Abroad program in Jerusalem in 1976. He has also conducted several tours to Israel. He currently chairs the BYU Faculty General Education Council, serves on the board of trustees for the Society for Early Historic Archaeology, and is a member of the BYU Study Abroad Advisory Committee.

Robert J. Matthews

Robert J. Matthews is professor in religious instruction and chairman of the Department of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. Before joining its faculty, he was a seminary and institute teacher, and then director for academic research and editor for the Department of Seminaries and Institutes. He received his Ph.D. in Old Testament from Brigham Young University. He has had articles published in Church magazines and BYU Studies, particularly on the Joseph Smith Translation, and has published the standard treatment on this work, "A Plainer Translation": Joseph Smith's Translation of the Bible, A History and Commentary (Provo, Utah: Brigham Young University Press, 1975).

C. Wilfred Griggs

C. Wilfred Griggs is an associate professor in the Department of Ancient Scripture at Brigham Young University. He has taught New Testament, ancient philosophy, ancient history, and classical languages. He received his doctorate from the University of California at Berkeley in ancient history and Mediterranean archaeology. Professor Griggs has received research grants from Brigham Young University to translate and publish Coptic Christian documents and is presently an assistant editor for the Coptic Encyclopaedia Project and field director for an archaeological excavation in Egypt, sponsored jointly by Brigham Young University and the University of California at Berkeley.

Aziz S. Atiya

Aziz S. Atiya, distinguished professor of language and history at the University of Utah in Salt Lake City, is founder of that university's Middle East Center and Middle East Library. He obtained his Ph.D. in London in 1933, his D.Litt. in Liverpool in 1938, and has since received three honorary doctorates at American universities. Born in a small Egyptian village, Dr. Atiya has studied and taught at major universities all over the world, and has published more than a hundred books, monographs, and articles. His latest major project is a Coptic Encyclopaedia in several volumes, now in progress under the sponsorship of the National Endowment for the Humanities.

Because his presence was required elsewhere during the symposium, Dr. Atiya made this presentation on videotape.

Arthur Wallace

Arthur Wallace is a professor at the University of California at Los Angeles. He received a Ph.D. from Rutgers University in soil and plant sciences and is a graduate of Utah State University, where he majored in chemistry. He is the author of numerous articles and books, including Can Mormonism Be Proved Experimentally? and Successful Family Organizations.

Noel B. Reynolds

Noel B. Reynolds, associate academic vice-president at Brigham Young University, obtained his Ph.D. from Harvard University in political theory and philosophy. He was awarded a Liberal Arts Fellowship from Harvard Law School (1976-77) and a Harvard Graduate Prize Fellowship (1968-70). A former chairman of the BYU Department of Philosophy, he has been a visiting faculty member with BYU's J. Reuben Clark Law School.

John L. Sorenson

John L. Sorenson is chairman of the Department of Anthropology and Archaeology at Brigham Young University. He was an archaeologist in Tabascio and Chiapas, Mexico, with the New World Archaeological Foundation in 1953. He has been director of behavioral sciences with the General Research Corporation in Santa Barbara (1964-69) and president of Bonneville Research Corporation in Provo, Utah (1969-71). His Ph.D. in anthropology comes from the University of California at Los Angeles, and he has over ninety publications to his credit.

Paul R. Cheesman

Paul R. Cheesman is a professor of religious instruction at Brigham Young University, where he has been director of the Book of Mormon Institute and at the time of this symposium was director of the scripture area of the Religious Studies Center. He taught in California public schools and was also a seminary teacher before joining BYU's faculty, where he earned a doctorate in religious education in 1967. He has been involved in the production of films, filmstrips, and illustrated books dealing with the scriptures and Church history.
