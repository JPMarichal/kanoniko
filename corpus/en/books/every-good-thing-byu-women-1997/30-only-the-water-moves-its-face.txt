Only the Water Moves Its Face

NANCY BAIRD

I no longer look at people's faces when I run— smeared moons in the dark caves of their cars. The sight is too instructive in the subtle ways of wickedness. Ask Jeremiah, down in the stinking pit, looking up at the blinded circle of sky, the children of darkness peering down its crumbling lip like rotten teeth. And the hands are important. . . . It is possible, while driving, to use both hands to show contempt and violence— those hands—elegant, fluid creatures of heart and brain reduced, degraded to this purpose. It is so still in the canyons . . . deserted, but for the slick of snake in rocks, the occasional astonishment of deer. Only the water moves its face, and the hands of a deeper mind than this world can remember push the wind around the rock, shake the loosened leaves to the road, are busy in the fragrant, steadfast work of life.
