258"It's All Ready"

258At the war's end President Smith set to work. In November he called on Harry S. Truman. "When I called on him," President Smith reported later, "he received me very graciously—I had met him before—and I said: 'I have just come to ascertain from you, Mr. President, what your attitude will be if the Latter-day Saints are prepared to ship food and clothing and bedding to Europe.'

258"He smiled and looked at me, and said: 'Well, what do you want to ship it over there for? Their money isn't any good.'

258"I said: 'Of course; we would give it to them. They are our brothers and sisters and are in distress. God has blessed us with surplus, and we will be glad to send it if we have the cooperation of the government.'

258"He said, 'You are on the right track,' and added, 'we will be glad to help you in any way we can.'

"I have thought of that a good many times. After we had sat there a moment or two, he said again: 'How long will it take you to get this ready?"

259"I said: 'It's all ready.'

259The government, you remember, had been destroying food and refusing to plant grain during the war, so I said to him:

259"Mr. President, while the administration at Washington were advising the destroying of food, we were building elevators and filling them with grain, and increasing our flocks and our herds, and now what we need is the cars and the ships in order to send considerable food, clothing, and bedding to the people of Europe who are in distress. We have an organization in the Church that has over two thousand homemade quilts ready."

259Robert B. Day, They Made Mormon History (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Co., 1968), pp. 332-33.
