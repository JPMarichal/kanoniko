CourageSmith, George Albert246"All Fear and Anxiety Left"

246I remember, as a young man and missionary in the Southern States (1892-94) the first conference I attended. It was out in the woods on a farm in Mississippi. We didn't have comfortable seats to sit on. The brethren had been permitted to cut down a few trees and lay the trunks of those trees across the stumps which were left. We balanced ourselves on these, or else sat on the ground.

246Our meeting started right after breakfast time, and we didn't even think it was necessary to have anything more to eat until evening. We stayed and enjoyed the blessings of the inspiration of the Almighty, and we certainly were blessed, not-withstanding the inconveniences and discomforts which surrounded us.

246At that time there was considerable hostility manifested in Mississippi and other states in the South, but we just felt as though we had walked into the presence of our Heavenly Father, and all fear and anxiety left. That was my first experience in a mission field, attending a conference, and from that time until now I have appreciated the fact that the companionship of the Spirit of the Lord is an antidote for weariness, for fear, and all those things that sometimes overtake us in life.

246George Albert Smith, Sharing the Gospel, pp. 16-17.
