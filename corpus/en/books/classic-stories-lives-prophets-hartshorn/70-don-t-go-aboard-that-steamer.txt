Leon R. Hartshorn, Classic Stories from the Lives of Our Prophets ( 1971)

117"Don't Go Aboard That Steamer"

117After spending two years and one-half in New England and Canada, getting the Saints out, I started back with the last lot, about a hundred, from Boston. We landed in Pittsburg at dusk. We were anxious not to stay there, but to go on to St. Louis. I saw a steamer making steam ready to go out. I went to the captain and asked him how many passengers he had. "Three hundred and fifty." "Could you take another hundred?" "Yes." The Spirit said to me, "Don't go aboard that steamer; you nor your company." All right, said I. I had learned something about that still, small voice. I did not go aboard that steamer, but waited till the next morning.

117In thirty minutes after that steamer left, it took fire. It had ropes instead of wheel chains, and they could not go ashore. It was a dark night, and not a soul was saved. If I had not obeyed the influence of that monitor within me, I would have been there myself, with the rest of the company.

117The Thirteenth ward would not have had an Atwood for a bishop; the Church would not have had a Leonard W. Hardy as bishop. They were both with me, and their families, including Brother Samuel Hardy, who is in St. George now, upwards of ninety years old.

117I never disobeyed that Spirit but once in my life; I did it then through the urgency of other persons, and it nearly cost me my life. I have been acquainted with this spirit. It was not the blow of trumpets, nor thunder and lightning; it was the still small voice to me. All the way from my boyhood I have been governed and controlled by that Spirit.

117Nibley, Faith Promoting Stories, pp. 24-25.
