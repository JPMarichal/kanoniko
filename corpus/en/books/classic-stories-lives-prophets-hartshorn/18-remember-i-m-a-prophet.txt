24Josiah Quincy comments on his visit with the Prophet as follows:

24I should not say quite all that struck me about Smith if I did not mention that he seemed to have a keen sense of the humorous aspect of his position.

24"It seems to me, General," I said, as he was driving us to the river, about sunset, "that you have too much power to be safely trusted to one man."

24"In your hands or that of any other person," was the reply, "so much power would, no doubt, be dangerous. I am the only one man in the world whom it would be safe to trust with it. Remember, I'm a prophet!"

24The last five words were spoken in a rich, comical aside, as if in hearty recognition of the ridiculous sound they might have in the ears of a Gentile. I asked him to test his powers by naming the successful candidate in the approaching presidential election.

"Well, I will prophesy that John Tyler will not be the next president, for some things are possible and some things are probable; but Tyler's election is neither the one nor the other!"
