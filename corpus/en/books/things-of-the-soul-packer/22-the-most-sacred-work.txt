The Most Sacred Work [^1]

Once in a while we see a sign along the road that advertises the sale of a piece of property with the words, "Will build to suit tenant." I think that's what the Church is involved in as we plan and construct temples.

Why do we build temples at all? Let's move through a quotation or two and some thoughts.

In the coasts of Caesarea Philippi, with His disciples gathered around Him, Jesus asked them what people were saying about Him. "Whom do men say that I am?"

It's very interesting that the first response was, "They think you are John the Baptist," or "They think you're Elijah."

Then He said to them, "Whom say ye that I am?" And Peter answered: "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God."

The Lord then said, "Flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee." He also said that upon that rock, the rock of revelation, He would build His church.

Then He said to Peter, "I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom." That's very significant. "I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven." (Matthew 16:13-19.) This meant that Peter was to become the President of the Church.

Temple Work

When we talk about temple work, which involves the use of these keys, there comes to mind an impressive statement by Elder George Q. Cannon, who later became First Counselor in the First Presidency of the Church. "Every foundation stone that is laid for a temple, and every temple completed according to the order the Lord has revealed for His Holy Priesthood, lessens the power of Satan on the earth and increases the power of God and Godliness, moves the heavens in mighty power in our behalf, invokes and calls down upon us the blessings of the Eternal Gods and those who reside in their presence." (In Gospel Truth, Jerreld L. Newquist, ed. [Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Co., 1987], p. 366.)

President Wilford Woodruff said:

I want to say, as the president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, that we should now go on and progress. We have not got through with revelation. We have not got through with the work of God. But at this period we want to go on and fulfill this commandment of God given through Malachi-that the Lord should send Elijah the prophet, "and he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse." (Malachi 4:6.)

And then this powerful statement:

Ye sons of men, I say unto you, in the name of Israel's God, those very principles that God has revealed are what have stayed the judgments of the Almighty upon the earth. Were it not for these principles, you and I would not be here today. We have had prophets and apostles. President Young, who followed President Joseph Smith, led us here. He organized these temples and carried out the purposes of his calling and office. He laid the foundation of this great temple on this block, as well as others in the mountains of Israel. What for? That we might carry out these principles of redemption for the dead. (The Discourses of Wilford Woodruff, G. Homer Durham, ed. [Salt Lake City: Bookcraft, 1969], pp. 153-54.)

Elijah

I would like to go back and trace a thread through scriptural history. Whenever you get on the subject we're on now, somewhere you begin with Elijah.

Ahab was king of Israel about 875 B.C., and the Old Testament says he provoked the Lord God of Israel more than all of the kings of Israel who were before him (see 1 Kings 16:33). He was married to Jezebel, who was a pagan, and he had adopted paganism.

Into his court one day came Elijah the Tishbite. No one knows where Elijah originally came from. In the scriptures it says he was an inhabitant of Gilead, which was across the Jordan. It was an area of wilderness. And that's all we know. Nobody knows what a Tishbite was. So he was just Elijah the Tishbite.

Elijah told Ahab he was going to seal the heavens, and he did. He said, "There'll be no rain come until I say so," which is interesting. He didn't say "until conditions are right," or anything else. He said, "There'll be no rain until I say so."

From 1 Kings 17 we can follow the account of Elijah's going into hiding, being fed by the ravens, and other events, but pretty soon Ahab was looking for him. After three and a half years without rain they were desperately trying to find him.

Elijah's ministry is well worth the reading (in l and 2 Kings, and then a mention in 2 Chronicles 21), but still he is somewhat obscure. The scholars of the world talk about major and minor prophets, and quite often they don't put Elijah on either list. The Jews do. In the last part of his recorded ministry Elijah anointed a new king for Israel and a new king for Judah, and also anointed Elisha to succeed him as prophet. Then he was taken to heaven in a fiery chariot without tasting death.

As prophets go, Elijah is interesting because we don't have a lot of his prophecies. Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, Hosea, Amos, Obadiah, and the rest of them left prophecies. And we know they are prophets because we have the prophecies. But not with Elijah. And not with Moses.

Elijah and Moses have something else similar in that they both were "taken to heaven." The Old Testament says that Moses went up on the mountain and he didn't come back; so the people said then the Lord buried him there, evidently (see Deuteronomy 34:4-6). Well, the Lord didn't bury him there.

That's all there is about Elijah in the Old Testament until you move to about 430 B.C. and Malachi the prophet. We refer to Malachi 3:8-12 with reference to tithing-but before that, in condemning Israel the Lord said: "I am the Lord, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed. Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away from mine ordinances." (Malachi 3:6-7.)

I emphasize that word ordinances, because that's where we begin in this discussion of building a house to suit the tenant.

Then Malachi prophesied: "For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the Lord of hosts." And then this remarkably significant phrase: "that it shall leave them neither root nor branch."

Then the prophet said: "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord: and he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse" (Malachi 4:1, 5-6).

And so closes the Old Testament. About four hundred years later the book of Matthew opens with the account of the birth of Christ.

Now, as to this matter of turning the heart of the fathers to the children and the heart of the children to the fathers, scholars have wrestled with the words but have been unable to come up with the meaning.

As we have noted, to the Jews Elijah is a prophet. Even now when they celebrate the Passover, during one of the courses of the meal someone gets up and goes to the door and opens it. Why? Because Elijah might be standing there. During that meal there is always an empty seat for Elijah at the table.

Elijah and Elias

When John the Baptist appeared on the scene some two thousand years ago, one of the first questions he was asked by the Jews was "Are you Elijah?" John 1:21 reads, "Art thou Elias?" Elias is the Greek translation of the Hebrew Elijah. In many places where you find Elias it means "Elijah," that is, the prophet we are talking about.

There are three definitions of Elias. Elias equals Elijah in those cases where it's talking about the latter. Elias equals Elias in those cases where it's talking about a particular man, a prophet-we know very little about him, but he existed.

Third, Elias can mean "a messenger." John the Baptist was "an Elias," and others have been too. Now, that kind of wording is not unusual. We talk about someone being a Michelangelo. We see an artist at work and say, "Why, he's a real Michelangelo." Or we see a teacher at a chalkboard and say, "Why, he's an Einstein." We don't mean that it's the Einstein or the Michelangelo.

Well, Elias equals messenger. And in the scriptures, as you follow the thread through, you have to know that.

John was an Elias, a situation which is explained in the Joseph Smith Translation pages at the back of the LDS Bible for John 1:20-26 (p. 808) and for Matthew 17:10-14 (p. 803).

A remarkable event happened when the Savior went up on the mountain with Peter, James, and John. While He was there He was transfigured before them; and Elias (meaning Elijah) and Moses appeared with Him.

Elijah in the Book of Mormon

There are quite a few references to Elijah in the New Testament, but with the conclusion of the Lord's earthly life we shift to the Book of Mormon. The Lord is speaking to the Nephites: "And it came to pass that he commanded them that they should write the words which the Father had given unto Malachi, which he should tell unto them." In other words, the Lord dictated to them what we know as the third and fourth chapters of Malachi. "And it came to pass that after they were written he expounded them. And these are the words which he did tell unto them, saying: Thus said the Father unto Malachi."

And then there's the third chapter of Malachi as it appears in the Old Testament, and then the fourth chapter, which concludes: "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord; and he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse." (3 Nephi 24:1; 25:5-6.)

Moroni's Wording on Elijah

Centuries later the Father and the Son appeared to the young Joseph Smith. Four years after that, being anxious as to what his standing was with the Lord, Joseph was praying earnestly in the upper room in that little home in the state of New York when the room filled with light and there stood before him an angel, a celestial being. Moroni introduced himself; then he began to teach Joseph.

In three visits the angel taught him for most of that night, each time giving the same instruction. The next day, when Joseph attempted to cross the fence because his father had sent the weary boy home, he collapsed. Moroni was there again, and for the fourth time he taught Joseph exactly the same things. After he was through, he commanded Joseph to return and tell his father everything that he'd heard. That's highly significant.

In those four visits Moroni quoted from Joel, from Isaiah, from Acts, from elsewhere; and he quoted from the third chapter and all of the fourth chapter of the book of Malachi. Also, "He quoted many other passages of scripture" (Joseph Smith-History 1:41). Moroni's wording of Malalchi's words on Elijah, more explanatory than those of our King James Bible, was as follows:

Behold, I will reveal unto you the Priesthood, by the hand of Elijah the prophet, before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord,

And he shall plant in the hearts of the children the promises made to the fathers, and the hearts of the children shall turn to their fathers.

If it were not so, the whole earth would be utterly wasted at his coming. (D&C 2:1-3.)

Point number one: The earth would be smitten with a curse. Point number two: The whole earth would be utterly wasted at His coming.

Later, when for the first time the Church was going to publish what is now the Doctrine and Covenants, the Prophet received a revelation, given as the Lord's preface to the book, which became section one. In part it said:

And the anger of the Lord is kindled, and his sword is bathed in heaven, and it shall fall upon the inhabitants of the earth.

And the arm of the Lord shall be revealed; and the day cometh that they who will not hear the voice of the Lord, neither the voice of his servants, neither give heed to the words of the prophets and apostles, shall be cut off from among the people;

For they have strayed from mine ordinances, and have broken mine everlasting covenant. (D&C 1:13-15.)

And that was what was wrong with the churches of the world. They had lost or strayed from the ordinances.

Visitations in the Kirtland Temple

Thirteen years after Moroni's first visits to Joseph Smith, the Saints had finally built a temple-the Kirtland Temple. This wasn't like the temples we build today. It was more like what we would call a meetinghouse. But it was sufficient to have accomplished what needed to be accomplished, because the Lord came and accepted it.

A week after the dedication of that temple, the Saints held a regular Sunday service there. The Prophet Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery assisted the other Brethren in distributing the sacrament, which that day had been blessed by the Council of the Twelve Apostles. After this, the two men went up by the pulpits in the one end, the dividing curtains having been dropped for privacy. Joseph recorded: "I Â� bowed myself, with Oliver Cowdery, in solemn and silent prayer. After arising from prayer, the following vision was opened to both of us" (note that they were not asleep, they were not kneeling with their eyes closed, they were standing there): "We saw the Lord standing upon the breastwork of the pulpit."

One of the things the Lord told them would have sounded extremely unlikely if it had come from anyone else. In effect He told them that from that house, that day, the message given there would go to all the world.

Others appeared. Moses came and committed to Joseph and Oliver the keys of the gathering of Israel. Elias-the Elias, not a messenger but the prophet Elias-appeared and committed the keys of the dispensation of the gospel of Abraham.

Then "after this vision had closed, another great and glorious vision burst upon us; for Elijah the prophet, who was taken to heaven without tasting death, stood before us, and said: Behold, the time has fully come, which was spoken of by the mouth of Malalchi-testifying that he [Elijah] should be sent, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord come-"

And then the message again: "To turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the children to the fathers, lest the whole earth be smitten with a curseÂ� . By this ye may know that the great and dreadful day of the Lord is near, even at the doors." (D&C 110:1-16.)

Joseph Smith talked twice to the Twelve and gave a couple of funeral sermons-one the King Follett funeral sermon-in which he explained a great deal about the purpose of doing temple work.

The Sealing Power

The Prophet asked the questions: Why send Elijah? What keys did he bring? What would he tell us if he could be here?

Elijah could not die until the keys he held had been delivered to Peter, James, and John, which took place on the Mount of Transfiguration. He had to stay in the flesh to give those keys. Why? Because, as the Prophet Joseph Smith explained, he was the last prophet of the Old Testament to hold the keys of the priesthood, including the sealing power. (See Teachings, pp. 158, 172, 337.)

Let me point out something here. Every worthy man in the Church holds the priesthood. When you're an elder in Israel, you hold the Melchizedek Priesthood. And you hold "all the priesthood." No office brings anything to the priesthood, but the priesthood brings everything to the office.

Let me say it another way. Let's say you are a father and your son is to be ordained an elder. You are an elder or a high priest. It is perfectly in order for a stake president to invite you to ordain your son an elder, because you hold the Melchizedek Priesthood. It is not in order for the stake president to ask you to set your son apart as the president of an elders quorum. That is something different. Keys are involved.

The sealing power is something like that. It is the most sacred, supernal power that anyone can hold on earth. Joseph Smith said: "If a man gets a fulness of the priesthood of God he has to get it Â� by keeping all the commandments and obeying all the ordinances of the house of the Lord" (Teachings, p. 308). He also said, "Wherever the ordinances of the Gospel are administered there is the priesthood" (Andrew F. Ehat and Lyndon W. Cook, eds., The Words of Joseph Smith [Provo: Religious Study Center, BYU, 1980], p. 9). I was interested when I read that, because I would have thought he would say it the other way-where you have the priesthood you have the ordinances. But he said that where the ordinances are, there is the priesthood. Without the ordinances you don't have the priesthood.

The priesthood keys, which incorporate the sealing power, are held by fifteen men. For instance, working according to the pattern that the prophet designates, I can exercise the sealing power, making something binding on earth and in heaven; similarly I can loosen. I hold the sealing power, but I can't give it to anyone else. The prophets have made it very clear that those keys rest with the President of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Nothing is more closely held than the keys of the sealing power. When the prophet bestows the sealing power on a temple worker the recipient has the power to seal, but he cannot pass it on-as, when authorized, an elder can ordain another person, for example. And that sealing power can be withdrawn.

Now, the question might be asked, What did Elijah and Moses give to Peter, James, and John on the Mount of Transfiguration? President Joseph Fielding Smith said-and he is in harmony with all of the prophets on this-Elijah gave them "exactly the same keys that he bestowed upon the heads of Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery [in the Kirtland Temple], Â� the keys of the everlasting priesthood, the keys of the sealing power" (Doctrines of Salvation 2:111-12). That is the sealing validation of all ordinances.

The Aaronic Priesthood, or the preparatory priesthood, has the right to baptize. Joseph Smith said, "I went into the woods and there I met an angel, and he instructed me." And then he told what the angel told him, and essentially it was this. He talked about the Aaronic Priesthood. He said that the Aaronic Priesthood has the power to baptize, but it can't confirm, so that a person can't become a member of the Church without the operation of the higher priesthood, the greater priesthood.

Elder Joseph Fielding Smith said:

I hope we understand that if we want to receive the fulness of the priesthood of God, then we must receive the fulness of the ordinances of the house of the Lord and keep his commandments. This idea that we can put off our salvation because of some weaknesses of the flesh until the end Â� will get us nowhere.

Â� No man can get the fulness of the priesthood outside of the temple of the Lord. There was a time when that could be done, for the Lord could give these things on the mountain tops-no doubt that is where Moses got it, that is no doubt where Elijah got it-and the Lord said that in the days of poverty, when there was no house prepared in which to receive these things, that they can be received on the mountain tops.

But now we have temples, and you cannot get these blessings on the mountain tops; you will have to go into the house of the Lord, and you cannot get the fulness of the priesthood unless you go there.

Then this is tempering: "Do not think because somebody has a higher office in this Church than you have that you are barred from blessings, because you can go into the temple of the Lord and get all the blessings there are that have been revealed, if you are faithful." (Doctrines of Salvation 3:131-32.)

Elder Joseph Fielding Smith talked also about a common misperception as to what Elijah restored. "Some of you may be saying [it was] the keys of baptism for the dead. No, it was not just that. Some may be thinking it was the keys of the salvation of the dead. No, it was not just that, that was only a portion of it. [He brought] the keys of the everlasting priesthood, the keys of the sealing powerÂ� . That included a ministry of sealing for the living as well as the dead." (Doctrines of Salvation 2:111-12.)

If you read sections 127 and 128 of the Doctrine and Covenants you don't find the word dead except you find living. Sometimes they are spaced apart, but they are there. The text talks about ordinances for the "living" and ordinances for the "dead."

Speaking on this subject the Prophet Joseph Smith said: "Elijah, what would you do if you were here? Would you confine your work to the living alone? No." (Teachings, p. 337.)

In the Church we have the priesthood. We have the manifestations of it as it relates to the ordaining of worthy men and setting the Church in order and managing Church organizations. But almost separate and apart from that we have the ordinances. A modern scripture says: "And this greater priesthood administereth the gospel and holdeth the key of the mysteries of the kingdom, even the key of the knowledge of God. Therefore, in the ordinances thereof, the power of godliness is manifest. And without the ordinances thereof, and the authority of the priesthood, the power of godliness is not manifest unto men in the flesh." (D&C 84:19-21.)

In the early days of the Church the Saints didn't know how to use this power in behalf of the dead. Overjoyed when given the knowledge that they could baptize for the dead, they went to the river and began doing so. Elder Wilford Woodruff recalled those days as follows: "[Joseph Smith] never stopped till he got the fulness of the word of God to him concerning baptism for the dead. But before doing so he went into the Mississippi River, and so did I, as well as others, and we each baptized a hundred for the dead, without a man to record a single act that we performed."

It seems they just ran out to the river and began baptizing for their ancestors.

And then this: "Why did we do it? Because of the feeling of joy that we had, to think that we in the flesh could stand and redeem our dead. We did not wait to know what the result of this would be, or what the whole of it should be." (Discourses of Wilford Woodruff, p. 153.)

Similarly it took time for the Saints to understand the matter of sealings. They knew that there was a sealing, yet they didn't know quite what it was, so they began by getting themselves sealed to living prophets. I remember a story about that. Mantua, the little valley up above Brigham City, Utah, originally belonged to Abraham Hunsaker. Elder Lorenzo Snow, who founded the city and was president of the stake, persuaded Abraham Hunsaker to take Honeyville and give him Mantua, because they had a lot of Saints coming in from Scandinavia, and that little valley up in the mountains looked more like Scandinavia.

There was a little Danish woman up there about whom we in Brigham City used to hear this story. It seems that sometimes when she had visitors she felt close to-home teachers, for example-she would take them into the bedroom and then close the door. They'd wonder what this was all about until they would see on the back of the door a picture of President Lorenzo Snow, and she would say: "That's President Snow. I sealed to him."

Well, people used to be sealed to the prophets.

In April 1894, this changed. In general conference President Wilford Woodruff explained a little about what the Saints had been doing, and from the pulpit he talked to the temple presidents-"President Lorenzo Snow of the Salt Lake Temple, President M. W. Merrill of the Logan Temple, President J. D. T. McAllister of the Manti Temple, and President D. H. Cannon of the St. George Temple, and those associated with them." He told them: "You have acted up to all the light and knowledge that you have had; but you have now something more to doÂ� . We have not fully carried out those principles in fulfillment of the revelations of God to us, in sealing the hearts of the fathers to the children and the children to the fathers."

For some time he had not felt satisfied with the way this important matter was being handled.

When I went before the Lord to know who I should be adopted to (we were then being adopted to prophets and apostles), the Spirit of God said to me, "Have you not a father who begot you?"

"Yes, I have."

"Then why not honor him? Why not be adopted to him."

"Yes," said I, "that is right."

I was adopted to my father and should have had my father sealed to his father, and so on back. And the duty that I want every man who presides over a temple to see performed from this day henceforth and forever, unless the Lord God Almighty commands otherwise, is, let every man be adopted to his father. That is the will of God to this people.

And that's when we got the lineage straightened out and we knew what this sealing of families was all about.

President Woodruff continued:

What business have I to take away the rights of the lineage of any man. What right has any man to do this? Â�

I say to all men who are laboring in these temples, carry out this principle, and then we will make one step in advance of what we have had before.

Myself and counselors conversed upon this and were agreed upon it, and afterwards we laid it before all the apostles who were here (two were absent) Â� and the Lord revealed to every one of these men-and they would bear testimony of it if they were to speak-that that was the word of the Lord to them. I never met anything in my life in this Church that there was more unity upon than there was upon that principle. They all feel right about it, and that it is our duty. That is one principle that should be carried out from this time henceforth.

We want the Latter-day Saints from this time to trace their genealogies as far as they can, and to be sealed to their fathers and mothers. Have children sealed to their parents, and run this chain through as far as you can get itÂ� . This is the will of the Lord to this people, and I think when you come to reflect upon it you will find it to be true. (Discourses of Wilford Woodruff, pp. 153-57.)

The Prophet Joseph Smith very specifically said the word turn as Elijah used it or as it is used in the book of Malachi means "seal." Seal, bind, turn-that's what we're about.

Ordinances in the Growing Church

A major goal of the Church is as far as possible to make the fulness of the ordinances available to a growing membership worldwide. Now, what ordinances? Fundamental is the ordination to the priesthoods. When we get to the higher ordinances there are the washings and the anointings, the endowment, then the sealing, in which we organize the only permanent unit of the Church. This total process progressively moves the soul toward the perfection we all seek.

On one occasion I was dedicating some little chapels in Guatemala, and in one of the meetings there was a little Indian woman who had one tooth-a small shriveled-up Indian woman in native clothes. I thought she was beautiful. She came up and hugged me and held on to me; then she said, in simple Spanish which I could understand, that she had been to the temple. And she was glorious. She said, "This is my son." And there stood a branch president in a white suit, white shirt, and a necktie. And there sat his wife, an attractive, modestly dressed woman in a lovely dress, with her hair styled beautifully. You could have taken that couple into any social setting anywhere in the world and immediately they would have been accepted as refined people. Why? They had their temple ordinances. They were transformed, transfigured.

There is nothing over which I feel more deeply than the temple experience. It seems we never stop learning its meanings. I can remember President McKay in his advanced years, on an occasion when he stood in the temple among the Brethren and quoted the temple ceremony at great length. Just quoted it, and explained it. Then finally he stopped, clasped his large hands together, and stood silent for a little while. Then he said, "Brethren, I think I am finally beginning to understand."

I have a deep conviction that the sealing power, which is the authority to perform the sacred ordinances, is crucial to the Church. The name linkage in our genealogies is crucial to the Church members.

Elsewhere in this book I have mentioned the occasion when President Marion G. Romney expressed to me his great joy at the anticipation of being beyond the veil with his beloved Ida. And another time, on his birthday, I sat in his office with him and Elder LeGrand Richards and listened for twenty minutes as those two venerable Apostles talked about going through the veil and meeting their wives. That was a moving experience.

I know such joy is real. I know that the ordinances we perform and the sealings both in marriage and in family lineage are true and efficacious. True also is Mormon's statement: "Has the day of miracles ceased? Or have angels ceased to appear unto the children of men? Or has he withheld the power of the Holy Ghost from them? Or will he, so long as time shall last, or the earth shall stand, or there shall be one man upon the face thereof to be saved? Behold, I say unto you: Nay." (Moroni 7:35-37.)

No, the ministering of angels has not ceased. It has its part in the glorious work that goes on in our temples. And that work is the most sacred work we can have a part in on this earth.

Address given to Temple Planning Committee November 1979.
