Missionary calls to Church educational institutions, August 16, 1898

1898-August 16-An Autobiography of Joseph G. Nelson, 1965, p. 86.

In a letter dated October 2, 1888 (see earlier in this volume) the First Presidency had changed a "call" of Joseph G. Nelson from a foreign mission to that of a teacher in the Church Academy school system. Again ten years later the same individual is asked to accept a "call" to teach in the Oneida Stake Academy at no salary other than board and lodging, in place of serving as a missionary in a foreign land. These two documents emphasize the point that teachers in the L.D.S. Church school system have a "call" to service equivalent to a missionary "call" to foreign missions. A similar statement of this same philosophy concerning service in the L.D.S. Church school system was made by the First Presidency of the Church again in 1942.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Salt Lake City, Utah, August 16, 1898.

Elder Jos. G. Nelson, Viento

DEAR BROTHER:

I am directed by the First Presidency to enquire if it would be agreeable to your feelings and consistent with your circumstances to fill a mission of about one year's duration, not in a foreign land, but in the educational interests of the youth of Zion. The proposition is this, that you accept as a mission the duty of acting as an instructor in the Academy of the Oneida Stake of Zion for the school year 1898-9 (which commences on the second Monday in September) that you do so without other pay than that your board and lodging will be provided, and your labors will be accounted so much missionary work to your credit in building up the kingdom of God on the earth. Kind]y give this your prayerful consideration and answer as promptly as your circumstances will permits.

Your brother, GEORGE REYNOLDS, Sec.
