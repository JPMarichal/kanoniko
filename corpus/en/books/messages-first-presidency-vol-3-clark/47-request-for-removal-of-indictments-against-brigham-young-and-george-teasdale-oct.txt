James R. Clark, Messages of the First Presidency, vol. 3 ( 1966)

Request for removal of indictments against Brigham Young and George Teasdale, October 6, 1892

1892-October 6-Letter, Church Historian's Library, Salt Lake City, Utah.

Charles S. Varian, to whom this letter was addressed, was United States Attorney in Utah. The Brigham Young referred to was Brigham Young, Jr., member of the Quorum of Twelve Apostles. George Teasdale, Franklin D. Richards and Francis M. Lyman, who signed the letter along with President Woodruff and his counselor, were also members of the Quorum of Twelve. George Q. Cannon, the other counselor in the First Presidency, was absent in Washington, D.C., during much of this period as the "Mormons" sought amnesty from the Government of the United States and the statehood for Utah. The letter is written on the letterhead of the First Presidency of the Church.

Office of The First Presidency of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Salt Lake City, Utah, Oct. 6, 1892.

Hon. C. S. VARIAN, City.

DEAR SIR:

It would afford us much pleasure, if you deem it consistent with the public welfare, to have you take such action as you may consider advisable towards the removal of the indictments standing against Messrs. Brigham Young and George Teasdale for violations of the anti-polygamy laws of Congress.

These gentlemen have both ben [sic] absent from the United States for several years, and while thus abroad have joined us in asking the President for amnesty and in making the several promises with regard to their future observance of the law. We believe them to be worthy of your kindest consideration.

Yours very respectfully, WILFORD WOODRUFF, JOSEPH F. SMITH, FRANKLIN D. RICHARDS, FRANCIS M. LYMAN.
