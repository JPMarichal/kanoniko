Leon R. Hartshorn, Outstanding Stories by General Authorities, vol. 2 ( 1971)

"See What You Have Done For Me"

I was down in the Southwest Indian Mission. I went into church one day, and a fine-looking Navajo woman came in. And the missionary said to me, "I want to introduce you to this sister." So he took me up and introduced me to her, and we had a little chat, the best we could. Then after meeting this young missionary came to me and said, "Well, I am glad you met her. A few months ago my companion and I went to the Navajo reservation. We went into a hogan. There lay this woman on her back—on a sheepskin. She had been there for six long years. She had never stood up. When we were about to leave, she said to us, 'Isn't there something you people do for people who are sick and afflicted?' I said, 'Yes.' She said, 'Will you please do it for me?' So we got down on our knees; one anointed her with oil, the other one sealed the anointing. After we left—we were only a short distance away—she came running out from the hogan and said, 'Come back and see what you have done for me.' She has been walking ever since."

(Speeches of the Year, "Miracles," February 18, 1953, p. 10.)
