I learned about Konaâs people from touring the district. Missionary Work in Kona

On this trip, Elder Cox and I were gone from the mission home for twenty-one days. We slept in nineteen different homes and walked about a hundred miles. Religions in Kona

The various religious backgrounds in Kona made missionary work particularly challenging. As to the religion of the people in Kona, it was somewhat as follows. The Hawaiians, of a naturally religious nature, belonged either to the Catholic, the Protestant, or the Latter-day Saint religions. Memories of Kona

As I tell the story of my labors in Kona, I see the faces of so many people who, though I have not seen them for years, remain fixed in my memory and dear to my heart. Here they are at random.

I remember a sister who lived near the volcano and who was the wife of a rancher. He did not belong to the Church, but she was a most devout member, deprived of attending church meetings because of her isolated abode. She read the scriptures regularly and made up for her lack of association with the members by heaping hospitality and love upon the missionaries when they came to her home.

I recall a dear couple down in Kamilo who lived alone on the seashore, eking out a living from the sea.
