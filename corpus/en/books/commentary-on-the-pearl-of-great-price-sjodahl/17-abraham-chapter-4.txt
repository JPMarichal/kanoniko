ABRAHAM CHAPTER 4

SCAbr 4 1Abr 4 2VERSES 1-2. The Gods organized and formed the heavens and the earth. The noble and great ones, the Gods, who in Council assembled had together with all the sons of God ratified the Plan of Salvation which was presented to them by their great High Priest Who was their Father. Events then followed in rapid succession.

SCAbr 4 1Abr 4 2Under the leadership of Him who was like unto the "son of man" they, that is the Gods, went unto that far-off space where a plentiful supply of "materials" existed (3:24) and therein "organized and formed the heavens and the earth."

"The earth, after it was formed, was empty and desolate." Nothing but a vast, forbidding waste covered its face. There was no light to illumine the darkness that pervaded its vast expanse. It was a bleak and lifeless orb. Nevertheless, no time was lost, nothing was left undone that might fit it for man's survival thereon. "The Spirit of the Gods" infused the whole Creation, and work went on to make the earth a habitable place for man's abode. (See Moses 2:2.)

SCAbr 4 3Abr 4 4Abr 4 5VERSES 3-5. Let there be light . . . The Gods saw that their efforts so far in building an earth and bringing it to an issue of full success, were only the preliminary steps necessary to the accomplishment of their purposes. They saw that darkness hid their work from its culmination, and forthwith said: "Let there be light." And the text says: "There was light." In this we may be sure; the Gods did more than just talk about it. "And they (the Gods) comprehended the light." Comprehend the light, means that not only they observed the light, but They put it to its highest use.

SCAbr 4 3Abr 4 4Abr 4 5Light is to the earth what knowledge is to the human mind. Light is life whether on the earth, or in man. Light and life are twins begotten by the soul's nature and being, which cannot be blacked out. Darkness is death and destruction; light is growth and development. The Gods saw and recognized that truth, and life-giving light is what they provided.

SCAbr 4 3Abr 4 4Abr 4 5The Gods caused the light to be divided from the darkness. Here, we may judge that this was the beginning of that great phenomenon—the revolution of the earth upon its axis. The text says: "This was the first, or the beginning, of that which They called day and night." (See Moses 2:3-5; Genesis 1:3-5.)

SCAbr 4 9Abr 4 10Abr 4 11Abr 4 12Abr 4 13VERSES 9-13. Let the waters . . . be gathered together unto one place, and let the earth come up dry. (See Moses 2:9-13; Genesis 1:9-13.)

SCAbr 4 14Abr 4 15Abr 4 16Abr 4 17Abr 4 18Abr 4 19VERSES 14-19. It was the fourth time. (See Moses 2:14-19; Genesis 1:14-19.) The noun, time, is used here instead of day, the word used in the Writings of Moses as they appear in both the Pearl of Great Price and the Book of Genesis. Evidently Moses used a term that was prevalent in the language of the Oriental people among whom he was the leader. It denotes a season wherein work can be done. It may properly be translated, day, but it is subject to various interpretations as to its meaning. Moses did not mean a day of twenty-four hours; neither did he mean as some suppose a thousand years, but he meant a day of indefinite length of time. A period of action, or state of being, without definite reference to the calendar.

SCAbr 4 14Abr 4 15Abr 4 16Abr 4 17Abr 4 18Abr 4 19There are a number of Hebrew words that when translated into English are spelled the same and are pronounced alike, but are to be interpreted to mean that to which they refer. Day is one; rock is another. The Hebrew Rock sometimes refers to the Creator. The word, rock, also means a stony, or a pebbly-mass. Thus we read: "They remembered that God was their Rock"; that is: They remembered that God was their Creator." (Psalm 78:35.) Also we read where Moses struck the rock, and there gushed forth a bounteous stream of pure water to slake the thirst of the Israelites as they wandered in the hot Arabian Desert. (Numbers 20:11.) Rock may refer to several different things.

SCAbr 4 14Abr 4 15Abr 4 16Abr 4 17Abr 4 18Abr 4 19The same may be said of the word, day. Day is a period of time which the Hebrews reckoned as beginning at sunset, and represents one revolution of the earth upon its axis. (Genesis 7:24.) However, again it may stand for a period of time not defined as one of any definite duration between two points of the calendar. This is the meaning Moses had in mind when he used the word whose English equivalent is day. Again we read: "For I know that my Redeemer liveth, and that He shall stand at the latter day upon the earth." (Job 19:25.) The day expressed by Job is a day of an indefinite period of time. Zechariah asks a question which also illustrates this meaning: "For who hath despised the day of small things?" (Zechariah 4:10.)

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23VERSES 20-23. It was the fifth time. (For Comments, See Moses 2:20-23; Genesis 1:20-23.)

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23In his writings as they appear in the Pearl of Great Price and the Book of Genesis, we need not wonder because the language used by Moses is similar in many ways to that used by Abraham. Corresponding verses which deal with the same facts although written by one, are almost a duplication of the other. Words and terms are often alike. This is understandable when we realize that Moses had access to many records and documents now lost. Noah must have left some records to his descendants; Books of Remembrance and what not. Abraham and Joseph both wrote books. And the former one mentioned, Abraham had in his possession certain records "Which delineate the chronology running back from myself to the beginning of the Creation, for the records have come into my hands, which I hold unto the present time." (Abraham 1:28.)

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23Moses had in his possession the Urim and Thummim which the Lord gave Abraham while he was yet in Ur. It became part of Aaron's vestments when that worthy clothed himself in the garments of Priesthood. It is not unlikely that Abraham's writings were also in Moses' keeping, and from them he copied much that was written by the Father of the Faithful. Moses would certainly avail himself of all literary helps that came within his reach. This would account for any similarity of speech that exists in their writings.

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23It is well to remember that the division of the Books of the Bible (including Genesis) into Chapters and verses, and even sentences, is not part of the original arrangement of the Sacred Book. "The present division of the scriptures into chapters and verses . . . is not of divine origin, nor are they of great antiquity. The Vulgate was the first Version divided into Chapters; a work undertaken by Cardinal Hugo in the 13th Century, or as Jahn thinks, by Langton, Archbishop of Canterbury 1227. He introduced the division into Chapters only. The Hebrew Scriptures were similarly divided by Mordecai Nathan in 1445, and in 1661, Athias added to his printed text, the division into verses. The New Testament was divided in the same way by Robert Stephens, who is said to have completed it in the year 1551. . . . As might be expected, these divisions are very imperfect, and even when not inaccurate, they tend to break the sense and to obscure the meaning." (The Bible Handbook, Dr. Joseph Angus, p. 60.)

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23Let us remember also that the ancient Hebrew scribes did not write the vowels; they used few, if any, conjunctions or prepositions. They did not punctuate as we do and wrote their words using a long, single scroll which formed, as it were, a continuous block of writings.

SCAbr 4 20Abr 4 21Abr 4 22Abr 4 23The division of the Pearl of Great Price into Chapters and verses also was not part of the original manuscript, but in 1902 was so arranged by James E. Talmage, who without doubt followed the pattern laid down by Hugo, Langton, Nathan, Athias, and others in so dividing the Bible text.
