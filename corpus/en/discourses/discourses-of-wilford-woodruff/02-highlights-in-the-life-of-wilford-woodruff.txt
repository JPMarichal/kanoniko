viHighlights in the Life of Wilford Woodruff

vi1807—March 1: Born at Farmington, Hartford County, Connecticut.

vi1821—May 1: Goes to live with Colonel George Cowles to learn trade of a miller with subsequent occupational experience in various communities.

vi1832—Spring: First learns of the "Mormons" through a newspaper article. Later moves to Rushland, Oswego County, New York, with his brother Azmon, purchasing a farm.

vi1833—December 29: Elders Zera Pulsipher and Elijah Cheney call at the Woodruff farm in Rushland and appoint a meeting. December 31: Baptized a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints by Elder Zera Pulsipher.

vi1834—January 25: The Rushland Branch organized. Ordained a teacher by Zera Pulsipher. April 1: P. P. Pratt at Rushland recruiting for Zion's Camp, communicates with Wilford Woodruff. April 11: Leaves for Kirtland, Ohio, having settled his affairs, April 25: Arrives at Kirtland. Meets Joseph Smith and makes his home with him. May 1: Leaves Kirtland with vanguard of Zion's Camp. June 24: Arrives Clay County, Missouri, and spends summer there with Lyman Wight. November 5: Ordained a priest at Adam-ondi-Ahman, by Simeon Carter. November 13: Licensed by Edward Partridge, leaves for mission to Arkansas and Tennessee.

vi1835—April 4: Ordained an elder by Warren Parrish, near Memphis, Tennessee.

vi1836—April 21: While on mission learns from D. W. Patten that he has been called to membership in the Second Quorum of Seventy. May 31: Ordained a seventy by D. W. Patten and Warren Parrish. December 20: Called and ordained to First Quorum of Seventy at Kirtland, taking his place January 3, 1837.

vi1837—April 13: Married to Miss Phoebe Carter at Kirtland by Frederick G. Williams. April 15: Receives patriarchal blessing from Joseph Smith, Sr. May 14: Leaves Kirtland for mission to the eastern states, the Fox Islands.

vi1838—July 8: Called to the Quorum of the Twelve by revelation to the Prophet Joseph Smith. August 9: Learns of his call to the Twelve. October 9: Leaves the mission field, Scarboro, Maine, to return to the west, arriving in Illinois December 19. Spends the winter in Rochester and Quincy.

vi1839—April 26: Ordained to the apostleship by Brigham Young at the temple site, Far West, Missouri. August 8: Leaves home in Montrose, Iowa, for mission to England with the Twelve Apostles, arriving in New York City, November 8, and sailing for Liverpool December 19.

vi1840—January 11: Arrives at Liverpool, going first to Preston, then laboring in Staffordshire until March 3. March 4: Arrives at home of John Benbow, Ledbury, Herefordshire, and baptizes about 600 people in the next 38 days. Goes to London August 11.

vi1841—February 8: With Heber C. Kimball, secures copyright to Book of Mormon at Stationer's Hall, London, in name of Joseph Smith, Jr, April 20: Sails for the United States with Brigham Young and six additional members of the Twelve, arriving New York, May 20; Nauvoo, October 5. October 5: Appointed a member of the Nauvoo City Council. November 21: Participates in dedication of baptismal fount, Nauvoo Temple. December 26-27: Views Urim and Thummim at Joseph Smith's home, Nauvoo.

vii1842—January: Hauls stone for the Nauvoo Temple, later becomes business manager of the Times and Seasons.

vii1843—April 19: Appointed to a mission in the eastern states to raise funds for the Nauvoo Temple, leaving July 7, arriving in Philadelphia on August 5, and returning to Nauvoo on November 4.

vii1844—March 4: Moves into a new home in Nauvoo and leaves shortly for another mission to the eastern states. June 1: In Michigan. June 26: Arrives Boston, Massachusetts, where, on July 9, sees press account of death of Joseph Smith June 27. Leaves for Nauvoo on July 20, arriving with the absent twelve on August 6. August 8: Attends the special conference in Nauvoo sustaining Brigham Young and the Twelve apostles. August 12: Chosen to preside over the European Mission, leaving Nauvoo on August 28 and sailing from New York December 8.

vii1845—January 3: Arrives Liverpool, stays one year, returning to New York City March 6, 1846.

vii1846—April 13: Arrives in Nauvoo from his European mission and finds the initial exodus had begun in February. April 30: With Orson Hyde, dedicates the Nauvoo Temple. May 16: Leaves Nauvoo to join the "Camp of Israel," arriving at Mount Pisgah, June 15.

vii1847—April 7: Leaves Nebraska for the west in the pioneer company. July 24: Enters Salt Lake Valley, Brigham Young riding in the Woodruff carriage, plants a bushel of potatoes and irrigates them. August 26: Returns with Brigham Young to Winter Quarters, arriving October 31.

vii1848—June 21: Leaves Winter Quarters for mission to gather the eastern Saints.

vii1850—April 9: Leaves New York with company for the west.

vii1851—May 21: Leads an emigrant company overland from Kanesville, arriving in Salt Lake City on October 14, and was immediately appointed to a vacancy in the territorial legislature, serving one term in the lower house, and subsequently twenty sessions in the upper house.

vii1856—April 7: Appointed Assistant Church Historian.

vii1858—Chosen President of the Deseret Agricultural and Manufacturing Society, retaining the position until December 15, 1877.

vii1867—December 16: Participates in the re-establishment of a School of the Prophets in Salt Lake City.

vii1871—Pioneers in Rich County, Utah.

vii1876—November: To St. George for the dedication of the Temple.

vii1877—January 1: Offers a dedicatory prayer and is appointed to preside over the St. George Temple. August 21: Is baptized for the signers of the American Declaration of Independence, St. George Temple. On the 29th goes to Salt Lake City: death of President Young.

vii1879—February 7: In Arizona, to escape prosecution under the anti-Polygamy act, and does missionary work among the Indians.

vii1880—January 26: Receives a revelation concerning the duty of the apostles and elders of Israel, which was submitted to the Twelve on April 4 and accepted by that body as the word of the Lord. October 6: Sustained as President of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. On December 28, writes a prefatory prayer conforming to his revelation of January 26, 1880, which receives the approval of President John Taylor.

viii1881—January: Becomes Superintendent of the Y. M. M. I. A.

viii1884—May 17: Attends dedication of the Logan Temple, and afterwards tours Idaho settlements with Heber J Grant.

viii1885—January 1: Attends dedication of the Brigham Young College, Logan. January 14 goes into exile (Edmonds Act), with headquarters at St. George, until 1887

viii1887—July 17: Watches funeral procession of President John Taylor from veiled windows of the historian's office.

viii1889—April 7: Sustained as President of the Church. December 12: Issues significant manifesto on Church-State relationships.

viii1890—September 24: Issues manifesto on plural marriage.

viii1893—April 6: Dedicates the Salt Lake Temple.

viii1896—November 5: Changes Fast Day observance from Thursday to first Sunday of each month.

viii1897—March 1: Attends public celebration of his ninetieth birthday.

viii1898—July 24: Makes last public appearance in Salt Lake City at dedication of Pioneer Park. September 2: Died in San Francisco, California, honored and respected by his nation and by its forty-fifth state, which he helped create.
