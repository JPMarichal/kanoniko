3. Missionary Conduct

3.0 Introduction

As a representative of Jesus Christ, your conduct reflects on Him, His Church, and the message you carry. Missionaries are expected to maintain the highest standards of personal behavior at all times. These standards of conduct are not restrictions but protections that help you remain worthy, effective, and joyful in your service.

The way you speak, act, and treat others communicates more than words alone. People will judge the gospel message in part by the character of the missionaries who deliver it. Strive to be an example of Christlike living in every interaction.

3.1 Christlike Behavior

Missionaries should embody the attributes of Christ described in the scriptures: faith, hope, charity, virtue, knowledge, patience, humility, diligence, and obedience. These attributes are developed through consistent effort, prayer, and reliance on the Atonement of Jesus Christ.

Speak kindly and respectfully to all people, regardless of their response to your message. Avoid contention, argument, or debate. The Spirit of the Lord cannot dwell where there is contention. When faced with opposition or rejection, respond with grace and dignity.

Show genuine interest in the lives and well-being of those you meet. Listen attentively, express empathy, and offer sincere encouragement. Building authentic relationships is central to effective missionary work and Christlike discipleship.

Be punctual, dependable, and honest in all your dealings. Keep your commitments to investigators, members, and fellow missionaries. Your reliability builds trust and demonstrates the integrity of the message you bear.

3.2 Temple Worthiness

Missionaries are expected to maintain temple worthiness throughout their service. This means living the standards of the gospel as outlined in the temple recommend interview questions. If at any time you feel your worthiness is in question, speak promptly and honestly with your mission president.

The mission president is authorized to conduct worthiness interviews and provide counsel and support. He acts with the authority of a common judge in Israel and can help you navigate challenges and receive the blessings of repentance.

Do not allow feelings of shame or fear to prevent you from seeking help. The Savior's Atonement provides healing and hope for all who sincerely repent. Your mission president will treat your confidences with respect and help you move forward.

3.3 Law of Chastity

The law of chastity is a fundamental commandment of God. Missionaries must abstain from all sexual relations outside of marriage between a man and a woman who are legally and lawfully married. This includes sexual intercourse, passionate kissing, necking, petting, or any other form of sexual contact or intimacy.

Missionaries should avoid all forms of pornography, including images, videos, written material, or any other media that is sexually explicit or arousing. Viewing, possessing, or distributing pornography is a serious violation of missionary standards and will be addressed by the mission president.

Guard your thoughts and feelings. Avoid situations that could lead to temptation. Do not be alone with someone of the opposite sex. Do not engage in flirtatious or romantic behavior. If you experience inappropriate feelings or attractions, seek the Lord's help through prayer and speak with your mission president for counsel and support.

Violations of the law of chastity are serious matters that require confession to the mission president and sincere repentance. The mission president will provide counsel and may involve the appropriate ecclesiastical process depending on the nature and severity of the transgression.

3.4 Honesty

Missionaries must be honest in all their interactions. This includes being truthful in teaching, reporting, and personal conversations. Do not exaggerate, deceive, or misrepresent the truth in any circumstance.

Honest reporting is essential. Report your activities, numbers, and progress accurately. Do not inflate statistics, fabricate contacts, or misrepresent your work. Honest reporting enables mission leaders to provide appropriate support and make inspired decisions.

Be honest with yourself about your strengths, weaknesses, and areas for growth. Self-honesty is the foundation of genuine repentance and lasting change. The Lord blesses those who are honest in heart.

3.5 Interactions with Adults

Missionaries should treat all adults with respect and courtesy. When interacting with members, investigators, or community members, maintain a professional and friendly demeanor. Avoid developing exclusive or inappropriate relationships.

When teaching individuals of the opposite sex, always have your companion present and, when possible, a member of the same sex as the person being taught. Do not meet privately with adults of the opposite sex under any circumstances.

Be mindful of cultural norms and local customs regarding personal space, physical contact, and social interaction. Adapt your behavior to show respect for the culture in which you serve while maintaining gospel standards.

3.6 Interactions with Children and Youth

Missionaries must follow strict guidelines when interacting with children and youth. Never be alone with a minor. Always ensure that another adult, preferably a parent or guardian, is present during any interaction with someone under the age of 18.

Avoid physical contact with children and youth beyond a brief, culturally appropriate handshake or greeting. Do not carry, hold, or tickle children. These guidelines exist to protect both the children and the missionaries.

If you become aware of any abuse or neglect of a child, report it immediately to your mission president. The mission president will follow established procedures for reporting suspected abuse to appropriate authorities.

3.7 Recreation, Photos, and Videos

Approved recreational activities are those that are safe, wholesome, and consistent with missionary standards. Missionaries may participate in approved sports, visit approved sites, and engage in other uplifting activities during preparation day. Activities that are dangerous, competitive to an extreme degree, or likely to result in injury are not permitted. Examples of prohibited activities include contact sports, water sports beyond approved swimming, rock climbing, bungee jumping, and similar high-risk activities.

Missionaries may take photographs and videos to document their mission experience. Photos and videos should be appropriate, uplifting, and consistent with the dignity of missionary service. Do not take or share photos or videos that are irreverent, embarrassing, or that could be misinterpreted.

When posting photos or videos to approved social media platforms, follow all guidelines for technology use outlined in this handbook and the supplement. Obtain consent before photographing or recording others, especially investigators and children.

3.8 Technology

Technology is a powerful tool for missionary work when used righteously. Missionaries are provided with approved devices and should use them according to established guidelines. Technology should be used to support finding, teaching, and communication purposes.

General standards for technology use include using devices only for approved purposes, following mission-specific technology policies, and maintaining appropriate digital conduct. Do not use technology for entertainment, personal social media browsing, gaming, or other unapproved purposes.

The internet and social media can be used to find and teach when done according to mission guidelines. Missionaries should use approved platforms and follow content guidelines established by the mission president and the Church's Missionary Department.

Email and messaging should be used for communication with family, mission leaders, and approved contacts. Keep communications brief, uplifting, and focused. Do not engage in excessive or inappropriate digital conversations.

The use of technology requires self-discipline and accountability. Your companion and mission leaders can help you maintain appropriate use. If you struggle with technology misuse, speak with your mission president immediately. Detailed guidelines for safe technology use are provided in the supplement (section 7.5).

3.9 Family Communication

Missionaries are encouraged to maintain regular communication with their families. Weekly emails or letters on preparation day are the standard means of family communication. Mission presidents may also authorize phone or video calls on designated occasions, including Christmas Day, Mother's Day, and other approved times.

Communication with family should be uplifting and focused on sharing positive experiences, testimony, and growth. Avoid excessive complaining, homesickness-driven conversations, or discussions that may cause unnecessary worry for family members.

If a family emergency arises, the mission president should be contacted first. The mission president will facilitate communication and provide support during difficult times. Do not contact family directly in emergency situations without first informing mission leadership.

Missionaries should not communicate with friends of the opposite sex or former romantic interests during their mission. Keep your focus on the work and your relationship with the Savior.
