2. Missionary Organization and Activities

2.0 Introduction

This chapter describes how missions are organized and how missionaries carry out their daily work. Understanding the organizational structure, the role of companions, and the rhythm of daily activities will help you serve more effectively. A mission is organized to provide support, accountability, and spiritual growth for every missionary.

2.1 Mission Leadership

The mission president is called by the First Presidency and holds the keys to preside over the mission. He is responsible for the welfare, training, and direction of all missionaries within his mission boundaries. The mission president's wife serves alongside him and provides vital support, mentoring, and counsel to missionaries, particularly in matters of emotional well-being and personal adjustment.

The mission presidency, which includes the mission president and his counselors, oversees the spiritual and temporal welfare of missionaries. They conduct interviews, provide training, make transfer decisions, and ensure that the work moves forward in an orderly manner.

Young missionary leaders are called from among the missionaries to assist the mission presidency. These callings include senior companions, trainers, district leaders, sister training leaders, zone leaders, and assistants to the president. Each of these roles carries specific responsibilities outlined in the supplement (section 7.1). Young missionary leaders serve under the direction of the mission president and should lead with love, humility, and diligence.

Every missionary bears personal responsibility for their own spiritual growth, obedience, and effectiveness. Regardless of calling or assignment, each missionary is expected to study diligently, work faithfully, and support their companion and fellow missionaries. Leadership callings do not confer greater authority in matters of personal worthiness or salvation; they are opportunities to serve.

2.2 Companions

Missionaries are assigned companions by the mission president or his designees. Companions are to remain together at all times, except in specific circumstances approved by mission leadership. The companionship is a fundamental unit of missionary work, providing mutual support, safety, and accountability.

Supporting each other is essential to a successful companionship. Missionaries should pray together, study together, plan together, and work together. They should be patient with one another's weaknesses and build on each other's strengths. Disagreements should be resolved through kind communication, mutual respect, and prayer. If a companionship is experiencing persistent difficulty, missionaries should seek counsel from their district leader, zone leaders, or mission president.

Staying together means remaining within sight and hearing of your companion during the day and sleeping in the same apartment at night. Missionaries should never be alone with someone of the opposite sex or with a minor without another adult present. There are limited exceptions to the stay-together rule, such as using the restroom, but these should be brief and infrequent.

Working within your assigned area is important. Missionaries should not leave their assigned area without permission from the mission president or a designated leader. When working outside the area is necessary, such as for zone conferences or special assignments, missionaries should follow established procedures and travel with approved companions.

2.3 Missionary Work Activities

Exchanges involve temporarily pairing with a different companion for training or evaluation purposes. Exchanges are arranged by district leaders, zone leaders, or the mission president and should follow approved guidelines. During an exchange, missionaries continue to follow all standards and remain within approved boundaries.

Transfers occur periodically as directed by the mission president. Missionaries may be reassigned to a new area, a new companion, or both. Transfers should be accepted with faith and a willing heart, trusting that the mission president receives revelation for the mission.

Teaching with members strengthens both the investigators and the members involved. Missionaries should actively seek opportunities to include members in teaching appointments, fellowshipping efforts, and service activities. Member participation in missionary work is one of the most effective ways to help people come unto Christ and remain active in the Church.

Referrals from members, friends, and other missionaries are valuable opportunities. Missionaries should follow up promptly on all referrals and communicate with the referring party about progress when appropriate.

Meetings are an important part of missionary life. Missionaries attend district council meetings, zone conferences, mission leadership council, and other gatherings as directed by mission leadership. These meetings provide training, coordination, and spiritual nourishment. Missionaries should come prepared, participate actively, and apply what they learn.

Baptismal interviews are conducted by authorized priesthood holders, typically the district leader or zone leader, to determine the readiness of candidates for baptism. The interviewing missionary should ensure the candidate understands and is committed to the baptismal covenants, meets the requirements outlined in Preach My Gospel, and is making the decision freely and without pressure.

Baptisms and confirmations are sacred ordinances and should be conducted with reverence and careful preparation. Missionaries should coordinate with local ward or branch leaders to ensure proper scheduling, facility use, and participation of local members. The baptismal service should be a spiritual and uplifting experience for the candidate, their family, and all who attend.

2.4 Daily Activities and Schedule

Missionary life follows a structured daily schedule designed to balance spiritual growth, study, teaching, and personal care. Maintaining a consistent schedule builds discipline, increases effectiveness, and supports physical and emotional health.

A typical daily schedule includes the following activities:

6:30 a.m. - Wake up, prayer, exercise, prepare for the day
8:00 a.m. - Personal study (scriptures, Preach My Gospel, language study)
9:00 a.m. - Companion study (shared study, planning, role-playing)
10:00 a.m. - Begin proselyting activities (teaching, finding, service)
12:00 p.m. - Lunch
1:00 p.m. - Continue proselyting activities
5:00 p.m. - Dinner
6:00 p.m. - Evening proselyting activities (teaching appointments, visits)
9:00 p.m. - Return to apartment, plan for the next day
9:30 p.m. - Personal time, journal writing, preparation for bed
10:30 p.m. - Lights out

This schedule may be adjusted by the mission president to accommodate local conditions, climate, cultural norms, or specific mission needs. Missionaries should follow the schedule approved for their mission.

Goal setting is an important part of daily and weekly planning. Missionaries should set specific, measurable, and faith-driven goals for finding, teaching, baptizing, and retaining. Goals should be discussed with companions and reviewed regularly with district and zone leaders. Setting and pursuing goals helps missionaries focus their efforts and recognize the Lord's hand in their work.

Study sessions are vital to missionary effectiveness. Personal study should focus on the scriptures, Preach My Gospel, and language learning as applicable. Companion study provides time to prepare lessons, practice teaching, study together, and strengthen the companionship. Both study periods should be conducted with purpose and diligence.

Meals should be simple, nutritious, and taken at regular times. Missionaries should be mindful of the time spent on meals and avoid using meal appointments primarily as social occasions. When eating with members or investigators, missionaries should steer conversations toward gospel topics and missionary purposes.

2.5 Preparation Day

Each week missionaries are given a preparation day, commonly known as P-day. This day provides time to handle personal affairs, rest, and refresh. Preparation day activities typically include laundry, shopping for food and supplies, writing letters and emails to family, cleaning the apartment, and approved recreational activities.

A sample preparation day schedule includes:

6:30 a.m. - Wake up, prayer, exercise
8:00 a.m. - Personal and companion study
9:00 a.m. - Apartment cleaning, laundry, shopping
12:00 p.m. - Lunch
1:00 p.m. - Approved recreational activities or personal errands
4:00 p.m. - Prepare for evening proselyting
6:00 p.m. - Evening proselyting activities
9:00 p.m. - Return to apartment
10:30 p.m. - Lights out

Missionaries should use preparation day wisely and not waste it. Approved activities include visiting approved cultural or historical sites, participating in approved sports and recreation, and other wholesome activities. Activities that are dangerous, expensive, or inconsistent with missionary standards are not permitted.

Preparation day should not be skipped or traded for additional proselyting time. The Lord has established this day for rest and renewal, and missionaries who neglect it risk burnout and diminished effectiveness.

2.6 Sabbath Day Activities

The Sabbath is a holy day set apart for worship, rest, and spiritual renewal. Missionaries should attend all scheduled Church meetings in their assigned ward or branch. They should participate reverently, take the sacrament, and look for opportunities to minister to members and investigators.

When appropriate, missionaries may be invited to speak, teach, or assist with Church meetings. They should accept such invitations willingly and prepare thoughtfully.

Fasting is observed on the first Sunday of each month in harmony with the Church's standard fast day. Missionaries should fast with a purpose and offer sincere fast offerings. The handbook cautions against excessive fasting beyond the standard monthly fast. Fasting more frequently or for extended periods is not encouraged and should not be undertaken without the approval of the mission president. Excessive fasting can harm physical health and is not consistent with the balanced approach to missionary service.

On the Sabbath, missionaries should avoid shopping, recreational activities, and other temporal pursuits. The focus should be on worship, study, teaching, and quiet reflection.

2.7 Service in the Community

Service is a core expression of discipleship. Jesus Christ spent His ministry serving others, healing the sick, feeding the hungry, and lifting the downtrodden. Missionaries should follow His example by seeking opportunities to serve in their communities.

Service projects should be organized in coordination with local Church leaders and mission leadership. Missionaries should follow all guidelines for service activities, including safety protocols and time limitations. Service should not interfere with the primary purpose of missionary work, which is to invite others to come unto Christ.

When serving, missionaries should wear appropriate clothing, use proper safety equipment, and avoid dangerous activities. They should not operate heavy machinery, work on rooftops, or engage in activities that pose undue risk of injury.

Missionaries should approach service with genuine love and without expectation of return. Service blesses the lives of those served and opens hearts to the gospel message. It also strengthens the missionary's own testimony and Christlike character.
