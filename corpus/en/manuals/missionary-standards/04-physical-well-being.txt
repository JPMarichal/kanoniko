4. Physical Well-Being

4.0 Introduction

Your physical health is essential to your ability to serve effectively as a missionary. The Lord expects you to care for your body as a sacred gift. Physical well-being encompasses nutrition, exercise, stress management, medical care, safety, housing, transportation, money management, and personal appearance. Taking proper care of your physical needs enables you to serve with energy, clarity, and endurance throughout your mission.

The Word of Wisdom (Doctrine and Covenants 89) provides foundational health principles for all members of the Church. As a missionary, you should follow these principles diligently and also attend to the practical aspects of daily health maintenance.

4.1 Nutrition

Good nutrition fuels your body and mind for the demands of missionary service. Eat balanced meals that include fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein. Follow the principles of the Word of Wisdom by avoiding alcohol, tobacco, coffee, tea, and harmful substances.

Drink adequate water throughout the day. In most climates, missionaries should consume 6 to 12 glasses of water daily, adjusting for heat, humidity, and physical activity. Dehydration can cause fatigue, headaches, and reduced concentration.

Eat regular meals at consistent times. Do not skip meals, particularly breakfast, as this leads to low energy and poor focus during study and proselyting. When eating with members, be gracious but mindful of portion sizes and nutritional balance.

In areas where food safety is a concern, take precautions to avoid foodborne illness. Wash fruits and vegetables, cook food thoroughly, and avoid street food or unclean water sources unless you are certain of their safety. If you have dietary restrictions or food allergies, communicate these clearly to your companion and those who prepare food for you.

Avoid excessive consumption of sugar, processed foods, and caffeinated energy drinks. These may provide short-term energy but lead to crashes and long-term health problems.

4.2 Exercise

Regular physical exercise promotes physical health, mental clarity, and emotional well-being. Missionaries should exercise for at least 30 minutes each morning as part of their daily schedule. Approved exercises include walking, jogging, calisthenics, stretching, yoga, cycling, and other moderate activities.

Exercise should be conducted in appropriate clothing and in safe locations. Avoid exercising in isolated areas, on busy roads, or in extreme weather conditions. Always exercise with your companion.

Do not engage in high-risk or contact sports during exercise time. Activities such as basketball, soccer, or other competitive sports are reserved for preparation day and must be conducted according to approved recreational guidelines. During daily exercise, focus on activities that maintain fitness without risk of injury.

If you have a pre-existing medical condition that limits your ability to exercise, consult with the mission office and follow their guidance. Modified exercise programs can be developed to accommodate individual health needs.

4.3 Stress Management

Missionary service can be emotionally demanding. Stress, homesickness, discouragement, and interpersonal challenges are normal experiences. Recognizing and managing stress is essential to long-term well-being and effectiveness.

Healthy stress management techniques include regular prayer and scripture study, maintaining a consistent daily schedule, adequate sleep (7 to 8 hours per night), regular exercise, journaling, and confiding in trusted companions or leaders.

If you are experiencing persistent sadness, anxiety, hopelessness, or other mental health concerns, do not suffer in silence. Speak with your companion, mission president, or the mission office. Professional mental health resources are available through the Church's Missionary Department and should be accessed without stigma or shame.

The mission president and his wife are key resources for emotional support. They are available to listen, counsel, and connect you with additional help when needed. You may also contact the Missionary Department's mental health hotline for confidential support.

Avoid unhealthy coping mechanisms such as withdrawal, excessive sleep, overeating, or misuse of technology. These behaviors may provide temporary relief but ultimately worsen stress and diminish your ability to serve.

4.4 Medical Care

Missionaries should seek appropriate medical care when they are ill or injured. Do not ignore symptoms or attempt to serve through serious illness. Contact the mission office for guidance on accessing medical services in your area.

The Church provides medical insurance or medical care arrangements for missionaries serving away from home. Familiarize yourself with the medical care procedures for your mission, including how to contact the mission nurse or medical advisor, where to seek emergency care, and how to submit claims or reports.

Keep an updated record of your medical history, allergies, medications, and emergency contacts. The In Case of Emergency form at the beginning of this handbook should be current at all times.

If you develop a fever above 101 degrees Fahrenheit (38.3 degrees Celsius), experience persistent vomiting or diarrhea, sustain an injury that limits your mobility, or exhibit any other concerning symptoms, contact the mission office immediately. Do not wait for symptoms to worsen before seeking help.

Take prescribed medications as directed. Do not share medications with your companion or other missionaries. If you need a prescription refill or a new medication, work through the mission office to obtain it through approved channels.

Dental care, vision care, and routine health maintenance should be addressed through the mission office. Schedule preventive care appointments as recommended and do not neglect ongoing health needs.

4.5 Dangerous Situations

Missionaries must be aware of potential dangers in their environment and take steps to avoid them. Dangerous situations include natural disasters, civil unrest, criminal activity, unsafe neighborhoods, and hazardous conditions.

Follow all safety guidance provided by the mission president and the Missionary Department. Obey local laws and curfews. Avoid areas known to be unsafe, particularly after dark. Do not open the door to strangers at night. Keep doors and windows locked.

If you encounter a dangerous situation, prioritize your personal safety and that of your companion. Remove yourselves from the situation as quickly and safely as possible. Do not confront or engage with aggressive individuals. Report all dangerous incidents to the mission president immediately.

Be aware of emergency procedures for your area, including evacuation routes, emergency shelters, and contact information for local authorities. The mission office maintains emergency protocols that should be reviewed regularly.

Detailed guidelines for preparing for, recognizing, responding to, and reporting dangerous situations are provided in the supplement (section 7.8).

4.6 Housing

Missionary housing should be safe, clean, and conducive to the needs of missionary service. The mission office arranges housing for missionaries. If you have concerns about the safety or condition of your housing, report them to the mission office promptly.

Keep your apartment or housing clean and orderly. A clean living environment supports physical health, mental clarity, and the spirit of your service. Establish a cleaning schedule with your companion and maintain it consistently.

Respect the property of your landlord and neighbors. Avoid loud noise, particularly during evening and early morning hours. Follow all terms of the housing agreement and report any maintenance issues to the mission office.

Privacy and safety in housing are important. Lock doors and windows when you leave and when you sleep. Do not invite strangers into your home. If you feel unsafe in your housing, contact the mission office immediately.

Do not make unauthorized changes to your housing, such as painting, drilling holes, or rearranging furniture belonging to the landlord. Leave the housing in the same or better condition than you found it.

Detailed housing safety guidelines are provided in the supplement (section 7.9).

4.7 Transportation

Missionaries use various modes of transportation depending on their mission assignment. These may include walking, bicycles, public transportation, and, in some missions, vehicles. Regardless of the mode of transportation, safety is the highest priority.

When walking, stay on sidewalks and designated pedestrian paths. Obey traffic signals and crosswalk rules. Be alert to your surroundings, especially near busy streets, intersections, and areas with limited visibility. Avoid walking in isolated or dangerous areas, particularly after dark.

Bicycle safety requires wearing an approved helmet at all times, following traffic laws, using lights and reflective gear in low-light conditions, and maintaining your bicycle in good working condition. Do not ride at excessive speeds, perform stunts, or ride in hazardous conditions.

Missionaries authorized to use vehicles must possess a valid driver's license, follow all traffic laws, wear seatbelts at all times, and never drive while fatigued, distracted, or under the influence of any substance. Vehicle use is a privilege that carries serious responsibility. Accidents and violations must be reported to the mission office immediately.

Public transportation should be used according to mission guidelines. Be aware of schedules, routes, and safety considerations. Keep personal belongings secure and maintain situational awareness while traveling.

Detailed transportation safety guidelines are provided in the supplement (section 7.10).

4.8 Money Management

Missionaries are expected to manage their finances responsibly. Living within your means and handling money honestly are important aspects of personal stewardship.

The missionary fund provides a monthly living allowance to cover basic expenses such as food, transportation, and personal necessities. Use this allowance wisely and avoid unnecessary or extravagant purchases. Keep a record of your expenditures and budget carefully.

Do not borrow money from or lend money to other missionaries, members, or investigators. Financial entanglements create complications and can damage relationships. If you experience financial difficulties, speak with the mission president for assistance.

Tithing and fast offerings should be paid from personal income, not from the missionary living allowance. If you receive gifts of money from family, manage them wisely and within the guidelines provided by the mission president.

Do not engage in business activities, investments, or employment during your mission. Your full attention and energy should be devoted to missionary service.

4.9 Dress and Appearance

Your appearance as a missionary communicates respect for yourself, for the people you serve, and for the Lord. Dress and grooming standards help missionaries maintain a professional, approachable, and respectful appearance.

Elder missionaries should wear dress shirts with ties, dress slacks, and appropriate dress shoes. Suits or sport coats are worn for Church meetings, baptismal services, and other formal occasions. Hair should be clean, neatly styled, and of a conservative length. Facial hair should be clean-shaven unless otherwise approved by the mission president for medical or cultural reasons.

Sister missionaries should wear modest dresses, skirts, or dress slacks with blouses or sweaters. Hemlines should be at or below the knee. Clothing should be professional, neat, and appropriate for the climate and culture. Hair should be clean and neatly styled. Makeup, if worn, should be modest and natural in appearance.

All missionaries should maintain good personal hygiene, including regular bathing, dental care, and use of deodorant. Clothing should be clean, pressed, and in good repair.

Name tags should be worn at all times when engaged in missionary activities. The name tag identifies you as a representative of Jesus Christ and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

The mission president may provide additional guidance on dress and appearance standards specific to the mission's climate, culture, or circumstances. Follow these guidelines with a willing and obedient heart, understanding that your appearance is part of your witness of the Savior.
