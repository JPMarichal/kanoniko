6. Completion of Your Mission

6.0 Introduction

The completion of your full-time mission is a significant milestone, but it is not the end of your discipleship or your missionary service. The principles, habits, and spiritual growth you have developed during your mission are intended to bless you throughout your life. This chapter provides guidance on staying connected to the work, returning home with honor, and continuing to serve as a disciple of Jesus Christ.

6.1 Staying Connected

As your mission draws to a close, it is important to remain fully engaged in the work until your final day of service. Do not mentally or emotionally check out before your release date. The Lord expects your best effort from the first day to the last.

Continue to follow all missionary standards, maintain your daily schedule, and support your companion. Avoid the temptation to begin planning post-mission activities, employment, or education at the expense of your current responsibilities.

Stay spiritually grounded through consistent prayer, scripture study, and reliance on the Savior. The final weeks and months of your mission can be among the most productive and spiritually enriching if you remain focused and faithful.

Maintain strong relationships with your mission leaders, fellow missionaries, and the members and investigators in your area. The connections you build during your mission can be a source of lifelong friendship and spiritual support.

6.2 Returning Home

Your mission president will coordinate the details of your return home, including travel arrangements and your release from full-time missionary service. Follow all instructions from the mission office regarding departure procedures, travel, and reporting.

Upon returning home, you will be released from your missionary calling by your stake president. This release marks the formal conclusion of your full-time service. Approach this meeting with gratitude and a willingness to share your experiences and testimony.

The transition from missionary life to post-mission life can be challenging. You may experience a range of emotions, including joy, sadness, disorientation, and uncertainty. These feelings are normal. Give yourself time to adjust, and lean on the Lord, your family, and your Church leaders for support.

Maintain the habits and standards you developed on your mission. Continue to pray regularly, study the scriptures, attend Church meetings, and serve others. These practices are the foundation of a lifelong relationship with the Savior.

Seek to apply the skills you developed as a missionary, such as goal-setting, time management, teaching, listening, and working with others. These skills are valuable in education, employment, relationships, and Church service.

Be patient with yourself and others as you adjust. Not everyone will understand your mission experience, and the pace and priorities of life at home may feel very different. Stay close to the Lord and trust His plan for your life.

6.3 Serving after Your Mission

Your missionary service does not end when you are released. As a returned missionary, you carry a lifelong responsibility to share the gospel, minister to others, and build the kingdom of God wherever you are.

Look for opportunities to assist the full-time missionaries in your home ward or stake. Offer to accompany them on teaching appointments, provide referrals, and fellowships new members and investigators. Your experience and testimony can be a powerful support to those currently serving.

Continue to develop your discipleship through temple worship, Church service, family life, and personal scripture study. The covenant path extends far beyond the mission, and each step brings you closer to the Savior and the blessings of eternal life.

Share your mission experience with others in appropriate settings. Your testimony of the Savior and the blessings of missionary service can inspire others to serve, strengthen their faith, and deepen their commitment to the gospel.

The Lord is pleased with your service. He knows your sacrifices, your struggles, and your growth. As you continue to walk the covenant path with faith and diligence, He will bless you with peace, purpose, and joy. Remember the promise of the Savior: "And if it so be that you should labor all your days in crying repentance unto this people, and bring, save it be one soul unto me, how great shall be your joy with him in the kingdom of my Father!" (Doctrine and Covenants 18:15).
