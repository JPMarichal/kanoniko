Joshua 1–8; 23–24: Lesson 63

# Joshua 23–24

“Choose You This Day”

At the end of his life, Joshua reminded the Israelites who the Lord is and what He had done for them. Joshua invited them to choose to serve the Lord instead of false gods. This lesson can help students choose to serve the Lord over other influences.

## Possible Learning Activities

### The influence of distractions

- What about these distractions might make them seem more appealing or more important than serving the Lord?

- What might be the long-term effect of continually choosing these influences over the Lord?

- How might you be serving the Lord or serving something or someone else through your daily choices?

- What might be the long-term effect if you continue to make these choices?

### The Lord our God

After leading the Israelites over the Jordan River on dry ground (see Joshua 3–4), the Lord blessed the Israelites in their many battles with the Canaanites. The Israelites took possession of the land that God had promised them (see Joshua 5–12). The land was divided among the twelve tribes (see Joshua 13–22). Toward the end of his life, Joshua gave one final message to the Israelites (see Joshua 23–24).

Read Joshua 23:3–11; 24:3–11, looking for what the Lord had done and would do for Israel.

- What are some examples of how the Lord has blessed us today?

Read Joshua 24:14–15. Rewrite what Joshua taught as if he were teaching the same thing today.

- How would you summarize Joshua’s teachings in verse 15?

- What are ways that we can serve the Lord today?

- How can what you know about the Lord help you choose to serve Him?

### Choosing to serve the Lord

Read Joshua 24:24–25, looking for what the Israelites chose to do.

In your study journal, respond to one of the following sets of questions:

- What do you want to remember about the Lord that can help you serve Him when it might be difficult? How do you think remembering the Lord in those moments could help you?

- What will you start doing or stop doing to choose to serve the Lord? What blessings might you experience from doing that?

Read Joshua 24:26–27, looking for what Joshua did to remind the Israelites of their choice to serve the Lord.

Elder Dale G. Renlund of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles taught about the long-term effect of choosing to serve the Lord:

  

  We need to choose whom we will serve. The magnitude of our eternal happiness depends on choosing the living God and joining Him in His work. …

  Choosing to follow the Father’s plan is the only way we can become inheritors in His kingdom. (“Choose You This Day,” Ensign or Liahona, Nov. 2018, 106)

Ask them to share why a rock might be a good symbol for Jesus Christ or how He has been a rock for them.</p> </section> <section> <header> <p class="title" data-aid="158897612" id="aside2_title4"><a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/6?lang=eng">Joshua 6</a>. Blessings of obedience</p> </header> <p data-aid="158897616" id="aside2_p3">To learn about the blessings of obeying the Lord, students could study the account of the battle of Jericho. Consider asking students to think about what would happen if someone didn’t follow protocol—for example, a surgeon not cleaning their instruments properly, a farmer not plowing their field properly, and so on. Invite students to think about why it is important to follow instructions we receive with exactness.</p> <p data-aid="158897621" id="aside2_p4">Then you might invite students to read <a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/6?lang=eng&amp;id=p1-p5%2Cp10#p1">Joshua 6:1–5, 10</a>. Ask them to mark the instructions the Lord gave Israel for their battle with Jericho. Note that the word <em>compass</em> means to “go around.” Ask students what they would have thought if they were an Israelite receiving these instructions.</p> <p data-aid="158897626" id="aside2_p5">Invite students to read <a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/6?lang=eng&amp;id=p12-p16%2Cp20#p12">Joshua 6:12–16, 20</a>, looking for how the Israelites responded. Invite students to share what impresses them about the battle and what truths they identify from this account. Students might identify a truth like the following: <strong>As we faithfully obey the Lord with exactness, He will bless us.</strong> You could invite students to share how they have seen the Lord bless them or others for obedience.</p> </section> <section> <header> <p class="title" data-aid="158897631" id="aside2_title5"><a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/7?lang=eng">Joshua 7</a>. Consequences of disobedience</p> </header> <p data-aid="158897636" id="aside2_p6">To discuss the negative consequences of disobeying the Lord, you could help students study the account of Achan. Explain that the Lord commanded the Israelites not to keep anything for themselves after they defeated the city of Jericho (see <a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/6?lang=eng&amp;id=p18-p19#p18">Joshua 6:18–19</a>). Unfortunately, an Israelite man named Achan took some clothing, gold, and silver from the city for himself (see <a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/7?lang=eng&amp;id=p1%2Cp21#p1">Joshua 7:1, 21</a>).</p> <p data-aid="158897641" id="aside2_p7">Invite students to read <a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/josh/7?lang=eng&amp;id=p1-p7%2Cp10-p13#p1">Joshua 7:1–7, 10–13</a>, looking for how Achan’s disobedience affected the people of Israel. Then help students identify a truth like the following: <strong>When we choose to disobey the Lord’s commandments, we bring negative consequences upon ourselves and others.</strong> Ask students to think about how small acts of disobedience can affect us or the people around us over time.</p> </section> [/SIDEBAR]
