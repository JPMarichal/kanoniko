# Joyfully Bound

Text: Anna M. Molgard; original language: English

Music: Anna M. Molgard and Rachel Pettit Mohlman

Anna Molgard was navigating a series of physical, emotional, and spiritual challenges when she heard President Russell M. Nelson teach that “the joy we feel has little to do with the circumstances of our lives and everything to do with the focus of our lives” (“Joy and Spiritual Survival,” Ensign or Liahona, Nov. 2016, 82). She didn’t understand how joy could be possible amidst such hardship and felt prompted to begin a pattern of consistent temple worship. “Over time,” she said, “the joy of my ancestors and of other temple patrons started to permeate my life. My circumstances didn’t change, but I began to experience my own joy through my covenant relationship with God.”

Six years later, while still in this time of personal trial, Sister Molgard watched a friend testify at the pulpit with remarkable faith and optimism despite his own significant challenges. As he spoke, the text and music for “Joyfully Bound” began to come into her mind. “It felt like a message from a loving Father, describing the joy of being bound to Him by covenant,” she said. “I could sing the song of everlasting joy, even as storms raged in my life” (see Doctrine and Covenants 45:71).

Sister Molgard and her sister-in-law Rachel Mohlman collaborated to create simple, joyful music that would help others feel the joy of being bound to the Lord as covenant sons and daughters.

Verse 1 of this hymn is based on Alma’s invitation to people gathered at the Waters of Mormon to be baptized as a witness of their covenant with God. The chorus reflects their joyous acceptance (see Mosiah 18:8–11).

## Principles

- The Lord invites each of us to come unto Him by making sacred covenants with Him.

- Through our sincere efforts to keep our covenants, we bind ourselves to Jesus Christ.

- As we keep our covenants with God and obey His commandments, we can receive His help and feel His love.

## Questions to Ponder

- How have receiving ordinances and making covenants with God brought blessings into your life?

- Why is being bound to Jesus Christ through covenants a joyful blessing rather than a restriction?

- How can your worship in the house of the Lord help you draw near to Him?

## Activities for Families

- Learn about being joyfully bound. After singing the hymn together, use a simple object lesson to help your family understand the idea of being bound and strengthened through covenants. Pretend a family member’s finger is broken, and gently wrap it using a strip of cloth or small bandage along with a pencil or wooden stick as a splint. As you do, talk with your family about what it means to be bound by covenant to Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ. Discuss how this kind of binding provides us with strength and protection. Invite your children to share why they think being joyfully bound to the Savior through covenants is a blessing.

- Sing to discover answers to questions. With your family, take turns reading one question at a time. Then sing the related part of the hymn. After singing, find the answers in the words of the hymn.

  - When we make our baptismal covenant, what do we show we are willing to do? (Sing verse 1.)

  - During the sacrament, what do we promise to do? (Sing verse 2.)

  - What are some of the blessings of temple covenants? (Sing verse 3.)

  - To whom are we bound? (Sing the chorus.)

  - How are we bound? (Sing the chorus again.)

  After discussing each answer, talk about the joy and strength that come from being bound to the Lord through covenants.
