7. Appendix

7.0 Introduction

This appendix provides detailed supplementary guidelines for topics covered in the Missionary Standards for Disciples of Jesus Christ. These guidelines support the standards outlined in the main handbook and provide specific instructions for leadership responsibilities, service, respect for others, temple attendance, technology use, difficult situations, health, safety, housing, transportation, and priesthood ordinances.

Missionaries should study this appendix thoroughly and refer to it regularly. The guidelines in this appendix carry the same authority as the standards in the main handbook and should be followed with the same diligence and faithfulness.

7.1 Young Missionary Leadership Responsibilities

Young missionary leaders are called from among the missionaries to assist the mission presidency in leading and supporting the work. These callings provide opportunities for growth, service, and leadership development. All young missionary leaders serve under the direction of the mission president and should lead with humility, love, and the guidance of the Spirit.

Senior Companion

The senior companion is responsible for leading the companionship in daily activities, planning, and decision-making. The senior companion sets the tone for the companionship through personal example, diligence, and faithfulness to standards.

Responsibilities of the senior companion include:
- Leading daily planning sessions and ensuring the companionship follows the approved schedule.
- Mentoring the junior companion and helping them develop teaching, finding, and language skills.
- Ensuring the companionship maintains obedience to all missionary standards.
- Communicating regularly with district leaders and zone leaders about the work in the area.
- Taking initiative in finding and teaching activities while encouraging the junior companion to participate fully.
- Modeling Christlike behavior, patience, and positive attitude.

The senior companion should not dominate the companionship but should lead through collaboration and mutual respect. Both companions share responsibility for the work, and the senior companion's role is to guide, not to control.

Trainer

A trainer is a senior companion assigned to train a newly arrived missionary. The trainer has a sacred responsibility to help the new missionary adjust to mission life, learn effective missionary skills, and develop confidence and faith.

Responsibilities of the trainer include:
- Providing a warm welcome and orientation to the area, the mission, and missionary life.
- Teaching the new missionary how to plan, study, find, teach, and follow up effectively.
- Modeling proper use of Preach My Gospel, the missionary handbook, and approved resources.
- Helping the new missionary develop language skills (in applicable missions).
- Providing regular, constructive feedback and encouragement.
- Ensuring the new missionary understands and follows all missionary standards.
- Being patient with the new missionary's learning curve and celebrating their progress.
- Reporting regularly to the district leader and zone leaders on the new missionary's development.

The training period is a critical time for the new missionary. A good trainer can set the foundation for a successful and fulfilling mission experience. Trainers should take this responsibility seriously and seek the Lord's guidance in their efforts.

District Leader

The district leader is responsible for the spiritual and temporal welfare of the missionaries in the district. The district leader reports to the zone leaders and works under the direction of the mission president.

Responsibilities of the district leader include:
- Conducting weekly district council meetings focused on training, planning, and spiritual nourishment.
- Conducting regular companionship exchanges to provide training and assessment.
- Conducting baptismal interviews for candidates within the district (as authorized by the mission president).
- Monitoring the health, safety, and well-being of missionaries in the district.
- Reporting key indicators, concerns, and needs to zone leaders and the mission president.
- Providing mentoring and support to missionaries in the district who are struggling or in need of encouragement.
- Ensuring that all missionaries in the district are following approved schedules, standards, and guidelines.
- Facilitating communication between the district and zone leadership.

The district leader should be a servant-leader who prioritizes the needs of the missionaries in the district while maintaining their own companionship responsibilities.

Sister Training Leaders

Sister training leaders provide specialized support, training, and mentoring for sister missionaries within their assigned zones or districts. They work closely with zone leaders and the mission president to address the unique needs and challenges of sister missionaries.

Responsibilities of sister training leaders include:
- Conducting regular exchanges with sister companionships for training and mentoring.
- Providing training on teaching skills, finding strategies, and personal development.
- Offering support and counsel to sister missionaries who are facing challenges.
- Reporting on the welfare and progress of sister missionaries to zone leaders and the mission president.
- Modeling excellence in missionary work and personal standards.
- Facilitating unity and collaboration among sister missionaries.
- Addressing concerns related to housing, safety, health, and well-being specific to sister missionaries.

Sister training leaders should be mature, experienced, and spiritually strong missionaries who can inspire and uplift their fellow sisters through example and loving guidance.

Zone Leaders

Zone leaders oversee the work within a designated zone of the mission. They serve as key liaisons between the missionaries in the zone and the mission president. Zone leaders carry significant responsibility and should be models of dedication, wisdom, and spiritual maturity.

Responsibilities of zone leaders include:
- Planning and conducting zone conferences focused on training, inspiration, and mission goals.
- Conducting regular exchanges with companionships throughout the zone for training and evaluation.
- Working closely with district leaders to monitor the progress and well-being of missionaries.
- Conducting baptismal interviews as authorized by the mission president.
- Reporting regularly to the mission president and assistants on the state of the zone.
- Providing mentoring, support, and guidance to district leaders and missionaries.
- Addressing conflicts, concerns, and challenges within the zone with wisdom and compassion.
- Coordinating transfers, housing, transportation, and other logistical matters within the zone as directed.
- Ensuring that all missionaries in the zone are living standards and following approved guidelines.

Zone leaders should balance their leadership responsibilities with their own companionship work. They should not neglect their own teaching, finding, and personal development in the pursuit of zone-wide objectives.

Assistants to the President

Assistants to the president (commonly known as APs) work directly with the mission president to coordinate and lead the mission. They carry the broadest scope of responsibility among young missionary leaders and serve as the mission president's primary representatives in the field.

Responsibilities of assistants include:
- Assisting the mission president in planning transfers, training, and mission-wide initiatives.
- Conducting exchanges with missionaries throughout the mission as directed.
- Providing training and support to zone leaders, district leaders, and sister training leaders.
- Facilitating mission leadership councils and other key meetings.
- Reporting to the mission president on the state of the mission, including key indicators, concerns, and trends.
- Serving as a communication link between the mission president and missionaries in the field.
- Addressing mission-wide challenges, concerns, and opportunities with the mission president's guidance.
- Modeling the highest standards of missionary conduct, obedience, and effectiveness.

Assistants should remember that their calling is one of service, not prestige. They should lead with humility, seek the guidance of the Spirit, and always point missionaries to the Savior as the true leader of the work.

7.2 Guidelines for Service

Formally Organized Service

Service projects should be formally organized in coordination with local Church leaders, community organizations, or the mission office. Before participating in any service project, missionaries should ensure that:

- The project is approved by the mission president or a designated leader.
- The project has a clear purpose, defined tasks, and an expected duration.
- Adequate supervision and safety measures are in place.
- The project does not expose missionaries to undue physical risk.
- The project does not interfere with primary proselyting responsibilities.

Missionaries should not independently organize large-scale service projects without approval. Small acts of service, such as helping a neighbor carry groceries or assisting an elderly member with yard work, are encouraged and do not require formal approval, but should still be conducted with a companion and within the bounds of common sense and safety.

When working with community organizations, missionaries should clearly represent themselves as volunteers from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. They should not engage in political activities, commercial ventures, or projects that could create a conflict of interest or embarrass the Church.

Safety during Service

Safety is paramount during all service activities. Missionaries should:

- Wear appropriate clothing and footwear for the type of work being performed.
- Use proper safety equipment, including gloves, eye protection, and hard hats when necessary.
- Follow all instructions from project supervisors and safety coordinators.
- Avoid operating heavy machinery, power tools, or equipment they are not trained to use.
- Avoid working on rooftops, scaffolding, or elevated surfaces.
- Avoid working with hazardous materials, chemicals, or in environments with known safety risks.
- Stay hydrated and take breaks as needed, especially in hot or physically demanding conditions.
- Report any injuries, no matter how minor, to the mission office.
- Stop work immediately if conditions become unsafe or if a missionary feels unwell.

If a service project involves activities that are not clearly safe, missionaries should err on the side of caution and consult with the mission office before participating.

7.3 Respect for Others

Missionaries represent Jesus Christ and should treat all people with dignity, kindness, and respect, regardless of their beliefs, culture, race, ethnicity, or social status.

Conversation Topics to Avoid

While missionaries should engage in meaningful conversations with people they meet, certain topics should be avoided to prevent contention, misunderstanding, or offense:

- Political opinions, candidates, or parties.
- Criticism of other religions, churches, or their leaders.
- Controversial social issues not directly related to gospel teaching.
- Sensational or speculative doctrine not supported by official Church teachings.
- Personal financial matters, either the missionary's own or those of others.
- Gossip about other missionaries, members, or investigators.

When asked about sensitive topics, missionaries should redirect the conversation to the principles of the gospel and the message of the Restoration. If pressed, they may politely decline to share a personal opinion and direct the inquirer to official Church resources.

Local Laws

Missionaries must obey the laws of the country, state, province, and municipality in which they serve. This includes immigration laws, traffic laws, noise ordinances, curfews, and any other applicable regulations.

Missionaries are not exempt from local laws by virtue of their religious status. Violating local laws can result in legal consequences, damage the reputation of the Church, and interfere with missionary work in the area.

If a missionary is uncertain about the legality of an activity, they should consult with the mission president or the mission office before proceeding. Ignorance of the law is not an acceptable excuse.

In countries or regions where proselytizing is restricted or regulated, missionaries must follow all applicable laws and the specific guidance provided by the mission president and the Missionary Department.

7.4 Temple Attendance

Missionaries who serve in missions with a temple nearby may be authorized by the mission president to attend the temple periodically. Temple attendance is a sacred privilege and should be approached with reverence and preparation.

Guidelines for temple attendance include:

- Missionaries may attend the temple only with the permission and scheduling of the mission president.
- Temple attendance should not interfere with regular missionary activities or schedules.
- Missionaries should attend in companionships or groups, never alone.
- Missionaries should be temple-worthy and hold a current temple recommend.
- The temple experience should be a time for spiritual renewal, personal revelation, and service.
- Missionaries should come prepared with the names of ancestors or others for whom ordinance work may be performed, when possible.

Temple attendance is not mandatory during the mission but is strongly encouraged when circumstances permit. The blessings of temple worship can strengthen missionaries spiritually and provide peace and perspective during challenging times.

7.5 Technology

General Standards

Technology is an integral part of modern missionary work. Missionaries are provided with approved devices and are expected to use them responsibly and in accordance with established guidelines.

General standards for technology use include:

- Use approved devices and applications only for missionary purposes.
- Do not download, install, or use unauthorized applications, games, or media.
- Do not alter device settings, bypass security features, or circumvent content filters.
- Keep devices charged, updated, and in good condition.
- Report lost, stolen, or damaged devices to the mission office immediately.
- Do not lend devices to non-missionaries or allow unauthorized access.

Safe Use

Safe technology use requires self-discipline, accountability, and mutual support within the companionship. Missionaries should:

- Use devices in shared spaces where the screen is visible to the companion.
- Avoid using devices during personal time, study time, or in bed.
- Set and respect personal boundaries for technology use.
- Discuss technology use openly with the companion and establish mutual accountability.
- Report concerns about personal or companion technology use to the mission president.

Pornography Avoidance

Pornography is a serious spiritual danger and a violation of missionary standards. Missionaries must avoid all forms of pornography, including visual, written, and audio content that is sexually explicit or arousing.

To protect against pornography exposure:

- Use only approved devices with active content filters.
- Do not search for or view sexually explicit content under any circumstance.
- If you accidentally encounter inappropriate content, close it immediately and report it to your companion and mission president.
- If you struggle with pornography addiction or recurring temptation, speak with your mission president immediately. Confidential help and resources are available through the Church's addiction recovery programs.
- Support your companion in maintaining clean technology use and be a non-judgmental resource if they need help.

Overcoming pornography is possible through the Atonement of Jesus Christ, professional support, and consistent effort. There is no shame in seeking help, and early intervention leads to better outcomes.

Teaching with Technology

Technology can enhance teaching when used thoughtfully. Missionaries may use approved videos, images, scriptures, and other digital resources during lessons to supplement their teaching.

Guidelines for teaching with technology include:

- Prepare technology resources in advance so they are ready to use during the lesson.
- Use technology to support the lesson, not to replace personal testimony and Spirit-led teaching.
- Keep technology use brief and focused; do not allow devices to become distractions.
- Ensure that content shared during lessons is from approved Church sources.
- Be mindful of the investigator's comfort with technology and adapt accordingly.

Email

Email is the primary means of communication with family, mission leaders, and approved contacts. Missionaries should:

- Check and respond to email on preparation day as part of their weekly routine.
- Keep emails brief, positive, and focused on sharing experiences, testimony, and growth.
- Avoid forwarding chain emails, spam, or inappropriate content.
- Do not use email for personal business, romantic communication, or unapproved purposes.
- Follow mission-specific email policies regarding timing, recipients, and content.

Social Media

Social media can be a powerful tool for sharing the gospel when used according to approved guidelines. Missionaries may use approved social media platforms to:

- Share uplifting content about the gospel, the Church, and their mission experience.
- Connect with investigators and potential contacts.
- Support member missionary efforts online.
- Direct people to official Church websites and resources.

Guidelines for social media use include:

- Use only approved platforms and accounts.
- Follow the Church's social media guidelines and mission-specific policies.
- Post content that is uplifting, positive, and representative of the Savior.
- Do not engage in arguments, debates, or negative interactions online.
- Do not post content that reveals sensitive personal information, location details, or security-sensitive information.
- Do not spend excessive time on social media at the expense of proselyting or study.
- Obtain consent before posting photos or information about others.

7.6 Difficult Situations

Negative Situations

Missionaries may encounter negative situations including discouragement, rejection, interpersonal conflict, cultural adjustment difficulties, homesickness, and feelings of inadequacy. These experiences are a normal part of missionary service and should not be cause for alarm.

When facing negative situations:

- Recognize that challenges are opportunities for growth and reliance on the Savior.
- Talk openly with your companion about your feelings and concerns.
- Seek guidance from your district leader, zone leaders, or mission president.
- Maintain your daily routine, including prayer, study, exercise, and sleep.
- Focus on the people you are serving rather than your own discomfort.
- Remember the promises the Lord has made to His servants and trust in His timing.
- Seek professional help through the mission office if feelings persist or intensify.

Do not compare your mission experience to others'. Each missionary's journey is unique, and the Lord blesses individual efforts according to His wisdom.

Confrontational People

Missionaries may encounter people who are hostile, aggressive, or confrontational. In these situations:

- Remain calm and do not respond with anger, defensiveness, or argument.
- Politely disengage from the conversation. You may say something like, "Thank you for your time. We respect your views and wish you well."
- Do not attempt to convince or debate someone who is clearly hostile.
- Remove yourselves from the situation if you feel unsafe.
- Do not return to a location where you have been threatened or harassed unless directed by mission leadership.
- Report all confrontational encounters to the mission president or mission office.
- If you are physically threatened or assaulted, call local emergency services immediately and then contact the mission office.

Remember that your safety is more important than any conversation or teaching opportunity. The Lord does not require you to put yourself in harm's way. Trust the Spirit to guide you to those who are ready to receive the message.

7.7 Physical and Mental Health

Contacting the Mission Office

Missionaries should contact the mission office for health-related concerns including:

- Any illness that prevents normal missionary activities for more than one day.
- Fever above 101 degrees Fahrenheit (38.3 degrees Celsius).
- Persistent vomiting, diarrhea, or dehydration.
- Injuries that limit mobility or normal function.
- Severe headaches, chest pain, difficulty breathing, or other acute symptoms.
- Mental health concerns including persistent sadness, anxiety, panic attacks, suicidal thoughts, or self-harm urges.
- Any medical emergency requiring immediate attention.

Do not delay contacting the mission office when health concerns arise. Early intervention leads to better outcomes and faster recovery. The mission office has medical professionals and resources available to help.

Physical Health

Maintaining physical health requires consistent daily habits:

- Eat balanced, nutritious meals at regular times.
- Drink 6 to 12 glasses of water daily, adjusting for climate and activity level.
- Exercise for at least 30 minutes each morning.
- Sleep 7 to 8 hours each night. Do not sacrifice sleep for additional work or study.
- Practice good hygiene, including regular handwashing, dental care, and bathing.
- Take prescribed medications as directed and keep the mission office informed of any changes.
- Wear sunscreen and appropriate clothing for the climate.
- Use insect repellent in areas where mosquito-borne or tick-borne diseases are prevalent.

Common health concerns for missionaries include colds, flu, gastrointestinal illness, skin infections, dental problems, and injuries from falls or accidents. Most of these can be prevented or managed with proper care and early treatment.

If you are prescribed rest by a medical professional, follow the prescription. Resting when ill is not a sign of weakness; it is responsible stewardship of your body and enables a faster return to full service.

Mental Health

Mental health is as important as physical health. Missionaries may experience a range of mental and emotional challenges during their service, including:

- Homesickness and loneliness.
- Anxiety about performance, worthiness, or the future.
- Depression, persistent sadness, or loss of motivation.
- Difficulty adjusting to a new culture, language, or environment.
- Interpersonal conflict with companions or other missionaries.
- Grief or loss related to family events at home.
- Trauma from negative experiences during the mission.

The Church provides mental health resources for missionaries, including access to licensed therapists and counselors through the Missionary Department. These services are confidential and available at no cost to the missionary.

If you or your companion are experiencing mental health challenges:

- Talk openly and without judgment. Listening is one of the most powerful forms of support.
- Encourage the affected missionary to speak with the mission president or mission office.
- Maintain normal routines as much as possible, as structure provides stability.
- Do not minimize or dismiss mental health concerns. They are real and valid.
- If a missionary expresses suicidal thoughts or intent to self-harm, do not leave them alone. Contact the mission president and emergency services immediately.

Seeking mental health support is a sign of strength, not weakness. The Savior understands all suffering and offers healing through His Atonement.

Animal and Insect Bites

Missionaries in some areas may encounter animals or insects that pose health risks. Guidelines for dealing with bites include:

- Avoid approaching, petting, or feeding stray or wild animals.
- If bitten by a dog or other animal, wash the wound thoroughly with soap and water and contact the mission office immediately. Rabies is a serious concern in many parts of the world, and post-exposure treatment may be necessary.
- If bitten by a snake, remain calm, immobilize the affected limb, and seek emergency medical attention immediately. Do not attempt to suck out venom or apply a tourniquet.
- For insect bites (mosquitoes, ticks, spiders), clean the bite area, apply antiseptic, and monitor for signs of infection or allergic reaction. Contact the mission office if symptoms develop.
- Use insect repellent, wear long sleeves and pants in areas with high insect activity, and check for ticks after outdoor activities.
- If you experience swelling, difficulty breathing, or other signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) after a bite or sting, call emergency services immediately and use an epinephrine auto-injector if available.

7.8 Dangerous Situations

Preparing

Preparation is the best defense against dangerous situations. Missionaries should:

- Familiarize themselves with the safety characteristics of their area, including high-crime zones, natural disaster risks, and local emergency resources.
- Know the location of the nearest hospital, police station, and embassy or consulate (for international missionaries).
- Keep emergency contact numbers readily accessible, including the mission office, local emergency services, and the Church's 24-hour emergency hotline.
- Discuss emergency plans with your companion, including what to do in case of fire, earthquake, flood, severe weather, or civil disturbance.
- Keep a basic emergency kit in your apartment, including water, non-perishable food, a flashlight, batteries, a first-aid kit, and important documents.
- Stay informed about local news and conditions that may affect safety.

Recognizing

Learn to recognize signs of potential danger:

- Unusual activity in the neighborhood, such as gatherings, loud arguments, or the presence of unfamiliar vehicles.
- People who appear to be following or watching you.
- Areas that feel unsafe or where the Spirit prompts you to leave.
- Signs of severe weather, such as darkening skies, sudden temperature changes, or emergency alerts.
- Individuals who are under the influence of alcohol or drugs and may behave unpredictably.
- Situations involving weapons, threats, or aggressive behavior.

Trust your instincts and the promptings of the Spirit. If something feels wrong, it probably is. Do not ignore warning signs.

Responding

When faced with a dangerous situation:

- Stay calm and think clearly.
- Remove yourself and your companion from the situation as quickly and safely as possible.
- Do not confront, argue with, or attempt to reason with aggressive individuals.
- If threatened with a weapon, comply with demands and do not resist. Your life is more valuable than any possession.
- Call local emergency services (police, fire, ambulance) as appropriate.
- Contact the mission office as soon as it is safe to do so.
- If separated from your companion, get to a safe location and contact the mission office immediately.
- Follow any specific emergency procedures established for your mission.

Reporting

All dangerous incidents must be reported to the mission office, regardless of severity. This includes:

- Threats, harassment, or intimidation.
- Theft, robbery, or attempted robbery.
- Physical assault or injury.
- Encounters with weapons.
- Natural disasters or severe weather events.
- Traffic accidents.
- Any situation that made you feel unsafe.

Report incidents as soon as possible while details are fresh. The mission office will document the incident, provide support, and take appropriate action to protect missionaries in the area. The Missionary Department may also be notified for serious incidents.

Do not post about dangerous incidents on social media or share details in emails to family without first consulting the mission office. Sensitive information must be handled appropriately.

7.9 Housing Safety

Privacy and Safety

Missionary housing should provide a safe and private environment for rest, study, and preparation. Guidelines for housing safety include:

- Lock all doors and windows when leaving the apartment and when sleeping.
- Do not open the door to strangers, especially at night. Use a peephole or window to identify visitors before opening the door.
- Do not invite non-missionaries into your apartment without approval from mission leadership.
- Do not share your address or apartment location with people you do not know or trust.
- Keep curtains or blinds closed at night.
- Know the location of fire exits and emergency escape routes in your building.
- Test smoke detectors regularly and report non-functioning safety equipment to the mission office.
- Do not use candles, space heaters, or other fire hazards without approval.
- Report any security concerns, such as broken locks, inadequate lighting, or suspicious activity in the area, to the mission office.

If you feel unsafe in your housing at any time, contact the mission office immediately. The mission office can arrange alternative housing or address security concerns.

Cleanliness

A clean apartment supports physical health, mental well-being, and the Spirit of the Lord. Missionaries should:

- Establish a weekly cleaning schedule with your companion and adhere to it.
- Clean the kitchen after each meal, including washing dishes, wiping counters, and sweeping floors.
- Keep the bathroom clean and sanitary.
- Take out trash regularly and do not allow garbage to accumulate.
- Keep common areas tidy and organized.
- Do laundry weekly and keep clothing clean and in good repair.
- Clean the apartment thoroughly before transfers so the next missionaries arrive to a clean environment.

Apartment inspections may be conducted by district leaders, zone leaders, or other authorized missionaries. Maintain your apartment to a standard you would be proud of at all times.

7.10 Transportation Safety

Walking

Walking is the most common mode of missionary transportation. Safety guidelines for walking include:

- Stay on sidewalks and designated pedestrian paths.
- Obey traffic signals and use crosswalks.
- Walk facing oncoming traffic when sidewalks are not available.
- Wear reflective clothing or accessories when walking in low-light conditions.
- Be alert to your surroundings and avoid distractions such as using your phone while walking.
- Avoid walking in isolated, poorly lit, or known dangerous areas, especially after dark.
- Walk with confidence and purpose; avoid appearing lost or vulnerable.
- Carry minimal valuables and keep personal belongings secure.

Bicycles

Missionaries assigned bicycles should follow these safety guidelines:

- Wear an approved bicycle helmet at all times while riding. This is a non-negotiable safety requirement.
- Follow all traffic laws, including stopping at stop signs and red lights, signaling turns, and riding with the flow of traffic.
- Use front and rear lights and reflective gear when riding in low-light conditions or at night.
- Maintain your bicycle in good working condition, including brakes, tires, chain, and gears. Report maintenance needs to the mission office.
- Do not ride at excessive speeds, perform stunts, or ride with no hands.
- Do not ride in severe weather conditions, including heavy rain, ice, snow, or strong winds.
- Lock your bicycle securely when not in use.
- Do not give rides to others on your bicycle.
- Wear appropriate clothing that will not get caught in the chain or wheels.

Vehicles

Missionaries authorized to use vehicles must adhere to strict safety standards:

- Possess a valid driver's license recognized in the country of service.
- Wear seatbelts at all times, both driver and passengers.
- Follow all traffic laws, including speed limits, signal rules, and right-of-way laws.
- Never drive while fatigued, distracted, or under the influence of any substance.
- Do not use mobile phones while driving, even with hands-free devices, unless it is an emergency.
- Drive defensively and be aware of road conditions, weather, and other drivers.
- Do not drive in severe weather conditions unless absolutely necessary.
- Report all accidents, no matter how minor, to the mission office immediately.
- Do not transport non-missionaries in mission vehicles without approval.
- Keep the vehicle clean, maintained, and fueled. Report maintenance needs promptly.
- Park in safe, well-lit areas and lock the vehicle when unattended.
- Do not exceed the passenger capacity of the vehicle.

Public Transportation

Missionaries using public transportation should:

- Familiarize themselves with routes, schedules, and fare systems.
- Keep personal belongings secure and within sight at all times. Be aware of pickpockets.
- Maintain situational awareness and avoid displaying valuables.
- Travel with your companion at all times.
- Avoid crowded or isolated stops and stations, especially at night.
- Have a plan for alternative transportation in case of delays or cancellations.
- Follow mission-specific guidelines for approved public transportation options.

7.11 Priesthood Ordinances and Blessings

General Guidelines

All priesthood ordinances and blessings must be performed according to the established procedures of the Church. The following general guidelines apply:

- Ordinances must be authorized by the mission president or his designee.
- The person performing the ordinance must hold the appropriate priesthood office and be worthy.
- Ordinances should be performed with reverence, care, and the Spirit of the Lord.
- The exact wording prescribed by scripture and Church policy must be used for saving ordinances.
- Ordinances should be witnessed and recorded as required by Church policy.
- If a mistake is made during an ordinance that affects its validity, the ordinance should be performed again.

Baptizing

Baptism is performed by immersion by one having authority (see Doctrine and Covenants 20:72-74). The person performing the baptism must hold the Aaronic Priesthood (office of priest or higher) or the Melchizedek Priesthood and must be authorized by the mission president.

The baptismal prayer is prescribed in Doctrine and Covenants 20:73:

"Having been commissioned of Jesus Christ, I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. Amen."

The person being baptized and the person performing the baptism both enter the water. The person performing the baptism:
1. Stands in the water with the candidate.
2. Holds the candidate's right wrist with his left hand (for support). The candidate holds the missionary's left wrist with their right hand.
3. Raises his right arm to the square.
4. States the full name of the candidate and recites the baptismal prayer exactly as written.
5. Immerses the candidate completely, including clothing, hair, and body.
6. Brings the candidate up out of the water.

If any part of the candidate's body, clothing, or hair is not fully immersed, the ordinance must be repeated. A witness (typically another priesthood holder) observes the baptism to confirm proper immersion and the correct recitation of the prayer.

The baptismal service should be a reverent and joyful occasion. Missionaries should coordinate with local ward or branch leaders to ensure appropriate facilities, clothing, towels, and a change of dry clothes for the candidate.

Confirming

Confirmation follows baptism and is performed by the laying on of hands by one or more Melchizedek Priesthood holders. The confirmation is usually performed during sacrament meeting on the Sunday following the baptism but may be performed at the baptismal service if authorized.

To perform a confirmation:
1. One or more Melchizedek Priesthood holders place their hands on the head of the newly baptized member.
2. The person serving as voice states the full name of the person.
3. States that the confirmation is performed by the authority of the Melchizedek Priesthood.
4. Confirms the person a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.
5. Confers the Holy Ghost by saying, "Receive the Holy Ghost."
6. Offers words of blessing as guided by the Spirit.
7. Closes in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

The confirmation should be performed with reverence and spiritual sensitivity. The elder performing the confirmation should seek the guidance of the Spirit in offering words of blessing and counsel.

Consecrating Oil

Olive oil used for anointing the sick must be consecrated before use. Only Melchizedek Priesthood holders may consecrate oil.

To consecrate oil:
1. Hold the open container of olive oil.
2. Address Heavenly Father.
3. State that you are consecrating the oil by the authority of the Melchizedek Priesthood.
4. Dedicate the oil for the anointing and blessing of those who are sick and afflicted.
5. Close in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Consecrated oil should be stored in a clean, sealed container and carried by missionaries for use when needed. Replace the oil periodically to keep it fresh.

Administering to the Sick

Administering to the sick is a two-part ordinance performed by Melchizedek Priesthood holders: the anointing and the sealing of the anointing (the blessing).

Part 1: Anointing
1. One Melchizedek Priesthood holder places a small amount of consecrated oil on the crown of the sick person's head.
2. Places his hands on the person's head.
3. States the person's full name.
4. States that he is anointing with consecrated oil.
5. States that the anointing is performed by the authority of the Melchizedek Priesthood.
6. Closes in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Part 2: Sealing the Anointing (Blessing)
1. Two or more Melchizedek Priesthood holders place their hands on the person's head.
2. The one serving as voice states the person's full name.
3. States that the anointing is sealed by the authority of the Melchizedek Priesthood.
4. Offers words of blessing as guided by the Spirit. These may include blessings of comfort, healing, counsel, or other spiritual direction.
5. Closes in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Administering to the sick requires faith on the part of both the priesthood holder and the recipient. Missionaries should approach this ordinance with prayer and humility, trusting in the Lord's will for the individual.

If only one Melchizedek Priesthood holder is available, he may perform both the anointing and the sealing of the anointing.

Comfort Blessings

Missionaries may give blessings of comfort to those who are in need of spiritual encouragement, reassurance, or peace. Comfort blessings do not require consecrated oil and are not part of the ordinance of administering to the sick.

To give a comfort blessing:
1. One or more Melchizedek Priesthood holders place their hands on the head of the person receiving the blessing.
2. The person serving as voice states the full name of the person.
3. States that the blessing is given by the authority of the Melchizedek Priesthood.
4. Offers words of comfort, encouragement, counsel, and blessing as directed by the Spirit.
5. Closes in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Comfort blessings may be given to members, investigators, or any person who requests one and is willing to receive it. Missionaries should be sensitive to the spiritual needs of those they serve and offer blessings when prompted by the Spirit and when appropriate.

The power of priesthood blessings comes from faith in Jesus Christ and the authority of the priesthood. Missionaries who worthily exercise the priesthood in blessing others will find that their own faith and testimony are strengthened in the process.
