## 1. What Are Stress and Anxiety?

## 2. Understanding Levels of Stress

[SIDEBAR] <header> <p class="title" data-aid="145667584" id="aside1_title1">ACTIVITY (5 minutes)</p> </header> <p data-aid="145667589" id="aside1_p1"><strong>Step 1:</strong> On your own, read through the four levels of stress. You will likely experience different levels of stress at different times. You may feel like you are at an orange level today and a green level tomorrow. Becoming more emotionally resilient will help you experience a green level of stress more often. Mark the level that best describes how you feel right now.</p> <p data-aid="145667799" id="aside1_p30"><strong>Step 2:</strong> Think of the times when your stress level was at the green level. What helped you to feel that way?</p> <span class="record-your-impressions" data-rows="2"></span> <p data-aid="145667809" id="aside1_p31"><strong>Step 3:</strong> As a group, discuss your answers from step 2.</p> [/SIDEBAR]

## 3. Use Mindfulness to Reduce Stress

[SIDEBAR] <header> <p class="title" data-aid="145667850" id="aside2_title1">ACTIVITY (5 minutes)</p> </header> <p data-aid="145667853" id="aside2_p1"><strong><i>Facilitator Note:</i></strong> As with all activities, do not change or enhance this activity. Simply read the steps for this activity in a calm voice and slowly enough that group members can understand you.</p> <p data-aid="145667856" id="aside2_p2">In this mindfulness exercise, you will practice stillness. This activity is just one example. The facilitator will read the steps below while the class members follow along.</p> <p data-aid="145667859" id="aside2_p3"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Sit in a comfortable place where you can be still for a few minutes. (When doing this at home, you can either sit or lie down.) Close your eyes.</p> <p data-aid="145667862" id="aside2_p4"><strong>Step 2:</strong> Begin by taking several long, slow breaths, breathing in fully through your nose and exhaling fully through your mouth. Allow your breath to find its own natural rhythm. Notice the physical sensations of your lungs expanding and contracting as you breathe in and out. If your mind wanders to other things, don’t worry; that happens. If you get distracted, just notice the types of thoughts, feelings, and sensations that distract you. Observe them without trying to control them or judge them, and simply bring your attention back to your breathing. Your breathing provides an anchor to which you can return over and over again.</p> <p data-aid="145667865" id="aside2_p5"><strong>Step 3:</strong> Practice being present with your breathing. Again, if your mind wanders, notice the thoughts, feelings, and sensations that distract you and allow them to flow past you like clouds in the sky. Breathe with this perspective for one minute.</p> <p data-aid="145667868" id="aside2_p6"><strong>Step 4:</strong> As this exercise comes to an end, slowly allow your attention to expand, and notice your body and the room around you. When you’re ready, open your eyes and come back to being fully aware of your surroundings. Remember that breathing is a tool that can help you relax and be present at any time.</p> <p data-aid="145667871" id="aside2_p7"><strong>Step 5:</strong> On a scale of 1 to 10, rate how stressed you feel now.</p> [/SIDEBAR]

## 4. Accessing Godly Power

[SIDEBAR] <header> <p class="title" data-aid="145667902" id="aside3_title1">ACTIVITY (10 minutes)</p> </header> <p data-aid="145667904" id="aside3_p1"><strong>Step 1:</strong> On your own, read the following ways to manage stress and anxiety. Circle the ideas you may want to try.</p> <ol class="decimal" start="1"> <li data-marker="1."> <p data-aid="145667908" id="aside3_p2"><strong><i>Talk with God.</i></strong> Know that He understands. Imagine Him sitting close to you, listening and offering support.</p> </li> <li data-marker="2."> <p data-aid="145667912" id="aside3_p3"><strong><i>Ponder.</i></strong> Reflect on times you’ve felt blessed by the Lord and seen His hand and mercy in your life. “Be still, and know that I am God” (<a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/ot/ps/46?lang=eng&amp;id=p10#p10">Psalm 46:10</a>).</p> </li> <li data-marker="3."> <p data-aid="145667916" id="aside3_p4"><strong><i>Look at your expectations.</i></strong> Sometimes our greatest worries and stress come when life doesn’t turn out the way we hoped or planned. We can learn from our stressful experiences. “All these things shall give thee experience and shall be for thy good. … Therefore, hold on thy way” (<a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/122?lang=eng&amp;id=p7%2C9#p7">Doctrine and Covenants 122:7, 9</a>).</p> </li> <li data-marker="4."> <p data-aid="145667920" id="aside3_p5"><strong><i>Take a short break.</i></strong> Take several slow, deep breaths, stretch, and relax physically. When your body and mind are calm again, you will be able to think more clearly. Take a walk, get some food or a drink, or just sit and think for a few minutes.</p> </li> <li data-marker="5."> <p data-aid="145667924" id="aside3_p6"><strong><i>Be aware of when you’re stressed.</i></strong> Sometimes you may not realize you’re stressed. You may experience difficulty sleeping, get angry easily, feel depressed, or have low energy. Check in with yourself at least once a day to see how you are doing.</p> </li> <li data-marker="6."> <p data-aid="145667928" id="aside3_p7"><strong><i>Focus on gratitude.</i></strong> Notice what is around you. Focus for a few minutes on what is right, good, and positive about yourself and the world. Start a gratitude journal, and write at least five specific things you are grateful for each day.</p> </li> <li data-marker="7."> <p data-aid="145667933" id="aside3_p8"><strong><i>Be active.</i></strong> We have been given a body and a world to enjoy. Make a plan to use your body to get out and enjoy these gifts. Even a few minutes of walking can help boost your mood and reduce stress.</p> </li> <li data-marker="8."> <p data-aid="145667939" id="aside3_p9"><strong><i>Limit technology use.</i></strong> Using social media, computers, and TVs, especially at the start and end of the day, can increase feelings of anxiety or stress. Try starting and ending your day without phones or computers. Go a day or week without social media or TV and see how you feel.</p> </li> <li data-marker="9."> <p data-aid="145667944" id="aside3_p10"><strong><i>Be kind to yourself.</i></strong> Talk to yourself with the same kind, comforting words you would use with a loved one. Thoughts of helplessness, hopelessness, or harsh condemnation are not from God and cause more stress and anxiety.</p> </li> <li data-marker="10."> <p data-aid="145667949" id="aside3_p11"><strong><i>Help someone else.</i></strong> Refocus your energy by serving someone else. Our ultimate goal is to love others like Christ did, even when we are struggling. Christ gave us an example of this love when He forgave His persecutors and made sure His mother would be cared for even as He suffered on the cross. As hard as it can be, we can get better perspective on our lives when we stop and think of others and look for ways to help.</p> </li> <li data-marker="11."> <p data-aid="145667953" id="aside3_p12"><strong><i>Try a relaxing activity.</i></strong> Everyone relaxes in different ways. Maybe you enjoy painting, listening to music, reading a book, or being outside. When you are stressed or anxious, take time to remember what brings you joy and calms you. Make time to do those things, even if you think you are too busy.</p> </li> <li data-marker="12."> <p data-aid="145667957" id="aside3_p13"><strong><i>Stay connected with friends and family.</i></strong> Being with those who love and support you can help reduce stress. You were made for connection. There is no shame in sharing what you’re going through. Help your friends and family understand what you are experiencing.</p> </li> <li data-marker="13."> <p data-aid="145667961" id="aside3_p14"><strong><i>Take it one step at a time.</i></strong> You can ask yourself, “What is the most important thing I can be doing right now?” Your answer might be something like “All I need to do right now is wait for my bus” or “All I have to do right now is clean the dishes.” Decide what must get done now and what can wait. Learn to not feel guilty for saying “no” to something that can wait.</p> </li> <li data-marker="14."> <p data-aid="145667966" id="aside3_p15"><strong><i>Practice mindfulness.</i></strong> Use the exercises in the previous <a class="cross-ref" href="/study/manual/emotional-resilience-for-self-reliance/4-managing-stress-and-anxiety/4-learn?lang=eng&amp;id=aside2#aside2">activity</a> and at the <a class="cross-ref" href="/study/manual/emotional-resilience-for-self-reliance/4-managing-stress-and-anxiety/4-resources?lang=eng">end of this chapter</a> to practice being present. Find ways to remind yourself to be more present throughout the day.</p> </li> </ol> <p data-aid="145667971" id="aside3_p16"><strong>Step 2:</strong> In the table below, individually write a few of the ideas to reduce stress and anxiety that you want to try.
