[SIDEBAR] <div class="image-cropper"></div> <p data-aid="127247185" id="aside1_p1"><span class="page-break" data-page="22"></span>“The gift of the Holy Ghost, if you consent, will guide and protect you and even correct your actions. It is a spiritual voice that comes into the mind as a thought or a feeling put into your heart. … It is not expected that you go through life without making mistakes, but you will not make a major mistake without first being warned by the promptings of the Spirit. This promise applies to all members of the Church.”</p> <div class="citation-info"> <p data-aid="127247186" id="aside1_p2">President Boyd K. Packer, President of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, “Counsel to Youth,” <em>Liahona</em> and <em>Ensign,</em> Nov. 2011, 17–18.</p> </div> [/SIDEBAR] [SIDEBAR] <p data-aid="127247188" id="aside2_p1"><span class="page-break" data-page="23"></span>Elder Dallin H. Oaks of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles taught that the Spirit communicates for eight purposes:</p> <p data-aid="127247189" id="aside2_p2"><em>To testify:</em> The Spirit can witness that Jesus is the Christ and that the gospel is true.</p> <p data-aid="127247190" id="aside2_p3"><em>To prophesy:</em> Within the limits of one’s areas of responsibility, a person may be inspired to predict what will come to pass in the future. The prophet, patriarchs, and even we can receive prophetic revelation.</p> <p data-aid="127247193" id="aside2_p4"><em>To comfort:</em> The Spirit can comfort us as He comforted Joseph Smith in Liberty Jail: “Peace be unto thy soul; thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment” (<a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121?lang=eng&amp;id=p7-p8#p7">D&amp;C 121:7–8</a>). Comfort also comes in connection with priesthood blessings, visions of departed loved ones, and forgiveness of sins.</p> <p data-aid="127247196" id="aside2_p5"><em>To uplift:</em> The Spirit can lift us up from depression, feelings of inadequacy, or a plateau of spiritual mediocrity. He comes as we read scriptures or enjoy wholesome music, art, or literature.</p> <p data-aid="127247198" id="aside2_p6"><em>To inform:</em> You may be given the words to speak on a particular occasion. The Lord told Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon, “For it shall be given you in the very hour, yea, in the very moment, what ye shall say” (<a class="scripture-ref" href="/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/100?lang=eng&amp;id=p6#p6">D&amp;C 100:6</a>). On some sacred occasions, information has been given face to face from heavenly personages. Other times, needed information is communicated by the quiet whisperings of the Spirit.</p> <p data-aid="127247201" id="aside2_p7"><em>To restrain:</em> This form of revelation will deter us from things we should not do. It often comes by surprise, when we have not asked for guidance on a particular subject.</p> <p data-aid="127247204" id="aside2_p8"><em>To confirm:</em> You can receive a confirmation through the Spirit after proposing a particular course of action and praying to know if it is the right choice.</p> <p data-aid="127247206" id="aside2_p9"><em>To impel:</em> This type of revelation is not sought after but comes to call a person to some action not proposed. This type of communication from the Spirit is rare but, therefore, significant.</p> <div class="citation-info"> <p data-aid="127247208" id="aside2_p10">From “Revelation,” in <em>Brigham Young University 1981–82 Fireside and Devotional Speeches</em> (1982), 20–26.</p> </div> [/SIDEBAR]
