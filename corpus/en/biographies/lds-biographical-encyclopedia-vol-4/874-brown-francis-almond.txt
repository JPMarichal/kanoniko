Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 4 ( 1936)

Brown, Francis Almond

Brown, Francis Almond, president of the Netherlands Mission in 1867 and again from 1889 to 1891, was born Nov. 14, 1822, in Milfford, Otsego Co., New York, a son of Jesse Brown and Roxana Grant. He was baptized Feb. 11, 1844, by John Lane. For two years he studied in the Genesee Wesleyan Seminary at Lima, New York. In the summer of 1844 he filled a short mission among relatives and friends, and in October of that year he gathered to Nauvoo. He taught school in Iowa and in the Music Hall at Nauvoo. In 1851 he filled a second short mission to Nova Scotia, and moved to Utah in 1856. In 1865 he was called to labor as a missionary in the Netherlands, and in 1867 he succeeded Joseph Weiler as president of the mission. During his second mission, he published the Book of Mormon in the Dutch or Netherlands language, the translation having been made by his predecessor, John W. F. Volker.

He taught school in Ogden, becoming the leader, if not practically the founder of education in Weber county. He served as probate judge, alderman and justice of the peace, and also was secretary and later president of irrigation companies. He died June 9, 1894, at Ogden, Utah. (See also "Historical Record", Vol. 5, p. 114.)
