Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 4 ( 1936)

Ensign, Horace Datus

Ensign, Horace Datus, one of the original pioneers of Utah, was born Aug. 8, 1826, at Westfield, Hampden Co., Mass., a son of Horace Datus Ensign and Mary Brunson. His parents became members of the Church in 1843 and with a family of five sons and one daughter located in Nauvoo in 1846 but soon afterwards were forced to leave their home and with the exiled saints made a temporary home at Winter Quarters. The father died in September, 1846, and Horace, the oldest son, was called in the spring of 1847 to join the company of pioneers. Horace remained in Salt Lake Valley until Pres. Young returned to Winter Quarters, when he accompanied him as far as the Big Sandy where he met his widowed mother and her family en route for the "Valley" in Capt. Daniel Spencer's company. With them he returned to Salt Lake Valley and located in Ogden where he worked for Captain James Brown. He took an active part in the Echo Canyon War in 1857-1858, married Eliza Jane Stewart and was the father of three sons and three daughters. He died in Ogden Sept. 1, 1866.
