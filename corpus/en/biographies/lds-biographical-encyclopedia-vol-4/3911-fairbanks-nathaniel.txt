Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 4 ( 1936)

Fairbanks, Nathaniel

Fairbanks, Nathaniel, one of the original pioneers of Utah, was born May 10, 1823, at Queensbury, Washington Co., N.Y., a son of Joseph Fairbanks and Polly White. He was baptized Aug. 28, 1843, by John Cairns, located at Nauvoo in 1844 and with the exiled saints made a temporary home at Winter Quarters. He was one of Pres. Brigham Young's famous company of pioneers. He returned East with Pres. Young but en route met his brother John B. Fairbanks and family coming to Salt Lake Valley in Daniel Spencer's company and accompanied him to Salt Lake Valley, and took part in the early settlement of Salt Lake City. In 1853, as he was driving a herd of cattle from Salt Lake Valley to Sacramento, California, he was accidentally thrown from a mule and drowned in a river near Sacramento. (Bio. Ency. Vol. 2, p. 355.)
