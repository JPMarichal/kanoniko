S. Dilworth Young, Here Is Brigham . . . Brigham Young: The Years to 1844 ( 1964)

April 1835

There was still much to be learned by the newly ordained Twelve. The evening after receiving their charge they met at the home of the Prophet. If the Prophet was free with his counsel, the men newly appointed had an insatiable thirst to receive it. They were like blotters absorbing the sweet milk of the word. Brigham listened carefully to the Prophet.

First, he told them, was the lamentable fact that many inspired decisions had been made but not recorded, so that they were forgotten. Nearly every point of doctrine would now have been covered in writing if only the record had been kept.

Second, each time the Twelve met they were to "let one be appointed to preside during the meeting, and one or more to keep record of their proceedings. Let, especially, decisions be written and these then become covenants or doctrine. An item may appear at the time of little importance, but when published and one of you lay hands on it you will find it of infinite worth; not only to your brethren, but a feast to your own souls."

Third, "By keeping minutes and careful account of the meetings, should you ever be accused of unrighteous acts, or conspiracy form against you, the minutes, and the testimony of your brethren will show that you were in a certain place at a certain time, and you will escape their hand."

Finally the Prophet proposed the following question: "What importance is attached to the calling of the twelve apostles, different from other callings or affairs of the Church?

Brigham felt constrained to speak to this subject and did so. Others did also. Then the Prophet gave a decision: "They are the Twelve Apostles who are called to the office of the Traveling High Council, who are to preside over the churches of the Saints among the gentiles, where there is a presidency

Brigham and the others of the Twelve thus received their first lessons in Church government.
