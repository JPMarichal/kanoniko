S. Dilworth Young, Here Is Brigham . . . Brigham Young: The Years to 1844 ( 1964)

March-December 1838

A person who witnesses a tragedy in the theater shares the emotions of the characters in the play. He wants to scream out: "Stop! Go no further or you are doomed!" But he does not shout because he knows it is not real-it is but a play. So the actors enter the scene, the situation picks them up, the action is carried to its inexorable end, and the principals die, victims of the events which they can neither control nor stay.

One could say in retrospect that the year 1838 was a tragic drama. One living today can scarcely believe that it took place. The actors in the drama look almost awkward; they appear drained of emotion. They seem to move like cardboard figures, like sleep-walkers. Surely someone will awaken them and end the nightmare! But there is no end. The actors die, or struggle deathlike from the scene; the curtain falls. One wants to cry out at the injustice of it all. Did no one hear the cry of the innocent? Do the guilty perpetrate acts of violence and crime without fear of reprisal or punishment? Is death the only reward awaiting the weakened victims? There the scene stands stark in memory.
