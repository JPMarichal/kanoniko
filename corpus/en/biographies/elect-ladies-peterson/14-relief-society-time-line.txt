Janet Peterson, LaRene Gaunt, Elect Ladies ( 1990)

Appendix A

Relief Society Time Line

1820 First Vision

1829 Restoration of the Aaronic and Melchizedek priesthoods

1830 Book of Mormon is published

1830 Church is organized in Fayette, New York

1830 D&C 25 is revealed; Emma Smith is identified as "an elect lady" (v. 3)

1830-1844 Joseph Smith, first president of Church (membership—6 to 26,146)

1831-1838 Kirtland period

1831-1839 Missouri period

1834 Zion's Camp

1835 First book of hymns is compiled by Emma Smith

1836 Kirtland Temple is dedicated

1838 Cornerstone is laid for temple in Far West, Missouri

1840-1846 Nauvoo period

1842 Female Relief Society of Nauvoo is organized

1842-1844 EMMA HALE SMITH, first president of Relief Society; organization is known as Female Relief Society of Nauvoo

1843 Necessity Committee is organized (forerunner of visiting teaching)

1844 Relief Society is disbanded

1844 Joseph and Hyrum Smith are martyred

1846 Nauvoo Temple is dedicated

1846 Exodus from Nauvoo

1846-1848 Winter Quarters

1846-1848 Mexican War; Mormon Battalion

1847 California Gold Rush

1847-1877 Brigham Young, second president of Church (membership—34,694 to 115,065)

1847 First wagon company enters Salt Lake Valley

1847 Construction begins on the Salt Lake Temple

1850 University of Deseret (University of Utah) is established

1852 Plural marriage is publicly announced

1855 Endowment House is dedicated

1856-1860 Handcart pioneer companies arrive in Salt Lake City

1861-1865 Civil War

1866-1873 Women's auxiliary is organized and renamed Female Relief Society

1866-1880 Eliza R. Snow leads all women's auxiliaries

1869 Transcontinental railroad is completed

1869 Susan B. Anthony and Elizabeth C. Stanton organize National Woman's Suffrage Association

1869 Young Ladies' Retrenchment Association (forerunner of YWMIA) is organized

1870 Women in Utah receive vote

1872 Woman's Exponent is published

1873-1892 Women's organization is renamed Relief Society

1876 Relief Society begins sericulture and grain storage

1877 St. George Temple is dedicated

1878 Primary is organized

1880-1887 ELIZA R. SNOW, second president of Relief Society

1880 Leadership is established for Relief Society, YLMIA, and Primary

1880 John Taylor, third president of Church (membership—133,628 to 173,029)

1882 Deseret Hospital is established

1884 Logan Temple is dedicated

1887 Edmunds-Tucker Act is passed, disfranchising women

1888-1901 ZINA D. H. YOUNG, third president of Relief Society

1888 Manti Temple is dedicated

1889-1898 Wilford Woodruff, fourth president of Church (membership—180,294 to 267,251)

1889 First Relief Society general conference

1890 Manifesto is issued, ending plural marriage

1891 Relief Society joins National Council of Women

1892 Relief Society Jubilee

1892-1945 Women's organization is renamed National Women's Relief Society

1893 Salt Lake Temple is dedicated

1896 Utah is granted statehood; women regain vote

1898-1901 Lorenzo Snow, fifth president of Church (membership—267,251 to 292,931)

1901-1918 Joseph F. Smith, sixth president of Church (membership—292,931 to 495,962)

1901-1910 BATHSHEBA W. SMITH, fourth president of Relief Society

1902 Uniform course of study begins in Relief Society

1909 Relief Society, YLMIA, and Primary are housed in Bishops' Building

1910-1921 EMMELINE B. WELLS, fifth president of Relief Society

1914 Woman's Exponent is discontinued; Relief Society Magazine begins

1914-1918 World War I

1918-1945 Heber J. Grant, seventh president of Church (membership—495,962 to 954,004)

1919 Establishment of Relief Society social services

1920 Nineteenth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution grants suffrage to women

1921-1928 CLARISSA S. WILLIAMS, sixth president of Relief Society

1924 Maternity hospital is founded

1928-1939 LOUISE Y. ROBISON, seventh president of Relief Society

1929 Great Depression begins

1930 Centennial of the Church

1933 Singing Mothers Chorus is named

1937 Mormon Handicraft is established

1939-1945 World War II

1940-1945 AMY BROWN LYMAN, eighth president of Relief Society

1942 Relief Society Centennial is curtailed by war

1945-1951 George Albert Smith, eighth president of Church (membership— 954,004 to 1,111,314)

1945-1974 BELLE S. SPAFFORD, ninth president of Relief Society; name of organization is again Relief Society

1950-1953 Korean War

1951-1970 David O. McKay, ninth president of Church (membership— 1,111,314 to 2,807,456)

1956 Relief Society Building is dedicated

1965-1973 VietNam War

1966 Relief Society presents campanile tower for Nauvoo Bell on Temple Square to the Church

1970-1972 Joseph Fielding Smith, tenth president of Church (membership— 2,807,456 to 3,218,908)

1970 Relief Society Magazine is discontinued

1971 All adult LDS women become members of the Relief Society

1972-1973 Harold B. Lee, eleventh president of Church (membership— 3,218,908 to 3,306,658)

1973-1985 Spencer W. Kimball, twelfth president of Church (membership— 3,306,658 to 5,920,000)

1974-1984 BARBARA B. SMITH, tenth president of Relief Society

1978 Revelation on priesthood

1980 Consolidated meeting schedule begins

1980 Sesquicentennial of Church

1982 Sarah M. Kimball home in Nauvoo is dedicated

1984-1990 BARBARA W. WINDER, eleventh president of Relief Society

1984 Wives of international area presidencies serve as general board representatives for Relief Society, Young Women, and Primary

1985-1994 Ezra Taft Benson, thirteenth president of Church (membership— 5,920,000 to 8,479,600)

1987 Relief Society lessons are coordinated with study emphasis on scriptures

1988 Relief Society, Young Women, and Primary offices are housed in remodeled Relief Society Building

1988 Visiting teaching is changed, to accommodate worldwide church

1989 Changes made in budgets for local units in the United States and Canada

1990 ELAINE L. JACK, twelfth president of the Relief Society

1992 Relief Society Sesquicentennial

1992 Gospel Literacy Effort

1993 General Relief Society Meeting (for adult women; young women have separate general meeting)

1994-1995 Howard W. Hunter, fourteenth president of Church (membership— 8,479,600 to 9,111,100)

1995- Gordon B. Hinckley, fifteenth president of Church (membership— 9,111,100 in March 1995)
