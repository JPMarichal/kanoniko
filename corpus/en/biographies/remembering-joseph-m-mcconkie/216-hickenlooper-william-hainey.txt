Hickenlooper, William Hainey

(September 22, 1804-January 14, 1888)

Discerning Hickenlooper had money, Joseph asked for it to provide lumber for Nauvoo Temple; Hickenlooper loaned it, and it was later repaid; also remembers sorrow of martyrdom.

In the year 1842, William paid a visit to Nauvoo, purchased a lot, and had a house built on it. Here he first saw the Prophet Joseph Smith. One day the Prophet met him and said, "You're the man I want to see. I want some money to send up the river for lumber for the Temple." William loaned the amount desired, which was all he had with him, and went off wondering how the Prophet knew he had any money. Some of the people tried to discourage him, saying he would never get it back, but it was returned according to agreement. In April, 1844 he moved with his family to Nauvoo, Ill., and was there at the time of the martyrdom of the Prophet and the Patriarch and during the eventful scenes that followed.
