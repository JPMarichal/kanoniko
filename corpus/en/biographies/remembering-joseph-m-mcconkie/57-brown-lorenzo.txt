Brown, Lorenzo

(b. February 2, 1823)

Fleeing persecutors, the Prophet stays in the Brown's home.

In the spring, 1837, we were visited by Presidents Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon, who on account of persecutions heaped upon them, after staying three days and nights in the woods concealed, had finally left Kirtland for a season and were directed to our house. They stayed with us for a few days. I became acquainted with them but more particularly with the Prophet Joseph, who was peculiarly adapted to the acquaintance of great and small.

"Lorenzo Brown Journal, vol. 1, (1823-1846)," typescript copy, BYU Special Collections, Harold B. Lee Library, Provo, Utah, 3.

On Lorenzo's ordination to office of elder.

1842 At the April Conference President Joseph Smith requested all young men to come forward and be ordained elders. I went forward.

"Lorenzo Brown Journal, vol. 1, (1823-1846)," typescript copy, BYU Special Collections, Harold B. Lee Library, Provo, Utah, 12

Reading the Bible with the Urim and Thummim, Joseph saw much more, "like a grand panorama."

[Lorenzo Brown remembers hearing Joseph say:] After I got through translating the Book of Mormon, I took up the Bible to read with the Urim and Thummim. I read the first chapter of Genesis and I saw the things as they were done. I turned over the next and the next, and the whole passed before me like a grand panorama; and so on chapter after chapter until I read the whole of it. I saw it all! . . . (This was spoken at the house of Benj. Brown, N. Y., 1832, Sidney Rigdon being along)

Lorenzo Brown, "Sayings of Joseph, by Those Who Heard Him at Different Times," LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah. Also cited in Robert J. Matthews, A Plainer Translation (Provo, Utah: BYU Press, 1975), 25.
