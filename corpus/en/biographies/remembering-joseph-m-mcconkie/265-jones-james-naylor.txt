Mark L. McConkie, Remembering Joseph (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Company, 2003)

Jones, James Naylor

(April 3, 1810-August 14, 1865)

Served as bodyguard to Joseph; his house in Nauvoo burned by mob.

JONES, James Naylor, a Utah pioneer of 1849, was born April 3, 1810, at Baltimore, Maryland. He was residing in Ohio when missionary operations were commenced in that State. Becoming a convert to the restored gospel he participated in the early activities of the Church there and later became associated with the Prophet Joseph Smith, acting as his bodyguard upon many occasion in Missouri and Illinois. Bro. Jones and his wife were the only members of the family who accepted the gospel, the others being bitterly opposed to "Mormonism." While living in Nauvoo James N. Jones and his wife (Sarah Ann Malerny Jones) suffered much persecution and mob violence which on one occasion resulted in their house being burned and their only cow driven off, although it was known that Bro. Jones had a family of little children to support. . . .
