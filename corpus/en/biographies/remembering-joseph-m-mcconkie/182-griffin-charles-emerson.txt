Griffin, Charles Emerson

(May 10, 1836-July 18, 1900)

Recalls martyrdom, and that it intensified anti-Mormon sentiment, leading to fights among himself and other school boys.

My father sold out and was preparing to move to Nauvoo in the state of Illinois, where the Saints were gathered, when we heard of the martyrdom of the Prophet Joseph Smith and Patriarch Hyrum Smith.

I well remember the taunts and slurs cast at me by my school mates, after my parents joined the Mormons, and when the news came of the death of the Prophet and Patriarch, they were worse than ever in trying to aggravate me and they very often succeeded to the extent that I would often resent it with blows, and many were the combats I had with them.

"Autobiography of Charles Emerson Griffin," LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
