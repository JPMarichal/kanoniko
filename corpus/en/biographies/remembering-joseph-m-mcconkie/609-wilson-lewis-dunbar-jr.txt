Wilson, Lewis Dunbar Jr.

(September 21, 1840-January 28, 1922)

As a young boy he sat on Joseph's knee and heard him tell stories; he remembers the noise surrounding the martyrdom and his mother hiding Joseph from a mob while his father took a gun to protect Joseph.

He was born four years after his father and mother were converted and baptized into the Church, at the time when the Saints were being so severely tried and persecuted in Nauvoo. He remembered as a small boy, sitting upon the knee of the Prophet Joseph Smith while he was telling stories. He remembered hearing the noise and confusion at the time of the Prophet's martyrdom: of seeing the dead body after his life had been taken. He used to tell of one time when the mob had chased the Prophet into his father's home, of seeing his mother hide the Prophet under the bed while his father went out with a gun to keep the angry mob back.

His father being one of the High Council (see D&C 124:127-133) was given a sword by the Prophet and this sword, along with one of the first printed Books of Mormon was kept by Lewis Dunbar until the time of his death. The sword is now found in possession of his grandson, Lloyd Wilson. [This is a typed manuscript; in handwriting it adds: "It has since been given into the museum at Charleston, Ida."]
