Bracken, Levi

(May 14, 1792-August 1, 1852)

Knew Joseph and heard him preach; tells of being divinely protected from the mob.

[Levi Bracken] was highly favored by the acquaintanceship of the Prophet Joseph Smith, and many times he enjoyed the wonderful discourses delivered by the Prophet.

With the Saints, he suffered severe hardships and persecutions at the hands of the mobs, as the Mormon people were being driven from place to place. James considered it an honor to act as bodyguard for the Prophet and was always willing to perform this duty or service when called upon. He was at the Battle of Nauvoo, which took place 12 September 1846. He was also prominent in the defense of the Latter-day Saints in Illinois and Missouri when they were being severely persecuted. One incident he related was at the time the Saints were at Caldwell County, Missouri. The Prophet took eighty men and stationed them on a raise about half a mile from the river. He then placed each man one rod apart making a row eight rods long. A large group of the mob crossed the river, and after advancing toward them for some distance turned and fled. Later James talked to one of the mob who asked how many men there were. When he was told eighty he said that was another Mormon lie, as the mob had seen lines of men running into the thousands.
