Spilsbury, George

(April 23, [or 21] 1823-January 25, 1919)

Heard voice saying he would preach the gospel thus preparing him to receive it; he served as a bodyguard to the Prophet and saw the bodies of Joseph and Hyrum.

"At the age of sixteen," says he, "under a large oak tree, in a field near my home, a voice declared to me that I should be a minister of the Gospel; which prediction has been fulfilled." [He was later baptized on Oct. 11, 1840].

. . . While a resident of [Nauvoo], Mr. Spilsbury occupied himself in various waysbuilding, chopping wood, and quarrying stone for the Temple and the Nauvoo House. He also spend much time on guard, protecting the Prophet and the people from mob violence. He was at Nauvoo the day the martyred Prophet and Patriarch were brought from Carthage amid the sorrow and lamentation of a grief-stricken people.

"George Spilsbury," in Orson F. Whitney, History of Utah, 4 vols. (Salt Lake City, Utah: G. Q. Cannon & Sons Co., 1904), 4:400.
