Wilkinson, Susan Hough Conrad

(1818-1888)

In a letter written to Mary Woolley on 5 August 1844, she speaks of the feelings among the Saints after the martyrdom; she says following the martyrdom the Saints were melancholy but restrained and without resenting it prevented reactions which would have given their enemies an excuse for driving them away.

I received a letter from sis. Kimball last week telling me all the news of Nauvoo it was a melancholy time with you there and so it has been with me. I would much rather have been there than to have been kept in the suspense I was and while yet no correct information from you from that quarter I am glad the saints bore it as well as they did without resenting it because the world was itching for them to do something that they could rush in upon them and drive them. I pray the Lord to hasten the time when the saints shall possess the earth and when the wicked shall be cut off. Dear sister Woolley, I often think what Joseph and Hyrum said to me the last time I saw them to talk with them I expect you had more opportunity of seeing after your returned if you did when you write tell me yes or no as I want to know every particular. I have no time to write more now as brother Snow is waiting. Give my love to Ellen and Louis, Sammy and all the children. Tell Samuel . . .

"Susan Hough Conrad Wilkinson to Mary Woolley, August 5, 1844," LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah. Spelling and punctuation have been modernized.
