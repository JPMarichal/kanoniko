Van Orden, William

(November 15, 1894-July 14, 1844)

Gave "Old Charlie" to the Prophet, the same horse said by the little boy to have taken Joseph and Hyrum to heaven; shared friendship with Joseph, who borrowed quite a little money from Van Orden.

Grand Father William Van Orden and other relatives by the name Haight sold their farms in Moravia in the early part of 1843 and started for Nauvoo going by way of Kirtland, Ohio. He received a good price for his farm. He had a good outfit for the times. He had an extra good team of horses by the name of Mage and Charley this Charley horse is the one that my Grand Father gave to the Prophet Joseph Smith and the horse that the small boy told the officers that Joseph and Hyrum rode up to Heaven. William Van Orden arrived in Nauvoo in the fall of 1843, arriving in Nauvoo Grand Father rented a house for a short time, the he bought a story and a half brick house on Mulholland streetit was quite a nice house for those times. He also bought a farm of 160 acres of land about three miles from Nauvoo along the road to Carthage. The Prophet was at his house many times and he became a very good [friend] to my Grand Father. William Van Orden rented his 160 acre farm to his half brother E. J. Carbine, Grand Father lent quite a little money to the Prophet Joseph Smith. . . . Grand Father joined the Nauvoo Legion and caught cold while on guard at the time of the martyrdom of the Prophet and his brother Hyrum and died on the 14th of July 1844.

Peter Edmund Van Orden, "Incidents in the Life of William Van Orden, My Grandfather," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
