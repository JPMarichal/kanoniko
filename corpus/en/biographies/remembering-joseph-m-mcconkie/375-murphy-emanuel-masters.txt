Murphy, Emanuel Masters

(September 15, 1809-July 23, 1871)

Murphy meets Joseph in Far West and sells him a black horse; Murphy visited Joseph while in jail and gave him a five dollar gold piece; Joseph told him the Saints would be driven to the Rocky Mountains.

[It was] 1834 when two Mormon elders came to his [i.e., Emanuel M. Murphy's] home to preach gospel, and Grandfather listened to them and thought they could not be right; so [he] bought a Bible and was going to prove to them [that they] was wrong, but they stayed several days and before [they] left he said, "You are right. I'm wrong." So he was baptized and began to study [the] gospel and made his home a home for the missionaries. Then [in] 1837 he went to Marion, Caldwell, Missouri to see [the] Prophet and learn more of the gospel and stayed two years. [He] left [his] plantation [in Weakley County, Tennessee] with an overseer [to] run [it]. He was so thrilled he went back home and took [his] wife, [and] children in [a] wagon. [He] went to South Carolina to take the gospel to [his] father and family. [The] History of the Church tells us he was the first man to take the gospel to South Carolina. He stayed six months. [He] visited and preached [the] gospel to them but none of [his] father's family came back with him. He felt later [that he] could not rest till he took the word to them. Was while at (Far West) that he had taken some of five (fine?) [illegible]. [It was while he was] there that [the] Prophet Joseph Smith came to [his] home. [The Prophet] asked him to sell him a black horse he had. [Joseph] said, "My enemies are hard pressing" and he let grandfather [i.e., Emanuel M. Murphy] have enough black broadcloth to make him a suit of clothes and a note for a hundred twenty-five dollars.

One time while (enemies) had Joseph, Hyrum in prison and church leaders could not get in to talk to them, they asked Grandfather to go with a message to them to tell them they [should?] come and get them out. When he got (to the) jail, [he] asked the guards to let (him) [go] in to see Joe Smith. [When they] asked why, he told them he owed him money for a horse and he had to let them see note before he could get in, as Grandfather made out to guards he was angry with him. He had a five dollar gold piece to put (in) [the] Prophet's hand when he shook hands with him. Joseph said, "I don't know when I can pay you for the horse," and Grandfather told him to turn on temple, and while he talked with him that day he said, "The Saints will be driven to the Rocky Mountains."

He told Grandfather to go back to Tennessee and sell out and go with the body of the Saints when they moved, as he knew they would be driven out.

"A Short History of Emanuel Masters Murphy, by a Granddaughter," in Don E. Norton, Emanuel Masters Murphy 1809-1871, Ancestry, Life, Children (Provo, Utah: Stevenson's Genealogical Center, 1980), 20-21.

Tells of visiting the Prophet in Liberty Jail and of Joseph counseling him to warn his relatives in South Carolina and Georgia of the impending war and of the need to gather to Zion.

Meeting in the Tabernacle. Elder E. M. Murphy preached in the morning. He gave an account of his receiving the Gospelhis gathering to Missourihis visit to Joseph in prisonthe advice of Joseph the Prophet to him to go to South Carolina and Georgia and warn his friends of the wrath and desolation impending on the people in that country, and to gather his friends to Zion for the rebellion and war would break out in South Carolina.
