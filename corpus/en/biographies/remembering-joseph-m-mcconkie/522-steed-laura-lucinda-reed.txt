Steed, Laura Lucinda Reed

(May 22, 1828-November 22, 1903 [or 1901])

As a child, she played at feet of the Prophet Joseph; she was held on his lap while he was hiding from his enemies in her father's home; remembered martyrdom.

Laura Lucinda was the wife of Patriarch Thomas Steed. She was fourteenth of sixteen children. Laura's parents received the gospel and were baptized in 1830. They remained staunch supporters of the truth during their lives and assisted in building both the Kirtland and Nauvoo Temples. Her mother in her old age traveled to Salt Lake City as a widow with her two youngest children and was with the company of Saints whose lives were saved by the Lord sending quails to their tent doors after their expulsion from Nauvoo. Laura often played at the feet of the Prophet Joseph Smith and was held on his lap on many occasions when he was a guest of the Reed home while hiding from his enemies. She has often told her children of the eventful days of the martyrdom of the Prophet and Patriarch and how sad the Saints felt on hearing the news.

"Steed, Laura," in Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, 4 vols. (Salt Lake City, Utah: Western Epics, 1971), 3:247-48.

Note: Laura is the daughter of John Reed and Rebecca Bearce. John Reed defended the Prophet Joseph Smith in court; Reed was baptized on September 12, 1830, and ordained a High Priest in Nauvoo. He died in October 1846 in Bonaparte, Van Buren, Iowa. Laura's sister Clarissa married Levi Ward Hancock.
