Hubbard, Marshall Moore

(June 7, 1805-September 28, 1838)

Marshall Hubbard, at command of Joseph, healed of cholera on Zion's Camp by Freeman Nickerson.

In April 1834, he went to Missouri in Zion's Camp; there he was seized with cholera, bereft of his speech and hearing and finally of all sense, when the Prophet Joseph commanded Father Freeman Nickerson to administer to him, promising him restoration to health. He was healed and appeared among his family in August, 1834, but much reduced and very feeble.

"Hubbard, Marshall Moore," typescript copy, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.

Member of Zion's Camp; served as special body guard to Joseph, and sleeping at Joseph's tent door caused a crippling in his arm which he took to the grave.

In 1834 Marshall Hubbard and a number of others accompanied the Prophet Joseph Smith to the camp of Zion in Missouri for the redemption of the same and left his wife and two babies in New York State.

The Prophet told them their sacrifice was accepted and they were entitled to a martyrs crown for they had offered their lives for the redemption of afflicted Zion. .

They were all released and returned to their families, many living as far away as the state of Maine.

When a camp was reorganized on Salt River, Marshall Hubbard was chosen one of three as a special bodyguard to the Prophet, lying nights on his arm at the tent door, rifle in hand. From exposure he contracted a crooked arm, the effects of which he never overcame. Through all these trials he bore a strong testimony of the gospel.
