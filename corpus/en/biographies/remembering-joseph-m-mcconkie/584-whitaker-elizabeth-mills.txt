Whitaker, Elizabeth Mills

(March 7, 1839-June 7, 1937)

Remembers sitting on Joseph's lap, his love for children, and his counsel.

Mrs. Whitaker was born at Douglas, Isle of Mann, March 7, 1839. With her parents, who received the gospel as converts of the late President John Taylor, she emigrated to Nauvoo. Became well acquainted with the Prophet Joseph, as a child; remembers his love for children, sat on his lap, listened to his counsel. There, her father who was a tin craftsman made the forms for the oxen on the Baptismal Font in the Nauvoo Temple. The Prophet and President John Taylor, coming to her father's home, discussed the manner and method of the font and the purpose of the oxen representing the Twelve Tribes of Israel. She remembers vividly the excitement and terrible tragedy at Carthage when the bodies of President Joseph and Hyrum Smith were returned from Carthage to Nauvoo and the marshal music that vividly affected her to the day of her death.

She carried dinner for her father who worked on the Temple and remembers the brethren used to have their guns with them on the walls of the Temple for their protection during its erection. She was acquainted with the mobbings, the drivings, and extermination of the people from Nauvoo. She remembers crossing the river and coming across the plains in Captain Wilkes Company. During this journey she was taken by a band of Sioux Indian Braves and was held for ransom. She settled in Salt Lake Valley, where she shared from that day in 1852 all of the pioneer experiences, hardships, tests and trials of the history of Utah.
