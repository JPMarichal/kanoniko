Burket, George Jr.

(October 18, 1788-March 15, 1871)

Sought habeus corpus for Joseph when he was in Liberty Jail; sent by Joseph on mission in 1836.

I was through all the troubles in Jackson County, Clay, Davis, and Far West Counties, where I stood true to my brethren and the work of God. When the Prophet Joseph and his brethren were in Liberty Jail, Clay County, he wrote a letter to me requesting that I should bring a man and sign a habeus corpus, that their lives might be saved, for, he said, their lives were in danger. I complied with his request and entered into bonds to release Sidney Rigdon from jail; and would cheerfully have laid down my life to have saved their lives.

In 1836, I was called by Joseph, in connection with others, and preached the Gospel in Illinois, baptizing a goodly number and regulating the affairs of the Church in that Sate; then continued my journey to Kirtland, where I received my washings and anointings and returned back to Missouri. In February 1839, I was driven out of Missouri into the State of Illinois.

"Some History of George Burkett Jr. (account written at his own dictation)," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
