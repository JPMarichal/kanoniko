Mendenhall, William

(April 8, 1815-June 3, 1906)

Tells of Joseph's arrest on the charge of complicity in the assassination attempt on Lilburn W. Boggs.

Monday August 8th 1842. General Joseph Smith was arrested upon a requisition of Governor Carlin as the Officers stated in accordance with a process from Governor Reynolds of Missouri upon the affidavit of Ex Governor Lilburn W. Boggs complaining of the said Smith as being an accessory before the fact with an intent to kill made by one O. P. Rockwell on Lilburn W. Boggs on the night of the sixth of May A. D. 1842. Mr. Rockwell was arrested at the same time as principal. There was no Evasion of the officers though the municipal court issued a writ of habeas Corpus according to the constitution of the State article 8 and section 13. This write demanded the bodies of messers Smith and Rockwell to be brought before the aforesaid Court but these officers refused to do so, and finally without complying, they left them in care of the marshal without the original writ by which they were arrested and by which only they could be retained and returned back to Gov. Carlin for further instruction and messers Smith and Rockwell went about their Business. Some two days after the aforesaid officers returned, but Smith and Rockwell were absent. When the officers inquired of the Marshal (brother Sherwood) for the prisoners he said that he had no authority the Spirit was willing but the body was weak and while he slept the prisoners run away. . . .

25th (August, 1842) Joseph Smith preached again from the stand on the 19 chapter of 1st Kings.

"William Mendenhall Memorandum," LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah. Spelling has been modernized.
