Perry, Orin Alonzo

(September 11, 1817-June 1901)

While seriously ill, Perry has a vision, in which he sees Joseph, who lays hands on his head, blesses and heals him of his sickness.

[Bishop Perry] relates the following incident of his experience in the fall of 1893: "I had been confined to my bed for a long time with la grippe, and all efforts for my relief seemed unavailing. I grew daily worse and my sufferings were intolerable. Almost in despair I prayed one night that if my life's mission were ended I might be released to join with my brethren behind the veil. That same night a glorious vision flooded my room with its radiance, and I beheld advancing to my bedside the well known form and features of the Prophet Joseph Smith. Laying his hands upon my head he rebuked the disease, and the pain left me instantly. He spoke to me of a mission yet future, in the redemption of Zion. My spiritual eyes were opened, and I beheld the army of God's strength, the pride of His house, with resurrected beings among them, perfectly organized and equipped; with Joseph at their head. The word was given and a start was made for Zion. I was instantly healed of my sickness, and I hope to see a glorious fulfillment of the vision vouchsafed me."

"Orin Alonzo Perry," in Orson F. Whitney, History of Utah, 4 vols. (Salt Lake City, Utah: G. Q. Cannon & Sons Co., 1904), 4:440.
