Smith, Willard Gilbert

(May 9, 1827-November 21, 1903)

Prophet gave him affectionate, arm over the shoulder counsel; tells of playing with the Prophet and of very tender feelings about the martyrdom; he drove the Prophet's assassins to Carthage.

Father [i.e., Willard] told of occasions when the Prophet would place his arm affectionately about him and drawing him close would give him loving advice and counsel. Father told our family of the Prophet's love for boys, his interest in their sports and activities, how he would take off his coat and join the boys in a game of ball, or other sports. All of which made him become most reverenced by the boys. Father worked on the Nauvoo Temple as a stone cutter and glazier, assisted in making the oxen on which rested the baptismal font and when it was completed was one of the first to receive his blessings therein. Of the awful martyrdom of the Prophet and his brother Hyrum, Father couldn't talk without tears filling his eyes and choking his speech. Father was employed at the livery stable where the team of horses was stabled which carried visitors to and from Carthage Jail. The regular driver being ill, Father was asked to drive the very group to jail which killed the brothers. He heard their vile blasphemous boastings of their intended deed and was utterly helpless. After the murder, Father marched with muffled drum at the head of those who brought their bodies back to Nauvoo.

Cordelia Smith Reeder Ellingford, "Biography of Willard Gilbert Smith," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah, 4.

Note: This is the son of Warren and Amanda Smith, who were at Haun's Mill. Willard, years later, conversed with the mobber who killed his brother Sardius and the mobber sought Willard to kill him and thus put him out of his misery for the murder. Willard refused.
