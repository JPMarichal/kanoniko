Mark L. McConkie, Remembering Joseph (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Company, 2003)

Cannon, David Henry Sr.

(April 23, 1838-December 24, 1924)

Tells of his father's reaction to the martyrdom; his father built the drag that carried the prophet's body and made the death masks for Joseph and Hyrum.

At the time of the death of the Prophet Joseph Smith, I remember my father standing at the gate at the front of the house, his arms kind of leaning on the gate; he turned and as he did so said, "My God, they have killed our Prophet." That was the time the Prophet was martyred. He made the drag that they brought the body in on. At the time that the Prophet and his brother Hyrum were laying in state, my father was the one who made the death mask of the two. I remember going with my father at the time that this took place. A lock of the prophet's hair was caught in the plaster mask, and I remember father taking some scissors and clipping the hair and then giving me the scissors to hold while he went on with this work. Of course, I was only a youngster, six years old in April, and the prophet was killed the 27th of June. However, I remember distinctly holding the scissors at the time father was removing the mask.
