Wall, Nancy Haws

(August 23, 1823-May 18, 1904)

Present at the transfiguration of Brigham Young; her husband one of the few ever to throw Joseph in a wrestling match.

On the 12th of Nov., 1845, the Walls [i.e., Nancy, and her husband, William Madison Wall] were driven by a mob from their home at Ramus (Macedonia Branch), Hancock County, Illinois, and with the main body of the Saints to Council Bluffs, Iowa. Two of their children were born there. Nancy attended the conference held in the log tabernacle at Council Bluffs, Dec. 27, 1847, when Brigham Young was chosen to take Joseph Smith's place as President of the Church. She was one of the many who testified that the mantle of Joseph fell on Brigham Young, so much so, that even his voice sounded like the Prophet Joseph. They were both active members of the newfound religion and were personally acquainted with the Prophet Joseph Smith, before his martyrdom. Several times the Prophet Joseph and William M. Wall engaged in a friendly wrestle. Both were large, strong men over six feet tall and were well matched. It is said that Brother Wall was the only man who ever threw the Prophet.

Garth D. Mangum, William Madison Wall, His Life and Family (N.p.), 166-67.

Note: In telling the story Mangum seems to wed Brigham's transfiguration and the conference in which he was sustained as President of the Church; President Young was sustained on December 24-27, 1847, and confirmed by the conference of the Church April 6, 1848, at Kanesville, Iowa. Still, there is no reason to doubt that Nancy was in attendance at both meetings, in spite of the imprecise language of Mangum as an oral historian. For an account of Brigham being sustained as President of the Church, see B. H. Roberts, Comprehensive History of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, 6 vols. [Salt Lake City, Deseret News Press, 1930], 3:315-17.
