Pace, James

(b. June 15, 1811)

Met Joseph in 1839, who told him to live in Nauvoo; testifies of Joseph's purity.

The second of the following June [1839] he started with his family for Nauvoo arriving on the thirteenth of June and was introduced to the Prophet Joseph Smith. He was counseled to locate in that City which he did and soon after commenced work on the Temple. He soon became identified with the Nauvoo Police Force which position he served until the expulsion of the Saints from Nauvoo in 1846.

. . . In the autobiography of James Pace is found this statement: "From my first introduction to these men (Joseph and Hyrum Smith), until their martyrdom, I was an eye witness to most of their sufferings, and can faithfully testify that they were innocent of the charges brought against them, in every respect."

"Biography of James Pace," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
