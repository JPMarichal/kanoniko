Luddington, Elam

(November 23, 1806-March 22, 1893)

Tells of meeting Joseph Smith, being baptized and confirmed by him, and of later being counseled by Joseph to go on a mission to Quincy, Illinois.

A short history of my life and travels or missions since I became a Latter day Saint. I became acquainted partially (with) the work of the Saints of God (in) these last days in the winter of 1840. I embraced the first opportunity to see the Prophet on the 1st of May. Brother Joseph baptized me and confirmed me in Nauvoo, in the fall of 1841. (I was) ordained an Elder. For near three years I lived in New Orleans. I preached and exhorted the people to repent and be baptized for the Kingdom of God was at hand, (and) was often [illegible] and pelted with rotten eggs (which they) throwed at us. They [illegible] the seats burned them, broke in the floors and windows, and broke our meetings up till we were obliged to flee to a remote part of the City. Afterwards (we) held private meetings (as) a small branch of the Church of 16 members. . . . Being counseled by Brother Joseph and Hyrum I took a short mission to Quincy on Church business in June, 1844 . . .

"A Short History of Elam Luddington's Life, and Travels on Missions, Oct. 16, 1845," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah. Spelling, punctuation, and grammar have been modernized.
