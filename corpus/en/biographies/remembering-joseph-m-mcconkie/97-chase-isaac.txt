Chase, Isaac

(December 12, 1791-May 2, 1861)

Children baptized in Mississippi by Joseph; when daughter was ill, Joseph sent a handkerchief and she was healed; his wife set apart by Joseph to administer to the sick.

The two younger children of Isaac and Phoebe [his wife] were baptized by the Prophet Joseph, in the Mississippi River when George was 10 and Luisa 8. Isaac and Phoebe were sealed in the Nauvoo Temple Dec. 13, 1845 as husband and wife, where they worked day and night. During the epidemic of winter fever, their daughter Sylvia was stricken. She was very sick and they sent for the Prophet to come and administer to her but he was so busy with other sick people that he was unable to go. He sent his handkerchief and told her to hold it and it would be the same as a personal blessing. This she did and her fever left her and she began to get well immediately.

Phoebe Chase was a member of the first relief society of the church, though not present on the day that it was organized by Joseph Smith. She was set apart and ordained (women are not now ordained) by the Prophet to administer to the sick. She had the gift of healing sealed upon her. She was very faithful to this calling and many miraculous healings resulted. At one time her husband Isaac was so ill his life was despaired of. She went out under the trees alone and prayed for him and in a few moments he began to get better and soon recovered.

"History of Isaac Chase," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
