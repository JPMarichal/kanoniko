McDonald, William

(November 16, 1834-November 2, 1921)

Sailed up river on Maid of Iowa, which, because of overloading, broke down; during repairs, anti-Mormons boarded and harassed the Saints, calling them "old Joe's rats" and even setting fire to the ship; arriving in Nauvoo they were met by the Prophet; William McDonald's father gave Joseph money belonging to the Liverpool Branch.

We sailed from Liverpool, England, and landed at New Orleans. The Prophet Joseph had a steam boat there to take the passengers off the ship and up the Mississippi river to Nauvoo and as the boat belonged to the Prophet the whole ship crew on which we sailed wanted to ride on it; they got aboard of the boat and overloaded it so that it broke down often in going up the river and we were six weeks in making the trip that other steamboats made in one week. People knew the boat belonged to the Prophet and that we passengers were Mormons and they came on board when we were getting repairs and abused us will all the mean sayings they could think of. Among other things they called us "old Joe's rats." At one time they set the boat on fire but the fire was detected and no damage was done. We were met by the Prophet and he blessed the people and spoke words of encouragement to them.

My father was entrusted with some money sent to the Prophet by our branch and in presenting it to him got personally acquainted with him. We lived in Nauvoo two years and were driven out of there with the balance of the Saints after the Prophet and Patriarch were martyred.

"Autobiography of William McDonald," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
