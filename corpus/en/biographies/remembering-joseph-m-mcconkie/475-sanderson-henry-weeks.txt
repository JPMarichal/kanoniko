Sanderson, Henry Weeks

(March 13, 1829-November 1896)

His father shucked corn for the Prophet, and he played with the Prophet's sons; sometimes the Prophet joined in the play; he saw Joseph leave for Carthage, exchange canes with a stranger, and he later saw the martyred bodies.

From Pittsburgh to Nauvoo we went by steam boat arriving at Nauvoo the summer of 1843. When we arrived the basement of the temple was up. Everyone was very poor as all were refugees. My father took odd jobs, one being the shucking of corn for the Prophet Joseph Smith. We lived near the prophet. [I] became a playmate of the prophet's sons. We ate bread sprinkled with salt. Each tenth day father and I labored on the temple. . . .

In the winter I was hired as a shoemaker to learn the trade. We lived in a two story log house second from the Prophet's. Sidney Rigdon's house being in between. I was baptized just before the Prophet's death and he helped confirm me. I had played with his boys and oftimes the prophet would join us so it seemed natural for him to help confirm me. I saw him leave the house the last time and saw him exchange canes with a stranger. I also saw the murdered bodys brought home. His death left everyone confused in ideas and what to do.

"History of Henry Weeks Sanderson," LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
