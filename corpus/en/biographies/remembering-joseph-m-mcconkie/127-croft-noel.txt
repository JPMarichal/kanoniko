Mark L. McConkie, Remembering Joseph (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Company, 2003)

Croft, Noel

(May 22, 1911-April 7, 2001)

Heber J. Grant tells of Sidney Rigdon's early involvement with Joseph, then testifies that his mother [Rachel Ridgeway Grant] was present at the transfiguration of Brigham Young, August 8, 1844.

[In 1972 Noel B. Croft collected information about Sidney Rigdon, including a letter written April 5, 1932 by Heber J. Grant to Samuel Marshall Ellis, a grandson of Sidney Rigdon, from which the following paragraph is extracted:]

Sidney Rigdon was one of the original leading men in the Church of Jesus Christ or Latter-day Saints. The persecutions in Missouri and Illinois were so strong that he left the Church and returned to his home in the East. After the martyrdom of the Prophet Joseph Smith, Sidney Rigdon came to Nauvoo and claimed that he ought to be appointed guardian of the Church. His claim was not recognized and Brigham Young was made the leader of the Church, he being the president of the Twelve Apostles. At the time of Sidney Rigdon's making this claim Brigham Young's speech following that of Sidney Rigdon caused a sensation because he was transfigured before the congregation and spoke with the voice of Joseph Smith, although there was no resemblance between the two men. My mother and many others who were present on that occasion have borne witness to me of this transfiguration.

"Heber J. Grant to Samuel Marshall Ellis, April 5, 1932," in Noel B. Croft Collection, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
