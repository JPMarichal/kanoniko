Mark L. McConkie, Remembering Joseph (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Company, 2003)

Merrill, Clarance

(May [or December] 18, 1841-February 21, 1918)

As a young child remembers seeing the Prophet in Nauvoo; Joseph wore a swallow tail coat and carried a cane.

I went to Nauvoo, Illinois, with my parents, arrived there the day I was three years old. The first I remember was on the railroad going to Nauvoo and then on the steamboat where some men had some money in a chest that they took turns in guarding, $600 dollars . . . belonging to father. On arriving at Nauvoo, we moved into a room in Dr. Willard Richards' house. Father soon purchased a lot with a log house which was whitewashed on the outside. While staying at Richards' I saw the Potowatamie Indians have a dance; they were dressed in gay colored clothes. The Prophet Joseph Smith gave them a beef steer for their feast. Soon after moving into our new home I was hanging to Mother's dress while she was conversing with some neighbor woman, when she said, "There's the Prophet." I looked in the direction she said and saw Brother Joseph coming out of a neighbor's house. He looked down the street towards us, then up south and walked off that way. I can see him as he appeared to me then, he was quite tall, wore a swallow-tail coat and had a cane in his hand. He was then in hiding from his enemies.

"Clarance Merrill, Pioneer," in Kate B. Carter, comp. Our Pioneer Heritage, 20 vols. (Salt Lake City, Utah: Daughters of the Utah Pioneers, 1958-77), 9:319-20.
