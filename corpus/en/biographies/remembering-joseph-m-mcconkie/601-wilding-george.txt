Wilding, George

(November 9, 1829-July 26, 1913)

In a prophesy which later came true, Joseph prophesied George Wilding would be a great blessing to his family; after the Prophet's murder, Mary Wilding saw them lift his body onto the cart while the blood fell to the ground.

About the year 1841 he and his family emigrated to the United States and settled in Nauvoo. Soon after their arrival they became personally acquainted with the Prophet Joseph Smith. One day the Prophet placed his hand upon the head of my father, George Wilding (who was the son of David Wilding), and turned to his father David Wilding and made the statement that George would be a great blessing to his family. This was later proved to be true as George Wilding was the only Widling who remained in the church. David Wilding was employed as a carpenter and his son George as a stone mason, both working on the Temple at Nauvoo. George Wilding emigrated to Utah in the Gardiner Handcart company in 1852, with his wife and small baby.

My mother, Mary Elizabeth Layne Wilding, was a witness at the time the Prophet Joseph Smith was shot at Liberty Jail [Carthage Jail]. She saw them lift his body from the ground and place it on a cart, and saw his blood dripping from the cart as they carried the body away. George Wilding's father, David, came to Salt Lake a few years after George and his family and made their home on the corner of First North and Fifth West streets. He lived there only a short time and then left for Nauvoo again where he joined the Josephite church.

"Statement of Henry David Wilding," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah.
