Smith, John

(June 16, 1781-May 23, 1854)

Uncle John tells of a visit of Father Smith to his family in New York and of Jesse's objections to talk of Book of Mormon.

We [i.e., Joseph Smith Sr., and his brother, John] proceeded to the house, found he had had a fit but was recoveringit was thought advisable not to [let] father or mother know that Joseph was there. We returned to Brother Jesse's to stay over night. There Brother Joseph [and] Jesse entered into conversation respecting the condition of their farm. This [illegible] Joseph briefly related the history of his family and the death of his older son [illegible] and began to speak of the discovery and translation of the Book of Mormon. Jesse fiercely exclaimed "if you mention one word about the Book of Mormon you shall not stay a minute in my house. If I can't get you out any other way I will hew you down with my broad ax." The next morning we visited our father and mother. They were overjoyed to see brother Joseph and after the usual salutation the subject of the Book of Mormon was agitated. My father received it with gladness; said he had always known that some thing would appear to make known the true religion. In a few minutes Brother Jesse came in and he said "My father's mind is weak and I will not have it corrupted with that blasphemous stuff so just shut up your head." Brother Joseph reasoned mildly with him but to no purpose. Brother Silas said "Our brother has come to make us a visit and I am glad to see him and am willing he should talk as he pleases in my house." Jesse flew in a rage and talked so abusively that Silas was under the necessity of requesting him to leave the house. After Jesse had left my brother Joseph then proceeded in conversation [with] father [illegible] to believe all that he said but I must confess that I was too. My parents lived with Silas believe [?] one word of it at that time he continued with my father one week during which time Jesse came to my house and said that all my brothers viz. Joseph, Asael and Silas are coming to make you or [illegible] (as illegible) true as you live they will believe [illegible] they are setting a [illegible] [illegible] Mormon book every word [illegible] for you to make [Jos?] believe it. I thanked him kindly for taking so much trouble to warn me but I considered myself amply capable to judge for myself in such matters [illegible] know [illegiblesaid?] he you are a pretty good judge of such things but I tell you they are [illegible] [illegible] as the [illegible] devil I want you to go with me and see sister Susan and Aunt [Mary?] and we will bar their minds . . .
