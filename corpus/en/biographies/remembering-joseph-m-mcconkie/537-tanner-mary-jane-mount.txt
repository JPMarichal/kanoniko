Mark L. McConkie, Remembering Joseph (Salt Lake City: Deseret Book Company, 2003)

Tanner, Mary Jane Mount

(February 27, 1834-1890)

Often saw Joseph; remembers the mourning caused by the martyrdom, though but ten.

I often saw the Prophet Joseph Smith and his brother Hyrum, although I was too young to have a personal acquaintance with them; and I remember well the common grief when news came of their death, and a cry of mourning arose from the hearts of the people. It was a dreadful blow when their Prophet and Patriarch were murdered in cold blood. For days a concourse of people crowded to see them, each striving for a last look at those they had so loved and revered, and many went away disappointed.

Stratagem was used to keep their bodies from their enemies and few, I suppose, know even now where their honored ashes are reposing.

. . . Reader, did you ever have every earthly tie severed, leaving you in your weakness to trust in the Lord? Did you ever feel the anguish of knowing that of yourself you were entirely helpless, that your wisdom was as naught, and your strength could not avail?

I think that however pious we may be, however great our faith, we would rather see some tangible means of escaping evil, than rely on an invisible hand. Perhaps we all have that feeling sometimes in our lives.
