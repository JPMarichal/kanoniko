Henrie, James

(September 18, 1827-February 12, 1916)

Henrie's father a friend of the Prophet, who frequently visited the Henrie home, where he was often given buttermilk or baked potatoes; Joseph borrowed a Henrie horse for John Taylor to ride to Carthage.

We left our home in the spring of 1841 in company with two men, and traveled by land in company with our teams to Nauvoo, had some hardships to pass through, such as mud, cold streams and high water. I nearly choked to death for water on the prairies; once I got a splendid drink out of a tar bucket that [I] had filled crossing a river. I don't know of anything in life that I was more thankful for than that water.

We arrive at Nauvoo all right and helped to build it up, and improve on a farm we bought of the Prophet Joseph. I became acquainted with him as he frequently stopped at our home. Mother would give him a drink of buttermilk or a baked potato which he would enjoy very much while resting.

While Nauvoo was under martial law I stood guard although I was quite young. I well remember the Prophet Joseph, his brother Hyrum, Brother Taylor and Brother Richards [when they] went to Carthage. He borrowed one of our horses for Brother Taylor to ride. I remember well how they looked when they passed our house.

I can't describe my feelings when I heard they were killed by the mob. I saw them after they were dead. I saw the Prophet in his uniform and heard his last speech to the Nauvoo Legion. I have received blessings from his lips that made every hair on my head stand on end. I know that he was a man of God and a true Prophet. I loved him the best of any one I ever knew.

"A Short Sketch of the Life of James Henrie, Son of William and Myra Mayall Henrie," dictated to his sister Effie Heywood, 15 April 1902; copies in private family possession.
