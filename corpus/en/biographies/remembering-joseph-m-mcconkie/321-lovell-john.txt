Lovell, John

(March 6, 1812-January 13, 1881)

On first meeting Joseph, he was surprised that Joseph spoke of horse trading on the Sabbath; upon hearing Joseph preach, his pious notions changed.

Brother Taylor explained the gospel to them telling them of Joseph Smith, the plates, the priesthood, the persecution of the Saints, etc. Soon after this Joseph Smith, Sidney Rigdon, John Taylor, and Almon Babbitt came and stayed over Sunday, and held two or three meetings, and did missionary work in that vicinity.

John went to see the Prophet at Mr. Lawrence's home. When he first saw the Prophet he was telling how he obtained his horse in Kirtland, Ohio. The other brethren were washing and blacking their shoes. In his journal, John says: "I had been brought up so strict to the religion of the day that I thought it impossible for a Prophet to talk about horse trades on Sunday. But their preaching overbalanced any bad effects this may have made." John prayed fervently for a testimony of the work. He gained one by a sign. One night while sleeping deeply he heard a voice say, "See this!" He saw a light pass from one corner of the room to the other and disappear. This satisfied him and he was baptized in February, 1837.

"History of John Lovell," Mormon Biography File, LDS Church Archives, Salt Lake City, Utah, 3.
