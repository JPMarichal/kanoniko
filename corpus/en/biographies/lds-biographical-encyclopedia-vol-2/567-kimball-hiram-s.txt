Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 2 ( 1914)

Kimball, Hiram S.

Kimball, Hiram S., an Elder who died away from home as a missionary, was born May 31, 1806, in West Fairlee, Orange county, Vermont, the son of Phineas and Abigail Kimball. He was baptized July 20, 1843, by Eli Maguin and came to Utah in 1850. March 1, 1863, he was set apart for a mission to the Sandwich Islands under the hands of Apostles John Taylor and Wilford Woodruff, and the following day he left Salt Lake City for his field of labor in company with Elder Thomas Atkinson. On April 27, 1863, at San Pedro, Cal., the two missionaries boarded the "Ada Hancock", a small steamer employed as a tender in carrying passengers from the wharf to the steamer "Senator", which was at anchor in deep water, five miles from the landing. While making the voyage April 27, 1863, the "Ada Hancock" boiler exploded, killing forty of the passengers on board, among whom were the two Elders. They were the first Elders of the Church who lost their lives by such accident on land or on water.
