Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 2 ( 1914)

Dye, Richard

Dye, Richard, a prominent Elder in the Weber Stake of Zion, was born Jan. 2, 1832, at Hertford, Hertfordshire, England, the son of Thomas Dye and Sarah Gower. He was baptized Aug. 23, 1852, by William Hart; ordained a Teacher in 1853 by Charles W. Penrose; ordained a Priest in 1854 by E. L. T. Harrison; emigrated to America in 1856, crossing the Atlantic in the ship "Horizon"; stopped awhile in the States and presided over the Saints in Charleston, near Boston, Mass., being ordained an Elder by James F. Clary in 1856. He came to Utah in 1858, crossing the plains in a small company of Danish Saints in charge of Iver N. Iverson. On the journey they encountered Col. Johnston's army. Bro. Dye located near Ogden in October, 1857, and married Mary M. Peck, by whom he had several children, some of whom have become prominent in the Church. Bro. Dye followed farming and fruit-raising and filled a number of civil offices. Thus he acted for ten years as justice of the peace, and for many years as assessor and collector for school taxes. For forty years he acted as Ward choir leader and for thirty-one years as Sunday school superintendent.
