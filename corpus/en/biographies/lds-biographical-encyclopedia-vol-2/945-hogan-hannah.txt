Hogan, Hannah

Hogan, Hannah, wife of Eric G. M. Hogan, was born June 10, 1834, in the province of SkÃ¥ne, Sweden, the daughter of Carl Nilson. She joined the Church when twenty-one years of age and emigrated to Utah in 1862, crossing the Atlantic in the ship "Humboldt" and the plains in an independent company, arriving in Salt Lake City Oct. 2, 1862. Becoming acquainted with Elder Eric G. M. Hogan, she became his plural wife in December 1862, and settled in Bountiful, Davis co., where she gave birth to five children, namely, Hyrum, Josephine, Ephraim, Amelia M. and Chas. F. Only two of these children are now alive. Sister Hogan became a widow in 1876, but continued in her widowhood a faithful and earnest worker for the Church, raising her children in the fear of the Lord. She was an ardent Relief Society worker and was a teacher in that organization for thirty years. By carpet and cloth weaving she earned enough to sustain her family. Thousands of yards of her handiwork were used to decorate the floors of the homes of the saints in Bountiful and in other places. When Sister Hogan crossed the plains in 1862, she walked all the way and cooked food for six people on the journey.
