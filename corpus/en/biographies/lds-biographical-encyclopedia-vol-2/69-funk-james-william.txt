Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 2 ( 1914)

Funk, James William

Funk, James William, first counselor to the Bishop of Richmond Ward, Cache county, was born at that place Feb. 19, 1874. His father's name was Christopher Funk and his mother's, Annie Kofoed. His status in the Church began with his baptism June 1, 1882, at his birthplace, and he became a Seventy Nov. 17, 1897, and a High Priest April 30, 1900, C. D. Fjeldsted officiating on the former and Francis M. Lyman on the latter occasion. Other ecclesiastical stations held by him were Sunday school teacher, president of the Y. M. M. I. A., aid in Cache Stake M. I. A., and presiding teacher of the High Priests' quorum in Richmond Ward. Besides these, he has been deputy county treasurer of Cache county, justice of the peace, member of the city council and is at present mayor of the city. He was on a mission to the southern States from Nov. 17, 1897, to Dec. 18, 1899, beginning in Mississippi; and when work in Georgia was begun, was transferred to that conference. Later, when Ohio was made part of that mission, he was sent there and became president of the Ohio conference. He was married on June 20, 1900, to Lucy Merrill, and has two children, a son and daughter. He has served three years in the Utah militia, being first sergeant the latter part of the time; has been manager of the Richmond Co-operative Institution since 1902, and has followed the occupation of clerk and farmer.
