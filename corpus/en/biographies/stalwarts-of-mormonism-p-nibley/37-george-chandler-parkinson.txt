Preston Nibley, Stalwarts of Mormonism ( 1954)

George Chandler Parkinson

George Chandler Parkinson was a native son of Utah, a pioneer of Franklin, Idaho, in 1860, missionary to the Southern States in 1881, missionary to England in 1882, counselor in the Oneida Stake presidency in 1884, president of Oneida Stake in 1887, and first president of the Northwestern States Mission in 1897.

George C. Parkinson was born at Kaysville, Utah, on July 18, 1857. He was the son of Samuel and Arabella Chandler Parkinson, both of whom were born in England.

In the fall of 1860 Samuel Parkinson moved with his family to Franklin, Idaho, and there George C. grew to manhood. His early life was spent on a farm, but when he was nineteen years of age he went to Logan and entered the Brigham Young College. He was graduated from that institution in 1880 with honors, and was awarded a teacher's certificate.

On April 14, 1881, George C. Parkinson was married to Lucy Maria Doney of Franklin, Idaho. Shortly afterwards he departed for a mission to the Southern States. He remained in the South one year and was then transferred to England, where he completed his mission in July, 1883.

On his return home, he became the principal of the Franklin School. When the Oneida Stake was organized at Franklin on June 1, 1884, George C. Parkinson was chosen as one of the presidency. In 1885 he was elected Superintendent of Schools in Oneida County.

When Oneida Stake was reorganized in August, 1887, George C. Parkinson was chosen president with Solomon H. Hale and Mathias F. Cowley as counselors. In 1894 he was elected to the Idaho State Senate. It was during this time that he introduced the bill giving the franchise to the women of Idaho. In 1895 he was appointed one of the Regents of the University of Idaho. He held this position for four terms and became the president of the board.

During the years 1890 to 1894 President Parkinson gave his time and means towards the erection of a stake academy at Preston. When this building was completed at a cost of $50,000 it became one of the leading educational institutions of Idaho.

The Northwestern States Mission was organized in July 1897, with headquarters at Baker City, Oregon. George C. Parkinson was chosen as the first president. He established the Idaho, Oregon, and Washington Conferences and directed the labors of the elders, until his release in June, 1898.

After serving twenty-three years as president of Oneida Stake, George C. Parkinson was given an honorable release in 1910. The following year he moved to Salt Lake City, where he made his home for almost ten years. He died at his residence at 623 First Avenue on July 6, 1920. Had he lived two weeks longer he would have reached his sixty-third birthday. At his funeral held in the Twenty-first Ward Chapel on Friday, July 10, Elder Orson F. Whitney paid him this tribute:

"I have known George C. Parkinson intimately for thirty-eight years. I have never seen or known anything in his character or spirit that was contrary to the character and spirit of a man of God. He was a clean man, a pure-hearted, devoted, diligent servant of God."

George C. Parkinson left a distinguished family who have honored his name.
