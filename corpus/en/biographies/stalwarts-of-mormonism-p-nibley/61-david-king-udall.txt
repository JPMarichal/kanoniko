Preston Nibley, Stalwarts of Mormonism ( 1954)

David King Udall

David King Udall was a pioneer of Utah, early settler at Nephi, first bishop of St. Johns, Arizona, first president of St. Johns Stake, and first president of the Arizona Temple.

David K. Udall was born at St. Louis Missouri, on September 7, 1851. He was the son of David and Eliza King Udall, converts to the Church from England.

The family crossed the plains to Utah in 1852 and located at Nephi. Here David grew to manhood. On February 1, 1875, he was married to Eliza Luella Stewart. Six weeks later he was called on a mission to Great Britain. He served with honor in this capacity and was released to return home in June, 1877. In January, 1878, he was set apart as second counselor to Bishop John Andrews, bishop of Nephi First Ward.

In the fall of 1878 David K. Udall moved with his family to Kanab. Two years later he was called by President John Taylor to settle in Arizona and preside as bishop of St. Johns Ward. He was set apart for this position on January 5, 1880, by Erastus Snow. David arrived at St. Johns on October 6, 1880, and built one of the first houses on the new townsite.

When the Eastern Arizona Stake was divided, on July 23, 1887, two new stakes, St. Johns and Snowflake, were formed. David K. Udall was selected as the president of St. Johns Stake. At that time it consisted of seven wards: St. Johns, Ramah, Erastus, Union, Nutrioso, Alpine, and Heber. The stake population was about one thousand four hundred persons.

Always eager to advance the cause of education, President Udall was instrumental in establishing the St. Johns Stake Academy, which opened its doors on January 14, 1889. The school held its first sessions in the Tithing Office, but in a few years a new building was begun. This building was completed in 1900 and dedicated by President Joseph F. Smith on December 16 of that year.

On that occasion President Udall remarked, "My heart is full of joy that I have lived to see this day."

From 1881 to 1885 President Udall served as president and manager of a mercantile institution in St. Johns. In 1889 he purchased a large farm with a grist mill, located at Eagar in Round Valley. During the same year he was elected a member of the Arizona Territorial Legislature.

President Udall served faithfully as president of St. Johns Stake from July, 1887, to April, 1922, a period of thirty-five years. When he was released, his son, Levi S. Udall, was appointed in his place. On April 30, 1922, David K. Udall was ordained a patriarch in St. Johns Stake.

When the Arizona Temple was completed at Mesa, in 1927, David K. Udall was selected as its first president. He served faithfully in this capacity until 1934, a period of seven years.

David K. Udall died at St. Johns, the town which he had assisted in founding, on February 18, 1938. He was eighty-six years of age. He was honored, respected, and beloved by all who knew him.
