Andrew Jenson, LDS Biographical Encyclopedia, vol. 1 ( 1901)

Heiner, Daniel

Heiner, Daniel, third president of the Morgan Stake of Zion, is the son of Martin and Adel Heiner, and was born Nov. 27, 1850, in Franklin county, Penn. He was baptized April 11, 1859, and emigrated to Utah the same year, crossing the plains with ox-teams. During the first two winters in Utah he subsisted partly on bran bread. He moved to Morgan county in 1863, but spent most of his time on the ranch and at other occupations, and he never had much opportunity for attending school. In 1873 he married two wives, with whom he has lived happily and raised nineteen children, namely thirteen boys and six girls, all of whom are living, and are faithful members of the Church. Pres. Heiner was a member of the first State legislature and has always taken great interest in the affairs and progress of the State of Utah. For a long time he was an active Sunday school worker. He was set apart as president of the Morgan Stake Sept. 13, 1900, by Apostle Geo. Teasdale, as successor to the late Richard Fry, and he is to-day filling that responsible position. Pres. Heiner is noted for his wise and good counsel and is loved and respected by all who know him. He ascribes a great deal of his success in life to a good parentage; his father and mother were both good people who raised their children in the fear of the Lord.
