Adam and Eve

Well, it's lucky for me we have the greatest of all chapters today. I have to give a talk tonight and another one tomorrow. I've taken every known kind of antibiotic and antihistamine. I don't know whether I'm coming or going. But fortunately we have a chapter for which everybody can be held responsible. It's one of the really great documents in religious history. It goes along with the first chapter of Moses, the first chapter of the Book of Mormon, and D&C 76 of the Doctrine and Covenants. It's one of the great ones, one of the most instructive documents in the world. Nothing can help you out so much and answer more questions than this fifth chapter of Moses. It's a pretty long one too, you notice. I'm just going to go through it and marvel.

As it starts out, Adam and Eve have left the Garden of Eden. There is a great literature about the conditions of things under which they lived when they left it-the darkness they found themselves in. They had been cast out into another world, into another life. Life became short and miserable, and Adam didn't know whether he would ever get out of it again. The lights went out. When he saw the sun go down, he had no assurance the sun would ever rise again. He was utterly depressed. Then he comes and receives the instruction from the angel that tells him he doesn't have to remain there, it doesn't have to be that way. But at first, it is a lone and dreary world. Being driven out, he began to till the earth and have dominion over the beasts. Notice the distinction there. The King James Version translates that word kavash "to subdue," so he has to subdue everything. It's his prerogative to go out and slaughter anything he wants to because he is the lord and master of the earth. But it doesn't say that at all. My father believed that firmly. But properly translated, the word kavash means "to hoe, to till the earth," but "to have dominion over the beasts." Well, dominion is lordship, to be a dominus, to be a lord (I will make you lord over all things; I will give you dominion and make you lord over all things on the face of the earth). The lord is the hlafweard, the "loaf ward," the keeper of the loaf, the ward. A ward is a guardian, a keeper.

To have dominion is the Latin word to be a dominus. The domus is the "house." The dominus is the lord who is the master of the house and is responsible for all the creatures in the house. Just as the hlafweard, the "loaf ward" is the one to see to it that all family, all connections, all members of the estate, all dependents are properly fed and cared for. The same with the dominus. He has charge of the domus. At the end of the comedy [we get this still back in Aristophanes], he invites all the world to come and feast at his board. As the dominus, that's his obligation. We think of it as a license to exterminate. You are made lord of everything. You are given dominion and made responsible. You till the earth. Kavash is "subdue" all right.

Well, of course, you till the earth, but over the creatures you have dominion. And "to eat his bread by the sweat of his brow." He is the bread maker. This is a very important doctrine of the Ebionites and other early Christian sects that wheat is the food of fallen man. They would not eat wheat because it was forbidden. It was the sign of falling. As we say, Wheat is for man in his fallen condition. Wheat is for a semi-arid area. It's to be cultivated by the sweat of the brow. It's a staple for man. It's the one he must work to produce. When he is outcast, he lives on wheat. When he was in the garden, he was a fruit gatherer. He lived on whatsoever trees from which he would freely eat. The gathering of fruits and berries is a pleasant sort of thing. But this is wheat; this is an economy. This ties him down to the earth. And by the sweat of his brow he has to live now. He is cast out into a semi-arid area. He was in a lush garden, but when he goes out, he has to hump just to keep alive. The sweat of his brow is no mere a figure of speech because he is in a land of thistles and all sorts of noxious weeds, semi-arid plants. You have to cultivate if you are going to get anywhere. Briars, thorns, weeds. As Tennyson says, "Wearying in a land of sand and thorns." So here is Adam, and it's not a happy life, particularly. And his wife did labor with him. The same word is used for both of them. It was his curse that he would have to labor all the days of his life to bring forth from the earth. Exactly the same word is used for Eve. She would have to labor to bring forth offspring. Nevertheless, her life would be spared. They both have the same labor. Eve is under no worse a curse than Adam is. He is under the same. So they begin to have families and multiply and replenish the earth, which had been the commandment. The Lord had commanded all creatures, every form of life, to fulfill the measure of its existence and have joy therein, and also to reproduce, multiply and replenish the earth. This is before we get Cain and Abel. They begin to multiply, and, as you know, populations multiply very fast when there is nothing to restrain them. It's an astonishing thing. The population of Egypt has gone up and down phenomenally. Central America, the same thing, up and down. One generation would be eight or nine million; the next in the thirties or forties. My friend Woodrow Bora has spent his life studying population statistics in Central America. It's amazing how rapidly they grow. We find the same thing now. We find so many species, like the sea otter, that are almost completely exterminated. As soon as they are given protection, they just come back like that. You find them all over the place. It's a funny thing. Give animals half a chance and they will replenish the earth.

So they began to replenish the earth. They divided two and two. You notice they are not practicing polygamy here. They divide two and two. As you know from statistics, the birth rates are about equal for male and female in various species. So they divide two and two in the land to tend flocks and also beget sons and daughters. You notice that Adam had lived with the beasts, and now they are pastoral. Pastoral and agricultural always go together. We used to think there was originally a pastoral phase, and then they went into an agricultural phase, etc. That's not so at all. Everything shows a difference today. The two go together from the first, and they go naturally. You begin first with the hunting phase, and then with a pastoral phase, and then with agriculture. But you have all three together wherever you go. If you hunt the animals, you are herding them actually. If you are following the herds, you get to know each other. There are many examples of that. You pick up the strays, the calves, etc. They become family pets. You hunt and you also herd, and at the same time you get whatever kind of vegetation you need: fruits and vegetables, grains wherever you can pick them up.

After the last prophets, the rabbis didn't get inspiration, but they did have the bath-kol. They could hear the voice. They could hear the echo. You could have inspiration, intuition, etc. (not face to face anymore, but the bath-kol). Adam and Eve were cut off from his presence, but they had the bath-kol.
